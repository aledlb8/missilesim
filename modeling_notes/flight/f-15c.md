# F-15C flight model

Point-mass and six-degree data for a single-seat F-15C with F100 engines and no conformal tanks. The shape is [fighters/f-15c.md](../fighters/f-15c.md). This file does not change game code. The sim still flies the NASA F-16. No F-16 coefficient, inertia, or thrust table is copied here.

Tags: OFFICIAL (USAF, Boeing, Pratt & Whitney, or the National Museum of the U.S. Air Force), PRIMARY (NASA report), WIKI-ONLY, DERIVED (arithmetic on numbers printed in this file), NOT PUBLISHED. Nothing was averaged. No chart was digitized. A Mach number printed without both an altitude and a configuration is not a flight-model limit.

## Status

BROCHURE_ONLY. The F-15C card is fact-sheet weights, spans, and thrust statements. No opened page prints an F-15C drag polar, Cd0, Oswald factor, CLmax, Mach-drag table, structural-g schedule, angle-of-attack limit in degrees, roll-rate limit, spool time, or an installed military and afterburner thrust pair for one named F100 dash. NASA pages that do print numbers are geometry, a 3/8-scale model's inertias, wind-tunnel repeatability, or PW1128 and F100-PW-100 engine simulations. Their coefficient plots were left as plots.

## Variant

Model the clean single-seat C from the geometry sheet: one crew, two F100s, gear up, no conformal tanks.

The USAF Eagle fact sheet (current as of April 2019) prints crew F-15A/C one, and lists the C/D power plant as F100-PW-100, -220, or -229 in one line. That sheet does not say which dash is on a given C. Do not collapse those dashes onto one thrust.

NASA F-15 No. 8 (TM 72861) is a preproduction single-seater with two F100-PW-100 engines. Its wing table is the reference area paired with the geometry sheet's planform. Its weight and engines are not a C-model operating weight.

The Dryden jet in TM 4604 is a preproduction F-15A with a noseboom and two PW1128 engines. TM 86042, TM 86748, and TM 4705 use that same PW1128 / F100 EMD family on a NASA F-15. Those decks are labeled below and are not an F-15C deck.

The MIPCC paper's "typical F-15" weights are that paper's F-15A study, not a C empty weight. The Streak Eagle is a stripped F-15A. Wikipedia's F-15C specification block is a separate WIKI-ONLY block. It is not used to fill a NOT PUBLISHED cell.

Conformal tanks stay off. The USAF 36,200 lb fuel line is three external tanks plus conformal tanks. It is not the fuel of this jet.

## Engine

Two engines. The C fact sheet allows F100-PW-100, F100-PW-220, or F100-PW-229. A point-mass model has to pick one dash and then use only the row that names that dash. The USAF 23,450 lb line names all three and does not say military or afterburner. It is not the military rating of one and the afterburner rating of another.

The 18,000 lb and 27,500 lb PW1128 numbers are not averaged.

F100-PW-100 engine simulation, not an airplane installation. NASA TM X-3261 (Szuch, August 1975) models the F100-PW-100. Its design point is sea-level, static, military power at power-lever angle 73°. The text treats augmented operation as power-lever angle above 80°. No thrust in pounds is printed. Steady-state and transient comparisons are figures. A 0.5-second power-lever ramp from idle to military is the input schedule used for one simulation comparison. It is not a measured spool time. NASA TP 1034 is the later F100-PW-100(3) real-time model, matched to a manufacturer digital deck. Table I of that report is volumes, rotor inertias, and gas properties. It is not an aircraft inertia and it is not a thrust rating. Net thrust is plotted against power-lever angle. No sea-level static thrust number is printed. Those plots were not digitized.

F100-PW-220. Museum sheet: maximum thrust with afterburner 23,770 lb, engine weight 3,234 lb, length with afterburner 15 ft 11 in, diameter 3 ft 10.5 in. The narrative says early F100 models were about 24,000 lb and later versions greater than 29,000 lb. Those are not a dash number. Wikipedia's military/intermediate 14,590 lbf for the -220 is WIKI-ONLY and is the only military number opened for that dash. Wikipedia also prints intermediate specific fuel consumption 0.73 lb/(lbf·h) for the -220. That is not a fuel flow in lb/hr, and it was not multiplied by thrust.

F100-PW-229. Pratt & Whitney's February 2024 product card, F-15 column: thrust class 29,000+ lb (129.7 kN), specification maximum weight 3,826 lb (1,735 kg), length 191 in (4.85 m), inlet diameter 34.8 in (0.88 m), maximum diameter 46.5 in (1.18 m), airflow 248 lb/sec, bypass ratio 0.36:1. Wikipedia's 17,800 lbf intermediate and 29,160 lbf afterburning, and its specific fuel consumption 0.76 lb/(lbf·h) intermediate and 1.94 lb/(lbf·h) full afterburner, are WIKI-ONLY. Not an installed deck. The -229 is one of the three dashes on the USAF C/D line, not the only one.

F100-PW-100 object at the Smithsonian: thrust 23,830 lb, weight 1,377 kg (3,035 lb), bypass ratio 0.36:1, length 191 in, diameter 46.5 in. Power condition of the thrust line is not stated.

Customer bleed and horsepower extraction printed for the PW1128 deck in TM 86042, not for a C: 65 hp (48,470 W) and 0.7 lb/sec (0.32 kg/sec), used with the inlet recovery of that report's figure 4 to build thrust and fuel-flow plots. The plots were not digitized. The deck itself, PWA CCD 1194.2, was not opened.

Wing rock on F-15 No. 8, not a limit. With ±10 deg/s as the criterion, onset moved from 11° to 9° angle of attack as Mach went from 0.70 to 0.90. One run at Mach 0.89 and 10,700 m reached ±20 deg/s at about 10° angle of attack. Tracking runs were scheduled to 7 g. Those are test conditions.

The 3/8-scale unpowered model was flown from −20° to 53° angle of attack at Mach below 0.60. That range is the model test, not the C limiter. The report says some separation above 15° and extensive separation above 25°, and that results above 30° may be affected by nonlinearities.

## Six-degree-of-freedom data

No opened report prints an F-15C inertia, an F-15C stability-derivative table, or an installed F-15C thrust deck. The tables below are the small printed tables. They are not relabeled as the C.

### F-15 No. 8, NASA TM 72861

Table 1 is dimensions, control travels, and the approximate 180 kN takeoff gross weight. It is not a coefficient table. Normal-force coefficient versus angle of attack, with buffet, is Figure 4. Not digitized. Reference area for coefficients in that report is the 56.61 m² wing area in Table 1. The airplane is preproduction F-15 number 8 with two F100-PW-100 engines.

### 0.075-scale model, NASA TM X-62,360

Not an F-15C. Low-speed wind-tunnel force test. Basic configuration code B156 W118 D41 H36 V172 T25. Stores and alternate noses were also tested. Ducts were flow-through except from 40° to 90° and from −40° to −80°, where they were plugged. Inlet ramps were set at 0° or 11°. Moment center is model fuselage station 106.142 cm and waterline 22.131 cm.

Coefficient data are computer plots in Figures 4 through 9, indexed by Table 2. Axes are angle of attack, sideslip, and Reynolds number, for the six force and moment coefficients. Not digitized. There is no printed polar and no CLmax.

### 3/8-scale unpowered model, NASA TN D-8136

Not an F-15C. Unpowered, remotely piloted, 3/8-scale F-15. Inlets blocked. Air-launched from a B-52 near 15,000 m at Mach 0.65. Derivative maneuvers were below Mach 0.60, angle of attack −20° to 53°. Two shapes: basic, and a "production" update that rounded the wingtip trailing edges and cut a piece of the horizontal stabilizer. Neither shape is a C-model mass or an F100 installation. Do not scale these inertias to a full-size C. Mass and length scales are not given as a pair that would make that legal, and the model is unpowered.

Stability and control derivatives are maneuver estimates plotted in Figures 5 and 6 against angle of attack, with uncertainty bars. Not a printed coefficient table. Not digitized. The text says the normal-force slope CN is a maximum near 8° angle of attack. That is the slope, not a printed CLmax.

### F-15A study weights and the Streak Eagle

## Not published

Looked for in the pages opened. Not printed, and not filled from a plot or from another airplane.

- F-15C empty weight and a defined combat or operating weight. The USAF line is only "Weight."
- Clean F-15C internal fuel. The 2,000 lb figure is an increment. The 36,200 lb figure includes conformal tanks.
- Installed sea-level static military thrust and installed sea-level static afterburner thrust for the F100-PW-100, -220, or -229 in an F-15C, stated as such.
- A thrust deck or fuel flow in lb/hr for any of those dashes in the airplane.
- Spool time.
- Cd0, Oswald e, CLmax, and a Mach-drag table.
- Structural load factor with its weight and store condition. The Wikipedia +9 line is not that schedule.
- Angle-of-attack limiter setting in degrees.
- Maximum roll rate.
- Full-scale moments of inertia and the center-of-gravity position for a C.
- An F-15C flight manual. TM 86042 cites TO 1F-15A-1, November 1978, and AFFTC-TR-77-7. Neither manual was opened.
