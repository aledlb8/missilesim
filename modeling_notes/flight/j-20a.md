# J-20A flight model

Point-mass and six-degree-of-freedom card for the production single-seat J-20A. Exterior geometry is [modeling_notes/fighters/j-20a.md](../fighters/j-20a.md). This file does not add a length, a span, or a height.

**No official length, span, or height was found.** A Zhuhai hall-model placard (21.2 m / 13.01 m / 4.69 m) and a photo estimate (20.3 m / 12.88 m / 4.45 m) are not technical data. Wing loading is not computed from either pair, and it is not computed from any other mass and area on this page.

Tags: **OFFICIAL** (AVIC, Chengdu Aircraft Corporation, AECC, or the PLA), **SECONDARY** (press, a university outreach page, a compilation), **WIKI-ONLY**, **NOT PUBLISHED**. Nothing here is **DERIVED**. A speed or a thrust-to-weight that some other page computed is not recomputed, and it is not stored as a speed or a ratio.

## Status

**INSUFFICIENT.** No performance row on an opened AVIC, CAC, AECC, or PLA page gives a mass, a fuel load, an installed thrust, a structural g, a maximum Mach, or a ceiling for the J-20 or the J-20A. **BROCHURE_ONLY** is not used. A journalist's report of a hall-model placard, a People's Daily one-minute slogan, and a commentator on CCTV are not that row.

The feel cannot be implemented from a real rating. There is no honest empty mass, no honest fuel mass, no honest thrust, and no drag polar. Do not scale the F-16, the J-10, or the Su-27 onto the J-20 to fill the blanks. Do not form a scale factor from a secondary J-20 mass or area and the NASA TP-1538 F-16 tables.

Missing before any model can be honest:

- Which engine is installed on the production J-20A, in an AVIC, CAC, AECC, or PLA sentence. The WS-10 and the WS-15 are different engines. They do not share a thrust cell.
- Military thrust and maximum thrust for that engine, from one of those publishers. A sea-level static rating would still not be an installed lapse against Mach and altitude. Both are unpublished.
- Empty mass, an operating mass, and a maximum mass, each with the fuel, stores, and crew that belong to it.
- Internal fuel mass, or usable volume plus the density that converts it. External fuel, if it is allowed on this variant, on the same terms.
- Structural load factor, positive and negative, and the Mach, altitude, and mass at which it applies.
- Maximum Mach and the altitude, and whether the number is a limit or one demonstrated point.
- Ceiling, and whether it is a service ceiling, an absolute ceiling, or a combat ceiling.
- A manufacturer reference wing area, if a point-mass polar were ever added. Cd0, CLmax, and every derivative would still be unpublished.
- Inertias, center-of-gravity envelope, control powers, and surface limits.

Until those exist, the flight model has no card. Secondary numbers below are printed so they are not promoted later. They are not averaged. No thrust is divided by any mass on this page.

## Variant

Model the **production single-seat J-20A** in the sense of the geometry note: the single-seat upgrade CCTV described on 11 January 2026, not the 2011 demonstrator, not early single-seaters, and not the twin-seat J-20S.

CCTV, quoting commentator Zhang Xuefeng, calls the J-20 and the J-20A single-seat multirole fighters and calls the J-20A the upgraded one. The only exterior difference that page states is the canopy-to-body junction: the earlier canopy sits higher; on the J-20A the join is smoother, with a raised bulge. He says the smoother join reduces separation behind the canopy and lowers drag, and that this favors supersonic flight and supersonic cruise. He also says the raised volume could, in theory, take new equipment or a fuel tank. No Mach, no drag coefficient, no litres, and no thrust are attached to those sentences. The 澎湃 reprint of CCTV's *砺剑* says the same junction lowers transonic and supersonic drag by a large amount and favors supersonic flight. "大幅度" is not a number.

That is not a J-20A performance delta. Do not add a Mach increment, a fuel increment, or a thrust increment for the spine.

## Engine

The engine row is **NOT PUBLISHED**. No opened AVIC, CAC, AECC, or PLA page states the J-20A's engine or a thrust in kilonewtons or tonne-force. The feel cannot be implemented from a real rating.

The airframe is a twin-engine installation in the qualitative sense of the geometry note. "Twin" is not a thrust. WS-10 (太行) and WS-15 (峨眉) are different engines. An AL-31FM2 rating is a third engine, discussed by secondary pages for earlier jets, and it is not this row.

GlobalSecurity attributes to Zhang Yong, via a Janes report that was not opened, a March 2023 remark that mass production of the WS-10 and the WS-15 had been achieved. That sentence, as printed on GlobalSecurity, has no kilonewtons. Production is not a rating. The 16-ton paragraph lower on the same page is a different, unsourced block. It is not merged with Zhang Yong's sentence.

No thrust below is a flight-model thrust. The same refusal covers afterburner specific fuel consumption, airflow, bypass ratio, pressure ratio, and turbine temperature printed beside the GlobalSecurity and Wikipedia engine figures. Those figures are not transcribed as a deck.

Do not scale the F-16 onto the J-20. The simulator's fighter model is the NASA TP-1538 F-16: reference span 30 ft, wing area 300 ft², mean chord 11.32 ft, inertias at 20,500 lb, and an F100-PW-200 thrust deck. None of those tables is a J-20A. Do not multiply them by a ratio of secondary masses, secondary areas, or refused thrusts.

Do not scale a J-10 onto the J-20. The test-pilot comparison and the shared design bureau are not a derivative set, not a g limit, and not a thrust.

Do not scale a Su-27 onto the J-20. A canard-delta with diverterless inlets is not a Flanker polar, not a Flanker inertia tensor, and not an AL-31 installation with the Su-27's inlet.

A point-mass energy model is the same refusal in fewer axes. It still needs mass, fuel, thrust, and a drag polar. Those are not published. A handling feel set by hand would be fiction, not a J-20A.
