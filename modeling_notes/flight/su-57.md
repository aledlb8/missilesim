# Su-57 flight model

Point-mass brochure for the production single-seat **Su-57**. This is not a six-degree-of-freedom database. The exterior sheet is [fighters/su-57.md](../fighters/su-57.md). Nothing in `src/` was changed.

The geometry note found no official length, span, or height. This file does not add one. Wing area on that note is unpublished. Press figures of 62 m², 78.8 m², and 82 m² conflict there. This file does not pick one, and it does not compute wing loading.

Tags:

- **OFFICIAL** — UAC model-line page, Rosoboronexport Su-57E page, the Rosoboronexport Su-57E specification sheet, or a Rostec news page that states the engine in its own text.
- **PRIMARY** — Krasnaya Zvezda interview with Sukhoi’s Alexander Davidenko, 24 March 2010, as decoded from the archive. A manufacturer interview in the Ministry of Defence paper. It is not a thrust rating. Sukhoi patent wording already recorded on the geometry sheet is cited the same way, for the nozzle description only.
- **SECONDARY** — press. A news thrust stays in the engine section it names. It is not copied onto a different engine, and it is not promoted because a brochure mass sits on another row.
- **WIKI-ONLY** — English or Russian Wikipedia, as opened. Not promoted into the brochure.
- **DERIVED** — arithmetic on figures printed in this file, with the formula as the value. No such row is printed below. Thrust-to-weight is not formed.
- **NOT PUBLISHED** — no page opened for this note prints the item.

Conflicting prints are separate rows. They are not averaged. A range such as 17.5–19.5 tonnes stays a range. “Up to 15,000 kgf” stays “up to,” and the power setting stays unstated when the sentence does not name one. A Mach number is not rewritten as a speed in km/h, and 1,350 km/h is not rewritten as a Mach number.

## Status

**BROCHURE_ONLY.**

No Su-57 force table, drag polar, stability derivative, or control-derivative table was opened. The opened official pages are an export specification card and manufacturer news. That card prints crew, two takeoff masses, a payload, a high-altitude Mach number, a low-altitude speed, a ceiling, a range, a combat radius, and a pilot-limited endurance. It does not print fuel, a g limit, a supercruise Mach number, or a thrust. Rostec names the early-series engine and, on a later page, prints a thrust range for a different engine. Neither page prints a thrust-versus-Mach table or a specific fuel consumption for the early-series engine.

**Do not scale the F-16 polar onto the Su-57.** The simulator’s existing aero set is the NASA TP-1538 early F-16, reference span 30 ft and wing area 300 ft². **Do not scale a Su-27 polar onto the Su-57.** A Flanker table is a different airplane, a different wing, and a different inlet. Area scaling, mass scaling, or dynamic-pressure scaling of either polar is not a Su-57 model.

## Variant

Model the production single-seat **Su-57** built for the Russian Aerospace Forces, in the sense of the geometry note: blended wing-body, LEVCONs, all-moving canted vertical tails, all-moving horizontal tails, two nacelles, axisymmetric vectoring nozzles. Crew on the export card is one.

UAC’s model-line page (archived 16 May 2023) titles the aircraft “ПАК ФА, Т-50, Су-57”, project stage “Серийное производство”. First flight 29 January 2010. The first aircraft of the initial batch was delivered to the Ministry of Defence in 2020. The same page says a new-generation engine, with lower specific fuel consumption and higher thrust, had begun tests on the Su-57-2 flying laboratory and “впоследствии будет устанавливаться на серийные самолеты”. That sentence places the later engine on series aircraft afterwards. It does not say the series airframe of 2023 already had it, and it prints no product index and no thrust figure.

Early series, as Rostec states it, flew on the first-stage engine **AL-41F1 (изделие 117)**. **Изделие 30** is the second-stage engine in the December 2017 Rostec account, with its own thrust range. **Изделие 177** is a later flight-test engine, flown in December 2025 on a prototype in the aviation21 account. The three ratings are not interchangeable. Lenta.ru also uses the name **AL-51F1** for an engine it expected later aircraft to receive. That name has no thrust on the Lenta page. The 17.5–19.5 tonne range is not moved onto AL-51F1.

**AL-41F1S (изделие 117С)** is the Su-35S engine in the February 2017 Rostec account. Rostec says 117 differs from 117С and prints no numeric difference. The December 2017 Rostec page says the 117 is similar in design to the Su-35S engine. Both sentences are kept. Su-35S thrust and the Su-35S nozzle angle of 15° are not imported. The geometry sheet is where that 15° belongs, on the Su-35S.

The Su-57E is the name on the Rosoboronexport page and on the specification sheet. Those pages print the masses and performance in the point-mass card. No opened source says the Su-57E card is the domestic series jet’s flight manual. The card is used as the only official mass and performance print. It is not used as a statement that the domestic jet matches it, and it is not used as geometry.

Not this card: Su-57D, Su-75, a flat-nozzle test installation, and any T-50-only figure a source does not also attach to the series aircraft. The flat nozzle mentioned by aviation21 on prototype “052” is a test installation. It gets no thrust in this file.

## Engine

Three named engines. A thrust is recorded only on the engine the source names. The early-series engine is AL-41F1 (изделие 117). Изделие 30 is a different rating. Изделие 177 is a third rating, from a 2025 flight-test account. Изделие 177С is a fourth column on that same secondary table and is not the Su-57 early-series engine.

No opened UAC or Rosoboronexport page prints a kilogram-force, kilonewton, or tonne-force figure. UAC’s “higher thrust” for the new-generation engine has no number.

The phrase “about 15 tonnes” was not the wording on any page opened for this note. The opened news line that names AL-41F1 and a thrust is Lenta’s “до 15 000 кгс каждый”. That row stays SECONDARY. It is not rewritten into tonnes, and it is not given a dry or afterburning label the sentence does not have.

The geometry sheet already records the Sukhoi patent description: axisymmetric nozzles, able to deflect together or differentially, in planes inclined at an acute angle to the plane of symmetry. The angle is not printed. Patent RU 2503584’s “порядка 60°” is a nose-down deflection of controlled pivoting surfaces. It is a surface travel, not an angle-of-attack limit, and that patent does not name the Su-57. It is not a flight limit here.

Wikipedia’s ±16° / ±20° vector figures, the Russian Wikipedia 20° at 60°/s, and the Su-35S nozzle limit of 15° are not Su-57 flight-card angles. They are not copied.

**Do not scale the F-16 or the Su-27 polar onto the Su-57.** The NASA TP-1538 F-16 tables use a 30 ft span and 300 ft². A Su-27 polar is a different wing and a different installation. Neither becomes a Su-57 by multiplying by a mass ratio, an area ratio, or a refused thrust.
