# F-35A flight model

Point-mass brochure for one **F-35A** (CTOL), one **F135-PW-100**. Geometry is the lock in [fighters/f-35a.md](../fighters/f-35a.md): length **51.4 ft**, span **35 ft**, wing area **460 ft²**, empty weight **29,300 lb**, maximum weight **70,000 lb class**, from Lockheed. Those pages were opened again for this note. Where a sheet also prints metres, the metre is the sheet's rounding. It is not a second measurement. Do not convert 15.7 m, 10.7 m, or 42.7 m² back into feet.

This file does not average sources. It does not invent Cd0, CLmax, a drag polar, or inertias, and it does not scale the NASA TP-1538 F-16 (30 ft reference span, 300 ft²) onto this wing. No stealth construction and no tactics.

## Status

**BROCHURE_ONLY.**

The opened pages are manufacturer and service fact sheets. They print a geometry lock, two different thrust pairs, internal fuel, a g number, a Mach number, and one service ceiling. They do not print a drag polar, a thrust lapse, or a mass property. That is not PARTIAL_POLAR and not FULL_TABLE.

## Variant

Model the conventional-takeoff **F-35A** only, engine **F135-PW-100**.

Do not put the F-35B lift fan, roll posts, or three-bearing nozzle on this model, and do not use the F-35C wing. Lockheed's 1 November 2020 fast-facts table prints the other two variants beside the A. The C wing on that table is **43 ft / 668 ft²**, not 35 ft / 460 ft². The B engine on that table is the **F135-PW-600**. Those are not A data. B thrust and the vertical rating are isolated under Engine as a trap.

The 2009 Lockheed A page still offers "One P&W F135 or GE F136". The F136 is not the engine of this note. That page prints one thrust line for the airplane, not a separate F136 rating.

## Engine

One **F135-PW-100**. Lockheed's fast facts puts the A and the C on that engine and the B on the F135-PW-600. Pratt's CTOL sheet prints one F135-PW-100 column for "Conventional Takeoff and Landing (CTOL) / Carrier Variant (CV)" and does not split A thrust from C thrust. Sharing the engine rating does not make the C wing the A wing.

Lockheed does **not** call 40,000 / 25,000 a class. Pratt **does** call 43,000 / 28,000 a class. Pratt does not print the words "military" or "afterburner" on that table. Lockheed and the RAAF do: Max or maximum power is with afterburner, Mil or military power is without. Those are parallel labels on different sheets, not a license to average 25,000 with 28,000 or 40,000 with 43,000.

No opened page prints installed thrust, sea-level static installed thrust, thrust versus Mach and altitude, or TSFC. The numbers below are not a throttle model.

## Not published

Looked for on the pages opened, and not printed:

- Cd0, CLmax, a drag polar, an Oswald factor, and any lift, drag, or moment table.
- Moments of inertia and a center-of-gravity envelope.
- Stability derivatives, control derivatives, and control-surface deflection limits.
- Installed thrust, thrust lapse, TSFC, engine mass, bypass ratio, and mass flow.
- A single hard maximum weight. The Lockheed figure is a class. The RAAF figure is a different unit and a different number.
- External fuel capacity. A defined USAF range profile (only the name is printed).
- Stall, corner, and approach speeds. Takeoff and landing distances. Sea-level maximum Mach on a manufacturer sheet. A Lockheed ceiling.
- Mean aerodynamic chord. The 460 ft² reference area's treatment of the LEX.

Also not used:

- The USAF fact-sheet URL. It returned access denied and was not opened. Snippet figures that circulate for that page, including a length in whole feet and an internal-fuel figure other than 18,250 lb, are not in this note.
- Any scaling of the NASA TP-1538 F-16 coefficient tables by 460/300, by 35/30, or by any thrust ratio.
- F-35B lift-fan thrust and the 40,500 lb vertical rating, except as the trap above.
- The F-35C wing, 43 ft and 668 ft².
- Wikipedia kilometres and kilonewtons, Wikipedia's 1,500 nmi range, Wikipedia's sea-level Mach 1.06, and Wikipedia's thrust-to-weight pair 0.87 / 1.07.
