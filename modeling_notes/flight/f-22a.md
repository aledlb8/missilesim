# F-22A flight model

Point-mass brochure for the production single-seat **F-22A**. This is not a six-degree-of-freedom database. The exterior sheet is [fighters/f-22a.md](../fighters/f-22a.md). Nothing in `src/` was changed.

Tags:

- **OFFICIAL** — USAF fact sheet, National Museum of the USAF fact sheet, Lockheed Martin specification card or specifications page, or a Pratt & Whitney F119 product card.
- **PRIMARY** — NASA Langley “Partners in Freedom” page. Not a flight-manual callout. Where it disagrees with the USAF fact sheet, it is not substituted.
- **SECONDARY** — Forecast International market report. Estimates on that report are not ratings.
- **WIKI-ONLY** — English Wikipedia, F-22 or F119 article, as opened. Not promoted into the brochure.
- **DERIVED** — arithmetic on figures printed in this file. The formula is the value. Not a second measurement.
- **NOT PUBLISHED** — no page opened for this note prints the item.

Conflicting prints are separate rows. They are not averaged. A “35,000-pound class” thrust is not 35,000.0 lbf. “Mach 2 class” is not Mach 2.00 and not Mach 2.25. “Greater than 1.5 Mach” is not a supercruise point of Mach 1.50.

## Status

**BROCHURE_ONLY.**

The opened service and manufacturer pages print a card: two engines, a thrust class, weights, fuel weights, a Mach class, a supercruise floor, a ceiling stated as “above,” and a ferry range with external tanks. They do not print Cd0, CLmax, a drag polar, stability or control derivatives, an engine lapse, or a military (non-afterburning) thrust. That card cannot drive a six-degree-of-freedom model. It is also not enough, by itself, to invent the missing coefficients.

## Variant

Model the production single-seat **F-22A**, with two **F119-PW-100** engines.

The USAF fact sheet (March 2009, and the August 2022 issue of the same sheet) says the designation was F/A-22 for a short time and was renamed F-22A in December 2005. Crew is one. The first EMD flight was in 1997. This note does not use the YF-22.

Do not use these lookalikes:

- **YF-22.** NASA Langley quotes a 12 March 1991 Lockheed letter on the prototype flight-test program: from 80 knots and 60° angle of attack to Mach 2+, with supercruise demonstrated on both the GE and P&W prototypes. That letter is the YF-22. It is not an F-22A envelope. The same page says the production external geometry changed (span increased, leading-edge sweep decreased, vertical tails reduced and moved aft, horizontal tails reconfigured) and prints no production coefficient.
- **Engine name on that NASA page.** The specifications box prints “Pratt & Whitney F119-PW-1009.” The USAF fact sheet and the Pratt & Whitney card print F119-PW-100. Do not use the trailing 9.
- **NASA weight and speed box.** Empty 31,670 lb, max takeoff 60,000 lb, and max speed “above Mach number of 1.7” disagree with the USAF card. They stay PRIMARY rows below and are not the production card.
- **A different study airplane.** The geometry note already flags a NASA report whose own geometry is 61.5 ft long, 45 ft span, and 500 ft² of wing. This flight note did not open that report. It is not an F-22A source.
- **F-22B and FB-22.** No flight-card numbers for either were taken from the pages opened here.

## Point-mass card

Reference wing area is the area Lockheed prints. The USAF fact sheet does not print area. Wing loading below uses Lockheed’s 840 ft² and a weight that is also in this file. Thrust-to-weight uses the class wording, times two engines, over a weight that is in this file. Neither ratio was printed by USAF or Lockheed.

The fact-sheet kilogram beside each pound figure is that sheet’s rounding. It is not a second weighing. 43,340 lb × 0.45359237 kg/lb = 19,658.7 kg, not the printed 19,700 kg. 83,500 lb converts to 37,875.0 kg, not 38,000 kg. 18,000 lb converts to 8,164.7 kg, not 8,200 kg. 26,000 lb converts to 11,793.4 kg, not 11,900 kg. Use the pound figure in any ratio.

## Engine

Two engines. The aircraft sources say two. The Pratt & Whitney card is one engine, the F119-PW-100.

Military thrust was **not printed** on the USAF fact sheet, the Lockheed 2012 specifications page, the 2015 or 2019 Lockheed cards, or the Pratt & Whitney product card. Those pages also do not print a thrust-versus-Mach or thrust-versus-altitude table, a specific fuel consumption, or a bypass ratio. There is no engine lapse in this file. Do not build one.

## Six-degree-of-freedom data

No public coefficient tables were found.

Searches of the NASA technical-report server for an F-22 or F-22A force table, drag polar, or stability-derivative table did not return a production aerodynamic database. The NASA Langley page describes static-force and forced-oscillation tests of an F-22 model in the Full-Scale Tunnel, spin and rotary-balance tests in the Spin Tunnel, supersonic tests in the Unitary Plan Wind Tunnel, and a vertical-tail buffet test. It prints no CL, CD, Cm, or derivative. It says supersonic drag from the tunnel agreed within 1 percent with flight measurements. That sentence is not a drag coefficient.

**Do not scale the F-16 NASA tables onto the F-22.** The simulator’s existing aero set is the NASA TP-1538 early F-16 (30 ft reference span, 300 ft²). The F-22A’s printed reference area is 840 ft², on a different planform. Area scaling, mass scaling, or dynamic-pressure scaling of those F-16 tables is not an F-22A model.

No control-surface chord, span, or deflection limit was printed on the flight pages opened here. The geometry sheet already records that T.O. 00-105E-9 names the surfaces and does not size them.

## Not published

Looked for on the USAF fact sheets, the Lockheed 2012 specifications and capabilities pages, the 2015 and 2019 Lockheed cards, the Pratt & Whitney F119-PW-100 card, the museum technical notes, and the NASA Langley page. Not printed there. Wikipedia and Forecast International figures that do exist are in the tables above and are not modeling values.

- Cd0, CLmax, a drag polar, and any lift, drag, or moment increment.
- Stability and control derivatives, damping derivatives, and rotary derivatives.
- A public F-22A flight-manual envelope (the TO-1 series performance charts were not an opened public source).
- Military, intermediate, or dry thrust on an official page. Any thrust lapse, installation loss, or throttle table.
- Specific fuel consumption, bypass ratio, overall pressure ratio, and mass flow on an official page.
- A manufacturer or USAF nozzle angle in degrees.
- A structural g limit, a limit angle of attack, a corner speed, and a rate of climb.
- A combat or operating weight other than the unlabeled 43,340 lb, the Lockheed “weight empty” of the same pounds, and maximum takeoff weight 83,500 lb.
- Store drag, external-tank drag, and any weapon-employment or signature number. Supercruise in this note is only the published speed floor.
