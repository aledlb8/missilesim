# F-15C Eagle

Single-seat F-15C, F100 engines, gear up, no conformal fuel tanks. The mesh is the air-superiority jet. Conformal tanks, the two-seat canopy, and the F-15E gun-round count stay in [Variant traps](#variant-traps).

Metre values use 1 ft = 0.3048 m and 1 in = 0.0254 m. Where a source already printed metres, that printed metre value is kept. A foot-inch conversion is an extra row only when the source did not print metres. Conflicts stay on separate rows.

Tags: OFFICIAL (USAF or Boeing or the National Museum of the U.S. Air Force fact sheet), PRIMARY (NASA report or TO 1F-15C-34-1-1), SECONDARY (paint compilations), DERIVED (arithmetic on a printed number: unit conversion or the model's stated scale factor), NOT PUBLISHED.

Wind-tunnel stations below are model centimetres. They are not aircraft inches, and the 0.075-scale station set is not the 1/12-scale station set.

## Variant being modeled

Model one single-seat F-15C:

- Crew is one. The USAF Eagle fact sheet (March 2008) prints crew as F-15A/C one and F-15B/D/E two.
- Engines are Pratt & Whitney F100s. The same fact sheet lists F100-PW-100, -220, or -229, and prints C/D thrust as 23,450 lb each. The museum F-15C sheet prints two F100-PW-100 at 25,000 lb each. Boeing prints two F100s in the 25,000 lb (11,340 kg) thrust class. Keep those ratings apart. The nozzle contours in this note are the F100-PW-100 shapes from the NASA flight airplane and the 1/12-scale model.
- No conformal fuel tanks on the mesh. The C has provisions for them. The museum sheet calls those provisions FAST Packs, with AIM-7 mount points. The USAF sheet's 36,200 lb fuel figure is "three external plus conformal fuel tanks," so that fuel total is the loaded-tank case, not the clean jet.
- MSIP, started February 1983 with the first production MSIP F-15C in 1985, is an avionics and weapons-computer change on the USAF sheet. That sheet prints no exterior dimension for MSIP.
- Gear up is the flight shape. Gear down is a second shape.
- NASA wing and tail drawings are production-planform F-15 geometry. The airplanes those reports measured are preproduction or prototype F-15A airframes (F-15 No. 8, and the Dryden jet). Use their planform. Take overall length from a length row you choose, and do not treat a NASA test article's engines, weights, or noseboom as the C.

### Variant traps

**F-15A versus F-15C.** No opened page prints a different length, span, inlet, or nozzle for the A. The museum F-15C sheet says the C adds 2,000 lb of internal fuel, FAST Pack provisions, and avionics, and that the C is an updated A. The USAF sheet says the same Production Eagle Package items and prints one wingspan, length, and height for the Eagle. Early F-15A overall Air Superiority Blue FS 35450 is a paint trap; see [Colors](#colors-sufficient-to-block-out-a-model).

The NASA articles are not C-model mass or engines:

- NASA TM 72861, F-15 No. 8, preproduction, two F100-PW-100, length 19.06 m, takeoff gross weight with AIM-7F missiles about 180 kN.
- NASA TM 4604, single-seat preproduction F-15A with a noseboom and two PW1128 engines (upgraded F100-series, installed sea-level static thrust 18,000 lb each). Table 1 length 63.75 ft, empty weight 30,035 lb, maximum gross weight 40,835 lb, maximum internal fuel 10,800 lb. Those weights are that research jet.
- NASA TP 2017, F-15 with two prototype F100-PW-100 engines in the 110,000-newton thrust class, and a noseboom used for air data. Reference length L is 19.05 m from the airplane nose to the end of the tail boom.

**F-15E.** The USAF F-15E fact sheet (posted 22 October 2009) prints the same wingspan, length, and height numbers as the Eagle sheet: 42.8 ft (13 m), 63.8 ft (19.44 m), 18.5 ft (5.6 m). It prints no inlet or nozzle difference. It does print a second crew station (pilot and weapon systems officer), conformal tanks, and a different gun load. Each low-drag conformal tank carries 750 gallons, and the tanks hold weapons on short pylons. Weight on that sheet is 37,500 lb (17,010 kg). Maximum takeoff weight is 81,000 lb (36,450 kg). The internal gun carries up to 500 rounds, against 940 on the Eagle sheet. Engines are F100-PW-220 (25,000 lb each on that sheet's paired total of 50,000 lb) or F100-PW-229 (29,000 lb each). Boeing's 1999 page prints F-15E maximum gross takeoff 81,000 lb against an F-15A/B/C/D weight of the "45,000 lb (20,411 kg) class," and one length, height, and span for the F-15. No opened page prints a conformal-tank cross-section, a two-seat canopy outline, or an E length that differs from the Eagle sheet. A paint compilation puts the F-15E in overall Medium Gunship Gray FS 36118.

**F-15EX.** No page opened for this note prints an F-15EX length, span, inlet, or nozzle.

## How to use these numbers in Blender

- Units: metres.
- Origin: radome tip. State which length row you used. Several "length" rows do not say whether the aft point is the nozzle exit, the tail-boom tip, or another extremity, and the NASA flight-test jets carry a noseboom that their own length definitions sometimes exclude.
- Axes: +X pilot's right, +Y forward, +Z up. The airframe occupies Y ≤ 0.
- `aft_m` is positive aft of the nose tip. Blender Y = −aft_m.
- `right_m` is positive to the pilot's right, the same sign as Blender +X.
- `up_m` zero in this file is the radome tip only as the origin of the model. No opened source prints the vertical distance from that tip to the fuselage reference line. The weapon manual defines the fuselage reference line (FRL) as the water line, the armament datum line, and the zero water line. Wind-tunnel waterlines (WL) and the fuselage reference plane (FRP) are those models' planes. They are not the ground, and they are not proven equal to a height above the radome tip. Leave Z = 0 at the tip and shift the whole airplane in Z when a waterline drawing is used. TM 4604 Figure 4 heights are ground clearances in a landing attitude.
- Pick one length row and one span row. Keep every other printed length and span in the tables as conflicts. Scale a wing or tail drawing by that drawing's own span onto the span you chose.
- The 1/12-scale propulsion model (NASA TP 1521) has its nose at model F.S. 24.613 cm and its tail-boom tips at F.S. 183.302 cm. Reference length l = 158.689 cm. Full-scale metres on that model are `(F.S. cm − 24.613) × 0.12`. That product ends at 19.043 m, which sits with the NASA 19.05–19.06 m lengths, not with the 63 ft 9 in / 63.8 ft fact-sheet lengths. Place nozzles with that formula only on a fuselage you have built to 19.043 m. On a 19.43 m or 19.45 m fuselage, scale the nozzle shape and put the exit where the longer fuselage drawing puts it. Stretching this model's stations until the boom tips land on 19.45 m also stretches the wing.
- The 0.075-scale model (NASA TM X-62,360) has its nose tip at model F.S. 14.53 cm and an aft tick at F.S. 165.07 cm. `(165.07 − 14.53) / 0.075 / 100 = 20.072 m`. Its table fuselage length, 134.247 cm, scales to 17.900 m. Neither matches the fact-sheet length or the NASA 19.05 m length. Use that model's wing and tail drawings for planform, scaled by span. Its fuselage stations do not drop onto a fact-sheet-length airplane.
- The 1/12 model inlets are faired shut. Do not loft the inlet from that model. The 0.075 aft body between the fins was cut for a sting. The sting, the exit chokes, and the alternate test noses (hemispherical, and a 45° bevel) are model hardware.
- Gear up in the flight mesh. Build the wheels from the gear-down section as a second shape.

## Fuselage cross-sections

A closed fuselage loft (a set of X–Z sections at named stations) is not in the pages opened.

NASA TP 1521 Table II lists fuselage pressure orifices. Each orifice is given a fuselage station and one buttock line or one waterline, not a paired (BL, WL) point, and the printed brackets are easy to mis-pair. Those orifices are pressure taps on the 1/12 model. They are not a cross-section to loft. Use Figure 3 of that report if a tap location is needed, and keep the stations in that model's centimetres.

NASA TM X-62,360 Figure 2(k) is a plan view and a side view of the B156 fuselage with the M12 radome. It prints the two overall lines in the stations table and no cross-section diameters.

The general-arrangement drawing shows a canopy bubble, a wing-glove fairing, twin nacelles, a nozzle interfairing, and tail booms. Those are silhouette cues. They are not ordinates.

## Wing

The wing has no leading-edge devices. NASA TM 4604 says so in those words, and NASA TM 72861's airplane drawing labels a conical-camber fixed wing leading edge. Trailing-edge flaps deflect to a maximum of 30° down (TM 4604). Flap span and flap chord are not printed. Geometric twist is 0 and incidence is 0 in TM 72861; the leading edge is still cambered.

Quarter-chord sweep is a direct conflict. TM X-62,360 prints leading-edge sweep 45.0° and quarter-chord sweep 38.63°. TM 4604 Table 1 prints "Wing sweep at quarter chord, deg 45" and does not print a separate leading-edge sweep. Both statements stay in the table. The dimensioned planforms (TM X-62,360 Figure 2(b) and TP 1521) draw the leading edge at 45°.

## Leading-edge extension and strakes

The wing drawing is the published LEX break, and it is only one point.

On NASA TM X-62,360 Figure 2(b) the straight 45° leading edge continues inboard as a dashed line and meets the actual leading edge at model B.L. 15.215 cm (5.990 in) and F.S. 90.462 cm (35.615 in). On that model's scale that is 2.029 m to the right of the centreline and 10.124 m aft of that model's nose. It is the inboard end of the 45° line on that drawing. It is not a full strake station list, and it is not an aft_m on the 19.43 m airplane.

Outboard of the inboard glove the leading edge carries fixed conical camber. TM 72861 labels it on the airplane sketch. TP 1521 puts "modified conical camber" in the airfoil family name. No opened page prints the spanwise station where conical camber starts, beyond the four airfoil buttocks above.

A numeric LEX breakpoint list, strake height, and a strake cross-section are not in the pages opened.

Two all-moving horizontal stabilators and two vertical fins with rudders. Horizontal dihedral is 0. Vertical fins toe out 2° at the leading edge (both wind-tunnel models and the TM X-62,360 table).

Vertical leading-edge sweep is a direct conflict: NASA TM 72861 prints 34.57°. Both wind-tunnel models print 36.57°.

## Not published

These were looked for in the pages opened and were not printed:

- A production fuselage-station diagram in inches from the radome tip, and the Z offset from that tip to the FRL.
- Which point the fact-sheet "length" uses at the nose (radome skin versus pitot) and at the tail (nozzle exit versus boom tip). TP 2017 is the page that does define its own L, and that L is 19.05 m.
- A closed fuselage cross-section loft.
- Inlet capture height and width, and a full-scale ramp-angle schedule.
- LEX or strake stations beyond the single 45° break on the 0.075 wing drawing.
- Flap span and flap chord. Aileron planform that reconciles 3.30 m total span with the 13.283 cm model aileron. The two figures are both printed; they are not the same span.
- Rudder chord. Speed-brake span, chord, and deflection.
- Nozzle centreline spacing. Petal or flap count. A production drawing that says whether external "turkey feather" covers are present on the C. The NASA flight nozzles and the 1/12 model are exposed variable flaps.
- Canopy length, width, windshield angle, and transparency thickness. Single-seat versus two-seat is stated; the outlines are not.
- Production gear tire size, oleo stroke, door shape, and a dimension explicitly labeled wheelbase or track. The 17.8 ft and 9.09 ft lines are unlabeled dimension lines on the TM 4604 F-15A three-view.
- Wingtip-rail butt line and fuselage-station inches. The 1981 manual puts the AIM-9 LAU-114 on the sides of the inboard pylons. Lug spacing.
- Muzzle station. Light positions. Antenna sizes.
- Paint-boundary stations, and a technical-order paint drawing.
- Any F-15EX length, span, inlet, or nozzle figure.
