# F-22A Raptor

Exterior numbers for a true-size Blender model of one production single-seat F-22A. Gear-up is the primary state. Gear-down is a separate state on the only published vertical datum.

Tags used below:

- **OFFICIAL** — figure printed by a USAF fact sheet, by USAF T.O. 00-105E-9, or by a Lockheed Martin specification card or specifications page.
- **PRIMARY** — figure printed by Lockheed’s Code One design-evolution article, or by the NASA Langley “Partners in Freedom” page. Not a specification-sheet or drawing callout. NASA’s height and weights disagree with the USAF fact sheet; they are not substituted for it.
- **WIKI-ONLY** — figure printed by the English Wikipedia F-22 article. Kept off the official rows.
- **DERIVED** — exact conversion or arithmetic from a printed figure. 1 inch = 0.0254 m. 1 foot = 0.3048 m. 1 square foot = 0.09290304 m². Not a second measurement.
- **NOT PUBLISHED** — no page opened for this note prints the item.

Conflicting official figures are separate rows. They are not averaged. No pixel measurement of a drawing or a photograph was used. Where a technical-order arrow is described, the description is which printed witness the arrow meets.

## Variant being modeled

Model the production single-seat **F-22A**.

The USAF fact sheet (March 2009, and the August 2022 issue of the same sheet) says the designation was F/A-22 for a short time and was renamed F-22A in December 2005. Crew is one. T.O. 00-105E-9, 1 February 2006, Revision 11, says the official designation changed from F/A-22 to F-22A effective 13 December 2005. The dimension sheets still carry a struck F/A-22A header beside F-22A.

Lockheed Martin’s product page prints a first flight on 7 September 1997. The same fact sheets say the first EMD flight was in 1997. Lockheed Fast Facts (current as of September 2024) prints 195 aircraft delivered (186 production; 9 EMD) and does not print length, span, or height.

### Variant traps

**YF-22 versus F-22A.** The YF-22 is the flyable prototype. NASA Langley (page last updated 17 October 2003) says its first flight was 29 September 1990, and that the production external geometry changed from the YF-22: wingspan increased, wing leading-edge sweep decreased, vertical tails reduced in area and moved aft, horizontal tails reconfigured. That page prints no degrees, no cant, and no station numbers for either airplane.

Lockheed Code One (Eric Hehs, “F-22 Raptor Design Evolution, Part 2,” posted 16 October 1998) separates the two shapes:

- Prototype freeze. At Configuration 631 the vertical-tail cant went from thirty degrees to twenty-eight degrees. The prototype was frozen as Configuration 1131 in March 1988. Configuration 1132 (May 1988) changed the horizontal tails from trapezoidal to diamond, reshaped the forebody, and trimmed the aft end after the thrust-reverser requirement was removed. The 28° cant in that article is a YF-22 freeze statement. No opened official or primary source prints a production F-22A cant. Do not put 28° on the F-22A.
- Production proposal. Configuration 638 (early 1990): leading-edge wing sweep changed from forty-eight degrees to forty-two degrees; the inlet moved farther aft; the cockpit moved farther forward; the vertical tails became smaller; the wingtip trailing edge was clipped; overall length was shortened from sixty-four to sixty-two feet. A variation of 638 was the proposal shape. The same article says that by 1998 the design stood at Configuration 645 and that the external lines had changed very little from 638. The 42° figure is PRIMARY for that proposal. It is not printed on the USAF fact sheet or on the technical-order dimension plate. The “sixty-two feet” in that sentence is the design-change statement. The fact sheet and the technical order later print 62 feet 1 inch. Use those for the model length.

**FB-22.** Wikipedia says the FB-22 medium-range regional bomber was proposed in the late 1990s and early 2000s and was abandoned. No FB-22 length, span, or height was printed on any page opened for this note. It was not built. Do not model it.

**F-22B.** Wikipedia says the EMD contract originally ordered seven single-seat F-22As and two twin-seat F-22Bs, the F-22B was canceled in 1996, and those orders were converted to single-seaters. No two-seat exterior dimension was printed on the pages opened here.

**Other shapes named and not used.** Wikipedia also names the X-44 MANTA as a proposed derivative that was abandoned, and it links a naval F-22 variant. No exterior dimensions for either were taken from the pages opened here. A NASA technical report whose title uses “Raptor” and whose own geometry is 61.5 ft long, 45 ft span, and 500 ft² of wing is a different study airplane. It is not an F-22A source.

## How to use these numbers in Blender

Frame: metres. Origin at the nose tip. +X is the pilot’s right. +Y is forward. +Z is up. The airframe lies at Y ≤ 0. A published distance aft of the nose, `aft_m`, is placed at Blender Y = −`aft_m`.

**up_m datum.** For every gear-down height taken from T.O. 00-105E-9, `up_m` is metres above the line the plate labels **GROUND STATIC LINE**. The plate note says: “Height dimensions are typical for an aircraft fully serviced and with internal fuel.” The views that carry those heights are drawn with the gear down. This line is not a fuselage waterline. The fact-sheet word “Height” does not name a waterline. A gear-up waterline is **NOT PUBLISHED**. Do not put the nose tip at Z = 0 and treat that as an official waterline.

The nose-tip height above the ground static line is not a single published number. The side view and the front view on the same plate print different forward height stacks, and they are not averaged below. Gear-down `up_m` values therefore stay in that ground-line frame. They are not shifted onto the nose-tip origin by subtracting an assumed nose height.

Block-out, gear-up, using one source per dimension and the exact conversion of that source’s imperial figure:

- Length 18.923 m from the USAF and technical-order 62 feet 1 inch. Nose tip at Y = 0. Aft end of that length at Y = −18.923. Lockheed prints 62 ft / 18.90 m, with no extra inch. Leave that as its own official figure. The difference is the inch the fact sheet and the technical order both print.
- Span 13.5636 m from 44 feet 6 inches. Lockheed’s 44.5 ft is the same span. Lockheed’s printed metre for it is 13.56 m. The fact sheet’s printed metre is 13.6 m.
- Wing area: Lockheed’s printed 78.04 m² (840 ft²). The fact sheet does not print area.
- Horizontal tail span: 8.8392 m exact of 29 ft, which both Lockheed and the technical order print. Lockheed also prints 8.84 m.
- Vertical size of the gear-up mesh: no gear-up height is printed. The technical order’s fin-tip height is 5.0546 m above the ground static line (16 feet 7 inches), gear down. The fact sheet’s height is 16 feet 8 inches, which is 5.080 m exactly, and it also prints 5.1 meters. NASA Langley prints 16.4 ft. Keep those as separate rows. Do not drop the fuselage by the 3 ft 2 in belly clearance, or by any other ground clearance, and call the result a gear-up height. Strut length and gear travel are not printed.

Half-span placement at X = ±6.7818 m assumes the centerline bisects the 44 ft 6 in span. The plate prints the full width. It does not print a buttock line. That half-span is DERIVED under a symmetry assumption.

## Landing gear

Gear-down is the state drawn on the dimension plate. Gear-up dimensions are not printed. The primary Blender mesh stays gear-up. Build the gear-down layout as a separate arrangement on the ground static line. Do not invent a strut length to get from one to the other.

The side view shows the tires on the ground static line. The head-on view draws the main-gear tires as circles that do not sit on that line. Treat the head-on gear as a schematic. Do not read a tire diameter off it.
