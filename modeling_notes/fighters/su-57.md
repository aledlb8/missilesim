# Su-57

Production single-seat Sukhoi Su-57, blocked out for Blender at true size. A large fraction of the exterior loft is not in any open official table. This note records every exterior number found on pages that were actually opened, in the printed unit, and marks everything else NOT PUBLISHED.

No sweep, chord, station, or cross-section in this file was measured from a photograph, a Wikipedia three-view, or a pixel count. Conflicting secondary figures are listed separately and are not the aircraft. They were not averaged.

Su-75 (Checkmate) is a different airplane. It appears as its own entry on the UAC model-line page and is not used here. The two-seat Su-57D is not the airplane being modeled. Early T-50 prototypes are not the production loft; prototype-to-later differences are recorded only where a source states them.

## Variant being modeled

Single-seat production Su-57 built for the Russian Aerospace Forces: blended wing-body, LEVCONs (pivoting leading edges of the forward strakes), all-moving canted vertical tails, all-moving horizontal tails, two engines, axisymmetric vectoring nozzles.

What the opened official pages actually say about the variant:

- UAC’s model-line page (archived 16 May 2023) titles the aircraft “ПАК ФА, Т-50, Су-57”, design bureau OKB Sukhoi, project stage “Серийное производство”. First flight 29 January 2010 at Komsomolsk-on-Amur. Prototype manufacture finished in 2017. Serial production had started, and the first aircraft of the initial batch was delivered to the Ministry of Defence in 2020. A new-generation engine with lower specific fuel consumption and higher thrust had begun tests on the Su-57-2 flying laboratory and “впоследствии будет устанавливаться на серийные самолеты” (would later be installed on series aircraft). That page does not say the 2023 series airframe already had that engine, and it prints no exterior dimension.
- Rosoboronexport’s Su-57E export page and a Rosoboronexport sheet (the image English Wikipedia cites as a November 2023 Dubai brochure) are the export fighter, crew 1. Neither prints length, span, or height. No opened source states that the Su-57E loft differs from the domestic Su-57. Whether it does is NOT PUBLISHED. Do not borrow Su-57E weights as a shape.
- The configuration described in Sukhoi patent RU 2440916 / US 9,180,974 (filed 28 July 2010) matches the layout above: pivoting strake leading edges, trapezoidal wing blended into a flattened mid-fuselage, widely spaced nacelles, all-moving horizontal and vertical tails, axisymmetric vectoring nozzles. It is a patent embodiment, not a production loft with tolerances, and it names neither “Su-57” nor a linear dimension.

Not this model: T-50-1 through the early flight-test jets where a source says the airframe still differed; the static-test airframe T-50-7 as described by vpk.name; Su-57D; Su-75; any flat-nozzle test installation (no page opened here documents one).

## How to use these numbers in Blender

Units are metres. Do not scale the mesh to a “typical” Su-57 length. No official length was found, and the secondary lengths disagree, so a single scale factor is not available.

Frame, once a nose tip is chosen:

- Origin at the nose tip. Which physical point is the nose tip (radome skin versus pitot probe) is NOT PUBLISHED. Pick one, name it in the blend file, and do not move it later.
- +X is the pilot’s right. +Y is forward. +Z is up.
- The airframe lies at Y ≤ 0. The nose tip is Y = 0.
- `aft_m` is positive aft. Blender Y = −aft_m.
- `up_m` is a Z coordinate. The vertical datum is NOT PUBLISHED. Secondary “height” figures do not say whether they run from the ground, the tire contact, a waterline, or the fin tip, or whether the gear is compressed. Do not set Z = 0 to the ground and call a published height the fin-tip Z.

There are no longitudinal stations. Do not invent FS numbers, rib stations, or a waterline from a side photo.

Where a Sukhoi patent gives a geometric constraint without a number (“parallel”, “acute angle”, “flattened”), model that relationship and leave the angle or ordinate unset. Do not fill it from a different source’s degree value. Those degree values are stored below under their own tags and are not a loft.

## Not published

Official, and still unpublished after the pages below were opened:

- Length, wingspan, height, and the datum for height.
- Which point on the nose the length is measured to.
- Wing area, aspect ratio, sweep, chords, thickness, twist, dihedral, incidence.
- The numeric angle that the patent only calls “acute” (fin cant, inlet lips, engine toe-in, nozzle vector plane).
- LEVCON chord, span, and hinge line. The “about 60°” figure is a patent deflection, not a planform angle, and the patent does not name the Su-57.
- Every fuselage cross-section and every longitudinal station.
- Canopy size and the production canopy’s frame and slide direction.
- Landing-gear geometry.
- Inlet and nozzle diameters and nacelle spacing.
- Bay and door sizes. The 20°–70° figure is a range for a side-bay extension plane, not a door outline.
- Number of external hardpoints on an official sheet.
- Light, antenna, pitot, and gun-port geometry.
- A production paint specification.
- Whether the Su-57E exterior loft differs from the domestic series loft.
- A statement on the UAC page that current series aircraft use any particular nozzle outline other than the axisymmetric arrangement in the 2010 configuration patent.

Also unpublished here because the page was not a dimensioned drawing: composite percentage, radar-absorbent coatings, and inlet-duct internals. Those are omitted on purpose.
