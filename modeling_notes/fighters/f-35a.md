# F-35A Lightning II

Exterior block-in numbers for one real **F-35A** (CTOL, single seat, single engine) at true size. Gear-up is the primary mesh. Gear-down is a separate, almost undimensioned, add-on.

This file records printed figures only. It does not average sources, does not measure photographs, and does not invent planform angles. Where a loft, station, or angle was looked for and not printed, the cell is **NOT PUBLISHED**.

The 2023 Lockheed / F-35.com F-35A product card repeats the A length, span, and wing area only (51.4 ft / 15.7 m, 35 ft / 10.7 m, 460 ft² / 42.7 m²). It does not print height, tail span, or empty weight. Singapore MINDEF’s 28 February 2024 F-35A fact sheet repeats the A row: length 15.7 m / 51.4 ft, height 4.38 m / 14.4 ft, wingspan 10.7 m / 35 ft, wing area 42.7 m² / 460 ft², horizontal tail span 6.86 m / 22.5 ft, engine F135-PW-100, internal fuel 8,278 kg / 18,250 lb, weapon payload 8,160 kg / 18,000 lb.

**Visual traps that belong on the B, not the A.** Lockheed *Code One* (“F-35 Identification 101”, 15 September 2015, and “X to F”, 15 May 2008) and the Moog F-35 sustainment brochure:

- Lift-fan door behind the cockpit. It flattens the upper fuselage into a hump and shortens the aft end of the canopy. The A keeps the full canopy and does not have this door.
- Auxiliary inlet on the upper fuselage, lift-fan exhaust doors just behind the nose-gear doors, and a seam at the aft lower fuselage for the doors of the three-bearing swivel duct. The A and C instead have a hump there for the arresting / barricade tailhook.
- Diamond-shaped roll duct under each wing.
- Three-bearing swivel nozzle (3BSD / 3BSM). *Code One* (Kevin Renshaw, 12 August 2014) says the production STOVL nozzle is a compact axisymmetric convergent/divergent nozzle and that the CTOL and CV variants use a longer set of nozzle flaps. The B nozzle is slightly shorter than the A and C nozzle. The 3BSD can move through ninety-five degrees. None of that swivel hardware is on the A. Moog lists the 3BSM, lift-fan vane-box actuators, and variable inlet guide vanes as **B variant only**.
- No internal-gun blister. Probe-and-drogue refueling on the right side of the forward fuselage, not a dorsal receptacle.
- Weapon bay volume is smaller than the A/C bay. One secondary page says the B bay was made 14 inches shorter than the A bay in late 2004; that page does not print the A bay length. See hardpoints.

**Visual traps that belong on the C, not the A.**

- Wing span 43 ft / 13.1 m and area 668 ft² / 62.1 m². *Code One* says the C wing provides almost fifty percent more area than the A and B and is the only variant with folding tips. Moog’s wing-fold transmission is **C variant only**.
- Two trailing-edge surfaces per wing (inboard flaperon plus outboard aileron). The A and B have one trailing-edge surface per wing, called a flaperon. Moog’s flaperon electronic control unit and rudder EHA part numbers in the hardware table are printed for the C only; that is a parts split, not evidence that the A lacks rudders. Moog’s prose still lists the rudder among the primary surfaces.
- Larger horizontal tail (span 26.3 ft / 8.02 m, not 22.5 ft).
- Beefier landing gear, two tires on the nose gear, and a launch bar. Do not put those on the A.
- No internal gun. Right-side refueling probe, not the A’s dorsal receptacle.

An older Lockheed Martin specifications page, captured 5 August 2009, prints a different A set: length **51.5 ft**, height **14.2 ft**, wingspan **35 ft**, wing area **460 sq ft**, horizontal tail span **23 ft**, empty **29,300 lb**, maximum weight **70,000 lb class**. Do not average 51.5 with 51.4, 14.2 with 14.4, or 23 with 22.5. The later Lockheed table (2020), the 2023 A product card, and the 2024 Singapore sheet agree with each other. Use that later set for the mesh. The 2009 page is kept only so the older poster figures are not mixed back in.

National modifications are not the baseline USAF A. *Code One* says Norwegian F-35s have a small bump on the upper fuselage between the vertical tails for a drag chute. Do not add that unless the subject is a Norwegian jet.

The first F-35A test aircraft, AA-1, is not the production mesh. *Code One* says AA-1 had a single large nose-gear door; production aircraft have two smaller split doors.

## How to use these numbers in Blender

Units: metres. One Blender unit = 1 m.

- Origin at the nose tip.
- **+X** pilot’s right, **+Y** forward, **+Z** up.
- The airframe lies at **Y ≤ 0**. Forward is toward the nose, so every station aft of the tip has negative Y.
- `aft_m` is positive going aft. Blender `Y = -aft_m`.
- **up_m datum: NOT PUBLISHED.** No source opened prints a waterline, a ground plane, a buttock line, or the height of the nose tip. Do not put the nose at Z = 0 and then treat 4.38 m as “tail tip above the nose.” The 14.4 ft / 4.38 m figure is an overall height with no printed end-points and no statement of gear up versus gear down. Leave Z of the nose unset until a datum is chosen. Gear-up is still the mesh to build; the gear is a separate object.

When a source prints both feet and metres, the two do not always match 1 ft = 0.3048 m. The foot figure is the tighter printed original. For the mesh, convert **that foot figure** by 0.3048 and do not stack the rounded metre on top.

| Use this for the mesh | Exact conversion | What the same source also printed | If you use the rounded metre instead |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Length 51.4 ft | 15.66672 m | 15.7 m | 15.7 / 0.3048 = 51.509 ft, about 0.11 ft long |
| Span 35 ft | 10.668 m | 10.7 m | 10.7 / 0.3048 = 35.105 ft |
| Height 14.4 ft | 4.38912 m | 4.38 m | 4.38 / 0.3048 = 14.370 ft |
| Horizontal tail span 22.5 ft | 6.858 m | 6.86 m | 6.86 / 0.3048 = 22.507 ft |
| Wing area 460 ft² | 42.7354 m² (× 0.09290304) | 42.7 m² | area only; do not rebuild the planform from area |

Half-span follows from the printed span and is **DERIVED**, not a separate measurement: 17.5 ft = 5.334 m each side of X = 0. Horizontal-tail half-span: 11.25 ft = 3.429 m. These halves assume the published spans are tip-to-tip and symmetric. The sources do not say whether a tip fairing or a folded-tip break is included. The A has no wing fold.

No opened source defines whether “length” is radome tip to nozzle trailing edge, or at what nozzle schedule the feathers are measured. Put the whole airplane, nozzle included, inside the 51.4 ft envelope. Do not add a nozzle behind it.

## Colors sufficient to block out a model

No Federal Standard number, camouflage drawing, or paint-chip callout was printed on the Lockheed fast facts, the 2023 A product card, the Singapore fact sheet, or the *Code One* articles opened. Do not invent an FS code.

Block the airframe as **one gray**. The product-card photograph is a single-tone gray jet with a clear canopy. Do not split the tails into a second color because a photograph looks lighter there. National insignia and stencil markings are operator-specific and were not dimensioned. Panel-to-panel tone differences are not a published paint map in the sources opened.

The clear canopy and the faceted under-nose sensor window are the exterior breaks in that gray mass. The nozzle feathers read as a separate metallic/tiled surface in the Aviation Week description (metallic inner flaps, tiled outer “tail feathers”). No paint code was printed for them either.

## Not published

Looked for and not printed in the sources opened:

- Fuselage stations, waterlines, buttock lines, and cross-sections.
- Which point on the nose and which point on the nozzle define length.
- Whether overall height is gear-down, and to which point on the fin.
- Nose-tip Z, ground clearance, and any waterline called out as `up_m`.
- Wing leading-edge sweep, trailing-edge sweep, dihedral, incidence, twist, aerofoil, chords, and control-surface chord fractions. Sweep is stated to exist and to match the X-35 and the C wing’s sweep. The number of degrees is not printed.
- LEX/chine length and angle. Vertical-tail cant, span, area, and sweep in degrees. Horizontal-tail area, sweep, and arm.
- Inlet-bump height, cowl coordinates, capture area, and nozzle diameter or length.
- Canopy length and height. Landing-gear track, wheelbase, tire size, A wheel count, and door outlines.
- Weapon-bay length, width, and depth. The B bay being “14 inches shorter” does not supply the A bay length. Pylon buttock lines. Hinge lines.
- Gun muzzle station and port diameter. EOTS window size. DAS camera coordinates. Probe coordinates. Light and antenna drawings.
- A paint specification.

Also not used, because the page could not be opened: USAF fact-sheet mirrors that search snippets quote as length 51 ft, height 14 ft, thrust 43,000 lb, and internal fuel 18,498 lb. Those figures are not in this note. Wikipedia’s 43,000 lbf / 28,000 lbf pair is recorded above as WIKI-ONLY only.

Rejected on purpose: planform angles measured off photographs or three-views, including student reports that print a leading-edge sweep near 34°. Those are pixel measurements. They are not copied and not averaged with anything.
