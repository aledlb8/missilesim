# MiG-29 9.13 Fulcrum-C

Single-seat Mikoyan MiG-29, OKB product **9.13** (NATO Fulcrum-C). Gear-up airframe is the model. Gear-down is only in [Landing gear](#landing-gear).

No opened official MiG, UAC, or Rosoboronexport page prints a 9.13-only exterior drawing or a 9.13-only length, span, height, or wing area. RAC MiG prints one size for **MiG-29 version B** and **MiG-29SE** and does not print the product code 9.13 on that page. Where a 9.13 table exists, it repeats the same overall length, span, height, and wing area as the basic single-seater. Those shared numbers are tagged with the variant the page actually names. A 9.12 number is not applied to a part a source says changed on the 9.13.

## Variant being modeled

Model this:

- One seat. Product 9.13, first flight of the converted airframe No. 406 on 4 May 1984, series from 1986. Soviet (later Russian) single-seaters of this batch, not the carrier family and not the MiG-29M / MiG-35.
- The 9.12 planform, inlets, gun installation, and tail type, plus only the exterior changes a source actually prints for the 9.13.

Published exterior changes of the 9.13 relative to the 9.12:

- **Spine.** Enlarged dorsal spine (гаргрот) behind the cockpit, the “hump” / fatback. In side view the raised top runs aft and the taper is a straight line that curves down at the end. It is higher and longer than the 9.12 spine, which drops away just aft of the canopy. No opened source prints the hump’s height, length, or cross-section in metres. Do not inflate it from the fuel figure: the overlay tank that was sketched for this bay was not built; the bay was used for the Gardeniya jammer, and fuselage tank No. 1 was only rearranged by **240 litres** (printed; not a length).
- **Wingtip contour.** New tip outline so ECM antennas could sit in the tips. No metre size for the new contour. This is not a missile rail.
- **Tail-boom ends.** A recognition guide says the ends of the tail booms are slightly different. No measurement.
- **Chord.** No opened source says the **wing** chord changed on the 9.13. The rudder chord did change, but not as a 9.13-only part: from 1984 every MiG-29 got rudders whose chord is **21%** greater, with the trailing edge standing proud of the fin trailing edge. Series 9.13 (1986) has those later rudders. Early 9.12 aircraft do not. Do not draw a unique 9.13 wing chord.
- **Gun.** No opened source says the gun port, the left leading-edge-root fairing, or the gun installation changed on the 9.13. Keep the 9.12 arrangement: one fixed GSh-301 in the **left** leading-edge extension.
- **Chaff fairing.** Not unique to the 9.13. On 9.12 aircraft except the first series, and on the 9.13, ridges on the upper wing ahead of the fin leading-edge extensions (форкили) hold the BVP-30-26M dispensers. Aircraft built before 1984 lack those ridges. The first series had two ventral fins (фальшкили) under the rear fuselage; later aircraft, including the 9.13 prototype as tested, do not. Do not put chaff inside the spine and do not delete the over-wing ridges: those spine dispensers are a MiG-29M feature.

Do not build these other airframes and call the result a 9.13:

## How to use these numbers in Blender

- Units are metres. Convert only from the printed unit. Do not average two printed values. If two sources disagree, both rows stay.
- Origin: the **tip of the nose pitot boom** (штанга ПВД), on the aircraft centreline. That point is `(0, 0, 0)`. It is not the tip of the radome. The radome is aft of this origin.
- Axes: **+X** to the pilot’s right, **+Y** forward (toward the pitot tip), **+Z** up. The airframe lies at **Y ≤ 0**.
- `aft_m` is positive going aft. Blender **Y = −aft_m**.
- `up_m` is **+Z** in metres from the pitot-tip origin. No opened source prints the pitot tip’s height above the construction horizontal (строительная горизонталь) or above the ground, so `up_m` of the fin tip, the wing, or the contact patch is **not published**. The printed height **4.73 m** is an overall aircraft height. It is not a Z coordinate and it is not “fin tip above the pitot”.
- No opened source says whether 4.73 m is oleos extended, oleos compressed, or to a named point (fin tip is the usual reading, and it is not stated).
- Pose for the airframe sections is **gear up**, doors shut, main inlet ramps open, upper louvres shut. Gear-down is a separate section.
- Angles are the printed angles. A dash in a simulator sentence (“angle - 25°”) is that manual’s separator, not a minus, except where the value itself carries a sign (`-3°`, `-60°`, `+56°`).

## Fuselage cross-sections

Radii, waterline heights, and buttock lines are **not published**. No cross-section table was opened.

What is published, for the basic single-seat airframe, as shape rather than as metres:

- Blended body. The text assigns about **40%** of lift to the body and **60%** to the wing, and says that above **17°** angle of attack the body and the leading-edge extensions carry more of the lift. Those are lift shares, not cross-section areas.
- Nose is a radio-transparent glass-fibre cone over the radar antenna. The pitot boom, with horizontal plates that generate vortices, stands on that cone. A marker-beacon antenna is on the inner surface of the cone.
- KOLS sensors sit in a spherical glass fairing on the upper nose, **offset to the right** of the centreline, ahead of the windscreen. No offset distance.
- Lower nose: IFF and air-traffic transponder antennas, radio-altimeter antennas, a nav antenna, and a sideslip vane. Angle-of-attack vanes are on **both** sides. A reserve pitot is on the **right** side of the nose bay.
- IFF interrogator antennas are either ahead of the windscreen or under the nose, “depending on the modification”. Which of those the 9.13 uses is not stated on the construction page.
- Cockpit canopy geometry is in [Canopy](#canopy). Linear canopy size is not published.
- Engines sit in separate nacelles, at **4°** to the construction horizontal and **1.5°** to the plane of symmetry. The page does not say whether 4° is nose-up or nose-down along the engine, or whether 1.5° is toe-in or toe-out. Do not pick a sign.
- Between the nacelles: accessory gearbox, and the GTDE-117 exhaust on the **lower** surface, right of centre, behind a door. A generator-cooling intake is on the **upper** surface aft of frame No. 7, left of centre.
- The 9.13 spine is an added upper contour from behind the canopy toward the fin roots. No section radii. See the variant section. Do not replace this contour with an SMT spine or a MiG-29M beaver-tail spine.

## Wing

No opened source says the 9.13 wing planform, area, sweep, anhedral, or chords changed. The exception is the **tip contour**, which the 9.13 history says was redrawn for ECM antennas, without a new chord or span. Use the basic-airframe wing numbers and only change the tip outline. Do not take the MiG-29M’s longer ailerons or four-pylon wing.

The printed root chord, tip chord, span, and area are not a simple trapezoid of each other. `(5.6 + 1.27) / 2 × 11.36` is about **39.02 m²**, and `11.36² / 38.056` is about **3.39**, against the printed area **38.056 m²** and the printed aspect ratio **3.5**. Those products are **DERIVED** checks, not dimensions. Do not “correct” a chord or the span to force a match. The page itself says the chord ratio **is** the taper 4.41 (`5.6 / 1.27`).

## Empennage

Fins stand on the tail section, one each side of the engine nacelles. Lateral distance between fins, in metres, is **not published**. Cant is published. Do not invent a spacing from the nacelle width.

Stabilizer anhedral is **not** a clean number. The construction page prints **−33°**. That is the characters on the page. A 33° anhedral is not repeated anywhere else opened for this note. The simulator manual prints **3°30′** with the same dash it uses as a label separator (“angle - 25°”), and it does not put a minus on that 3°30′. Do not average −33 and 3.5. Do not silently rewrite −33° into −3°30′. Leave the stabilizer dihedral unset until a primary drawing is available, or treat both prints as unresolved.

## Colors sufficient to block out a model

No opened source prints a factory enamel code, FS number, or measured colour boundary in millimetres. Block the 9.13 in the pattern below and pick paints later.

For izdeliye 9.13 as delivered to a Soviet unit (1521st, the page’s example), the factory finish was **dark greenish grey over neutral grey**, and unlike the 9.12 it was a set pattern:

- Darker colour on the extremities: forward fuselage, outer wings, upper halves of the fins, and the stabilizer tips, so the jet looks more compact at a distance.
- On the wing, the colour split runs **chordwise** from the joint between the inboard and outboard leading-edge-flap sections to the joint between the trailing-edge flap and the aileron.
- The black anti-glare panel on the 9.13 continues to the **rear** of the windscreen. On the 9.12 it stops in line with the **front** of the windscreen.

That is a standard service scheme, not the special yellow-outlined wingtip and stabilizer-tip paint applied later to a few aggressor aircraft so they would read as an F-15 at long range. Do not use that aggressor paint as the baseline, and do not use a post-service digital scheme as the factory outline.

The 9.12 had no single camouflage pattern. Do not copy an arbitrary 9.12 splinter onto the 9.13 spine and call it factory.

## Not published

Kept here so they are not filled by guesswork or by another variant.

- 9.13-only length without the pitot. Two conflicting 9.12 prints exist (14.875 m and 15.95 m). Neither is labeled 9.13.
- Any station in metres from the pitot tip: frames, cockpit, wing apex, nozzle, fin root.
- Fuselage cross-section radii, waterlines, and buttock lines.
- Height of the pitot tip above the construction horizontal or the ground. Oleo state of the 4.73 m height.
- Spine height, spine length, and spine cross-section of the 9.13 hump.
- Distance between the fins.
- A reliable stabilizer anhedral. Prints are −33° and 3°30′ with an unclear sign.
- Which fin-sweep to trust where 47°50′ and 47°30′ both appear.
- Inlet first-panel angle, throat dimensions, and nozzle exit diameter.
- Sign of the engine’s 4° and 1.5° installation angles.
- Canopy length, height, and width. Do not import a MiG-29M “higher canopy”.
- Pylon coordinates and which side is station 1.
- Wingtip-rail geometry. There is no rail in the sources.
- Gun-port station, muzzle coordinates, and the size of the gun-bay vents.
- Chaff-fairing length and cross-section.
- Leading-edge-extension area from an OEM document (4.71 m² is only the simulator manual).
- Trailing-edge sweep as a hard number (“near 9°” only).
- Factory paint codes.
- Wing chord change, gun-fairing change, or inlet change specific to the 9.13. Sources do not publish those deltas.
- MiG-29K span, MiG-29M inlet geometry, and MiG-29SMT spine coordinates. Not this aircraft, and not copied in here.
