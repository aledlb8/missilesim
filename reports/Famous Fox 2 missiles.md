# Model Fox 2s by flight kind

A point-mass simulator should model Fox 2s that change the equations, not every suffix in the catalogs, and it should hard-code thrust only where a manual printed impulse and burn time. The rounds that earn a card are the rear-aspect reticle canards (AIM-9B, AIM-9D, AIM-9H, AIM-9J, R-3S, Magic I), the light R-60 and R-60M, the all-aspect canards with no thrust vectoring (AIM-9L, AIM-9M, AIM-9P-5, German AIM-9L/I, Magic II, PL-5EII, PL-9C), the high off-boresight weapons that actually publish a different control law (R-73E and R-73M, RVV-MD, AIM-9X Block I, AIM-9X Block II and Block II+ as one kinematic body, IRIS-T, Python 5, PL-10, AAM-5, A-Darter), the tail-controlled sprinter ASRAAM, and the medium-range infrared pair MICA IR and R-27T/ET. AIM-9X Block III was cancelled in fiscal year 2014 and is not a weapon. R-74M2 and MICA NG do not inherit a parent performance card. Only three motors are complete enough to integrate without invention: the AIM-9B’s **8,440 lbf·s in 2.2 s** at 70°F, the AIM-9D Mk 36 held as two unmerged figures (**3,500 lbf** average for 5 s versus a **2,645 lbf** sea-level rating), and the R-3S grain. Seeker stops stay paired the same way. The AIM-9B gimbal is both **25° and 30°**, the R-73 **cue is not its coordinator**, and Magic’s range is not a single number between the French and English tables. Navigation ratio stays inside **3 to 5**. Propellant mass is published for the R-3S alone.

## Flight kind, not the suffix, earns a card

The useful cut is the term in the point-mass equations that changes, not fame by itself. A rear-aspect canard on a short boost, an all-aspect canard on an unknown Mk 36, a jet-vane missile that can turn at low dynamic pressure, a large-diameter tail-controlled sprinter, and an inertially cued medium-range round are five different weapons. Two missiles that differ only by a classified range are one card with a blank impulse.

The calibration round is the AIM-9B. NAVWEPS OP 2309, not the later summaries, is the kinematic source: length **111.5 in**, diameter **5 in**, rolleron wing span **22 in**, launch weight **160 lb**, Mk 8 warhead **25 lb** of which about **10.5 lb** is explosive, lethal radius about **30 ft**. Later summaries say **155 lb**. Use the manual’s 160 lb and leave the 5 lb gap unresolved. It is a rail launch, uncooled lead-sulfide reticle, tail-cone only, canards, rollerons, no thrust vectoring, and it left production by 1962 ([OP 2309](https://archive.org/details/OP23093rdAIM9B); [Parsch](https://www.designation-systems.net/dusrm/m-9.html)). The R-3S is the Soviet copy that still has a motor manual: **75.3 kg**, length **2,838 mm**, diameter **127 mm**, span **528 mm**, warhead **11.3 kg**, uncooled PbS seeker, canards to **±18°**, rollerons, rail launch, rear hemisphere, and a launch veto when the fighter is already pulling about **2 g** (about **1.6 g** above 12 km). It is a limited or second-line weapon in the catalogs that were opened, not a current first-line Russian Fox 2 ([Missilery R-3S](https://en.missilery.info/missile/r3c)). Magic I is the French member of that generation: cooled PbS seeker AD3601, rear aspect only, canards, free-to-spin notched tails, no thrust vectoring, single-stage solid. The French table gives **89 kg**, **2.75 m**, diameter **157 mm**, span **0.66 m**, warhead **12.5 kg**, Mach 3 with no flight condition, and a non-frontal **140°** employment area. English text adds a **35 g** airframe claim, arming at **1.8 s**, and self-destruct at **26 s**, and it does not agree with the French card on length, warhead, or range ([French Wikipedia, Magic](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matra_R550_Magic); [English Wikipedia, Magic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R.550_Magic)).

The AIM-9D is the cooled middle case, not an all-aspect AIM-9L. Nitrogen-cooled PbS, instantaneous field **2.5°**, gimbal **40°** from the missile axis, track rate **12°/s**, single-stage Mk 36, continuous-rod warhead whose ring opens to **34 ft**, gas-generator endurance a nominal **60 s**, LAU-7 rail, about **1,000** rounds. Kopp’s “seeker field slightly beyond 25°” loses to the handbook’s 40° gimbal; it is not averaged with it ([OP 3353](https://archive.org/stream/op-2309-2nd-sidewinder-guided-missle-mark-2/OP%203353%20AIM-9C%20&D%20PIlot%27s%20Handbook_djvu.txt); [Kopp 1994](https://www.ausairpower.net/TE-Sidewinder-94.html)). The AIM-9H is the solid-state Navy son of that airframe and the parent of the AIM-9L: still rear-aspect PbS, still a Mk 36, track rate **20°/s** in Parsch and only **>12°/s** in Kopp’s table, weight about **186 lb**, production either about **3,000** or about **7,700**. Do not copy the AIM-9D’s thrust line onto the H. The AIM-9J is the Air Force branch rushed out in July 1972: Peltier-cooled PbS, track **16.5°/s**, double-delta canards, length **10.0 ft**, span **1.9 ft**, **170 lb**, gas generator for a **40 s** flight, and a Mk 17 in both Kopp and DoD 4120.15-L. Parsch’s shared **18 km** cell is not a J range. “Doubled single-plane g” is a ratio against the AIM-9E, which has no absolute g here, so the J has no absolute g either ([Kopp 1994](https://www.ausairpower.net/TE-Sidewinder-94.html); [Parsch](https://www.designation-systems.net/dusrm/m-9.html)).

The R-60 is the light high-g canard, not a small Archer. Missilery: **43.5 kg** and **44 kg** for the R-60M, lengths **2,095 mm** and **2,138 mm**, diameter **120 mm**, span **390 mm**, launch limit **7 g**, missile overload **47 g** against Weaponsystems’ **42 g**, uncooled Komar cued only to **12°** and tracking at **35°/s**. The low-altitude launch bracket is **0.25–1.5 km** and the high-altitude bracket is **0.50–8 km**. Those are altitude bands, not one range to average. The R-60M’s cooled seeker opens a close front-hemisphere shot out to a **±20°** cue. It is still rollerons and canards, with a thrust curve described as variable and never given as a burn time. Neither 42 g nor 47 g is a low-speed thrust-vector number ([Missilery R-60](https://en.missilery.info/missile/r60); [Weaponsystems R-60](https://weaponsystems.net/system/218-Molniya%20R-60)).

All-aspect capability arrived on canard airframes that still do not vector thrust. The AIM-9L puts an argon-cooled InSb seeker and an FM reticle on the Navy H airframe, with the DSU-15 laser fuze and the **WDU-17/B** annular blast-fragmentation warhead at **9.4 kg**. Length is **9.5 ft** (Kopp), **2.85 m** (Parsch), or **9 ft 5 in** (the Air Force sheet). Mass clusters at **190–191 lb**. Track rate is classified in the source that built the seeker table; a **22°/s** figure seen only as an unopened footnote is not used, and the AIM-9D’s **40° / 2.5°** optics are not copied onto the L ([Parsch](https://www.designation-systems.net/dusrm/m-9.html); [Kopp 1994](https://www.ausairpower.net/TE-Sidewinder-94.html)). The AIM-9M is that missile with a reduced-smoke all-boost Mk 36, a revised guidance section, and published background rejection whose circuit is not described. A 2025 directory still lists the M and M-9 as active. US production contracts in 2026 are not for the M ([ATK Mk 36 datasheet](https://web.archive.org/web/20060311205034/http://www.atk.com/datasheet_PDFs/sidewinder_mk36.pdf); [NAVAIR AIM-9X](https://www.navair.navy.mil/product/AIM-9X-Sidewinder)). The AIM-9P-5 is not an L with a new paint scheme. It is the long Air Force J/N body after the P-4 added an all-aspect InSb seeker and the P-5 added IRCCM: Kopp’s **10.0 ft**, span **1.9 ft**, **190 lb**, motor **SR.116**, track rate only **>16.5°/s**, and “less agile” than the L. Wikipedia’s “9.8 ft” conflicts with the 10.0 ft table it otherwise follows. Keep Kopp’s length and do not reuse a Mk 36 impulse ([Kopp 1994](https://www.ausairpower.net/TE-Sidewinder-94.html)). The German AIM-9L/I is a guidance-section upgrade of the Lima, hard-wired flare rejection rather than an imaging array, and the L/I-1 is described as a further flare-discrimination pass aimed at the same job as the AIM-9M. The Bundeswehr card that was opened matches the Lima airframe: **84 kg**, **2.87 m**, diameter **12.7 cm**, span **63 cm**. No German burn time was printed, so the motor stays an AIM-9L unknown ([Scramble, 2011 capture](https://web.archive.org/web/20110724165145/http://wiki.scramble.nl/index.php?title=Raytheon_AIM-9_Sidewinder); [Bundeswehr, 2024 capture](https://web.archive.org/web/20240421072453/https://www.bundeswehr.de/de/ausruestung-technik-bundeswehr/ausruestung-bewaffnung/aim-9l-i-sidewinder)).

Magic II, from 1986, replaces the AD3601 with the all-aspect AD3633 and is described as having a motor about **10 percent** more powerful. There is no baseline thrust, so that sentence produces no newton figure. An afterburner-ionisation filter is the specific behavior: a reheat spike must not be treated as the target. English text gives Magic 2 a **20 km** range, Mach 2, and **50 g**; the French unsplit table still says **0.3–15 km** and Mach 3. India was still replacing in-service Magic, along with the R-73, with ASRAAM in requests reported around April 2026 ([French Wikipedia, Magic](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matra_R550_Magic); [English Wikipedia, ASRAAM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIM-132_ASRAAM)). PL-5EII is the Chinese manufacturer card in this class: canard, dual-color multi-element seeker, laser proximity fuze, all-aspect, off-boresight launch modes with no degree printed, **Mach 3**, **35 g**, diameter **127 mm**, span **617 mm**, length **2,893 mm** on the AVIC page and **2,896 mm** on the 2009 LOEC page. The **16 km** figure is maximum detection range, not flight range. The 1998 PL-5E claims of **±25° / ±40°** and **40 g** belong to a different export variant and are not copied ([AVIC PL-5EII](https://www.avic.com/c/2021-05-08/513084.shtml); [LOEC PL-5EII, 2009](https://web.archive.org/web/20090521160043/http://www.loec.cn/e05.html)). PL-9C is the other open AVIC card: **115 kg**, diameter **157 mm**, span **856 mm**, brochure range **20 km** with no altitude or aspect, length **2,992 mm** versus the 2009 LOEC **2,900 mm**, multi-element infrared, radar and helmet slave, canard control, no thrust vectoring and no gimbal stop on the manufacturer pages. A CASI row at **123 kg** and **15 km** is an earlier PL-9, which matches the qualitative claim that the C is lighter and longer-ranged, and it is not the C’s data ([AVIC PL-9C](https://www.avic.com/c/2021-05-08/513083.shtml); [CASI, 2020](https://www.airuniversity.af.edu/Portals/10/CASI/documents/Research/Infrastructure/2020-11-%2030%20Air-to-Air%20Missiles%20and%20Guidance%20Systems.pdf)).

High off-boresight missiles split again, by whether the jet is deflected and by whether the seeker images. The R-73E brochure is **105 kg**, rod warhead **8 kg**, **2.9 × 0.17 × 0.51 m**, cue **±45°**, coordinator **±75°**, sight-line rate **60°/s**, front-hemisphere launch range **30 km**, rear minimum **0.3 km**, target up to **12 g**, combined aerodynamic and gas-dynamic control. Missilery also prints a **7.4 kg** warhead, a **20 km** table cell against its own prose of **30 km**, and **40 g** of own overload, with four gas-dynamic interceptors that work only while the single-mode motor burns and aerodynamic rudders after burnout. The R-73M / RMD-2 card is **110 kg**, cue **60°**, and a **40 km** prose range, plus digital processing and, on one described variant, a dual-band seeker and a target-designation area printed as **+90°** rather than ±90°. Do not stretch the 105 kg motor until a burn time exists. Rosoboronexport still publishes the R-73E ([Rosoboronexport R-73E](http://www.roe.ru/en/production/aerospace-forces/air-weapons/guided-weapons/air-to-air-guided-missiles/r-73e/); [Missilery R-73](https://en.missilery.info/missile/r73)). RVV-MD is the later export card that was actually opened: **106 kg**, **8 kg** rod, **2.92 m × 0.17 m**, wing span **0.51 m**, rudder span **0.385 m**, dual-band seeker, cue **±60°**, coordinator **±75°**, front-hemisphere launch range up to **40 km**, rear minimum **0.3 km**, and a stated lock at **10 km** against a fighter in the front hemisphere at aspect **15°**, maximum engine, medium altitude. Gas-dynamic control lasts only for the burn. R-74M2’s reduced span, inertial midcourse, and 2019 trials sit on a Wikipedia footnote that did not load. Omit R-74M2, or carry the name with no kinematics ([KTRV RVV-MD, 2015 archive](https://web.archive.org/web/20151127145058/http://eng.ktrv.ru/production_eng/323/503/566/); [Missilery RVV-MD](https://en.missilery.info/missile/rvv-md)).

The AIM-9X is the US imaging, jet-vane missile. NAVAIR’s unsplit specification is length **9.9 ft (3.02 m)**, launch weight **186 lb (84.37 kg)**, diameter **5 in**, wingspan **17.6 in**, power plant **ATK Mk 139**, annular blast-fragmentation, range and speed **classified**. The airframe is fixed forward wings, tail fins, and four jet vanes in a motor descended from the AIM-9M. The seeker that was published is a **128×128** mid-wave staring array. Block I reached Air Force IOC in **November 2003** and is lock-before-launch by boresight or radar slave. Block II adds the datalink and lock-on after launch, including targets behind the fighter; full-rate production was **August 2015**, and a directory gives IOC as **September 2016**. Block II+ has been procured since **December 2017** for the F-35 and was still on Lot 26 contracts in 2026. No opened source gives II+ a different mass, motor, or array. Model II and II+ as one kinematic card with two names. Block I is the same body without the fielded datalink mode ([NAVAIR AIM-9X](https://www.navair.navy.mil/product/AIM-9X-Sidewinder); [GAO-16-329SP](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-16-329sp.pdf); [RAND MG-109](https://www.rand.org/content/dam/rand/pubs/monographs/2004/RAND_MG109.pdf)).

IRIS-T is the German-led answer to that same requirement, and it is the one that kept thrust-vector vanes when Britain kept a big motor. In Luftwaffe service since **December 2005**. Saab prints length **2,936 mm**, diameter **127 mm**, speed **Mach 3**, range **approximately 25 km** with no condition, Sidewinder-compatible rail, imaging seeker, lock-on before and after launch, shots from point-blank including behind the fighter. English Wikipedia adds **87.4 kg**, span **447 mm**, warhead **11.4 kg**, and HTPB; the German body says about **90 kg**. The seeker is not a staring 128×128 array. A **128×2** two-color InSb line scans a **128×128** image **80** times a second, with look angle **±90°**. The motor description is a shaped single grain: a boost to leave the fighter, a low-thrust interval for a turn of up to **180°**, acceleration to Mach 3, then a sustain. That is not a dual-pulse motor, and no thrust in newtons was printed. The **60 g at 60°/s** sentence is Wikipedia citing Diehl, not a number on the Saab page; the German “well over 100 g” line is an author’s inference and is not used ([Saab IRIS-T](https://www.saab.com/products/iris-t); [German Wikipedia, IRIS-T](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/IRIS-T); [English Wikipedia, IRIS-T](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IRIS-T)).

ASRAAM is the opposite design, and Block 6 does not change it. MBDA’s live page: **88 kg**, **2.9 m**, diameter **166 mm**, cue from radar or helmet, lock-on before and after launch, agility credited to the motor diameter and a low-drag tailed body, not to vanes. Germany left the joint programme to obtain thrust vectoring, and that requirement became IRIS-T. Designation-systems gives a dual-thrust boost/sustain motor, low smoke, fin span **45 cm**, weight **87 kg**, minimum range around **300 m**, warhead **10 kg**, and **50 g** from the tail surfaces immediately after launch, which is an aerodynamic claim after the motor has accelerated the round, not jet-vane steering at zero airspeed. Range is **25+ km** in Wikipedia’s attribution to MBDA and a rough **15 km** estimate on designation-systems, which also says the true figure is probably higher. Neither is a no-escape range. Blocks 1–5 use a **128×128** imaging seeker and about **90°** off-boresight as a half-angle. Block 6 reached Typhoon initial operating capability on **1 April 2022** with an internal cooler and a higher-resolution British seeker. No opened source gives Block 6 a new motor, range, or g, and the new pixel count was not stated ([MBDA ASRAAM](https://www.mbda-systems.com/product/asraam/); [designation-systems.net, AIM-132](https://www.designation-systems.net/dusrm/m-132.html); [UK Defence Journal, 2022](https://ukdefencejournal.org.uk/asraam-block-6-enters-service-on-typhoon/)).

Python 5 is the Rafael full-sphere round with a manufacturer geometry and almost no envelope. The brochure prints **105 kg**, length **310 cm**, diameter **16 cm**, span **64 cm**, a dual-waveband imaging seeker that the product page specifies as infrared plus CCD, lock-on before and after launch, a two-stage motor, and a full-sphere launch picture. It does not print kilometres, g, gimbal degrees, or thrust-vector control. Mach 4 and range over **20 km**, and an **11 kg** warhead, are 2003 press figures, not the brochure. A **40 km** SPYDER-table figure and a trade-press “more than 100°” are not Rafael sentences. Thrust vectoring stays off unless a later Rafael page states it. Python 4, which Python 5 is said by trade press to inherit, is explicitly “thrust vectoring is not employed” in Kopp’s 1997 description, with off-boresight only “in excess of 60°” and most of the forward hemisphere. The FAS Python-4 specification table is the Python-3 table reused, including a mid-1980s date, and it must not be loaded ([Rafael Python 5 brochure](https://www.rafael.co.il/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/python-Air-to-Air-and-Air-defense-Missile.pdf); [Rafael PYTHON-5](https://www.rafael.co.il/system/python5/); [Kopp 1997](https://www.ausairpower.net/TE-Gen-4-AAM-97.html)).

PL-10 / PL-10E has no AVIC table in the sources that opened. What RUSI and CASI support without a degree is an imaging seeker, helmet cue, lock-on after launch, thrust vectoring, and very high g. The repeated secondary numbers are **105 kg** and **20 km**, with diameter **160 mm** from a 2018 airshow-parameter article and length **3.0 m** from CASI’s table. A **89 kg** photo estimate and a **33 kg** warhead on a 2026 aggregator are discarded. CASI’s own angle wording is “several times” a previous-generation **30°**; **±90°** is a secondary report, and **60 g** is CASI and Chinese media, not a factory limit. A simulated hit at **20 km** and bearing **38°** is one exercise, not a definition of maximum range. Pakistan displayed a PL-10E in February 2026. Do not copy A-Darter numbers across, and do not treat RUSI’s “superior kinematic performance to the AIM-9X” as a licence to invent a Mach or a kilometre the US round leaves classified ([RUSI, 2020](https://static.rusi.org/russian_and_chinese_combat_air_trends_whr_final_web_version.pdf); [CASI, 2020](https://www.airuniversity.af.edu/Portals/10/CASI/documents/Research/Infrastructure/2020-11-%2030%20Air-to-Air%20Missiles%20and%20Guidance%20Systems.pdf)).

AAM-5 is in JASDF service, including the AAM-5B, and the public MoD text still does not give range or gimbal degrees. The architecture that is described, rather than tabulated, is the card: no canards, thrust-vector motor plus all-moving tails, imaging focal plane, image-based flare rejection, inertial midcourse, lock-on after launch, laser proximity fuze, and a high off-boresight capability used with JHMCS on upgraded F-15Js. AAM-5B lengthens cooling with a Stirling cooler, adds a dual-wavelength infrared sensor, and in the Japanese text changes the gimbal from three-axis to two-axis. The English Wikipedia reversal of that gimbal story is the one to reject. The infobox **95 kg**, **3.105 m**, **35 km**, and **Mach 3** are a commercial-handbook row, not an ATLA table, and they stay tagged that way or they stay out of the integrator ([JASDF ADTW](https://www.mod.go.jp/asdf/adtw/adm/shiken/kakoshiken_missile2.html); [ATLA](https://www.mod.go.jp/atla/en/soubi_system.html); [Japanese Wikipedia, Type 04](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/04%E5%BC%8F%E7%A9%BA%E5%AF%BE%E7%A9%BA%E8%AA%98%E5%B0%8E%E5%BC%BE)).

A-Darter is the wingtip imaging missile whose mass Denel and Jane’s agree on: **93 kg**, length **2.98 m**, diameter **166 mm**, thrust-vector control, two-color thermal imager, helmet or radar or autonomous designation, lock-on after launch. Jane’s, after the September 2019 qualification, adds tail span **488 mm**, range **23 km** with no condition, look angle **180°**, track rate **120°/s**, and **100 g** from body lift and thrust vectoring. A 180° look angle reads naturally as **±90°** from the nose; Jane’s does not say “180° off boresight.” DefenceWeb’s earlier “approximately 20 km” stays as a conflict. Wikipedia’s **89 kg**, **10 km**, **8 s** burn, and **50 g** after burnout were not in the Jane’s article or the Denel data block and are not adopted. The IMU’s **30 g** is what the instrument is said to measure, not the airframe limit ([Janes, 7 October 2019](https://www.janes.com/osint-insights/defence-news/a-darter-aam-formally-qualified); [Denel A-Darter PDF](http://admin.denel.co.za/uploads/A-Darter.pdf)).

Two medium-range infrared rounds are not dogfight skins. MICA IR shares the **112 kg**, **3.1 m**, **160 mm** airframe with the radar MICA, entered service in **2000**, and is still the fielded infrared MICA. The 2022 MBDA datasheet gives a strapdown inertial unit, data link, lock-on before and after launch, a dual-waveband imaging seeker, tail control, thrust-vector control, and one high-impulse low-smoke solid motor. It does not say dual-pulse, and it does not print a loft. French sources put the infrared intercept at about **60 km** and the radar MICA at about **80 km**, with **50 g** at short range and still **30 g** at “several tens of kilometres.” An English discussion of the same 50 g / 30 g pair attaches 30 g to **12 km** inside a vertical-launch paragraph. Keep both contexts. Minimum range on the French and Airforce Technology pages is **500 m**. A rear-sector shot was demonstrated on **11 June 2007** using designation from another aircraft and Link 16. MICA NG, whose first air launch was in June 2026, is the dual-pulse missile, with deliveries expected around 2030 and a range growth MBDA states as up to about **40 percent**. Giving today’s MICA IR a second pulse is a contradiction ([MBDA MICA datasheet, 2022](https://www.mbda-systems.com/sites/mbda/files/2024-07/2022%20MICA%20datasheet.pdf); [French Wikipedia, MICA](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/MICA)).

The R-27T and R-27ET are the other medium-range infrared pair: launch masses **245 kg** and **343 kg**, warhead **39 kg** on every version, missile operational load factor **8 g**, canards and no thrust vectoring, rail launch rather than the catapult reserved for radar seekers. The T uses the single-mode **230 mm** motor and the ET the larger **260 mm** “double-mode” motor, which in this family’s English text means two thrust regimes in one burn, not a coast and a relight. No thrust split was printed. They are in the catalogs through at least a 2019 export order, not described as a turning fight inside 1 km ([Missilery R-27](https://en.missilery.info/missile/p27); [Airforce Technology, R-27](https://www.airforce-technology.com/projects/r-27-aa-10-alamo-guided-medium-range-air-missile/)).

Several famous names are real and still the wrong card to fill with borrowed numbers. Shafrir 2 is a rear-aspect canard, production ended in **1978**, with a **10°** off-boresight acquisition that is not Kopp’s **45°** target-aspect figure, and its “over 2 g” line is not a missile limit. Python 3 is an all-aspect canard of the AIM-9L generation, about **120 kg**, warhead **11 kg**, high-altitude effective range **15 km** and low-level **5 km**, acquisition about **30°** off the nose and in-flight tracking **40°**, with **40 g** only on a secondary compilation and no thrust vectoring. PL-8 is the licensed Python 3, and CASI’s own PL-8 row (**115 kg**, **15 km**, over **38 g**, over Mach 4, dual-thrust) already differs from the Python 3 compilations, so the Python 3 card is not pasted onto the PL-8. The R-13M is a cooled rear-aspect development with a **55 s** guided-flight time, which is control duration and not a burn, and the opened pages disagree on **87.7 kg** versus **90 kg**. AAM-3 is a dual-band infrared/ultraviolet canard with electric actuators and bank-to-turn, type-approved in **1990**, but the **91 kg** and **13 km** figures are a yearbook infobox, not a MoD table that was opened. Model any of these only with the numbers tagged secondary and the motor left blank ([WeaponSystems.net, Shafrir](https://weaponsystems.net/system/1193-Shafrir); [Kopp 1997](https://www.ausairpower.net/TE-Gen-4-AAM-97.html); [CASI, 2020](https://www.airuniversity.af.edu/Portals/10/CASI/documents/Research/Infrastructure/2020-11-%2030%20Air-to-Air%20Missiles%20and%20Guidance%20Systems.pdf)).

## Public impulse stops at the Atoll and two Sidewinders

The AIM-9B motor is the one clean Sidewinder curve. The Mk 15 or Mk 17 “provides a nominal impulse of **8,440 pound-seconds** with a **2.2-second** burning time at **70°F**.” Spread evenly, that is an average of **3,836 lbf** (**17,064 N**). The manual does not say the impulse is a sea-level static test, and it does not say the thrust is flat, so the average is a derived convenience, not a printed peak. The motor assembly is about **80 lb** and about **75 in** long, which bounds propellant mass from above and does not give it. Hold launch mass at 160 lb until a grain mass exists. The profile is boost-only. Kopp’s **8,200 lb·s** and Parsch’s **4,000 lbf** for 2.2 s disagree with the manual and are not blended into it. Under the manual’s typical conditions the burnout speed increment is about **Mach 1.5** above the firing aircraft, depending on altitude. The same page’s acceptance ranges, Mach **1.2** shooter against a Mach **0.9** co-altitude target, are **6,000 ft** at sea level and **28,000 ft** at **50,000 ft**. Those two ranges are what a body-referenced drag scale is allowed to be checked against. They are not a no-escape zone ([OP 2309](https://archive.org/details/OP23093rdAIM9B)).

The AIM-9D Mk 36 is published twice, and the two figures are not an average waiting to be taken. The pilot handbook says the motor gives an average of **3,500 lbf for 5 s** and a maximum of **Mach 2.5** over the firing aircraft. Impulse implied by that average is **17,500 lbf·s**, and the altitude and grain temperature of the 3,500 lbf sentence are not stated. The description manual’s table, same motor, lists normal-operation thrust **2,645 lb** at **70°F** at sea level, burning time **5 s** at **70°F**, and a minimum total impulse of **13,968 lb·s** at **70°F**. Because **2,645 × 5 = 13,225**, which sits below that minimum impulse, **2,645 lbf is not the table’s average thrust**. Do not replace the pair with a midpoint, and do not divide 13,968 by 5 and call the result the published thrust. Use either curve only as a labeled sensitivity. Do not fit the drag scale to the AIM-9D, and do not export either number onto the AIM-9H, L, M, P-5, or X. Parsch’s unconditioned **18 km** for the D has no altitude or target Mach ([OP 3353](https://archive.org/stream/op-2309-2nd-sidewinder-guided-missle-mark-2/OP%203353%20AIM-9C%20&D%20PIlot%27s%20Handbook_djvu.txt); [OP 3352](https://archive.org/download/OP3352AIM9D/OP%203352%20AIM-9D%201_text.pdf)).

From the AIM-9L onward the public Mk 36 record is a hardware description, not a thrust-time pair. The Alliant sheet for the AIM-9M motor says **all-boost**, a radial-burning finocyl of **reduced-smoke HTPB**, motor weight **99.0 lb**, length **71.0 in**, diameter **5.0 in**, and operating limits **−65°F to +160°F**. It prints no thrust, burn time, impulse, or propellant mass. Early M lots are Mod 9 in Kopp and M-8/M-9 are Mod 11 in Parsch; both can be true of different lots. The AIM-9X propulsion section is that motor modified to carry jet vanes, named **WPU-17/B** in the 2001 development description and **Mk 139** on the current NAVAIR page, with the development grain called **X-61**. NAVAIR still calls the rocket motor an AIM-9M legacy component. No new impulse was opened. A forum burn time, a video-game 30 g or 35 g on the AIM-9L, and a “60 g” AIM-9X that was not tied to an opened primary stay out ([ATK Mk 36 datasheet](https://web.archive.org/web/20060311205034/http://www.atk.com/datasheet_PDFs/sidewinder_mk36.pdf); [AIM-9X NTSP, 2001](https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/library/policy/navy/ntsp/aim-9x-a_2001.pdf)).

The R-3S is the other complete grain. The charge is **NMF-2**, **20.5 kg** with the inhibitor, an eight-point star that burns on the inner surface. Total impulse is **38,000–40,000 N·s** on the missile page and not less than **38,100 N·s** on the motor page. Under a mistranslated “Drag” heading the motor page prints maximum **25,000 N** and minimum **10,000 N** at **−54°C**, with burn time **1.7 s** at **+60°C** and **3.2 s** at **−54°C**. Dividing the 38,100 N·s floor by those burn times gives derived averages of about **22,400 N** hot and **11,900 N** cold. The printed 25,000 N sits above those averages, so it is a peak or a rated maximum, not the time average. Smoke class was not stated. Potassium sulfate in the grain is described as reducing jet impingement on the carrier, not as a smoke suppressant. An orange tracer burns at least **23 s**. Almost all of the **≥21 s** of control power is coast, because the motor is over in under about 3 s ([Missilery PRD-80A](https://en.missilery.info/missile/r3c/5-ots)).

Every other motor in this set is an input the user sets, constrained by the schedule words that were printed and by nothing else. The R-73 page’s **7,700 N** is unlabeled as peak or average, has no burn time, and is a poor match for a peak boost on a 105 kg missile. It is not divided into an invented burn. The R-60’s PRD-259 is “variable” thrust and not boost-sustain, dual-pulse, or smokeless in the opened text. IRIS-T’s four-step shape is real and its newton levels are not. ASRAAM’s dual-burn boost/sustain is real and the sustain fraction is not. Python 5’s two-stage motor is real and the split is not. The R-27’s double-mode motor is two regimes in one continuous burn. MICA IR’s motor is one high-impulse low-smoke solid with thrust vectoring, and MICA NG’s dual-pulse is a different missile. PL-8’s “dual-thrust” is CASI’s wording without a split. A-Darter’s motor is a signature-minimised solid; DefenceWeb calls it smokeless. No propellant mass, burn time, or thrust was printed for Python 5, PL-5EII, PL-9C, PL-10, AAM-5, A-Darter, Magic, IRIS-T, ASRAAM, MICA, or the R-27, so none of them receives a derived impulse.

## Seeker class decides the shot, not a flare slider

Rear-aspect seekers lock on exhaust. The forward fraction of signature is zero, so a nose-on target is invisible at any range. That class is the AIM-9B, the R-3S, Magic I, the basic R-60, the AIM-9H, and the AIM-9J. The AIM-9B images a **4°** cone on the reticle. Kopp’s track rate is **11°/s** and a Wikipedia comparison table prints **8–11°/s**; keep the band. Head-on acquisition range is zero. The pilot fires on the audio tone, with the fighter pointed at the tail, and there is no helmet cue and no lock-on after launch ([OP 2309](https://archive.org/details/OP23093rdAIM9B); [Infrared homing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infrared_homing)). The R-3S seeker page prints three different angles that are not one gimbal: axle angle **±28°**, a tracking field worded as a **50°** cone, and a caging field of **3°30′**. Acquisition of an Il-28 from the tail above 15 km is **7,600 m**, which is not a fighter head-on range. The seeker must be at least **20°** from the sun on that page; a GlobalSecurity list says **30°**. Both stand. Threshold sensitivity “not exceeding **0.03 mW/cm²**” is printed; a track rate in degrees per second is not, and the page’s “72±5 rpm” gyro figure is not usable ([Missilery seeker](https://en.missilery.info/missile/r3c/1-ots)).

The AIM-9D is not that binary and not the all-aspect function. Detection range falls as the shot leaves the tail, and the handbook gives no exponent. Nose-on homing exists only for an afterburning target and only within **20°** of dead ahead. The intensity inside that 20° cone was not published, so it is not filled with the all-aspect forward fraction below. Selecting afterburner **doubles** detection range. A target on blue sky is detected at almost twice the range of the same target against cloud or ground. The sun can pull the seeker, and the pilot holds fire until the missile is at least **30°** off the sun. That 30° is a cockpit rule, larger than the 2.5° field, not a second gimbal stop ([OP 3352](https://ia800806.us.archive.org/29/items/OP3352AIM9D/OP%203352%20AIM-9D%201_hocr.html); [OP 3353](https://archive.org/stream/op-2309-2nd-sidewinder-guided-missle-mark-2/OP%203353%20AIM-9C%20&D%20PIlot%27s%20Handbook_djvu.txt)).

All-aspect seekers also see the warm airframe, and the stern is still much brighter. An open countermeasure article’s planning figure is a flare-to-target ratio of about **2:1** in the stern to seduce an unhardened reticle seeker, and the same flare “can be much greater than **10:1**” in the forward quadrant. That inequality puts forward-quadrant intensity under about **0.2** of stern intensity, so a head-on lock range is under about **0.45** of the tail-on range and, on the words “much greater,” smaller still. The sim should expose that forward fraction and default it at or below 0.2. It should not invent 0.1 as a measurement, and it should not use the R-27’s head-on kinematic range as a signature. Wikipedia’s R-27T envelope, about **2–33 km** head-on and **0–5.5 km** tail-on at the same altitude, runs the opposite way from infrared brightness because it is a flight-range table ([Journal of Electronic Defense, via The Free Library](https://www.thefreelibrary.com/Advanced+infrared+missile+counter-countermeasures.-a015149906); [R-27, English Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R-27_(air-to-air_missile))). The all-aspect reticle rounds are the AIM-9L and AIM-9M, the AIM-9P-4/P-5, the AIM-9L/I, Magic II, the R-60M in a close front quarter only, Python 3 inside its moderate cone, PL-5EII, and PL-9C. None of those pages printed a head-on lock range in kilometres. The R-27T’s **36T** seeker is all-aspect inside a **±55°** designation field, cooled for **3 h**, and still usable with no coolant at a reduced range. Its track rate and its gimbal stop, as distinct from that designation field, were not printed ([Missilery R-27](https://en.missilery.info/missile/p27)).

Counter-countermeasures are rules about which source wins, not a single resistance number, and not a procedure for defeating a missile. Four classes are specified without invented processing constants, and a fifth is named and left without band ratios. Class none, which covers the AIM-9B, R-3S, Magic I, and the AIM-9D, gives the track to the hottest in-band source in the instantaneous field; a new source brighter than about twice the aircraft, using the stern planning figure, takes the track. There is no rate test and no shape test. Class rise is the open temporal rule: a sharp jump rejects a flare and a slow brightening, such as afterburner light-up, does not. The article’s **2.5:1 within 40 ms** is a hypothetical illustration. It is not AIM-9M firmware. Put any such threshold in a named constant that says so. The AIM-9M is publicly better at rejecting flares and background than the L, and on the M-9 it has expanded countermeasure-detection circuitry. Which of the classes implements that sentence is unpublished. The P-5 and the L/I-1 inherit the same gap: flare rejection is real, and the circuit is not. Magic II’s afterburner filter is its own narrow rule, not a generic flare model ([Infrared homing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infrared_homing); [Kopp 1994](https://www.ausairpower.net/TE-Sidewinder-94.html)).

Class kinematic is the rosette or gate behavior described in the open literature: a candidate that separates, then decelerates or drops, leaves the gate around the aircraft track and is rejected. It is not tied here to one missile’s classified mode. Class imaging keeps the aim point on an extended aircraft and ignores a flare once the flare is a separated point. A flare still inside the aircraft image is not yet a second object. That class is the AIM-9X, ASRAAM, IRIS-T, MICA IR, Python 5, PL-10, AAM-5, and A-Darter. IRIS-T adds two published details inside the class: a library with **eight** aim points per known aircraft, and home-on-jam if a laser floods the scan. No further discrimination logic was published, and none should be added. Two-color rejection is the fifth class the same article names. It is the right label for the RVV-MD, the dual-band R-73M variant, PL-5EII, MICA’s two infrared bands, AAM-3’s infrared-plus-ultraviolet seeker, and AAM-5B’s dual-wavelength sensor, and it has no published band ratio to code ([Rafael Python 5](https://www.rafael-usa.com/programs/python-5/); [German Wikipedia, IRIS-T](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/IRIS-T)).

The gimbal limit is an angle from the missile body axis, not from the velocity vector. The instantaneous field is the small cone about the seeker boresight. Track rate is how fast that boresight may slew. If the target leaves the instantaneous field and the seeker cannot catch it, lock is lost and commanded homing acceleration goes to zero. The round then coasts under drag, whatever thrust remains, and gravity. A numeric memory-track time was not found, so the coast-under-lock duration stays zero until a source gives one. Rollerons are a passive roll damper, not a homing mode. Angle of attack is why the velocity vector is the wrong axis for the stop ([Infrared homing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infrared_homing)).

Lock-on after launch is a different guidance segment, not a wider reticle. At release the missile has a cue and no seeker track. It flies inertially along that line, or toward a predicted intercept if a data link is updating the point, slews the seeker inside the body-axis gimbal basket, and switches to proportional navigation only when irradiance and the instantaneous field both pass. The basket’s angular size, the update rate, and whether the cue is a line of sight or a full track are unpublished; using the gimbal limit as the basket is an inference. The missiles publicly described with this mode are AIM-9X Block II and Block II+, ASRAAM, IRIS-T, MICA IR, Python 5, PL-10, AAM-5, and A-Darter. Block I “demonstrated potential” for it and is not the fielded datalink mode. The R-73 is described as leaving the rail, orienting toward the launcher’s cue, and then locking, with a helmet among the cue sources, so the behavior is modeled even though “lock-on after launch” is a paraphrase. It does not receive MICA’s long inertial cruise. A classic Sidewinder, Magic, the R-60, PL-5EII, and PL-9C are lock-before-launch. PL-9C’s helmet slave still assumes the seeker has acquired before launch ([NAVAIR AIM-9X](https://www.navair.navy.mil/product/AIM-9X-Sidewinder); [MBDA MICA datasheet, 2022](https://www.mbda-systems.com/sites/mbda/files/2024-07/2022%20MICA%20datasheet.pdf)).

## Flight rules follow the rail, the burn, and N from 3 to 5

An air-to-air Fox 2 leaves a rail already at fighter speed. No opened source states a rail-exit delta in metres per second, so none is added. The Aero 3A launcher is **84.2 in** overall, which is only an upper bound on a slide of about **2.1 m**, and the real stroke is shorter once hangers and nose are counted. Integrate along the rail for that unknown stroke, or start at shooter velocity with a zero rail increment. A trigger delay of about **0.8 s** in the AIM-9C/D handbook happens before motion. It is not the in-flight guidance inhibit. On an AIM-9B, releasing the trigger during that delay can abort the launch; the AIM-9D handbook says the trigger need not be held. MICA is the air-to-air round that also publishes ejection launch, and the missile still has the aircraft’s velocity at release. The R-27 catapult is restricted to radar seekers, so the T and ET start on a rail. Vertical ejection, pitch-over, and terrain avoidance belong to a surface round, not to these missiles ([OP 2309 text](https://archive.org/stream/op-2309-2nd-sidewinder-guided-missle-mark-2/OP%202309%20(3rd)%20AIM-9B_djvu.txt); [MBDA MICA datasheet, 2022](https://www.mbda-systems.com/sites/mbda/files/2024-07/2022%20MICA%20datasheet.pdf)).

Guidance for a classic Sidewinder that already has a lock is zero commanded lateral acceleration until the inhibit ends, then true proportional navigation. The only published inhibit is the AIM-9B’s **0.5 s**, and the manual’s reason is overcontrol at low speed, not safe separation from the fighter. AIM-9L/M/X, IRIS-T, and R-73 inhibit times are unpublished. A German Wikipedia claim that IRIS-T’s seeker can be looking behind the fighter in **0.5 s** is a different clock and is not applied to the AIM-9X. The AIM-9B’s achieved lateral acceleration lags the command with a first-order time constant of **0.25 s** maximum. Homing commands stop at about **20 s**, when the servo grain is exhausted, even though the mechanical self-destruct runs to about **24 s**. A straight tail-chase range check may keep integrating until 24 s or until closing speed dies, because that check does not steer. Nothing in the Sidewinder manuals describes a climb-then-dive. Proportional navigation starts when the inhibit ends and the seeker is tracking. MICA’s midcourse is inertial, with an optional data-link update, and proportional navigation begins only after the seeker locks. A loft inside that midcourse was not published and is not added ([OP 2309](https://archive.org/details/OP23093rdAIM9B); [MBDA MICA brochure](https://www.mbda-systems.com/media/20541/download)).

The R-27T’s midcourse is not settled. One family paragraph on Missilery, and a defense write-up that names the infrared versions inside “all missiles,” includes inertial guidance and radio correction. A specialist forum post says only the radar-homing R-27s have that midcourse and that basic R-27s do not loft. Until a manual is opened, the infrared round flies lock-before-launch proportional navigation, and inertial midcourse is an optional flag marked disputed. The Wikipedia head-on and tail-on kilometre bands are not used as proof of a head-on lock ([Missilery R-27](https://en.missilery.info/missile/p27); [Defence Express](https://defence-ua.com/index.php/en/publications/defense-express-publications/2569-r-27-air-to-air-guided-missile); [Key Aero forum](https://www.key.aero/forum/modern-military-aviation/missiles-and-munitions/28704-questions-about-the-aa-10-alamo)).

True proportional navigation, the form with acceleration perpendicular to the line of sight, uses an effective ratio **N of 3 to 5**. Siouris is cited for that band as the range that keeps miss distance acceptable without excess acceleration. Fleeman’s worked seeker example uses **N′ = 4**. The AIM-9B manual states the same band in hardware language: fin command produces a flight-path rate three to five times the gyro-axis rate. OP 3352 defines N for the AIM-9D and does not evaluate it. The only opened manual that changes N in flight is the radar AIM-9C, and it does not print the two values. That is not a licence to give an infrared missile two navigation constants. Default **N = 4**, and allow 3, 4, or 5 as a data field. A high off-boresight shot saturates structural g immediately because the heading error is large. Raising N does not create acceleration the airframe has already refused, and it is not set to 6 or to 1.5. Augmented proportional navigation needs target acceleration, which classic infrared seekers did not measure ([Proportional navigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proportional_navigation); [Fleeman slides](https://web.archive.org/web/20160104053657/http://faculty.kfupm.edu.sa/AE/aymanma/images/TMDPresentation.pdf); [OP 2309](https://archive.org/details/OP23093rdAIM9B)).

Thrust-vector acceleration exists only while thrust is positive. In the maneuver plane it is thrust times the sine of the vane or tab deflection, divided by mass, and the cosine term reduces axial thrust. Add it to aerodynamic lateral acceleration and then cap the sum at structural g. At burnout the deflection goes to zero and only aero remains. Fleeman’s sizing slide prints a jet vane of about **±10°**, as a typical value, not as a measured AIM-9X, IRIS-T, or MICA angle. Sine of 10° is about **0.17**, so vectoring alone is about 0.17 times thrust over mass. He lists AIM-9X, IRIS-T, and MICA as jet vane plus aero, and the R-73 as jet tab plus aero. The R-73’s tab angle was not printed. Python 5 is not in that vane list, and Rafael does not claim vectoring. ASRAAM is absent from it on purpose. Post-burnout g for the AIM-9X and IRIS-T is unpublished. The A-Darter pattern that was adopted is Jane’s **100 g** from body lift and thrust vectoring during powered flight, not a Wikipedia 8-second timer and not a 50 g coast copied onto anyone else. IRIS-T’s autopilot paper schedules the aero and thrust-vector controllers on dynamic pressure, which is the open statement that control depends on q ([Fleeman slides](https://web.archive.org/web/20160104053657/http://faculty.kfupm.edu.sa/AE/aymanma/images/TMDPresentation.pdf); [Buschek, 2003](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0967066102000631)).

A published “maximum g” is a cap, not a constant acceleration. Aerodynamic load factor is dynamic pressure times a body-referenced normal-force coefficient times body cross-section, divided by weight. It equals the cap only when dynamic pressure is high enough. For the AIM-9B the manual’s maneuver capability is **2.7 g** at 60,000 ft and Mach 2.3, **4.2 g** at 50,000 ft and Mach 2.3, and **10 g** at sea level with no Mach stated. The two Mach 2.3 points scale almost with density, so they are the same lift coefficient. Inverting the 50,000 ft point with the 160 lb launch mass and a 5-inch body gives a body-referenced **CN of about 5.5**. With that coefficient, 10 g appears only above about **1.02×10⁵ Pa**. Below that dynamic pressure the missile is aero-limited and must not be given 10 g. The same coefficient at sea level and Mach 2.3 would be on the order of 37 g, which is why 10 g is the cap. That coefficient belongs to the AIM-9B. The AIM-9D’s 195 lb airframe does not inherit it, and no manual g was found for the L, M, or X. Canard rounds stall at high angle of attack if they are statically stable; the AIM-9X, with fixed forward wings and a movable tail, is the opposite arrangement and can trim at a higher angle on the same diameter. Thrust vectoring does not raise the lift coefficient. It adds the thrust term at low dynamic pressure, and only during the burn. The R-60’s 42 g or 47 g, the PL-5EII’s 35 g, the R-73’s 40 g, and the A-Darter’s 100 g are caps for those rounds alone, each with the source-quality caveat already attached, and each still needs a lift coefficient or it will pull that g in thin air ([OP 2309](https://archive.org/details/OP23093rdAIM9B); [Fleeman slides](https://web.archive.org/web/20160104053657/http://faculty.kfupm.edu.sa/AE/aymanma/images/TMDPresentation.pdf)).

Zero-lift drag is Fleeman’s body build-up on body cross-section: skin friction, base drag, and supersonic wave drag. A Sidewinder fineness ratio near **20–24** (the AIM-9X is **3.02 / 0.127**) sits next to his high-fineness comparison case of **CD0 = 0.2**, while his own formulas at Mach 2 produce a body CD0 near **0.4** before fins. The band to stay inside, away from the transonic peak, is about **0.2 to 0.5**, and a scale factor on that Mach curve is acceptable only while the scaled curve stays inside about **0.2 to 0.6** outside the peak. Powered flight uses the reduced base term; coast uses the full base term. Wave drag is zero below Mach 1, and across Mach 1.0 to 1.2 the curve blends so the peak sits near Mach 1.0–1.05 rather than a step. Nose fineness and nozzle exit area are unknown. If no scale factor inside the band hits both AIM-9B acceptance ranges, the residual is reported. A CD0 of order 1.0 is not invented to force the match. The AIM-9X is not calibrated to a forum range ([Fleeman slides](https://web.archive.org/web/20160104053657/http://faculty.kfupm.edu.sa/AE/aymanma/images/TMDPresentation.pdf); [NAVAIR AIM-9X](https://www.navair.navy.mil/product/AIM-9X-Sidewinder)).

The warhead stays inert for a distance, not for a seeker event. On the AIM-9B both fuzes arm mechanically between **480 and 840 ft**, and the influence fuze arms electrically at motor burnout, about **2,000 ft** in front of the aircraft. On the AIM-9D the influence fuze will not function in the first **600 to 1,000 ft**, and the minimum firing range in a co-speed tail shot is **1,000 ft** at any altitude, growing as **1,000 (1 + 2 ΔMach)** feet when the shooter is faster. The worked example, Mach 1.5 against Mach 0.8, is **2,400 ft**. The radar AIM-9C formula is a different missile. The R-3S optical fuze arms **0.1–0.8 s after the motor ends**, so it is not live during the burn, and the controlled-flight timer is not less than **21 s**, with a self-destruct printed as **21–23 s** and a narrative **21–28 s**. Those two destruct windows are not averaged. The AIM-9X safe-and-arm device arms the warhead “after launch,” with no distance. Reusing the AIM-9D’s 600–1,000 ft window for later Sidewinders is an inference from the earlier round, not a 9X publication. The R-73’s **300 m** and the Magic’s **300 m** are minimum firing ranges, not a measured fuze clock ([OP 2309](https://archive.org/details/OP23093rdAIM9B); [OP 3353](https://archive.org/stream/op-2309-2nd-sidewinder-guided-missle-mark-2/OP%203353%20AIM-9C%20&D%20PIlot%27s%20Handbook_djvu.txt); [Missilery optical fuze](https://en.missilery.info/missile/r3c/4-ots)).

The only quantified “typical” Sidewinder shots in an opened manual are the two AIM-9B cases already used for drag: sea level and 50,000 ft, shooter Mach 1.2, target Mach 0.9, tail aspect. A separate visual-merge altitude was not found and is not invented. The manual’s firing rule is a pursuit interlock, not a guidance law: the pilot should be able to track with less than **2 g** below 40,000 ft, or less than **1.6 g** at 40,000 ft and above, while hearing the tone. The R-3S veto is a similar idea with its own numbers, not the same interlock copied across. If the shooter is a Block 50 F-16C, the wing area is **300 ft²**, the aerodynamic span to use with that area is the textbook **30 ft** at aspect ratio 3 rather than the **32 ft 8 in** tip-to-tip figure, military thrust of the F110-GE-129 is **17,155 lbf**, and afterburning thrust is **29,500 lbf** on the USAF-cited figure, against **28,984 lbf** on F-16.net and “about 29,000 lbf” in base captions. Empty weight is **18,900 lb**, not **18,238 lb** and not about **20,300 lb**. The structural limit is **+9 g**. A combat mass with half internal fuel, two wingtip AIM-9Xs, and a **200 lb** pilot estimate is **22,972 lb**; the pilot mass is an estimate, and combat maximum lift coefficient was not found, so corner speed in knots is not stated. Clean subsonic zero-lift drag on wing area sits about **0.016 to 0.019**. Those fighter coefficients do not enter the missile’s body-referenced drag or its seeker ([OP 2309](https://archive.org/details/OP23093rdAIM9B); [F-16, English Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Dynamics_F-16_Fighting_Falcon)).

## Conclusion

The simulator is faithful when two missiles that share a photograph still integrate differently, and when a blank stays blank. The AIM-9B is a 2.2-second boost, a half-second of suppressed steering, a twenty-second canard coast, and a seeker that cannot see a nose. The AIM-9D is a longer single burn whose thrust is a switch, a 40° gimbal, and a head-on gate that exists only in afterburner inside 20°. The AIM-9L and AIM-9M are all-aspect canards whose motor impulse is unpublished, with the M distinguished by reduced smoke and an IRCCM class that must not be guessed. The AIM-9X turns on jet vanes during one burn and, from Block II on, may leave the rail unlocked. The Archer turns on gas-dynamic paddles during the burn and may look 75° off the body after a 45° cue. IRIS-T spends a deliberate low-thrust interval on a reversal. ASRAAM does not, and spends a dual-burn motor on a low-drag body instead. MICA IR and the R-27T/ET are medium-range weapons, one with a published inertial midcourse and one whose midcourse is still disputed. Block III, R-74M2, and MICA NG are names for missiles this model does not fly.

What changes from here is the shape of the data card, not a hunt for a smoother number. Each round needs a seeker class, a body-axis gimbal that is allowed to disagree with its cue, a track rate where one was printed, a structural g that dynamic pressure has to earn, a motor schedule whose missing newtons are an input, and a navigation ratio of 3, 4, or 5. The AIM-9B’s two handbook ranges are the only drag calibration. Every other range in this report is a brochure bracket, an altitude band, or a classified blank, and the blanks are the part of the model that keeps it honest.
