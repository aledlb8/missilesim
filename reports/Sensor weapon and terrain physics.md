# Derive locks and misses from physics

missilesim already integrates thrust, drag, and a steering command, but it decides detection, track, seduction, and detonation with constants. Target heat is the number **1**, flare heat is **5.5**, there is no radar and no radar cross section, the ground is the plane \(y = 0\), and a hit is a swept sphere that does not ask what the seeker is tracking. Open-literature models replace each of those gates with an equation whose inputs are power or radiant intensity, wavelength or band, geometry, and a noise floor. A radar dwell computes signal-to-noise ratio from the monostatic or bistatic range equation, folds pulses, applies two-way atmosphere, diffraction, and a multipath factor, admits clutter or chaff only in the matching range-Doppler cell, and maps that ratio to a probability of detection. An infrared dwell computes band radiant intensity, multiplies by a path transmittance, divides by range squared, and holds lock only while contrast irradiance exceeds a multiple of noise-equivalent irradiance inside the field the gimbal can still follow. Chaff, flares, saturated noise jamming, range-gate pull-off, and a towed decoy are extra sources in those same sums, not a resistance score. Proportional navigation flies the line-of-sight rate the seeker actually reports, after lag and after load-factor limiting, and miss distance is the separation at the instant closing speed changes sign, scored against a fuze beam and a lethal-radius parameter. Several closures were not recovered from the pages that were opened, among them Shnidman’s fluctuation formula, a numeric clear-air gas table, the meteorological factor that turns a reflectivity factor into a weather cross section, a linear-jammer bandwidth term, a two-range stand-off jammer equation, and any generic lethal radius. Those stay empty inputs. Filling them from memory would put the hard-coded ranges back under new names.

## The integrator flies the round and still invents the sensor

The flight loop is already a force integrator in a Y-up world frame, in metres, with a default outer step of **0.01 s** split into sub-steps no longer than **0.0025 s**. Gravity is straight down. `Atmosphere::sample` returns an International Standard Atmosphere state from geometric altitude, using a spherical radius of **6,356,766 m** only to form geopotential height, and drag and thrust already consume density, the speed of sound, and ambient pressure. Thrust is sea-level momentum thrust plus the nozzle term \((P_e - P_a)A_e\), floored at zero. Drag is dynamic pressure times a coefficient times area. Catalog rounds build a Mach-dependent zero-lift body coefficient and add induced drag. Viscosity is computed and has no reader. There is no wind. None of that is a sensor ([PhysicsEngine.cpp](C:/Users/alede/Documents/code/missilesim/src/physics/PhysicsEngine.cpp), [Atmosphere.cpp](C:/Users/alede/Documents/code/missilesim/src/physics/Atmosphere.cpp), [Missile.cpp](C:/Users/alede/Documents/code/missilesim/src/objects/Missile.cpp)).

What stands in for a seeker is an algebraic score. `Target::m_heatSignature` is initialized to **1** and never written again. A Fox 2 measurement forms `intensity = heat * aspect`, then `irradiance = intensity / range²`, with range floored at 1 m. The target is visible when intensity is positive and, if a tail-acquisition distance is set, when range is at most that distance times the square root of intensity. That second test is a fixed threshold in heat units, \(E \ge 1/R_{\mathrm{tail}}^2\). If the distance is zero, any positive intensity is visible at any range. Aspect is not a radiometric integral. It is a rear lobe, a rear cone, an all-aspect mix whose forward fraction is capped at **0.2**, or, for the AIM-9D only, a tail lobe multiplied by **4** when an AI throttle is at least **0.85**, plus a **20°** nose gate that returns **0.05** of unboosted tail intensity. The header states that **0.2**, the nose fraction, and the factor of four are not radiometric fits. The player fighter’s afterburner is not what the throttle test reads. A flare uses the same inverse square on a scalar that starts at **5.5** and is multiplied by \(\exp(-r\,\Delta t)\) until it falls through **0.01** or its lifetime ends. There is no wavelength, no transmittance, and no rise ([MissileFox2.cpp](C:/Users/alede/Documents/code/missilesim/src/objects/MissileFox2.cpp), [Fox2Flight.h](C:/Users/alede/Documents/code/missilesim/src/sim/Fox2Flight.h), [Flare.cpp](C:/Users/alede/Documents/code/missilesim/src/objects/Flare.cpp)).

Geometry gates are real angles, but they are not a detection law. Fox 2 gimbal is a half-angle cone about the body axis. The boresight slews at `trackRateDegPerS` and then stops at that cone. Only the AIM-9B (**4°**), AIM-9D (**2.5°**), AIM-9H (**2.5°**, unpublished), and R-3S (**3.5°**) set an instantaneous field; every other round leaves that field at zero and tests the body cone instead. When a card leaves track rate or gimbal blank, `resolve` invents **180°/s** or **40°**, which means the gimbal, not a measured slew, is what stops the head. Those fills must not be read as measurements. The custom round’s pre-launch cue is a **44-pixel** screen circle, and its in-flight field is **85°** about the velocity vector, not about the body. The kinematic unit vector called line of sight in guidance is \(R/|R|\). It is not a ray cast. Ground collision is `y < 0`. The custom round alone adds an upward acceleration if predicted altitude is low; Fox 2 turns that avoidance off. Missile-approach warning is a closest-approach test inside **3,200 m**, **6 s**, and **140 m**, with no angular gate and no terrain mask ([codebase inventory](C:/Users/alede/Documents/code/missilesim/research_notes/Sensor%20weapon%20and%20terrain%20physics/codebase-inventory.md)).

## Free-space SNR is a probability, and the mode decides which cell it occupies

For a point target the monostatic peak power at the receiver falls as \(1/R^4\). With gains as power ratios and SI units throughout, the Naval Air Warfare Center handbook’s linear form is

\[
P_r = \frac{P_t G_t G_r \lambda^2 \sigma}{(4\pi)^3 R^4}.
\]

\(P_t\) is peak power during the pulse, or the average power inside the pulse if the pulse is not rectangular. Transmission-line loss is folded into gain. Polarization mismatch and the atmosphere are not inside that expression; they are extra factors. The bistatic form, which is the semi-active missile, replaces \(R^4\) with \(R_{Tx}^2 R_{Rx}^2\). Bistatic cross section equals monostatic cross section only when the two antennas share a line of sight ([NAWC monostatic equation](https://www.rfcafe.com/references/electrical/ew-radar-handbook/two-way-radar-equation.htm), [NAWC bistatic equation](https://www.rfcafe.com/references/electrical/ew-radar-handbook/two-way-radar-equation-bistatic.htm)).

Noise power is \((\mathrm{NF})\,k T_0 B\) watts, with \(k = 1.38\times 10^{-23}\,\mathrm{J/K}\) and the handbook’s reference temperature \(T_0 = 290\,\mathrm{K}\). The thermal floor at 1 Hz is **−174 dBm**. The handbook’s rule of thumb is \(B \approx 1/\tau\); a **1 µs** pulse wants about **1 MHz**. Barton warns that Blake’s bandwidth correction was defined for a visibility factor on a cathode-ray display and is about **2 dB** optimistic if it is stacked on an electronic detectability factor. Duty cycle for a rectangular pulse is pulse width times PRF. The handbook’s worked example, **1 kHz** and **1 µs**, is a duty cycle of **0.001**, so average power sits **30 dB** below peak. Using average power in the peak equation without multiplying by an integration time understates signal-to-noise ratio by that factor. Coherent integration of \(N\) constant-amplitude pulses multiplies single-pulse signal-to-noise ratio by \(N\). Swerling 2 and 4, which decorrelate every pulse, do not get that factor. Noncoherent integration is not a \(\sqrt{N}\) gain; it is the \(N\) inside Albersheim’s equation ([NAWC receiver noise](https://www.rfcafe.com/references/electrical/ew-radar-handbook/receiver-sensitivity-noise.htm), [NAWC duty cycle](https://www.rfcafe.com/references/electrical/ew-radar-handbook/duty-cycle.htm), [Barton preview](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9781608075225_A24132309/preview-9781608075225_A24132309.pdf)).

The single-pulse matched-filter ratio the simulator should evaluate, an inference from those two handbook equations with system temperature \(T_s = T_0\,(\mathrm{NF})\) when only a noise figure is known, is

\[
\mathrm{SNR}_1 = \frac{P_t G_t G_r \lambda^2 \sigma\,\tau}{(4\pi)^3 R_{Tx}^2 R_{Rx}^2\, k T_s\, L_{\mathrm{sys}}}.
\]

\(L_{\mathrm{sys}}\) is dimensionless and does not contain the atmosphere. Monostatic, the two ranges are equal. A coherent processing interval with frozen amplitude then uses \(N\,\mathrm{SNR}_1/L_{\mathrm{coh}}\) and a one-sample detection curve. Search dwell time cannot exceed time on target; track dwell time is however long the mode actually stares. No numeric beamshape loss was on the opened pages, so none is inserted. Aperture efficiency was not opened as a formula either. Beamwidth is stored, or computed as \(\theta = k\lambda/D\) with the factor documented: about **0.89** radians times \(\lambda/D\) for a uniformly illuminated rectangle, about \(70\lambda/D\) in degrees only as a tapered-dish rule of thumb ([Richards aperture extract](https://www.accessengineeringlibrary.com/binary/mheaeworks/e201c9145cbf4b50/173bbcf78de9285a156883a24c6d0354fd4596665d79b45fcb243916b6509451/book-summary.pdf)). The fourth-power law is the sensitivity check. Doubling range divides received power by **16**, about **−12 dB**. Twelve decibels more signal-to-noise ratio doubles free-space range. A factor-of-two cross section is only about 19 percent in range. Large gameplay effects need large data changes.

Detection probability is not a range. For a nonfluctuating target and a linear detector, Richards’s form of Albersheim’s equation gives the single-sample signal-to-noise ratio in decibels when \(N\) samples are integrated noncoherently:

\[
A = \ln\frac{0.62}{P_{fa}},\quad B = \ln\frac{P_d}{1-P_d},
\]

\[
\chi_{\mathrm{dB}} = -5\log_{10} N + \left(6.2 + \frac{4.54}{\sqrt{N+0.44}}\right)\log_{10}(A + 0.12AB + 1.7B).
\]

The inverse, which is what a dwell needs, solves that same \(B\) and returns \(P_d = 1/(1+e^{-B})\). Richards states the approximation error is under **0.2 dB** for false-alarm probabilities from \(10^{-3}\) to \(10^{-7}\), detection probabilities from **0.1** to **0.9**, and \(N\) from **1** to **8096**. At one sample and \(P_{fa} = 10^{-6}\), the formula gives **11.23 dB** at \(P_d = 0.5\) and **13.11 dB** at \(P_d = 0.9\), inside the textbook readings of **11.2 dB** and about **13.2 dB** ([Richards, Albersheim alternative forms](https://radarsp.weebly.com/uploads/2/1/4/7/21471216/albersheim_alternative_forms.pdf)). A slow Rayleigh target, Swerling I, has the exact one-sample relation \(P_d = (P_{fa})^{1/(1+\mathrm{SNR})}\). At the same false-alarm probability that is **12.77 dB** for \(P_d = 0.5\) and **21.14 dB** for \(P_d = 0.9\). Do not draw a Swerling cross section and also apply a Swerling probability: the probability is already the average over the distribution. Draw once per dwell for cases 1 and 3, or once per pulse for cases 2 and 4, and detect the frozen amplitude with Albersheim; or skip the draw and use the Swerling I formula on the mean. Swerling III’s averaged curve was not recovered in a clean Shnidman transcription. Falling back to Swerling I at \(P_d = 0.9\) is conservative and must be labeled as that fallback, not as case 3. Cumulative detection on a closing target is \(1 - \prod_i(1-P_{d,i})\) with each scan’s own probability. Using the current range for every past scan overstates it. An \(m\)-of-\(n\) confirmation is the binomial sum, not that product ([POMR Vol. I index](https://vdoc.pub/documents/principles-of-modern-radar-vol1-basic-principles-7akpiac61v90), [ADA274786](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/tr/pdf/ADA274786.pdf)).

Carrier frequency is data, not a band letter. IEEE-style edges used here put fire-control in **X, 8–12 GHz**, airborne surface modes in **Ku, 12–18 GHz**, and missile seekers in **Ka, 27–40 GHz**. **K, 18–27 GHz**, is the absorption-limited band. Wavelengths at the usual centers are **3.0 cm**, **2.0 cm**, and **8.6 mm**. Tropospheric refraction from VHF through SHF does not depend on frequency. Rain and beamwidth do ([NPS Module 3.1](https://www.oc.nps.edu/NWDC_EM_Course/course_materials/module3_1.html), [POMR Vol. III](https://ftp.idu.ac.id/wp-content/uploads/ebook/tdg/ADNVANCED%20MILITARY%20PLATFORM%20DESIGN/Principles%20of%20Modern%20Radar.%20Volume%20%203.pdf)).

Pulse-Doppler does not add a second range equation. It decides whether the thermal ratio above is the ratio that meets the threshold. Unambiguous range is \(R_u = c_0/(2\,\mathrm{PRF})\), shortened to \(c_0(T-\tau)/2\) when the whole echo must fit in the listening time. Complex samples are unambiguous over one PRF. Centered, the velocity window is \(\pm\lambda\,\mathrm{PRF}/4\). Treating \(\lambda\,\mathrm{PRF}/2\) as a one-sided closing speed is high by a factor of two. Range resolution is \(c_0/(2B)\) after compression, which is **1.5 m** at **100 MHz**, and about \(c_0\tau/2\) for a simple pulse. Doppler bin spacing is \(1/T_{\mathrm{CPI}}\). Blind ranges repeat at every multiple of \(R_u\), including zero, over a slice about \(c_0 t_{\mathrm{blank}}/2\) long. Partial eclipse scales the ratio by the fraction of the pulse that is received; that scale is an inference from the listening-time description, not a measured loss table. Low, medium, and high PRF are defined by which ambiguity is accepted. Textbook X-band illustrations, not limits, are about **0.3–2 kHz**, **5–30 kHz**, and **100–300 kHz**. A single PRF in the medium or high regime does not report both true range and true range rate. The dwell keeps the apparent pair and unfolds only when several PRFs agree ([Radartutorial, Doppler dilemma](https://www.radartutorial.eu/01.basics/en/rb50.en.html), [Wu thesis](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10123395/9/WU_10123395_Thesis.pdf), [POMR Vol. 3](https://ftp.idu.ac.id/wp-content/uploads/ebook/tdg/ADNVANCED%20MILITARY%20PLATFORM%20DESIGN/Principles%20of%20Modern%20Radar.%20Volume%20%203.pdf)).

Mainlobe clutter is not a notch at zero knots. Its center is \(2(\mathbf{V}_a\cdot\hat{u})/\lambda\), and its width is \((2V_a/\lambda)|\sin\gamma|\,\Delta\gamma\). The derivative is standard. The factor \(\Delta\gamma \approx 2.5\,\theta_{3\mathrm{dB}}\) is the Naval Postgraduate School null-to-null approximation and must be a named parameter. A target that is stationary in the earth frame has the ground’s radial speed at every look angle and sits in the notch. A target that matches the fighter’s velocity sits near zero Doppler, which is the altitude line in level flight and is mainlobe clutter only near broadside, where the notch is also widest. Clutter power enters the target cell only after folding in both range and Doppler, and only if the apparent cells coincide. Inside the altitude return but inside a closer range, only sidelobe clutter applies. Look-up, with the main beam off the terrain at the target range, is thermal. A **90-knot** speed gate in a simulation manual is a display rule, not this geometry ([Jenn, NPS notes](http://faculty.nps.edu/jenn/EC4610/Vol2v7.2.pdf), [VRS A/A radar manual](https://forums.vrsimulations.com/support/index.php?title=A/A_Radar)).

On a detection, the measurement covariance comes from that dwell. Monopulse angle error on boresight, for signal-to-noise ratio above about **13 dB**, is \(\sigma_\theta \approx \theta_3/(k_m\sqrt{2\,\mathrm{SINR}})\) with slope \(k_m\) between **1** and **2**. Off boresight the error grows. Range and range-rate standard deviations of the form \(\Delta R/\sqrt{2\,\mathrm{SINR}}\) and \((\lambda/(2T_{\mathrm{CPI}}))/\sqrt{2\,\mathrm{SINR}}\) match that structure, but the exact Cramér–Rao spectral factor was not extracted and is order unity and waveform-dependent. Do not cite those two lines as a Skolnik equation. Glint, below, is a separate angle term that grows as range falls. Multipath and refraction bias the mean; they are not a larger thermal sigma. An \(\alpha\)–\(\beta\) smoother uses \(\beta = \alpha^2/(2-\alpha)\). The recursion itself was not recovered as text from the photographed equations, so the usual predict-and-smooth form is an implementation of that gain relation, not a transcription. \(\alpha\) is not **0.5** for every mode. Kalata’s tracking index was not recovered cleanly enough to hard-code. A Kalman filter on Cartesian position and velocity should rebuild its measurement covariance from the dwell sigmas, and its gains must change when the update interval changes from a search frame to single-target track ([POMR Vol. I, Ch. 18](https://www.scp.byu.edu/long/papers/chapters/POMR2011_Ch18.pdf), [DTIC ADA260831](https://archive.org/details/DTIC_ADA260831)).

Mode names map onto that dwell. Range-while-search and track-while-scan scan a volume and emit plots at the frame rate. Velocity search reports Doppler and may omit unfolded range. Single-target track steers the beam every dwell. Flood is illumination with a wide beam and no monopulse update. A **12-track** cap and stepped aging times are manual limits, not radar laws. The weapon packet is a time, an apparent or unfolded range, a monostatic range rate, two angles, the four sigmas, and a coast flag, plus either an illumination window or discrete datalink states. It is not a unitless lock strength.

## Terrain, multipath, and rain scale that cell before the threshold

A flat infinite horizon is the wrong mask. Under a standard refractivity gradient of **−39×10⁻⁹ m⁻¹**, the effective earth radius is \(k R_e\) with \(k = 4/3\), or **1.3306** if the gradient is applied to a mean-radius earth. The difference is **0.2 percent**. Each terminal then has a radio horizon \(\sqrt{2 a_e h + h^2}\), and the smooth-earth limit is the sum. The factor does not depend on X versus Ka. Ducting, which begins when \(dN/dZ < -157\,\mathrm{km}^{-1}\), is not this \(k\), and no runtime duct model was opened. The simulator should store one earth radius, the **6,356,766 m** the atmosphere module already uses, and apply \(k\) to that radius. It should not bend the ray and also use a four-thirds earth ([MathWorks effearthradius](https://www.mathworks.com/help/releases/R2024a/radar/ref/effearthradius.html), [NPS Module 3.1](https://www.oc.nps.edu/NWDC_EM_Course/course_materials/module3_1.html)).

On a real surface the smooth horizon is only an upper bound. Along the ground track, ITU-R P.526 lifts each terrain sample by the bulge \(d_1 d_2/(2 a_e)\) and compares it with the straight ray drawn between the two heights. The worst clearance becomes a Fresnel parameter \(\nu = -\sqrt{2}\,c_m/F_1\), with \(F_1 = \sqrt{\lambda d_1 d_2/d}\). For \(\nu > -0.78\),

\[
J(\nu) = 6.9 + 20\log_{10}\left(\sqrt{(\nu-0.1)^2+1}+\nu-0.1\right)
\]

decibels, and \(J = 0\) otherwise. At \(\nu = 0\) the one-way field is half of free space, which is **6 dB** of power. A monostatic radar passes the obstacle twice, so the two-way power loss in decibels is \(2J\). That doubling is reciprocity applied to a one-way recommendation, not a sentence in P.526, and no monostatic ridge measurement of it was opened. A Boolean mask throws away targets that are still within a few decibels of free space and pops as a missile crests a ridge. Tens of decibels into the shadow can be culled. A **40 dB** cull is a frame budget, not an ITU number. At X-band the first Fresnel radius at the midpoint of a **40 km** path is about **17 m**, so a **30–90 m** elevation raster resolves ridges and does not resolve the Fresnel zone. The same walk is the line-of-sight flag the mode note consumes. Diffraction loss multiplies signal-to-noise ratio. It is not a separate lock range ([ITU-R P.526-16](https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/p/R-REC-P.526-16-202511-I!!PDF-E.pdf), [ITU-R P.526-5](https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/p/R-REC-P.526-5-199708-S!!PDF-E.pdf)).

Over a smooth sea or flatland the direct ray and one specular reflection interfere. For a reflection coefficient of **−1**, Mahafza’s monostatic factor is \(16\sin^4(2\pi h_r h_t/(\lambda R))\), so received power swings from zero to **16** times free space. Nulls in target height sit at \(n\lambda R/(2 h_r)\). Peaks sit on the odd subset, the first at \(\lambda R/(4 h_r)\). An index that writes every integer multiple of \(\lambda R/(4 h_r)\) includes the nulls. Below the first lobe the extra factor itself goes as \(1/R^4\), and the radar equation’s free-space \(1/R^4\) then becomes \(1/R^8\). The communications formula that already goes as \(1/d^4\) must not be multiplied in again. A calculated illustration, not a measurement: at **10 GHz**, a **15 m** antenna, and **20 km**, the first lobe peaks near **10 m** of target height. Dropping a missile from **20 m** to **5 m** inside the small-angle form is a factor of **256** in power, **−24 dB**, until the lobe spacing or a rough surface intervenes. Vertical polarization passes through a Brewster angle where the coefficient vanishes. Forcing **−1** at a steep look-down is a horizontal, low-grazing model. Complex sea permittivity was not opened, so permittivity stays data. The roughness factor that multiplies the coefficient was not pinned to one recommendation. If the Rayleigh phase \(4\pi\sigma_h\sin\psi/\lambda\) is not small, shrink the coherent coefficient or skip it. Woods and cities do not get perfect-image nulls. An elevation-angle bias from the image was not given as a curve. Apply the power factor, and leave the angle unbiased except for thermal noise and glint ([Mahafza, ch. 8](https://dsp-book.narod.ru/RSAD/C1828_PDF_C08.pdf), [Wikipedia, two-ray model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ground_reflection)).

Look-down is clutter because the competing cross section is reflectivity times a patch, not because the receiver gets hotter. Constant-gamma reflectivity is \(\sigma^0 = \gamma\sin\psi\), with \(\gamma\) from a class table at **10 GHz** plus \(5\log_{10}(f/10\,\mathrm{GHz})\). Published class values include sea state 3 at **−40 dB**, sea state 5 at **−30 dB**, farmland and woods at **−15 dB**, flatland at **−20 dB**, and metropolitan or rugged mountain at **0 dB**. A pulse-limited cell has area about \(R\,\theta_{az}\,(c\tau/2)/\cos\psi\), or \(c/(2B)\) in place of \(c\tau/2\) when the pulse is compressed. A calculated farmland case at **5°** grazing, **20 km**, a **2°** azimuth beam, and a **1 µs** pulse is about **92 m²** of clutter, roughly **20 dB** above a **1 m²** aircraft before any processing gain. Sea state 3 is **25 dB** lower than that farmland gamma, which is why a calm sea can be noise-limited where farmland is not. Do not use the GIT sea model as the default: against Nathanson’s tables it runs about **10 dB** low at low sea state, and its interference term would double-count a separate multipath factor. The NRL coefficients were printed, but the operators between them were not retyped and must be copied from the memorandum rather than reconstructed. Morchin’s decibel placement was not safe to code. Weather cross section waits on the same kind of gap. Specific rain attenuation is \(\gamma_R = k R^{\alpha}\) in decibels per kilometre, one way, and a monostatic radar pays it twice, over the rainy path only. The numbers that were actually computed use the 1992 coefficients of ITU-R P.838-0, not the in-force P.838-3. At **10 GHz**, horizontal polarization, **25 mm/h** is **0.614 dB/km** one way. At **35 GHz** the same rain is **6.15 dB/km** one way. A **10 km** path filled with that rain is about **12 dB** two-way at **10 GHz** and about **123 dB** at **35 GHz**. P.838-3 is the version to code when its coefficient tables are in hand. The versions are not interchangeable at the percent level, and one quoted comparison mixes a **45°** circular-polarization coefficient with a horizontal-path coefficient, so it is not a pure version delta. Marshall and Palmer’s \(Z = 200 R^{1.6}\) is a reflectivity factor, later revised from **220**, and not the only operational \(Z\)–\(R\) pair. The factor that converts \(Z\) into square metres per cubic metre was not on an opened page and must not be hard-coded. No verified clear-air gas figure was adopted. A secondary **0.01 dB/km** at **10 GHz** came from a thesis that could not be opened. Omit gas, or integrate ITU-R P.676, rather than insert that number. Sea-level gas on a high air-to-air path overstates the loss ([ITU-R P.838-0](https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/p/R-REC-P.838-0-199203-S!!PDF-E.pdf), [MathWorks surfacegamma](https://www.mathworks.com/help/radar/ref/surfacegamma.html), [NRL memorandum ADA559494](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/tr/pdf/ADA559494.pdf)).

Per dwell the terrain product is four numbers: two-way diffraction loss in decibels, a linear \(|F|^4\), two-way rain loss in decibels, and one clutter cross section in square metres. Visibility is the product of the power factors. The deep-shadow cull is the only Boolean. Fixed sites may cache an azimuth skyline. An airborne radar ray-marches the live target. Multipath is recomputed because both heights move. The current kinematic pop-up above \(y = 0\) is not this product, and the catalog missiles do not call it.

## RCS, chaff, glint, and textbook jamming share one contact

Radar cross section is the area that would intercept the incident power density and reradiate it isotropically to match the echo. In the far field, \(\sigma = 4\pi r^2 S_r/S_t\). It is not the physical silhouette, and it does not depend on transmitter power or range. A metal sphere in the optical region, circumference greater than about **20** wavelengths in one statement and \(2\pi r/\lambda > 10\) in the handbook, has \(\sigma = \pi r^2\), independent of frequency. A **1 m²** optical sphere is about **1.13 m** in diameter. A flat plate broadside has \(\sigma = 4\pi A^2/\lambda^2\), which is **139.62 m²** for **1 m²** at **1 GHz**, and four times that at **2 GHz**. Off-normal, the specular beam misses a monostatic radar. A half-wave dipole, polarization matched, peaks near **0.86 \(\lambda^2\)**. Those three closed forms are the regression tests. They are not an aircraft ([Radar Tutorial RCS](https://www.radartutorial.eu/01.basics/rb56.en.html), [Wikipedia, radar cross section](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radar_cross_section), [Stellenbosch thesis](https://scholar.sun.ac.za/server/api/core/bitstreams/0895adb7-d2ca-4931-9ac0-847db0b895bc/content)).

An aircraft is an aspect function. The handbook’s example cut runs from just over **1 m²** to **100 m²** at one elevation, and it says that over **3–18 GHz** a fixed aspect moves only by a factor of about **2 to 5**, while aspect moves the return by tens of decibels. Its typical bands, not measurements of a named fighter, are a missile near **0.5 m²**, a tactical jet **5 to 100 m²**, and a bomber **10 to 1,000 m²**. An encyclopedia page prints **2–3 m²** and **5–6 m²** for small and large combat aircraft in the same centimetre-wave context. Both sets are class typicals. They disagree, and neither is a pattern to hard-code. Current military patterns are classified and were not used. The data object is a mean \(\sigma(\theta,\phi)\) in the body frame, per band and at least one polarization, plus a Swerling case and a body extent. Grid spacing finer than the scintillation lobes is a precomputation. The real-time step interpolates and draws ([NAWC RCS](https://www.rfcafe.com/references/electrical/ew-radar-handbook/radar-cross-section.htm), [chi-squared target models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chi-squared_target_models)).

Glint is not that amplitude draw. An empirical rms angle error is \(\sigma_{\theta g}\approx 0.35\,L/R\) radians, so a **15 m** target at **1,500 m** is **3.5 mrad**, and ten times that at one-tenth the range. Add it in quadrature with the thermal monopulse term. The correlation between a deep fade and a large glint spike was not given as a formula, so the first implementation keeps the draws independent. Textbook noise jamming, in the saturated constant-power case the handbook actually prints, is a one-way jammer against a two-way skin return:

\[
J/S = \frac{P_j G_{ja}\,4\pi R^2}{P_t G_t \sigma}.
\]

Frequency does not appear. Jammer bandwidth and a processing gain do not appear. The handbook folds receiver behaviour into a required ratio \((J/S)_{\min}\), which is an input. The **6 dB** line on one worked plot is that plot’s requirement, not a default. Crossover, where \(J = S\), is the square root of \((P_t G_t\sigma)/(P_j G_{ja}4\pi)\). Burn-through, where the ratio has fallen to the required value, is crossover times the square root of that required ratio, so a **6 dB** requirement is about twice the crossover range. The same plot’s labelled ranges, about **1.29** nautical miles and **2.8** nautical miles, are one example, not a check that must be forced onto every radar. Self-protection and a jammer flying with the target are this one-range geometry. A stand-off jammer is a different range pair. The bistatic summary line in the extract was too garbled to quote, so stand-off is not implemented until that algebra is transcribed. Section 4-10, the linear jammer with bandwidth ratio, was not recovered. Do not insert \(B_r/B_j\) into the saturated equation ([NAWCWD TP 8347](https://ed-thelen.org/pics/Radar-NAWCWD-TP-8347.pdf)).

Deception, at the level a tracker needs and not as a repeater procedure, is another contact. Range-gate pull-off starts at the skin delay and increases it, so the reported range walks outward by \(c\,\Delta\tau/2\). Velocity-gate pull-off holds the delay and walks the Doppler. When the false contact disappears, a gate that followed it has no skin echo left. No walk rate in metres per second or hertz per second was opened. The rates are data on the contact. Angle deception was not extracted cleanly and stays out.

Chaff is the same kind of contact with a different trajectory and a resonant cross section. The broadside matched peak of one dipole is **0.86 \(\lambda^2\)**. The spatial average for random orientation and one linear polarization sits near **0.15 \(\lambda^2\)** to **0.17 \(\lambda^2\)**. Use **0.17** as the lossless theoretical average and **0.15** when a birdnesting allowance is wanted. Cross-polarized average near **0.057 \(\lambda^2\)** and a circular average near **0.11 \(\lambda^2\)** are orientation averages, not the peak. In-service length is nearer **0.48 \(\lambda\)** than \(\lambda/2\), because resonance sits low. A cut far from an odd half-wave, or a full-wave wire at broadside, is a different and much smaller return. A modeled S-band comparison of two cut families differed by **35 dB**. Effective count is not the number manufactured: screening matters below about **2 \(\lambda\)** of spacing, and clumps that never open do not add. After a short aerodynamic transient the cloud’s mean velocity is the wind plus a fall. Glass near **25 µm** falls at about **0.26** to **0.30 m/s** in still air near the surface. Foil and thicker nylon are about **0.4** to **0.6 m/s**. The time constant inferred from a **0.3 m/s** terminal speed is about **0.03 s**, and a packed bundle can coast longer. Bloom is a different clock. One handbook point, **60 percent** of mature cross section at **0.5 s**, implies a time constant near **0.55 s** if an exponential is forced through that single point. That constant is an inference, not a universal cartridge measurement. Early filament simulations show tens of milliseconds. The cloud’s Doppler relative to the aircraft is \(2\Delta v_r/\lambda\) once the fibres have joined the air. A zero-Doppler notch removes the cloud only when the wind’s radial speed is inside the notch. The training sentence that chaff velocity relative to the radar is zero should not be coded literally ([Balleri and Butters, 2024](https://ietresearch.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1049/rsn2.12654), [NOAA NSSL trajectories](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/17619/noaa_17619_DS1.pdf), [USAF Electronic Warfare Fundamentals](https://archive.org/details/electronicwarfarefundamentals_201908)).

While the cloud and the aircraft share a range gate, a Doppler gate, and the beam, the reported range and radial speed are the cross-section-weighted centroids. If the cloud is brighter, a gate that recenters on the centroid walks off as geometry separates them. If the aircraft leaves the gate first, the gate stays. That is seduction as a measurement fact. Distraction is the other fact: at the moment a track is started, the cloud already occupies a different cell, and acquisition can prefer the larger return. There is no separate miss probability. A towed decoy is a second source at a fixed line length. Equal power puts the angle halfway between the two while the line still subtends less than a beam and less than a range cell. Reported tow lengths of about **90–120 m** for a transport and about **100 m** for a fighter are practice, not an optimum. No generic passive decoy cross section was opened. Power, or cross section, and a Doppler offset are data. The current flare object cannot represent any of this. It has no frequency, and its scalar heat is brightest at release in every band ([Zhao and Wang, 2021](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1742-6596/1982/1/012023/pdf), [US8223061](https://patentimages.storage.googleapis.com/80/91/a6/640e52baf204ce/US8223061.pdf)).

## Band irradiance replaces the unitless heat score

A single heat number cannot stand in for three emitters. Wien’s law, \(\lambda_{\max} T \approx 2898\,\mu\mathrm{m\cdot K}\), puts a **300 K** skin near **10 µm**, inside the long-wave window, and a **2100 K** flare continuum near **1.4 µm**. Hot metal is a continuum. The plume is molecular, with CO₂ near **4.3 µm** and H₂O near **2.7 µm**, and the atmosphere eats the CO₂ band centre so a long-range seeker sees the wings, including the red spike near **4.5 µm**. Skin dominates **8–12 µm** or **8–14 µm**. Models put dry-plume energy in the mid-wave window and move the plume peak toward the short-wave window in reheat. One cosine from nose to tail is the wrong shape for all three: the tailpipe cavity is visible only from the rear, a plume volume can be brighter from the side than from behind at **4.35 µm** in one nozzle model, and the nose is skin, inlets, and whatever forward plume remains. Reflected sun, sky, and earth are extra terms and need the illumination direction ([Wien's displacement law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wien%27s_displacement_law), [Mahulikar, Sonawane, and Rao, 2007](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/222815037_Infrared_signature_studies_of_aerospace_vehicles), [Materials 15:7726](https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1944/15/21/7726)).

The frame quantities, and only these units, are band radiance from \(\varepsilon(\lambda) B_\lambda(T)\), band radiant intensity in watts per steradian, and irradiance \(E = I_{\mathrm{band}}\,\tau_{\mathrm{band}}/R^2\) in watts per square metre for a source that does not fill the field. Contrast irradiance is transmittance times projected area times the radiance difference against the background the target replaces, divided by range squared. Path radiance that is added to target and adjacent background cancels in the difference and still raises photon noise. Noise-equivalent irradiance is the aperture irradiance that produces signal-to-noise ratio **1**. Signal-to-noise ratio is contrast irradiance divided by that NEI. Designers do not all use the same threshold: one lock-range model uses **3**, and an infrared-search dissertation treats **6 to 10** as what is often required because unity is only the definition. Dome heating changes NEI without any change in the aircraft, so the same irradiance can fall below threshold as speed rises. Aperture diameter and \(D^*\) are how NEI was produced. The 2009 algebra from \(D^*\) to NEI was not cleanly recoverable and is not restated. Store NEI. Do not also store a lock range ([UCF dissertation](https://stars.library.ucf.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=2378&context=etd2020), [Ab-Rahman and Hassan, 2009](https://www.ajbasweb.com/old/ajbas/2009/3703-3713.pdf), [Klein, SPIE 2286](https://doi.org/10.1117/12.187366)).

Transmission is not one extinction coefficient. Beer’s law with a single \(\alpha\) cannot represent a saturated CO₂ line, a water-vapour column that is not well mixed, and two aircraft at the same slant range and different altitudes. The practical input is a table of band-averaged transmittance and path radiance on observer altitude, target altitude, slant range, and a few humidity and aerosol cases, interpolated in the frame and produced offline by a MODTRAN-class run. Do not copy a line file into the missile. Worked clear-air pairs already show the split: one runway study, rural aerosol and **23 km** visibility, reads mid-wave transmittance **0.72** and long-wave **0.82** on a short low path, and **0.35** and **0.46** on the longer slant, and fog in that study does not dim the two bands by the same factor. Those numbers are that airport geometry. They are not the world table. A **5 km** tropical example with path emissivity about **0.75** in **8–12 µm** is a warm veil, not a clear window. Sun glint follows the solar spectrum and is a short-wave and mid-wave problem. Look-down into warm earth is a long-wave problem because skin and ground both peak near **10 µm**. A CO₂-wing seeker rejects that earthshine because a **300 K** Planck curve is weak in the wings and the band centre does not pass the ground ([MODTRAN 2/3 report](https://www.gps.caltech.edu/~vijay/pdf/modrept.pdf), [KTH runway study](https://www.aphys.kth.se/polopoly_fs/1.934189.1600690015!/Thesis_Josefine_Ulrika.pdf)).

The seeker turns that irradiance into an angle, and the seeker class is data. A spin-scan reticle, the uncooled lead-sulfide generation, encodes radius as amplitude and clock angle as phase, and the carrier disappears on axis, which is why a separating flare can out-modulate an aircraft parked in the null. Conical scan keeps a carrier on axis. The error signal is frequency deviation and phase. Automatic gain control responds to contrast, which is why a brighter source in the same field steals the null. An imaging seeker returns a focal-plane aim point. The opened research head is a quadrant tracker, not a published centroid equation. Unresolved sources inside one instantaneous field still collapse to an irradiance-weighted angle, \(\sum E\theta/\sum E\), because the two ranges differ and intensity is the wrong weight. Two-colour rejection exists only because the spectra differ. A scalar that is brighter in every band cannot represent it. Rosette timing equations were not opened and are not invented. Instantaneous field, total field, and field of regard are three different angles. The unclassified imaging surrogate’s design aims, a **4.4°** field, a field of regard beyond **0.7 rad**, **650°/s**, NEI of **1.6×10⁻¹⁰ W/m²**, and **250 W/sr** at a **10 km** aim, must not be copied onto every round and must not be combined as if **10 km** were the NEI range. At unity transmittance, **250 W/sr** at **10 km** is **2.5×10⁻⁶ W/m²**, about four orders of magnitude above that NEI. No production missile NEI in watts per square metre was opened ([NPS infrared notes](https://calhoun.nps.edu/bitstreams/8d630ca0-f4cf-48f1-b901-fb9fc7d695dc/download), [Schleijpen et al., 2007](https://publications.tno.nl/publication/34611937/NAmFxx/schleijpen-2007-imaging.pdf), [Wikipedia, infrared homing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infrared_homing)).

Published Fox 2 cards already tell the simulator which of those mechanisms to select, and they do not supply the watts. The AIM-9B is an uncooled lead-sulfide reticle, tail aspect only, a **4°** cone, track rate **11°/s** in one table and **8–11°/s** in another. Its gimbal is **25°** in the later handbook’s account of what the D raised, and up to **30°** in an AIM-9B figure note. Those are not averaged. Head-on intensity is zero. The AIM-9D is cooled lead sulfide, instantaneous field **2.5°**, gimbal **40°**, track rate **12°/s**. Nose-on homing exists only for an afterburning target and only inside **20°**, and the intensity inside that cone was not published. The handbook says afterburner selection doubles detection range, which is a range observation for that seeker, not a license to keep a factor-of-four multiplier once a band table exists. A target on blue sky is detected at almost twice the range of the same target against cloud or ground. The **30°** sun rule is a cockpit hold-off, not a second gimbal stop. The AIM-9L is argon-cooled indium antimonide in what Kopp calls a **4 µm** band, with an FM reticle. Its track rate is classified. The AIM-9D’s **40°** and **2.5°** are not copied onto it. The AIM-9X seeker that was published is a **128×128** mid-wave focal plane in place of a single-element detector. Range and speed on the official card are classified. IRIS-T’s published seeker is not that staring array: a **128×2** two-colour indium-antimonide line scans a **128×128** image **80** times a second, with a look angle of **±90°**. None of these sentences is a radiant-intensity table. Brochure kilometres are kinematic claims or unclassified blanks, not NEI ([OP 2309](https://archive.org/details/OP23093rdAIM9B), [OP 3353](https://archive.org/stream/op-2309-2nd-sidewinder-guided-missle-mark-2/OP%203353%20AIM-9C%20&D%20PIlot%27s%20Handbook_djvu.txt), [Kopp 1994](https://www.ausairpower.net/TE-Sidewinder-94.html), [RAND MG-109](https://www.rand.org/content/dam/rand/pubs/monographs/2004/RAND_MG109.pdf), [German Wikipedia, IRIS-T](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/IRIS-T)).

A flare is a point mass under gravity and quadratic drag, with a spectral intensity \(J(\lambda, t)\) that is zero until ignition, then rises, peaks, and falls. The **10 percent** and **90 percent** points are the right axes for rise and burn. Altitude lengthens both. Open literature does not support one public rise time in milliseconds or one modern in-band peak in watts per steradian, and no composition belongs in the data. A continuum at **2000–2200 K** and a CO₂ plume do not produce the same number inside a narrow band. The current object, **0.9 kg**, drag coefficient **1.1**, area **0.018 m²**, starts at its maximum and is gone within **4 s**. With the drag law the integrator already uses, those defaults imply a sea-level terminal speed near **27 m/s**. That figure is arithmetic on the stored coefficients, not a measured cartridge. The seeker sees a new flare only on a later sub-step, because the dispenser queues it after the physics step that decided to launch it ([METU thesis](https://open.metu.edu.tr/bitstream/handle/11511/23023/index.pdf), [USAF Electronic Warfare Fundamentals](https://archive.org/details/electronicwarfarefundamentals_201908)).

Counter-countermeasures are rules about which source wins. An unhardened reticle tracks the centroid, or the brightest source away from the spin-scan null. An open planning figure is about **2:1** in the stern to seduce that class, and the same flare can be much greater than **10:1** in the forward quadrant. The forward-fraction cap of **0.2** is the bound implied by that inequality, not a fitted spectrum, and the code already labels it as not a radiometric fit. A **2.5:1** rise inside **40 ms** is the article’s hypothetical illustration. It is not AIM-9M firmware. The compiled constants that reject a source **1°** from the aircraft, or a source that has slowed below **0.85** of target speed beyond **2°**, are sim choices in the same header, not published gate widths. Imaging keeps the aim point on the aircraft once the flare is a resolved point outside the instantaneous field. A two-colour test compares band irradiances. Magic II’s published afterburner filter is a specific case of not treating a reheat spike as the target. No circuit for it was opened. Loss of lock is the or of three data conditions: contrast below the seeker’s multiple of NEI, angle outside the gimbal, or a line-of-sight rate the track loop cannot follow. The **3 s** memory coast in the Fox 2 code is a compiled timer. No manual memory time was found, so it stays a data field, not a physical constant ([Journal of Electronic Defense, via The Free Library](https://www.thefreelibrary.com/Advanced+infrared+missile+counter-countermeasures.-a015149906)).

## The reported line of sight, lagged and limited, sets the miss

The guidance law does not care whether the angle came from a reticle, a focal plane, or a monopulse radar. Zarchan’s command is \(n_c = N' V_c \dot\lambda\), perpendicular to the line of sight and in the same rotational sense, with \(N'\) usually **3 to 5** and \(V_c = -\dot R\). Zero line-of-sight rate is the collision triangle, and the command is then zero. Augmented proportional navigation adds \(N' n_T/2\) only when a target acceleration normal to the line of sight is actually estimated. A passive infrared seeker that outputs angle and angle rate does not measure that acceleration. The optimal-control derivation that minimizes the integral of acceleration squared produces \(N' = 3\). The closed-form zero-lag history \(n_c = [N'/(N'-2)] n_T [1-t/t_F]^{N'-2}\) was not recovered cleanly from the archive text and is not quoted. Early Sidewinder hardware did not form the product as a number. It torqued a spinning mirror onto the source and drove the fins from that voltage, so flight-path rate was three to five times gyro rate. The only published inhibit is the AIM-9B’s **0.5 s**, to avoid overcontrol at low speed. The only published guidance time constant is that round’s **0.25 s** maximum. Homing commands on that round stop near **20 s**, when the servo grain is exhausted, while the mechanical self-destruct runs near **24 s**. Zarchan’s plots use **1 s** because they are normalized, not because an air-to-air autopilot is that slow ([Zarchan, 2012](https://archive.org/stream/TacticalAndStrategicMissileGuidance2012/Tactical%20and%20Strategic%20Missile%20Guidance%20(2012)_djvu.txt), [OP 2309 text](https://archive.org/stream/op-2309-2nd-sidewinder-guided-missle-mark-2/OP%202309%20(3rd)%20AIM-9B_djvu.txt), [Wikipedia, proportional navigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proportional_navigation)).

The code has two different laws, and both should stay named. The custom round uses closing speed, with a floor at **0.1** times missile speed, and commands perpendicular to the line of sight. The Fox 2 round commands \(N\) times missile speed, perpendicular to the velocity, with \(N\) clamped to **3–5**, and blends in a full-authority turn while heading error is between **15°** and **40°**. Using missile speed keeps authority when closing speed is small or negative. It is not the law Zarchan linearizes. The blend is a heuristic around a missing autopilot. Achieved acceleration is applied in the same tick, then clipped by dynamic pressure, a normal-force coefficient, a structural \(g\), and thrust vectoring while the motor burns. There is no autopilot state. The smallest model that reproduces the adjoint is \(\dot n = (n_c - n)/T\), with \(T\) a data parameter, **0.25 s** on the AIM-9B card and unset elsewhere. Available load factor is the minimum of the structural cap and \(q S C_{N,\max}/m\). For the AIM-9B the manual’s **4.2 g** at **50,000 ft** and Mach **2.3**, inverted on the **160 lb** airframe and a **5-inch** body, is the coefficient the code stores as **5.49**. Ten \(g\) appears only above about **1.02×10⁵ Pa**. Below that dynamic pressure the round is aero-limited. High altitude saturates through angle of attack. Low altitude saturates through structure. A separate incidence-lag formula was not on the pages opened here ([MissileFox2.cpp](C:/Users/alede/Documents/code/missilesim/src/objects/MissileFox2.cpp), [Fox2Flight.h](C:/Users/alede/Documents/code/missilesim/src/sim/Fox2Flight.h)).

Miss distance is the separation when closing velocity changes sign. It is not a scripted penalty inside an arbitrary time-to-go gate. For a single lag at \(N' = 3\), the opened text gives a step-maneuver sensitivity \(0.5\,t_F^2 e^{-t_F/T}\), with adjoint time read as time of flight or as time-to-go at which the maneuver occurs, and says to multiply by target acceleration to get a length. The optical-character recognition may have dropped a factor. The shape is what the text supports: the sensitivity peaks near two time constants and is small both for a very long flight and as time-to-go goes to zero. A single lag underestimates maneuver miss relative to a higher-order binomial with the same effective time constant when the flight is not much longer than that constant. Integrate the lag. Do not add a canned miss. While two sources are unresolved, the boresight is their centroid, so the same command steers between them, closer to the brighter source. When the seeker steps to one source, that step is a heading-error impulse. If the remaining time is only a few guidance time constants, the airframe cannot abandon the wrong source even if the seeker later reassigns the track. One paper’s resolution rule, **60°** or an energy ratio of **0.6**, is that paper’s threshold and is not a default ([Zarchan, 2012](https://archive.org/stream/TacticalAndStrategicMissileGuidance2012/Tactical%20and%20Strategic%20Missile%20Guidance%20(2012)_djvu.txt), [Adamy, EW 102 excerpt](https://vdoc.pub/documents/ew-102-a-second-course-in-electronic-warfare-1sjt1858j480)).

Semi-active homing is valid only while the illuminator beam still contains the target and the missile still has a rear reference coherent with that illumination. The seeker mixes the forward reflection with the rear sample. The only printed closed form is a stationary illuminator and a head-on collision, \(f_d = (f_0/c)\,2 V_c\), about **20 Hz** per foot per second at X-band, which matches \(2v/\lambda\) at **10 GHz**. The fighter’s monostatic range rate is not that tone. Continuous-wave semi-active does not itself measure range. A scanning track-while-scan frame does not keep the target inside a narrow beam. Single-target track can. Flood can illuminate without a high-rate track. If either support condition fails, the seeker coasts or drops out. It does not silently track a perfect geometric line of sight. Active homing is the same proportional navigation after the missile’s own radar has locked, using the monostatic equation with the missile’s power, gain, and wavelength. Until lock, the command follows the last datalink state, propagated at constant target velocity. A maneuvering target walks out of that basket by about \(\tfrac{1}{2} n_T t^2\). The words “supported” and “unsupported” were not found as defined textbook terms. The physical split is whether illumination, or those state updates, are still arriving. Home-on-jam is stated. The logic that switches to it was not opened ([Skolnik, Radar Handbook, ch. 19](https://ww.helitavia.com/skolnik/Skolnik_chapter_19.pdf)).

Propulsion is already closer to the textbooks than the seeker is. Thrust rises with altitude through the pressure term for a fixed nozzle. Specific impulse converts a constant-thrust segment into mass flow by \(I_{sp} = F/(\dot m g_0)\). When thrust varies, the integral form is the definition, and a thrust-versus-time table is the motor. A rectangular average is the fallback already used when a manual prints impulse and burn time and no curve. The AIM-9B’s **8,440 lbf·s** in **2.2 s** is that case. It is not a measured peak, and it is not a boost–sustain shape to be invented for rounds whose impulse was never printed. Coast drag uses the full base term. Powered drag uses the reduced base. Induced drag grows with the normal force the guidance law is demanding, so a high-\(g\) coast bleeds energy faster than a zero-alpha coast. Specific energy \(h + V^2/(2g)\) and specific excess power \(V(T-D)/W\) are diagnostics. They are not a second integrator, and they are not a kill. No rail-exit speed increment was found, so launch velocity remains the shooter’s velocity ([Sutton preview](https://cdp.pageplace.de/cdp/public/publications/DT0400/9781394187225_A64502498/PREVIEW/9781394187225_A64502498_preview.pdf), [NASA drag equation](https://www1.grc.nasa.gov/beginners-guide-to-aeronautics/drag-equation/)).

The fuze is not the seeker and not a sphere. Record the miss vector when closing speed changes sign. Arm only after the card’s acceleration or distance gate. The AIM-9B arms mechanically between **480 and 840 ft**, and the influence fuze arms near motor burnout, about **2,000 ft** in front of the aircraft. The AIM-9D influence fuze is silent for the first **600 to 1,000 ft**, and the co-speed tail minimum range in that handbook is **1,000 ft**, growing as **1,000 (1 + 2 ΔMach)** feet when the shooter is faster. A generic lethal radius was not opened. The AIM-9B manual’s figure of about **30 ft** can be that round’s lethal-radius parameter. It is not a default for every warhead, and it is not both a sphere radius and a beam range. Declare a detection when the target enters a data-defined beam crossed with a range gate or a Doppler zero, apply the delay \(T = M - N/V_c\) whose coefficients were not given numerically, and score a kill only if the miss is inside the lethal radius and the fuze did detect. A fly-through that never crosses the beam is a miss. A beam detection outside the lethal radius is a fuze fire and a failed kill. The present sphere can still detonate on the aircraft while the seeker is on a flare, which accidentally preserves the fact that the fuze is not the seeker, and gets the volume wrong. A dynamic launch zone is the set of states from which this same integrator reaches an armed, lethal miss with the seeker still valid and energy above a declared floor. A no-escape zone is the intersection of those sets over a declared maneuver and aspect list. Rear-aspect seekers can make that intersection empty. Neither zone is a stored range ([NPS fuzing chapter](https://web.archive.org/web/20150504004413/http://fas.org/man/dod-101/navy/docs/fun/part14.htm), [AIAA flight-simulation papers, 1996](https://archive.org/details/AIAA_Flight_Simulation_Technologies_Conference_Technical_Papers_July_29-31_1996)).

The build order is the order in which a closed form can fail a unit test. Each later stage must leave the earlier expected number alone. Truth, pose and rate, stays on the flight step. A radar contact is produced on a dwell, which may span many flight steps. Swerling I holds across that dwell and is redrawn on the next one. Infrared point sources are cheap enough to score on the physics step, which is what the heat seeker already does, once the score is watts. The acceptance tests are deterministic and do not need a scene.
