# Non-US, non-European, non-Russian active-radar air-to-air missiles (Fox 3)

Scope is active-radar beyond-visual-range air-to-air missiles from China, Japan, Israel, India, Turkey, and Iran, plus the rumored PL-21. Infrared weapons are not cards: PL-10, AAM-5 (Type 04), Python, Bozdoğan, and A-Darter are Fox 2. Status of sources is 29 September 2026. Every number below is tagged with what the opened page actually is: **manufacturer**, **government**, **air-show claim** (a writeup of a display, not a photographed placard), or **secondary**. A parent missile’s figures are not copied onto a variant unless that source says they match. No opened page in this set gives propellant mass, burn time, thrust, or a plotted thrust curve.

Direct fetches of pib.gov.in, www.rafael.co.il HTML, IISS HTML/PDF, and the current Ministry of Defence of Japan evaluation PDFs failed (access denied, cookie wall, or 404). Where a press-release or brochure sentence is used anyway, the bullet says whether it comes from a fully retrieved page or from a search-index extract of that URL.

## Which of these have a documented active terminal seeker plus midcourse datalink, and which are only "beyond visual range" in a brochure?

### Takeaway
I-Derby ER, Gökdoğan, and Astra Mk-1 are the only rounds in this set where an opened manufacturer or government page states both an active radio-frequency terminal seeker and a midcourse update path, with a range figure attached. PL-12 and PL-15 are treated as active-radar missiles with datalinks across Western secondary literature, but the Chinese state or academy page that would lock seeker type, datalink, and motor class was not opened. PL-15E has a manufacturer range floor and, separately, a Chinese web column that describes an active terminal seeker plus a two-way datalink. PL-17 is a large missile in secondary analysis with a multimode seeker described, not a datasheet. PL-21 and Astra Mk-3 do not have an opened source that documents an active seeker on a real round. Fakour-90’s seeker type is not established: Iranian media language is compatible with active homing, and photographed markings are Hawk-derived.

### Inferences

## What is published about dual-pulse motors and what that does to a thrust curve?

### Takeaway
A dual-pulse solid motor is two propellant charges in one case, separated by a barrier, with the second charge ignited on command after a coast. That puts two thrust pulses on the curve with a near-zero coast between them, which the propulsion literature says is done to cut midcourse drag and to buy terminal speed. A dual-thrust motor is a different, continuous boost-then-sustain burn with no commanded shutdown. Open sources call some of these missiles one and some the other, and they contradict each other on the PL-15. Nobody published a thrust-time table for any missile in this set.

### Cited Findings
- Wikipedia “Pulsed rocket motor,” opened, and explicitly “not to be confused with” a dual-thrust motor: each pulse burns to completion; the next is ignited on command; pulses sit in one case with a barrier between them; “each pulse can have a different thrust level and burn time.” Separating the burn, “one starting at launch and the other much later,” “reduce[s] the velocity of the missile during the majority of the course, therefore reducing losses to drag.” **[secondary encyclopedia of the propulsion literature]** — [Pulsed rocket motor, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulsed_rocket_motor)
- Wikipedia “Dual-thrust rocket motor,” opened: two propellant densities, fast burn then slow burn, called boost and sustain, with no coast and no second ignition command. The point of the sustain, as that page states it, is to avoid the drag of an all-boost burn. **[secondary encyclopedia]** — [Dual-thrust rocket motor, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual-thrust_rocket_motor)
- A search-index extract of Kim and Lee, CEAS EuroGNC 2022, describes a dual-pulse trajectory as four phases, two boosting and two gliding, with the time between pulses as a control variable, “to relieve the energy loss from high drag.” The PDF was not rendered page by page. **[secondary, index extract]** — [CEAS-GNC-2022-040](https://eurognc.ceas.org/archive/EuroGNC2022/pdf/CEAS-GNC-2022-040.pdf)
- Who uses which word, and in what source class:
  - PL-15 domestic: Bronk 2020 says “dual-pulse,” footnoted to Rupprecht, not to a motor test. **[secondary]** — [Bronk 2020 PDF](https://static.rusi.org/russian_and_chinese_combat_air_trends_whr_final_web_version.pdf). TWZ 2022 repeats “dual-pulse.” **[secondary]** — [TWZ](https://www.twz.com/a-guide-to-chinas-increasingly-impressive-air-to-air-missile-inventory)
  - PL-15E: SCMP’s own sentences say “dual-thrust, solid-fuel motor,” not dual-pulse, and that wording is outside the AVIC quotation. **[press narrative]** — [SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/news/china/military/article/3150623/new-version-chinas-most-advanced-air-air-missile-pl-15-destined). The china.com Zhuhai column gives dimensions and guidance and does not name the motor. **[air-show claim, silent on the motor]** — [china.com](https://news.china.com/socialgd/10000169/20241113/47572016_all.html)
  - PL-12: TWZ says “reports suggest” a “variable-thrust rocket motor.” **[secondary]** — [TWZ](https://www.twz.com/a-guide-to-chinas-increasingly-impressive-air-to-air-missile-inventory). Japanese Wikipedia’s PL-12 article was not the source used; English Wikipedia attributes “dual thrust solid fuel rocket” to the unopened CASI paper. GlobalSecurity’s SD-10 number block does not name a motor class.
  - PL-17: TWZ 2022 says dual-pulse. Bronk 2020, on the pages extracted, does not. Wikipedia’s dual-pulse infobox cites an article that was not opened. Do not treat dual-pulse as a RUSI finding.
  - I-Derby ER: Airforce Technology says “dual pulse rocket motor.” **[secondary]** — [Airforce Technology](https://www.airforce-technology.com/projects/derby-bvr-air-to-air-missile/). The opened Rafael PDF does not. A blocked Rafael HTML URL was indexed with a dual-pulse sentence. The two Rafael surfaces disagree until the HTML is opened.
  - AAM-4: Japanese Wikipedia uses デュアル・スラスト and glosses it as a two-stage combustion pattern. That sentence has no footnote. It is not dual-pulse, and it is not an opened MoD statement. The ducted-rocket item is a 2000–2007 research vehicle, not the service motor.
  - Astra Mk-1: PIB releases opened via index do not name the motor. Mk-2 “dual-pulse” and “improved rocket motor” appear only in secondary copy. Mk-3’s opened ministry quotation is a solid-fuel ducted ramjet that “sustained thrust during the cruise phase” after a booster, which is a throttleable or at least continuous ramjet sustain, not a dual-pulse curve. **[government, as quoted by TNIE]** — [New Indian Express](https://www.newindianexpress.com/nation/2026/Feb/03/drdo-tests-sfdr-propulsion-for-next-generation-astra-mk-3-missile)
  - Gökdoğan: SAGE says “solid fuel rocket engine” and “high thrust - reduced smoke solid propellant,” with no pulse count. **[manufacturer]** — [SAGE](https://www.sage.tubitak.gov.tr/en/air-air-missiles/gokdogan/). A dual-pulse “Gökdoğan-ER” is a Wikipedia citation of an unopened Defence Security Asia piece, not the SAGE page.
  - Fakour-90: IranGeomil’s photographs are described as marked M112, the Hawk motor. No pulse count is stated. **[photograph reading]** — [IranGeomil](https://irangeomil.blogspot.com/2018/07/analysis-fakour-air-to-air-missile.html)

### Inferences
- For a sim thrust curve, dual-pulse means: pulse one, coast at thrust near zero, pulse two at a chosen time. Dual-thrust means: high thrust then lower thrust with no coast. They must not share a data field.
- Bronk’s comparison of PL-15 with Meteor is the only opened operational consequence attached to a named missile: a dual-pulse rocket can be given a similar maximum range to a ramjet and still be credited with a smaller no-escape zone past 100 km, because the ramjet is still thrusting. That is an analyst judgment, not a measured NEZ.
- Nothing opened here supports a numeric burn time, a second-pulse delay, or a thrust in newtons for any of these motors. Inventing them from mass and “Mach 4” or “Mach 5” would be a new calculation, not a source.

### Gaps
- No static-test curve, propellant mass, or specific impulse for PL-15, I-Derby ER, Astra Mk-2, or AAM-4 was found.
- Whether PL-15E’s export motor is a derated dual-pulse, a dual-thrust grain, or the same motor with a software range stop is not stated by AVIC in the sentence SCMP quoted. SCMP says only that the export range is lower and, outside the quote, that the motor is dual-thrust.
- The Rafael HTML dual-pulse sentence needs a successful page fetch before it overrides the silent PDF.
