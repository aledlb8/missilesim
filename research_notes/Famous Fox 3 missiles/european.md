# European active-radar air-to-air missiles (Fox 3): Meteor and MICA

Scope is open literature read on 2026-09-29. Principal rounds are MBDA Meteor (throttleable solid-fuel ducted rocket) and MBDA MICA in its active-radar form (MBDA label **RF MICA**; French DGA label **électromagnétique**; commonly called MICA EM) plus MICA NG. They are different weapons, not two range settings of one missile. MICA IR is only a seeker sibling on the same airframe. No motor-grain design, classified envelope, or employment procedure is in these notes. Figures below are quoted from pages that were opened. Where a PDF text layer failed to render, that failure is stated and the number is not treated as confirmed.

MBDA’s current air-dominance catalogue, as linked from the MICA product page, lists Meteor, MICA, MICA NG, and ASRAAM. ASRAAM is a within-visual-range infrared missile, not a Fox 3. No other European fighter-launched active-radar AAM in production appears on that opened catalogue.

## What does throttleable ramjet thrust buy in a kinematic model (thrust through the coast phase, altitude effect, specific energy) according to open propulsion references and MBDA or government descriptions?

### Takeaway
Opened MBDA, Saab, and RAF descriptions say Meteor’s ramjet keeps producing thrust until intercept, so the missile is not a boost-then-coast solid rocket, and they tie that sustained thrust to endgame energy and a larger no-escape zone. They publish no thrust, burn time, or specific impulse. A 1948 NACA theoretical study of a generic ducted rocket, not Meteor, shows why a single thrust-versus-time table is the wrong object: with a fixed inlet, net thrust and captured airflow change with altitude, and thrust also changes with Mach and with the air-to-fuel mass-flow ratio.

### Cited Findings
- MBDA’s current Meteor product page: the ramjet “provides the missile with thrust all the way to target intercept, providing the largest No Escape Zone of any air-to-air missile system, several times greater than current MRAAMs.” That sentence is a manufacturer claim. The page’s only numeric specifications are weight 190 kg, length 3.7 m, diameter 178 mm. No speed, range, or thrust. — [MBDA Meteor](https://www.mbda-systems.com/products/air-dominance/meteor)
- MBDA Meteor datasheet, copyright MBDA UK Ltd 2023-08-v03: “Meteor's 'ramjet' motor provides continuous thrust up to the point of target interception, generating the most extensive No Escape Zone among any air-to-air missile system, significantly surpassing current MRAAMs” and “unparalleled endgame kinematics.” Propulsion line: “Solid fuel variable flow ducted rocket (ramjet).” Same 190 kg / 3.7 m / 178 mm. No thrust, Isp, or range in kilometres. — [MBDA Meteor datasheet PDF](https://www.mbda-systems.com/media/19279/download)
- MBDA, 4 December 2025: “Unlike traditional missiles that rely on an initial boost and then coast unpowered, METEOR’s ramjet delivers sustained thrust right up until intercepting a threat.” Propulsion is described as “a solid-fuel, variable-flow ducted rocket design.” The no-escape zone is “significantly larger than that of any comparable system,” meaning “once METEOR is in flight, the threat it is defending against has far fewer options to evade it.” Manufacturer wording, still with no kilometres and no thrust table. — [MBDA, Meteor and F-35A](https://www.mbda-systems.com/meteor-missile-and-f-35a-one-step-closer-operational-capability)
- Saab, Meteor product page: engine “Solid rocket booster and throttleable ramjet”; speed “Over Mach 4”; operational range “100+ km (60+ mi)” and, separately, “Meteor’s operational range is more than 100 km.” Saab is an industrial partner, not the prime. “100+ km” is a floor claim, not a maximum range. The page does not define how the booster hands over to the ramjet or how throttle is scheduled. — [Saab Meteor](https://www.saab.com/products/meteor)
- RAF, 11 December 2023: “The missile is powered by a ramjet motor, which propels it until it hits its target. This gives it a long range and very large ‘no escape zone’.” Official operator description. No Mach number, no kilometres, no thrust. — [RAF, Typhoon Meteor firing with French Rafales](https://www.raf.mod.uk/news/articles/typhoon-from-raf-lossiemouth-take-part-in-exercise-with-french-air-force-rafales/)
- RAF, 10 December 2018, first Quick Reaction Alert launch from Lossiemouth: Air Chief Marshal Sir Stephen Hillier called it “the MBDA developed ramjet powered Meteor.” The release says the missile and launch aircraft “will be networked by a datalink.” No performance numbers. — [RAF, Typhoons launch with Meteor](https://www.raf.mod.uk/news/articles/royal-air-force-typhoons-launch-with-new-missile)
- NACA Research Memorandum E7I05, Reece V. Hensley, Flight Propulsion Research Laboratory, Cleveland, 13 February 1948, “Theoretical Analysis of the Performance of a Supersonic Ducted Rocket.” This is a calculated gasoline/liquid-oxygen ducted rocket with a rocket mass flow of 1 slug per second. It is not Meteor, not a solid fuel-rich gas generator, and not a boron motor. At Mach 1, 2, and 3 and 30,000 ft, the memo’s summary says thrusts per unit frontal area “were of the order of twice those available from a ram jet” and specific fuel consumption “about two to three times those of a ram jet, but about one-half to one-third that of the rocket alone.” At 30,000 ft with full expansion, net thrust at Mach 1 is “just under 10,000 pounds” at air-to-rocket mass-flow ratio r = 3 and “almost 14,000 pounds” at r = 10; at Mach 2 the range is 13,500 to 22,000 pounds; at Mach 3, 14,500 to 23,500 pounds. Those pounds are for that hypothetical unit. — [NACA RM E7I05 (NTRS 19930085734)](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19930085734/downloads/19930085734.pdf)
- Same NACA memo, fixed-inlet altitude case, designed for stoichiometric operation at Mach 1 and 30,000 ft, inlet geometry fixed, nozzle variable only up to the combustion-chamber area: “At sea-level operating conditions, the net thrust is about 13,000 pounds; the thrust decreases with increasing altitude and at 45,000 feet a thrust of about 8000 pounds is obtained.” Cause stated in the memo: “decreasing air-handling capacity of a unit of fixed-inlet geometry with increasing altitude.” At sea level the induced airflow is “three times the design air flow”; at 45,000 ft it “drops to one-half the design value.” Specific fuel consumption in that case goes from about 9 lb/hr per lb of thrust at sea level, through about 12 at the design altitude, to 14.5 at 45,000 ft, and the memo says that above 30,000 ft the mixture is richer than stoichiometric so part of the excess fuel’s heat cannot be used. Static (zero airspeed) ducting raised rocket thrust only about 9 percent. — [NACA RM E7I05](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19930085734/downloads/19930085734.pdf)
- DTIC accession ADP005719, “Speed Control of a Missile with Throttleable Ducted Rocket Propulsion,” abstract only. The HTML record was returned by search; the PDF download was blocked on 2026-09-29, so this is the abstract, not the report body. Abstract: a wide altitude band “makes the variation of thrust mandatory and leads ultimately to the control of propellant mass flow.” For a solid-propellant ducted rocket, “a propellant with a pressure sensitive burning rate” plus “a valve with variable throat area controls the mass flow out of the gasgenerator.” A Mach-number controller sets a gas-generator pressure demand. This is a general control description, not a Meteor grain or a valve schedule. — [DTIC ADP005719 record](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/html/tr/ADP005719/index.html)
- NATO STO meeting-paper record for Besser and Kurth, “METEOR – European Air Dominance Missile Powered by High Energy Throttleable Ducted Rocket” (2012, RTO-MP-AVT-208): the public page says “Abstract not available” and download requires an STO login. No performance number was taken from it. — [NATO STO record](https://www.sto.nato.int/document/meteor-european-air-dominance-missile-powered-by-high-energy-throttleable-ducted-rocket/)
- MICA, for contrast, is not this motor. MBDA MICA datasheet, copyright 2022-02-v02: propulsion is “High impulse / Low-smoke / Solid propellant,” with thrust-vector control, long-chord wings, and tail surfaces. No ramjet, no throttle, no dual-pulse line on that sheet. — [MBDA MICA datasheet PDF](https://www.mbda-systems.com/media/20541/download)
- MICA NG datasheet, “2023 MICA NG datasheet,” copyright 2023-08-v03: “High maneuvrability, even at end game, with dual pulse motor” and “up to +40% with MICA NG compared to MICA.” Propulsion block is still “High impulse / Low-smoke / Solid propellant,” plus “Dual pulse motor” under aerodynamics and control. The “+40%” is a manufacturer claim relative to MICA, not a kilometre range and not a thrust curve. — [MBDA MICA NG datasheet PDF](https://www.mbda-systems.com/sites/mbda/files/2024-07/2023%20MICA%20NG%20datasheet.pdf)
- DGA communiqué, Paris, 19 June 2025: MICA NG has “performances accrues, notamment grâce à son propulseur bi-pulse.” Official French procurement statement that the new motor is bi-pulse. No burn times, no thrust, no impulse split. — [DGA communiqué PDF, 19 June 2025](https://www.defense.gouv.fr/sites/default/files/ministere-armees/Communiqu%C3%A9_La%20DGA%20proc%C3%A8de%20au%20premier%20tir%20de%20d%C3%A9veloppement%20d%E2%80%99un%20missile%20MICA%20NG%20depuis%20un%20avion%20Rafale.pdf)

### Inferences
- In a kinematic model the purchase of throttleable ducted-rocket thrust is that the coast phase of a boost-coast Fox 3 is replaced by a powered sustain whose thrust still depends on captured air. Specific energy at long range is whatever the throttle law and the remaining gas-generator propellant can still add, not the integral of one short solid-rocket burn minus drag. MBDA and the RAF state the operational consequence (thrust until intercept, larger no-escape zone, fewer kinematic escape options). They do not state the throttle law.
- The NACA altitude case is the right kind of dependence, not the right magnitudes. Meteor thrust should be expected to fall as inlet air mass flow falls with altitude, and to rise with flight Mach at a given throttle, but the 8,000–13,000 lbf figures and the 11.5-square-foot frontal area in RM E7I05 must not be copied onto a 178 mm missile.
- Saab’s separate “solid rocket booster” plus MBDA’s “variable flow ducted rocket” implies at least two propulsion regimes: a booster that gets the missile to ramjet takeover, then a throttleable sustain. Neither source publishes takeover speed, booster impulse, or sustain duration.
- MICA NG’s dual-pulse claim buys a second powered segment, often associated in open writing with endgame energy, but the opened MBDA and DGA texts do not say when the second pulse is lit. A simulator that fires the second pulse at a guessed time is inventing employment logic. The published fact is only that there are two solid pulses and that MBDA claims up to 40 percent more range than MICA.

### Gaps
- No opened MBDA, Saab, RAF, or DGA page gives Meteor thrust, propellant mass, specific impulse, booster burn time, sustain burn time, throttle limits, or a thrust-versus-altitude table.
- Bayern-Chemie / Besser papers that are cited in secondary writing for Meteor motor layout, boron propellant, and a “several times” specific-impulse gain were not opened. The NATO STO 2012 paper is login-walled and has no public abstract. Those multipliers are not used here.
- Inlet count, inlet placement, inlet capture area, and whether port covers stay closed during boost are not stated in the opened MBDA or Saab text. The datasheet drawings show a full-fin variant and a cropped-fin variant and do not caption an intake arrangement.
- The DTIC throttle paper’s body was not readable. The pressure-sensitive burn-rate and variable-throat valve description is abstract-level only.
- No opened source states the MICA or MICA NG thrust-time history, pulse split, inter-pulse delay, or total impulse.

## What is actually published about Meteor seeker band, datalink, and two-way midcourse, versus what is inferred?

### Takeaway
Opened primary pages publish an active radio-frequency seeker, inertial mid-course, a datalink, and terminal autonomy. They do not publish a frequency band. “Two-way,” retargeting, and third-party target data are manufacturer statements, and they are stronger in a December 2025 MBDA article and on Saab’s page than in the 2023 MBDA datasheet, which says only “data link.” Nothing opened says the missile downlinks seeker-lock status in those words except material that could not be re-fetched.

### Cited Findings
- MBDA 2023 datasheet, seeker: “Active RF.” Navigation and guidance: “Inertial mid-course with data link” and “Autonomous terminal guidance.” Fuses: impact and “RF proximity.” Warhead: “Blast fragmentation.” The network-enabled paragraph says capability “is met through the weapons data link” and “Meteor can be operated using third party target data.” The sheet does not say two-way, does not name a band, and does not say the missile transmits its own state. — [MBDA Meteor datasheet PDF](https://www.mbda-systems.com/media/19279/download)
- MBDA product page: “Effective operation in dense electronic warfare environments.” No band, no waveform. — [MBDA Meteor](https://www.mbda-systems.com/products/air-dominance/meteor)
- MBDA, 4 December 2025, manufacturer article: “Interception is aided by an active radar seeker for terminal guidance, and supported by inertial mid-course navigation augmented by a two-way data link. This datalink enables target updates or mid-flight retargeting.” This is the clearest MBDA wording that the link is two-way and that retargeting is a designed function. It still does not list downlink message types or a band. — [MBDA, Meteor and F-35A](https://www.mbda-systems.com/meteor-missile-and-f-35a-one-step-closer-operational-capability)
- Saab product page, opened in full: “a two-way datalink and an active radar target seeker.” “The two-way datalink enables the launch aircraft to provide mid-course target updates or retargeting, including data from off-board third parties.” Guidance line in the table: “Inertial Mid-course with Datalinked Updates, Active Radar.” Proximity fuze described as a “jamming-resistant radar proximity fuze (from Saab).” The opened page describes aircraft-to-missile updates. It does not, in the text retrieved, say the missile reports kinematic status or seeker acquisition back to the aircraft. — [Saab Meteor](https://www.saab.com/products/meteor)
- RAF, 10 December 2018: “the Meteor and launch aircraft will be networked by a datalink ensuring highly accurate and intelligent air strikes.” Does not say two-way. — [RAF, 10 December 2018](https://www.raf.mod.uk/news/articles/royal-air-force-typhoons-launch-with-new-missile)
- RAF, 11 December 2023: “guided by an active radar seeker”; “network-enabled, meaning pilots can operate the missile using third-party target data.” Official, and consistent with third-party mid-course data. No band, no statement that the link is two-way. — [RAF, 11 December 2023](https://www.raf.mod.uk/news/articles/typhoon-from-raf-lossiemouth-take-part-in-exercise-with-french-air-force-rafales/)
- RF MICA, MBDA datasheet 2022-02-v02: “Active RF monopulse doppler seeker”; “Strap-down inertial reference unit”; “Data link”; “Lock-On After Launch”; “Lock-On Before Launch.” Target designation from “Onboard aircraft radar,” “Electro-optical sensors,” “Helmet mounted sight (HMS),” and, for the IR seeker, “Autonomous lock-on from the seeker’s own IRST scans.” The sheet does not say the RF datalink is two-way and does not give a band. Fusing: “RF proximity fuse,” impact, “Focused splinters.” — [MBDA MICA datasheet PDF](https://www.mbda-systems.com/media/20541/download)
- MBDA MICA product page: “RF MICA with radar seeker providing all weather shoot-up/shoot down capability” and “IR MICA with dual waveband imaging Infrared seeker.” The IR round “outperforms other BVR missiles with its unique stealthy interception capability provided by its silent IR seeker.” Same published airframe for both seekers: 112 kg, 3.1 m, 160 mm. Platforms Mirage 2000 and Rafale. — [MBDA MICA](https://www.mbda-systems.com/products/air-dominance/mica-family/mica)
- MICA NG datasheet: “Active RF AESA seeker providing all weather shoot-up/shoot-down capability” or “Passive Imaging IR seeker”; “Two way Data link”; lock-on after launch and lock-on before launch. The IR text repeats a 360° launch envelope and “over the shoulder” if the threat is in the rear sector, and says the IR seeker can provide “IRSTS capability to pilot.” Lethal chain: “Multi-mode RF / Laser Proximity fuze,” impact, “High explosive focused fragmentation warhead.” No frequency band. — [MBDA MICA NG datasheet PDF](https://www.mbda-systems.com/sites/mbda/files/2024-07/2023%20MICA%20NG%20datasheet.pdf)
- DGA, 19 June 2025: the first development firing validated “la chaîne complète de mise en œuvre du missile dans sa version infrarouge.” MICA NG “sera développé en deux versions : autodirecteur infrarouge et électromagnétique.” The electromagnetic (active-radar) version was not the round fired that day. Further firings are required “dans ses différentes versions,” including supersonic launch conditions. — [DGA communiqué PDF, 19 June 2025](https://www.defense.gouv.fr/sites/default/files/ministere-armees/Communiqu%C3%A9_La%20DGA%20proc%C3%A8de%20au%20premier%20tir%20de%20d%C3%A9veloppement%20d%E2%80%99un%20missile%20MICA%20NG%20depuis%20un%20avion%20Rafale.pdf)

### Inferences
- A simulator can store, as published data, that Meteor’s terminal sensor is an active RF seeker and that mid-course is inertial plus a datalink that MBDA and Saab say can carry target updates, including third-party tracks, and that MBDA’s 2025 article and Saab call two-way. Band, beamwidth, detection range, and update rate are not published and must not be filled from a look-alike missile.
- Calling the seeker “Ku-band” or “J-band,” or equating it with the MICA AD4A seeker, would be an inference from sources that were not opened. It is omitted.
- Downlink of missile kinematic status or of “seeker has acquired” is a common secondary description. The pages opened here support uplink of target updates and the phrase “two-way.” They do not quote a downlink message list. Model a bidirectional link as a manufacturer claim, not as a specified protocol.
- RF MICA’s published seeker is specifically “monopulse doppler,” which is more detail than Meteor’s “Active RF.” MICA NG’s published seeker is “Active RF AESA,” which is a stated change from the monopulse doppler wording, not a stated frequency change. Lock-after-launch is explicit for both MICA and MICA NG, so a shot may be fired before the onboard seeker is tracking.
- MICA IR, one-paragraph contrast: it is the same MBDA airframe and the same published mass and dimensions, with a passive dual-waveband imaging infrared seeker instead of an active radar seeker. MBDA’s claim for it is silent, stealthy interception and, with lock-on-before-launch and lock-on-after-launch, a 360° envelope including a rear-sector shot. It is not a Fox 3. MICA NG keeps that split: AESA radar or imaging infrared, and MBDA says the NG infrared seeker can also give the pilot an IRST picture.

### Gaps
- Seeker band, waveform, antenna size, acquisition range, and home-on-jam are unpublished on every MBDA, Saab, RAF, and DGA page opened.
- A second Saab Meteor URL that search snippets quote as saying the datalink transmits “functional and kinematic status” and “notification of target acquisition by the seeker” could not be fetched (Cloudflare interstitials on both attempts). That downlink detail is not treated as confirmed.
- MBDA Inc.’s air-dominance page, which search snippets quote as “network-enabled with a two-way data link,” returned almost no body text when fetched. The two-way claim used above rests on the December 2025 MBDA article and the Saab page that did render.
- No opened source says whether Meteor supports lock-on-before-launch. MICA and MICA NG do, in MBDA’s own sheets. Do not copy MICA’s lock modes onto Meteor.
- Warhead mass is not on the opened sheets (MICA says “focused splinters”; MICA NG says “high explosive focused fragmentation”; Meteor says “blast fragmentation”). A 12 kg MICA warhead figure was not confirmed from these pages.

## Which numbers come from MBDA, a UK/French/Swedish government page, a NATO or parliamentary document, or a secondary survey?

### Takeaway
The only dimensions that repeat as manufacturer specifications are Meteor 190 kg, 3.7 m, 178 mm (MBDA) and MICA / MICA NG 112 kg, 3.1 m, 160 mm (MBDA). Saab, a partner, prints a shorter Meteor length, “over Mach 4,” and “100+ km.” UK and French government pages that were opened confirm service, ramjet, active radar, datalink, and programme cost or test events, and they do not add a kinematic envelope. Numeric ranges and no-escape-zone radii that circulate in surveys were not on any opened primary page.

### Cited Findings
- **Meteor airframe, MBDA (manufacturer).** Weight 190 kg, length 3.7 m, diameter 178 mm, on both the product page and the 2023 datasheet. Rail and ejection launch. Drawings are captioned “Full fin variant” and “Cropped fin variant,” with no span and no statement of which aircraft uses which. In service with the partner nations UK, Germany, Italy, France, Sweden, and Spain; integration “well-established” on Eurofighter Typhoon, Rafale, and Gripen; “being integrated” on F-35A and F-35B and on KF-21. — [MBDA Meteor](https://www.mbda-systems.com/products/air-dominance/meteor); [datasheet PDF](https://www.mbda-systems.com/media/19279/download)
- **Meteor airframe and kinematics, Saab (partner, not prime).** Length 3.66 m (12 ft), diameter 0.178 m (7 in). No mass on the page that rendered. Engine “Solid rocket booster and throttleable ramjet.” Speed “Over Mach 4.” Operational range “100+ km,” also worded “more than 100 km.” Warhead “High Explosive Blast-Fragmentation.” Detection “Radar proximity Fuze and impact sensor.” Launch platforms in the table: JAS Gripen, EF Typhoon, Dassault Rafale, and F-35 JSF. Swedish Air Force name “Rb101.” “In 2016, Sweden became the first nation taking Meteor into service.” Gripen E: rail and eject, “up to seven Meteor missiles.” These are Saab statements, not an FMV fact sheet. An FMV page was not opened. — [Saab Meteor](https://www.saab.com/products/meteor)
- **Length conflict.** MBDA 3.7 m versus Saab 3.66 m, same 178 mm diameter. Neither page explains the difference (fin variant, measurement reference, or rounding). Do not average them into a false precision.
- **Meteor no-escape zone, what the words mean in opened sources.** MBDA product page: largest no-escape zone of any air-to-air missile, “several times greater than current MRAAMs,” caused by thrust all the way to intercept. All of the kilometre-style NEZ lines are manufacturer or, for the RAF sentence, an official qualitative claim. The lead sentence says it is “designed for land, sea and air environments,” but the body describes soft vertical launch, 360° coverage, and the land system EMADS for the Royal Artillery, using third-party target data for non-line-of-sight surface engagements. No fighter, no rail/eject aircraft loadout, and no Fox 3 seeker card. — [MBDA CAMM](https://www.mbda-systems.com/products/force-protection/camm-family/camm)
- **NATO document opened:** only the STO catalogue record cited under the propulsion question. It contributed no specification. — [NATO STO record](https://www.sto.nato.int/document/meteor-european-air-dominance-missile-powered-by-high-energy-throttleable-ducted-rocket/)

### Inferences
- Treat MBDA’s 190 kg and 178 mm, and MICA’s 112 kg / 3.1 m / 160 mm, as the manufacturer data to store. Store Saab’s 3.66 m and “over Mach 4” and “100+ km” as partner claims that conflict with or go beyond the MBDA sheet, not as measurements.
- “Several times” a current MRAAM’s no-escape zone is MBDA marketing language. It is not a factor a simulator should multiply into an AIM-120 range.
- Cropped versus full fin is a real airframe variant on the MBDA drawing. Which fin is for semi-recessed carriage is not stated on the sheet. Do not invent a span.
- MICA NG had not been declared in service by the June 2025 DGA communiqué. It is a development round with a stated bi-pulse motor and an AESA option, ordered toward deliveries by 2030 in that communiqué. It should not be marked fielded on the strength of the 2023 brochure.
- RF MICA is the fielded European active-radar missile in the MICA family: MBDA says full-scale production and in service on Mirage 2000-5 and Rafale. MICA IR is the non-radar sibling, not a second kinematic class.

### Gaps
- No opened primary source gives Meteor maximum range, no-escape-zone radius, or a Mach number from MBDA or the RAF. Survey figures such as 200 km, Mach 4 exactly, or a 60 km no-escape zone were seen in search snippets of encyclopaedia and trade pages and were not used, because those pages were not the measurement record and several were not opened.
- Saab’s “100+ km” is the only opened kilometre-class range, and it is a greater-than claim by a partner. It must not be stored as maximum range.
- The 29 June 2022 DGA PDF that search indexes as stating a November 2018 order for 567 MICA NG and deliveries from 2026 did not render readable text when opened (black bars). The quantity 567 and the 2026 date are therefore not confirmed here. If that index text is right, it conflicts with the readable 19 June 2025 communiqué’s “livraisons d’ici à 2030.”
- UK missile order quantities remain unpublished (withheld in the 2003 answer). A later National Audit Office unit-cost figure was not re-opened for this note.
- No Swedish FMV or French DGA page opened here gives Meteor mass, length, or speed. Swedish service identity and the 2016 first-in-service claim rest on Saab.
- No post-June 2025 DGA communiqué was opened, so a later supersonic MICA NG firing reported in the trade press is not confirmed here.
- No opened source shows a fielded air-launched CAMM used as a fighter Fox 3. The product-page phrase “air environments” is not an aircraft integration statement.

## What must a simulator store as a thrust-versus-time or thrust-versus-altitude table rather than as a single maximum range?

### Takeaway
Meteor cannot be a maximum-range number or one sea-level thrust-versus-time curve. The opened physics and the manufacturer description require a boost phase, then a throttleable air-breathing sustain whose thrust depends on altitude, Mach, and fuel-flow command. MICA is a solid rocket whose thrust-time history was not published; MICA NG needs two solid pulses, also unpublished. Published ranges, including Saab’s “100+ km” and MBDA’s “+40%” and “several times” no-escape-zone language, are claims about outcomes, not the thrust data that produce them.

### Cited Findings
- Meteor propulsion is a “solid fuel variable flow ducted rocket (ramjet)” that MBDA says provides thrust “all the way to target intercept” / “continuous thrust up to the point of target interception,” contrasted by MBDA in 2025 with missiles that “boost and then coast unpowered.” — [MBDA Meteor](https://www.mbda-systems.com/products/air-dominance/meteor); [datasheet](https://www.mbda-systems.com/media/19279/download); [MBDA, 4 December 2025](https://www.mbda-systems.com/meteor-missile-and-f-35a-one-step-closer-operational-capability)
- Saab splits the powerplant into “Solid rocket booster and throttleable ramjet,” which is a two-regime motor even though MBDA’s sheet uses a single propulsion bullet. — [Saab Meteor](https://www.saab.com/products/meteor)
- NACA RM E7I05: for a fixed inlet, net thrust at Mach 1 fell from about 13,000 lbf at sea level to about 8,000 lbf at 45,000 ft because captured airflow fell from three times design to one-half design, and specific fuel consumption worsened as the mixture went rich. At fixed altitude, thrust changed across Mach 1, 2, and 3 and across air-to-rocket mass-flow ratio. The memo also says static ducted thrust is only about 9 percent above the rocket alone, so the air-breathing benefit is a flight condition, not a property of the hardware on the rail. These magnitudes are for the 1948 hypothetical engine only. — [NACA RM E7I05](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19930085734/downloads/19930085734.pdf)
- DTIC ADP005719 abstract: altitude coverage makes thrust variation mandatory, implemented as gas-generator mass-flow control through a variable throat and a pressure-sensitive solid burn rate, with a Mach controller outside that loop. Abstract only; PDF blocked. — [DTIC ADP005719 record](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/html/tr/ADP005719/index.html)
- No-escape zone, in the opened MBDA and RAF sentences, is the result they attribute to still having thrust at intercept, not a range number. MBDA’s “several times greater than current MRAAMs” and “largest … of any air-to-air missile system” are manufacturer claims without a geometry. — [MBDA Meteor](https://www.mbda-systems.com/products/air-dominance/meteor); [RAF, 11 December 2023](https://www.raf.mod.uk/news/articles/typhoon-from-raf-lossiemouth-take-part-in-exercise-with-french-air-force-rafales/)
- Saab’s only opened range figure is “100+ km” / “more than 100 km,” beside “Over Mach 4.” MBDA publishes neither. — [Saab Meteor](https://www.saab.com/products/meteor)
- RF MICA propulsion on the MBDA sheet is one class of motor: high-impulse, low-smoke, solid propellant, plus TVC. No second pulse is listed. No thrust-time table is listed. — [MBDA MICA datasheet PDF](https://www.mbda-systems.com/media/20541/download)
- MICA NG replaces that, in MBDA’s and DGA’s wording, with a dual-pulse / bi-pulse solid motor and a claimed range increase of up to 40 percent versus MICA, still without a curve or an absolute range. External mass and dimensions stay 112 kg, 3.1 m, 160 mm. — [MICA NG datasheet PDF](https://www.mbda-systems.com/sites/mbda/files/2024-07/2023%20MICA%20NG%20datasheet.pdf); [DGA, 19 June 2025](https://www.defense.gouv.fr/sites/default/files/ministere-armees/Communiqu%C3%A9_La%20DGA%20proc%C3%A8de%20au%20premier%20tir%20de%20d%C3%A9veloppement%20d%E2%80%99un%20missile%20MICA%20NG%20depuis%20un%20avion%20Rafale.pdf)

### Inferences
- A Meteor thrust model has to be at least T(altitude, Mach, throttle) while gas-generator propellant remains, after a separate booster impulse that ends at ramjet light-up. A table that is only T(t) at one altitude will miss the NACA effect: the same throttle at two altitudes is not the same thrust, because the inlet’s air mass flow changed. Specific energy late in flight is an output of that table plus drag, not an input range.
- Throttle is a state, not a constant. The DTIC abstract’s Mach loop is one published reason throttle exists (hold a speed across altitude). MBDA’s “variable flow” and “thrust all the way” do not say whether the operational law is max-thrust, min-time, or a cruise-then-sprint. Those laws are not data.
- RF MICA should be stored as boost-coast: one solid thrust-versus-time history, then zero thrust, with TVC available only in the inference that vectoring needs motor mass flow. The history itself is unpublished. Inventing it so that a brochure range comes out true is the failure mode this question is about.
- MICA NG should be stored as two solid pulses and a gap whose duration was not published. It is not a ducted rocket and it is not “MICA with a longer range slider.” The +40 percent claim can be a check after a motor model exists. It cannot be the motor model.
- Seeker and datalink are separate stores from propulsion. Meteor: active RF, band unknown, inertial mid-course, datalink with third-party updates claimed, two-way claimed by MBDA (2025) and Saab, terminal autonomy claimed. RF MICA: active RF monopulse doppler, datalink, lock-on before and after launch. MICA NG: active RF AESA or imaging IR, two-way datalink explicit on the MBDA sheet, lock-on before and after launch.

### Gaps
- No opened source contains the actual table: booster impulse, ramjet thrust versus altitude and Mach, minimum and maximum gas-generator flow, or propellant mass for Meteor; burn time and thrust for RF MICA; pulse thrusts and the gap for MICA NG.
- Drag, reference area, and fin span are unpublished. Cropped-fin versus full-fin is shown and not dimensioned.
- Because those tables are absent, any reach that a simulation produces is only as good as the motor model the user supplies. It must not be pinned afterward to 100 km, 200 km, or a no-escape-zone radius taken from a brochure.

## Implementation notes for missilesim

A Meteor card is not a solid-rocket Fox 3 card with a larger range. Store it as its own motor class.

Data that are published and can be fields:

- Identity: MBDA Meteor; Saab/Swedish Air Force name Rb101 (Saab, not an FMV page). Partner nations UK, Germany, Italy, France, Spain, Sweden. Prime contractor MBDA.
- Airframe from MBDA: mass 190 kg, length 3.7 m, diameter 178 mm, rail and ejection launch, full-fin and cropped-fin variants with no published span. Flag Saab’s length 3.66 m as a conflicting partner figure. Do not store a fin span.
- Motor class from MBDA: solid-fuel variable-flow ducted rocket (ramjet). From Saab, also a solid rocket booster ahead of that ramjet. Speed class “over Mach 4” is Saab only; MBDA’s sheet has no speed.
- Seeker from MBDA: active RF, terminal guidance autonomous, RF proximity fuze plus impact, blast-fragmentation warhead. No band, no detection range, no warhead mass.
- Guidance: inertial mid-course with datalink; third-party target data (MBDA datasheet and RAF). Two-way and retargeting: MBDA article of 4 December 2025 and Saab. The 2023 datasheet does not say two-way. Store the link as datalink-with-updates, and mark bidirectionality as a manufacturer claim.
- Service: Sweden first, 2016, per Saab. RAF Typhoon QRA carriage from 10 December 2018, per the RAF. F-35 not in service on the December 2025 MBDA account (ground tests; UK leads F-35B, Italy sponsors F-35A).

Data that are not published and must not be invented to hit a range:

- Thrust versus time at one altitude. Wrong shape for this motor.
- What to store instead, as inputs the simulation integrates, knowing the numbers are not in open MBDA or government sheets: booster impulse and burn; then ramjet thrust as a function of altitude, Mach, and throttle (or gas-generator fuel flow), limited by remaining sustain propellant; inlet on only after booster handover. The NACA memo is a warning about altitude and Mach dependence, not a Meteor table. Do not paste its 8,000–23,500 lbf figures into the card.
- Specific energy at intercept is an output. MBDA and the RAF attribute a large no-escape zone to thrust that continues through the phase in which a solid rocket would be coasting. That zone is a manufacturer or official qualitative claim (“several times” current MRAAMs; “very large”; “far fewer options to evade”). It is not a radius to hard-code. Saab’s “100+ km” is a partner floor, not maximum range. No opened MBDA or RAF page gives a maximum range in kilometres.

RF MICA card, separate from Meteor:

- Same MBDA airframe numbers for the radar and infrared rounds: 112 kg, 3.1 m, 160 mm. Platforms on the MBDA page: Mirage 2000 and Rafale. In service, full-scale production, more than 5,900 MICA-family missiles sold (MBDA, family sales, not a stock count).
- Motor: low-smoke high-impulse solid propellant. One burn, then coast. Thrust-versus-time is required and was not published. TVC, long-chord wings, tail controls (MBDA). TVC while thrust exists is an inference, not a sentence on the sheet.
- Seeker: active RF monopulse doppler, strapdown inertial, datalink, lock-on before launch and lock-on after launch (MBDA). Band unpublished. Do not mark the MICA datalink two-way; the sheet does not say that. MICA NG’s sheet does.
- MICA IR contrast only: passive dual-waveband imaging infrared seeker on that same airframe, which MBDA calls a silent seeker with a 360° short-range envelope including rear sector. Not a Fox 3 and not a different mass.

MICA NG card, not a range-scaled MICA and not a ramjet:

- MBDA reprints 112 kg, 3.1 m, 160 mm, so the airframe store can match MICA until a better source exists. Seeker store changes: active RF AESA or passive imaging IR, and the sheet explicitly says “Two way Data link,” plus lock-on before and after launch. TVC is still listed, including for helmet-cue and very short shots.
- Motor store: dual-pulse solid, two thrust segments and an unpublished gap. DGA, 19 June 2025, confirms bi-pulse in an official communiqué and says first deliveries by 2030. The “up to +40%” versus MICA is an MBDA claim. Do not enter 40 percent more range as a hard-coded reach. Do not enter a kilometre range at all.
- Status: development. The opened DGA text is an IR development firing on 19 June 2025, not an operational clearance of the electromagnetic round.

CAMM: MBDA lists 99 kg, 3.2 m, 166 mm and describes a soft-vertical-launch land and naval weapon. Do not add an air-launched Fox 3 CAMM card. No opened source shows one fielded.

What must not become a hard-coded reach: Meteor “100+ km,” any 200 km survey figure, MBDA’s “several times” no-escape zone, the RAF’s “very large no escape zone,” and MICA NG’s “+40%.” Those are claims about the result of the motor. The card’s job is the motor, the mass, and the seeker class. Range is whatever the integration produces from thrust, altitude, Mach, and drag, and the thrust tables for these three missiles are not in the open sources above.
