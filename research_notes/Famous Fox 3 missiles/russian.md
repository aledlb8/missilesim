# Russian and Soviet active-radar air-to-air missiles (Fox 3)

Open-literature dossier for a real-time simulator. Every figure below is printed on a page opened for this note. Conflicting printed numbers are both kept. Export and brochure ranges are manufacturer or compiler claims, not kinematic envelopes and not no-escape zones. No employment procedure, seeker circuit, or classified envelope is included. Today is 2026-09-29.

Direct fetches of some `www.roe.ru` URLs failed earlier in the session. The product cards and the 29 July 2026 press release were then opened on `roe.ru` and are cited from those reads. A Rosoboronexport YouTube item linked from the RVV-BD card was not replayed; a search-indexed transcript of it is not used as a fact.

NATO names in the opened pages do not agree: Missilery calls the long-range round AA-X-13 Arrow; English Wikipedia calls it AA-13 Axehead. The R-77 page on Wikipedia uses AA-12 Adder. Those are reporting names only.

## What is the published guidance sequence: inertial, command or datalink updates, then active seeker?

### Takeaway

For every fielded or export-catalogued round opened here, the published sequence is inertial flight, radio correction (a datalink update, not semi-active illumination), then an active radar terminal phase. Short-range shots can skip the inertial phase and fly active from launch. The R-33 is inertial then semi-active, so it is a Fox 1. The active R-27 variants were not produced. RVV-SDM, marketed by Rosoboronexport on 29 July 2026, adds a passive channel on the same inertial-plus-radio-correction backbone and does not publish a kilometre range.

### Cited Findings

#### R-77 / RVV-AE (izdeliye 170 / export izdeliye 190)

- Seeker: active radar. Combined guidance is inertial with radio correction, then active radar homing when the onboard computer decides the seeker can capture. The seeker named on this page is 9B1348E. At the shortest ranges the active head is switched on at once and the command-inertial phase is not used. If lock breaks, the carrier keeps a mathematical model of the target so the missile can recapture on trajectory or be retargeted. In jamming the seeker can passive-home on a jammer that is co-located with the target. The guidance law in all modes is modified proportional navigation. [published] — [Missilery R-77](https://missilery.info/missile/r77)
- The same page’s surface-launch cousin article describes strapdown inertial flight toward a “mathematical” target whose motion was loaded in the pre-launch flight task, then active lock after launch. That article does not mention in-flight radio correction. It is a SAM write-up, not the air-to-air card. [published] — [pvo.guns.ru RVV-AE SAM variant](http://pvo.guns.ru/r77/)
- English Wikipedia’s guidance box instead says “Inertial guidance with mid-course SARH and datalink update,” then active (or IR / passive on unfielded variants). That mid-course SARH wording is not on the Missilery page or the Rosoboronexport RVV-SD card. [published, contradicted] — [Wikipedia R-77](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R-77)
- Control: normal aerodynamic scheme, wings reduced to narrow thin plates, folding lattice tail controls. Hinge moment “does not exceed 1.5 kgf·m,” so small electric actuators are used. The lattices stay effective to about 40° angle of attack. The page states a turn rate up to 150°/s. Folded, the missile fits a 300 mm square, for conformal and internal carriage. Not thrust vectoring. [published] — [Missilery R-77](https://missilery.info/missile/r77)
- A 2005 Vympel article on the SAM variant says the lattices bring only an “insignificant” rise in drag and radar cross-section, a small hinge moment that is stable across the flight envelope, attached flow to about 40°, and folding so the round fits a fuselage bay or a tube. Nose is parabolic “to minimize wave drag.” [published] — [pvo.guns.ru RVV-AE SAM variant](http://pvo.guns.ru/r77/)
- General grid-fin aerodynamics, not an R-77 measurement: short chord cuts hinge torque; fins fold flatter than planar fins, which matters for tubes and internal bays; they “perform very well at subsonic and supersonic speeds, but poorly at transonic speeds” because a normal shock forms inside the lattice, flow spills around the fin, and wave drag rises; at high Mach the lattice can have lower drag and more maneuverability than planar fins. [published] — [Wikipedia Grid fin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grid_fin)
- English Wikipedia’s R-77 design section says the lattices also “increase drag and radar cross section,” and repeats the 150°/s turn-rate claim. [published] — [Wikipedia R-77](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R-77)
- Motor: solid. Prose says speed up to M = 4. The characteristics table on the same page says M = 4.5. The SAM article says a single-mode solid and Mach 3. Three printed speeds, two of them on air-to-air versus surface-launch pages. [published, conflicting] — [Missilery R-77](https://missilery.info/missile/r77); [pvo.guns.ru RVV-AE SAM variant](http://pvo.guns.ru/r77/)
- Mass, size, warhead, fuze, as printed on the Missilery characteristics table: length 3600 mm; diameter 200 mm; wing span 454 mm (400 mm in parentheses); fin span 750 mm (740 mm); launch mass 175 kg; warhead 22 kg (18 kg); rod warhead with micro-cumulative elements; laser fuze plus a contact fuze, the contact path also used if the round hits the surface; maximum launch range 80 km; minimum 0.3 km; target load factor 12; single-shot probability 0.7. Narrative on the same page: high-altitude launch range up to 80 km with own-seeker capture at 20 km; low-altitude targets to 20 km; tail-chase to 25 km; target speed to 3600 km/h; target altitude 20 m to 25 km; vertical separation up to 10 km; aspect up to 90° from the carrier; target load factor 12. [published] — [Missilery R-77](https://missilery.info/missile/r77)
- The SAM article prints target load factor “up to 9,” not 12. [published, conflicting] — [pvo.guns.ru RVV-AE SAM variant](http://pvo.guns.ru/r77/)
- Wikipedia infobox: mass 175 kg, length 3.6 m, diameter 200 mm, wingspan 700 mm (neither 750 nor 740), warhead 22.5 kg HE fragmenting, laser proximity fuze, solid motor, range 80 km, flight altitude 5–25 km (not 0.02–25 km), Mach 4. [published, conflicting] — [Wikipedia R-77](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R-77)
- Launch gear: APU-170 and catapult AKU-170, the catapult for internal bays. [published] — [Missilery R-77](https://missilery.info/missile/r77)
- Dates and carriers: design cycle at Vympel 1982–1989; “adopted in February 1994”; listed for MiG-29SD/SE/SM/M, Su-35, Su-37, MiG-31M “and others.” The SAM article also says adopted in 1994 for tactical aviation and PVO fighters, MiG-29 and Su-27. [published] — [Missilery R-77](https://missilery.info/missile/r77); [pvo.guns.ru RVV-AE SAM variant](http://pvo.guns.ru/r77/)
- Izvestia, citing unnamed industry sources rather than a ministry communique, says series production was planned at Artem in Ukraine and was not established; limited batches were built at Vympel with Ukrainian cooperation; the domestic air force was not supplied; a small buy in 2009 was for Su-35S then on test. Wikipedia says the basic R-77 is not thought to have entered Russian service in significant numbers, and that for years only the export RVV-AE was produced. [published] — [Izvestia, 23 Aug 2020](https://iz.ru/1051494/anton-lavrov-roman-kretcul/istrebitelskii-spros-vks-poluchat-noveishie-rakety-po-rekordnomu-kontraktu); [Wikipedia R-77](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R-77)
- Missilery also says ramjet variants were developed to reach AEW aircraft out to 150 km, and that an infrared seeker with lock-on in flight was envisaged. Those are development directions on that page, not a produced Fox 3. [published] — [Missilery R-77](https://missilery.info/missile/r77)

The 110 km line is an export maximum-range claim. [published, manufacturer claim] — [Rosoboronexport RVV-SD](http://roe.ru/en/production/air-to-air-guided-missiles/rvv-sd/)
- Missilery characteristics block, which matches that card and adds geometry: minimum range in the rear hemisphere 0.3 km; maximum in the front hemisphere 110 km; target altitude 0.02–25 km; target load factor 12; length 3710 mm; body diameter 200 mm; wing span 420 mm; fin span 680 mm; launch mass 190 kg; warhead 22.5 kg; missile available load factor 40. Motor: single-mode solid. Fuze: laser proximity. Warhead: rod, multi-cumulative. [published] — [Missilery R-77-1](https://missilery.info/missile/rvv-cd)
- Still lattice tails. The stated aerodynamic changes versus RVV-AE are a longer radio-transparent nose, a faired tail, and recessed attachment fittings for the lattice fins. Those are the drag-reduction features this page actually names. It does not say the fins became planar. [published] — [Missilery R-77-1](https://missilery.info/missile/rvv-cd)
- Wikipedia says the R-77-1 is 15 kg heavier (190 vs 175 kg), 3.71 m vs 3.6 m, range 110 km vs 80 km, with seeker upgrades and a boat-tail “to reduce drag.” It also says “new fins,” without saying the lattices were deleted. [published] — [Wikipedia R-77](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R-77)

The Agat page opened below offers an active-passive head as a candidate for item 170-1. It does not say that head is the seeker in the missiles Izvestia describes as delivered. The July 2026 Rosoboronexport release treats the passive channel as a new feature of RVV-SDM, not of RVV-SD. [published, not the same claim] — [Defence Express, 27 July 2025](https://en.defence-ua.com/weapon_and_tech/izdelie_180_k_77m_r_77m_what_we_know_about_russias_new_long_range_missile-15268.html); [Rosoboronexport, 29 July 2026](http://roe.ru/media/posts/kategoriya-2/rosoboroneksport-predstavlyaet-na-mirovom-rynke-noveyshuyu-raketu-dlya-su-57e/)
- Launcher AKU-170E, pyro-hydraulic piston catapult. Printed separation: speed up to 7 m/s; pitch angle from −2° to 0°; pitch rate from −10 to −70 °/s; forced-follow stroke about 100 mm. [published] — [Missilery R-77-1](https://missilery.info/missile/rvv-cd)
- Carriers named: Su-35S and Su-34 first, T-50 external stations, MiG-31BM up to four. Foreign carriers “possible” after adaptation. [published] — [Missilery R-77-1](https://missilery.info/missile/rvv-cd)
- Dates: MoD development task 2003; first four rounds December 2005; six more in 2006 opened the joint state-test phase, which the page says did not actually run until the Su-35S complex existed; complex ready at the end of 2010; final launches that year were direct hits; series production at Vympel. [published] — [Missilery R-77-1](https://missilery.info/missile/rvv-cd)

[published] — [Izvestia, 23 Aug 2020](https://iz.ru/1051494/anton-lavrov-roman-kretcul/istrebitelskii-spros-vks-poluchat-noveishie-rakety-po-rekordnomu-kontraktu)

#### R-27EA / R-27EM

- Not Fox 3s in service. English Wikipedia: plans for an active-radar R-27EA “were cancelled in 1989.” R-27A / K-27A, active, seeker 9B-1103, “several examples” and “12 test flights” on a MiG-29 plus “2 guided launches,” then funding diverted in 1989; “never left the prototyping stage.” R-27EA / K-27EA, active 9B-1103, range “> 130 km,” the long-motor version of K-27A, “cancelled at the same time”; “none were made.” R-27EM / K-27EM is semi-active (RGS-31), not active, with inertial control and linear radio correction aimed at very low targets; range 130 km on that page; “no missiles were produced”; several RGS-31 heads were bench-tested; funding cut in July 1991. The guidance box lists R-27EM with the SARH rounds and R-27A/EA as the active ones. [published] — [Wikipedia R-27](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R-27_(air-to-air_missile))

## What open sources say about seeker lock-on after launch and the size of the acquisition basket?

### Takeaway

Lock-on after launch is the published normal case: the missile flies inertially, with radio updates, until its own seeker can capture, and a short-range shot can be active immediately. The opened pages print capture range, sometimes against a stated radar cross-section. They do not print the angular width of the terminal search basket. The ±60° figure on the RVV-BD side is a pre-launch designation sector, not that basket.

### Cited Findings

- R-77 / RVV-AE: “capture by its own seeker at a range of 20 km,” with the computer choosing the handover range. No RCS is attached to that 20 km on the missile page. At minimum range the active head is on from the start. If capture fails, the carrier’s mathematical model can support a later trajectory capture. [published] — [Missilery R-77](https://missilery.info/missile/r77)
- Wikipedia repeats a handover “within 20 km (12 mi),” and says the fighter radar keeps a computed track in case the missile’s lock breaks. [published] — [Wikipedia R-77](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R-77)
- 9B-1103M, the head tied to RVV-SD: lock not less than 20 km against 5 m²; radio-correction channel up to 50 km with a MiG-29-class system; autonomous active mode needs no further radar support after the initial cue. [published] — [Missilery 9B-1103M](https://missilery.info/missile/p27/9b1103m)
- Izvestia’s test pilot Igor Malikov, speaking about R-77-1, not reading a brochure: after launch the missile goes to a programmed point and “about 30 km” from the target turns on its own radar. That is one expert’s approximation, beside the 20 km brochure and missile-page figures. [published] — [Izvestia, 23 Aug 2020](https://iz.ru/1051494/anton-lavrov-roman-kretcul/istrebitelskii-spros-vks-poluchat-noveishie-rakety-po-rekordnomu-kontraktu)
- 9B-1103M-200PS brochure, potential fit for RVV-SD and RVV-AE-PD: against 3 m², about 200 km in the inertial-passive mode and 15 km in the active mode. Passive lock on a jammer or an emitting radar is a different observable from active lock on skin return. Not evidence that series R-77-1 rounds lock actively at 200 km. [published] — [Missilery 9B-1103M-200PS](https://missilery.info/missile/rvv-cd/9b-1103m-200ps)
- K-37 seeker 9B-1388: active lock 40 km against 5 m²; radio correction from the carrier up to 100 km. [published] — [Missilery R-37](https://missilery.info/missile/r37)
- 9B-1103M-350: lock greater than 40 km against 5 m²; pre-launch cue inside ±60° in the forward hemisphere; autonomous active mode does not require in-flight radar support. [published] — [Missilery 9B-1103M-350](https://en.missilery.info/missile/r37/9b-1103m-350)
- RVV-BD missile table: designation angles ±60°. Same number, printed as a missile cue angle, not as a scan cone. [published] — [Missilery R-37](https://missilery.info/missile/r37)
- R-77 narrative aspect limit: attack at up to 90° off the carrier’s axis. That is a launch-geometry claim, not a seeker field of view. [published] — [Missilery R-77](https://missilery.info/missile/r77)
- SAM-variant article: the active head “captures the target after launch,” so illumination after launch is not required. [published] — [pvo.guns.ru RVV-AE SAM variant](http://pvo.guns.ru/r77/)
- KS-172 Wikipedia body: transition to active 9B-1103M “at a range of 80–100 km.” That number is not on the Agat cards opened for either 9B-1103M or 9B-1103M-350. [published, unsupported by the seeker cards] — [Russian Wikipedia KS-172](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%9A%D0%A1-172)
- No opened page prints a terminal basket in degrees (search cone, beamwidth, or look angle after go-active) for the R-77, R-77-1, or R-37M.

### Inferences

- The 20 km and 40 km figures are seeker capture ranges against a stated or unstated target, used as the handover condition. They are not the missile’s maximum fly-out, and they are not a fixed timer.
- A 5 m² capture range cannot be reused as a fighter, a cruise-missile, or a stealth-target range. The pages that state 5 m² say 5 m². The R-77 missile page’s 20 km does not say 5 m².
- Passive-channel ranges and active-channel ranges are different modes. Mixing the 200 km passive brochure figure into the active terminal model would be a different weapon.

### Gaps

- Angular acquisition basket: not found on any opened page.
- RVV-SD and RVV-BD catalog cards print no seeker-lock range at all. A search-indexed transcript of the Rosoboronexport RVV-BD video is said to quote 30 km front-hemisphere and 10 km rear-hemisphere seeker ranges; the video was not opened, so those two numbers are not used.
- No opened page gives capture range versus aspect, altitude, or closing speed, except the R-77 page’s separate launch-range bands (high, low, tail) and the 9B-1103M-200PS split between passive and active modes.
- Seeker ready times (1–1.5 s warm, 5–8 s cold) are printed for the Agat heads. How much of that must already have elapsed on the rail is not stated.

## What a simulator should store so that an R-77 and an R-37M are different dynamics, not two copies of a max-range constant?

### Takeaway

Store a different airframe, motor class, launch method, seeker handover, and fuze for each family. The export “up to 110 km” and “up to 200 km” lines are placard claims under unnamed conditions. They are not the integration limit of the flight model. An R-77 card that only changes that number into an R-37M card will still be the same missile.

### Cited Findings

- R-77 / RVV-AE versus RVV-SD, both opened: 175 kg vs 190 kg; 3.60 m vs 3.71 m; wing span 400–454 mm vs 420 mm; lattice span 700–750 mm vs 680 mm; warhead about 22 kg vs 22.5 kg; both 200 mm diameter; both laser proximity; both target g of 12 on the air-to-air pages (9 g only on the SAM article); placard range 80 km vs “up to 110 km”; RVV-SD missile load factor printed as 40, which the R-77 table does not print. RVV-SD motor is stated single-mode solid. R-77 motor is stated solid, with M = 4, M = 4.5, and, on the SAM page, Mach 3. [published] — [Missilery R-77](https://missilery.info/missile/r77); [Missilery R-77-1](https://missilery.info/missile/rvv-cd); [Rosoboronexport RVV-SD](http://roe.ru/en/production/air-to-air-guided-missiles/rvv-sd/); [pvo.guns.ru RVV-AE SAM variant](http://pvo.guns.ru/r77/)
- RVV-BD versus those two: launch mass up to 510 kg, about 2.7 times an RVV-SD; length 4.06 m; diameter 0.38 m; wing span 0.72 m; fin span 1.02 m; warhead 60 kg HE-frag, not a 22 kg rod; fuze radar proximity plus contact, not laser; target load factor 8, not 12; minimum range printed 3.7 km, not 0.3 km; target altitude floor 0.015 km rather than 0.02 km; target speed on the export card 2500 km/h, whereas the R-77 page prints 3600 km/h; placard “up to 200 km” against some target types, not 80 or 110. Eject, then light a dual-mode solid. Conventional folding tails, not lattices. Seeker diameter class 350–380 mm, not 200 mm. [published] — [Rosoboronexport RVV-BD](http://roe.ru/en/production/air-to-air-guided-missiles/rvv-bd/); [Missilery R-37](https://missilery.info/missile/r37)
- Handover ranges that are actually printed, and must not be swapped: R-77 page 20 km with no RCS; 9B-1103M not less than 20 km at 5 m² and a 50 km correction channel on a MiG-29-class system; Malikov “about 30 km” as interview speech; 9B-1388 40 km at 5 m² and correction out to 100 km; 9B-1103M-350 greater than 40 km at 5 m². [published] — seeker and missile URLs in the previous sections.
- Lattice versus planar is a stored aerodynamic flag, not a single Cd. Opened Russian text: small hinge moment (≤1.5 kgf·m), control to about 40°, a 150°/s claim, folding into a 300 mm square, and an “insignificant” drag and RCS penalty. Opened general aero text: a transonic wave-drag rise, and possible lower drag than planar fins once the lattice is fully supersonic. R-77-1’s printed drag work is the longer nose, the boat-tail, and recessed lattice mounts, while the lattices remain. Izdeliye 180’s printed change on Missilery is flat non-folding fins “for internal carriage,” with Western pages adding drag, RCS, and a dual-pulse motor as claims. RVV-SDM’s 2026 release says aerodynamics and motor energy improved, and does not say how. [published] — [Missilery R-77](https://missilery.info/missile/r77); [pvo.guns.ru RVV-AE SAM variant](http://pvo.guns.ru/r77/); [Wikipedia Grid fin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grid_fin); [Missilery R-77-1](https://missilery.info/missile/rvv-cd); [Rosoboronexport, 29 July 2026](http://roe.ru/media/posts/kategoriya-2/rosoboroneksport-predstavlyaet-na-mirovom-rynke-noveyshuyu-raketu-dlya-su-57e/)
- Launch method differs: R-77 rail APU-170 or catapult AKU-170; R-77-1 catapult AKU-170E with a printed separation speed up to 7 m/s and a nose-down pitch rate; R-37M ejector AKU-410-1 or AKU-620, motor start after the round is clear. [published] — [Missilery R-77](https://missilery.info/missile/r77); [Missilery R-77-1](https://missilery.info/missile/rvv-cd); [Missilery R-37](https://missilery.info/missile/r37)

### Inferences

- The dynamics that actually separate an R-77 from an R-37M in open sources are mass (about 175–190 kg vs up to 510 kg), diameter (200 vs 380 mm), fin type (lattice vs conventional), motor wording (solid / single-mode vs dual-mode), eject-then-ignite versus a smaller catapult, seeker aperture class, fuze type, and a lower printed target-g for the big round. The placard range is a label on top of that, not the model.
- A transonic drag hump on lattice fins is a regime flag from general grid-fin literature. It is not a measured R-77 Cd, and it should not be applied unchanged at high Mach, where the same literature allows the lattice to be cleaner than a planar fin.

### Gaps

- No thrust-time curve, propellant mass, specific impulse, or Cd for any round here.
- No opened source says whether “dual-mode” on RVV-BD is boost-sustain in one burn or two pulses with a coast.
- No no-escape zone was printed on a manufacturer card. GlobalSecurity’s 40–70 km fighter band is commentary on that page.
- RVV-SDM cannot yet be given its own mass, fin, or range card from an opened primary page.

### Implementation notes for missilesim

Store one card per variant. Do not alias RVV-AE to RVV-SD, RVV-SD to RVV-SDM, or K-37 to RVV-BD. Do not treat R-33, R-27R/ER, or R-27EM as Fox 3s. Do not instantiate R-27EA or KS-172 as in-service weapons; the opened pages do not show production.

Fields that differ across the families, and the only values an opened page supports:

- `guidance_sequence`: for R-77, RVV-SD, RVV-BD, and RVV-SDM, inertial, then radio-correction updates, then active terminal. RVV-SDM adds a passive channel whose seeker model is not named. Short-range R-77 can start active. R-33 is inertial then semi-active. A Wikipedia SARH midcourse on the R-77 is not the card.
- `handover_range_m` and `handover_rcs_m2`: keep them paired. 20 km with RCS unknown (R-77 missile page); ≥20 km at 5 m² (9B-1103M); ~30 km as interview speech only (R-77-1); 40 km at 5 m² (9B-1388); >40 km at 5 m² (9B-1103M-350). The 15 km active / 200 km passive pair belongs only to the 9B-1103M-200PS brochure, which is a candidate head.
- `datalink_range_m`: 50 km printed for 9B-1103M with a MiG-29-class system; 100 km printed for 9B-1388. Not printed on the RVV-SD or RVV-BD catalog cards.
- `designation_sector_deg`: ±60° pre-launch for RVV-BD / 9B-1103M-350. This is not the in-flight search basket. Basket half-angle is unknown. Do not invent one.
- `seeker_band`: unknown for the series R-77 and R-77-1. “X, Ku” only on the 9B-1103M-350 card. “Ku” / “Kp” only on the 9B-1103M-200PS card.
- `control`: `lattice_tails` for R-77 and R-77-1; `planar_tails_nonfolding` only as Missilery’s description of izdeliye 180, which is not yet identified with RVV-SDM; `conventional_folding_tails` for K-37 / RVV-BD, with only the upper fins folded on RVV-BD. No thrust vectoring on any opened card. Lattice parameters worth storing as claims, not as a Cd: hinge moment ≤1.5 kgf·m, effective to ~40° angle of attack, 150°/s turn-rate claim, folded envelope about 300 mm. Transonic wave drag is a regime flag from grid-fin literature, not an R-77 measurement.
- `motor`: `solid_unspecified` for R-77 (do not pick M = 4, 4.5, or the SAM page’s Mach 3 as the only truth); `solid_single_mode` for RVV-SD; `solid_dual_mode` for RVV-BD. Do not store `dual_pulse` unless the card is explicitly the secondary izdeliye 180 claim, and tag it as a claim.
- `launch`: rail or catapult for R-77; AKU-170E numbers for R-77-1 (≤7 m/s, pitch −2° to 0°, pitch rate −10 to −70 °/s); eject-then-ignite for R-37M.
- `mass_kg`, `length_m`, `diameter_m`, `warhead_kg`, `fuze`: R-77 about 175 kg, 3.6 m, 0.2 m, warhead 22 kg (also printed 18 kg and, on Wikipedia, 22.5 kg), laser plus contact; RVV-SD 190 kg, 3.71 m, 0.2 m, 22.5 kg, laser; RVV-BD ≤510 kg, 4.06 m, 0.38 m, 60 kg HE-frag, radar proximity plus contact.
