# Expendable countermeasures: chaff, flares, and a towed decoy

Physics of radar cross section, radiometry, and trajectories for a simulator that flies countermeasures as objects. Not a design for a munition, and not a dispense program. Dates of sources are those on the documents; this note was compiled on 2026-09-29.

Books that were requested but not opened in full: Knott, Shaeffer, and Tuley, *Radar Cross Section* (Internet Archive copy is access-restricted); Schleher, *Introduction to Electronic Warfare* and *Electronic Warfare in the Information Age* (access-restricted); Van Brunt, *Applied ECM*; Neri, *Introduction to Electronic Defense Systems*; *The Infrared Handbook* / EO systems handbook volume 7 (listed on Archive, not fetched). Where those authors are the usual citation, the number below is taken from an open secondary source that was actually read, and the gap is stated.

DTIC PDF fetches were often blocked or under maintenance. A few DTIC formulas are from the indexed text of the public PDFs, not from a successful page render. Those are marked.

## Chaff cloud: dipole RCS, effective count, polarization, bloom, fall, and separation

### Takeaway

A chaff cloud is a bundle of thin dipoles whose mean RCS is about \(0.15\lambda^{2}\) to \(0.18\lambda^{2}\) per randomly oriented resonant dipole, times an effective count that is smaller than the number cut, and that turns on as the cloud blooms. After a very short aerodynamic transient the cloud’s mean velocity is the local wind plus a slow fall (about \(0.25\)–\(0.6\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\) for common glass and foil types). The aircraft keeps its own velocity, so the cloud separates in range and Doppler whenever the line-of-sight component of that velocity difference is nonzero.

### Cited Findings

- Peak RCS of a half-wave dipole, broadside, polarization matched to the wire, is given as \(\sigma_{\max}=0.86\lambda^{2}\). Copper-wire measurements from \(5\) to \(11\,\mathrm{GHz}\) followed that theoretical curve (peaks within roughly \(1\,\mathrm{dB}\) on the published table) and the measured resonance sat below the frequency implied by \(\lambda=2L\). Example row: length \(22.92\,\mathrm{mm}\), theoretical peak \(6.23\,\mathrm{GHz}\) and \(-27\,\mathrm{dBm}\) (lab scale), measured \(5.69\,\mathrm{GHz}\) and \(-27.57\,\mathrm{dBm}\). Aluminised glass and microwire were weaker than copper. **Measured versus the \(0.86\lambda^{2}\) model.** — [Balleri and Butters, IET Radar, Sonar & Navigation, 2024](https://ietresearch.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1049/rsn2.12654)

### Inferences

- The \(0.15\)–\(0.18\lambda^{2}\) band in the task is the spatial average per resonant dipole, not the broadside peak. Use \(\sigma_{\max}=0.86\lambda^{2}\) only for one known orientation and matched linear polarization. For a cloud, the defensible open-literature defaults are \(\sigma_{1}=0.17\lambda^{2}\) (theoretical random orientation, one linear polarization, no losses) or \(\sigma_{1}=0.15\lambda^{2}\) (same average with a birdnesting/loss allowance). Peebles’ \(0.158\lambda^{2}\) and the IET \(0.155\lambda^{2}\) sit in between. Cross-pol average \(0.057\lambda^{2}\) and circular \(0.11\lambda^{2}\) are orientation averages, not instantaneous draws.
- Effective dipole count is not the number manufactured. A simulator-scale mean is \(\sigma(f,t)=N_{\mathrm{eff}}(t)\,\sigma_{1}(f)\), with \(N_{\mathrm{eff}}=N\,\eta_{\mathrm{orient}}\,\eta_{\mathrm{pol}}\,\eta_{\mathrm{nest}}\,\eta_{\mathrm{screen}}\) and only the dipoles inside the resolution cell counted. \(\eta_{\mathrm{screen}}\to1\) only once typical spacing exceeds about \(2\lambda\). \(\eta_{\mathrm{nest}}<1\) while clumps persist. \(\sigma_{1}(f)\) is sharply peaked near \(L\approx0.46\lambda\)–\(0.50\lambda\) (end effects pull resonance low; \(0.48\lambda\) is the stated shop rule) and is small for a cut that is far from an odd number of half-wavelengths.
- Single-dipole speed matches the air on a timescale much shorter than bloom. The USAF text says “almost instantly.” The drop tower reached \(99\%\) of a \(3\,\mathrm{ft\,s^{-1}}\) terminal fall in \(0.25\,\mathrm{s}\) from rest. If drag is linear near terminal speed, the air-relative velocity decays as \(\exp(-gt/v_{t})\). For \(v_{t}=0.30\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\), \(\tau=v_{t}/g\approx0.03\,\mathrm{s}\). Quadratic drag at aircraft speed is stronger than that linear law, so the first bleed-down is faster, not slower. This \(\tau\) is an inference from the terminal-speed definition, not a separate horizontal measurement. A packed bundle or birdnest has a larger ballistic coefficient than one free fibre, so the clump can coast longer than \(\tau\) while it opens. Bloom time (RCS growth) and velocity-relaxation time are different clocks.
- After that transient, cloud-mean velocity is \(\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{wind}}+v_{\mathrm{fall}}\hat{\mathbf{z}}_{\mathrm{down}}\). Fall speed to carry as data, still air: glass microfibre about \(0.26\)–\(0.30\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\) near the surface (NSSL/USAF \(0.50\,\mathrm{kt}\); Butters/Bilkent \(0.30\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\); Jiusto-type \(0.25\)–\(0.26\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\) at \(4\)–\(6\,\mathrm{km}\)); foil and thicker nylon about \(0.4\)–\(0.6\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\) (Butters table). Altitude, wetting (\(0.80\,\mathrm{kt}\)), and vertical wind move the realized fall rate; the storm observations of \(0.36\)–\(1.88\,\mathrm{kt}\) are not terminal speeds.
- Monostatic range rate and Doppler of the cloud relative to the aircraft, once the dipole has joined the air mass:
  \[
  \Delta v_{r}=(\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{ac}}-\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{wind}}-v_{\mathrm{fall}}\hat{\mathbf{z}}_{\mathrm{down}})\cdot\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{\mathrm{los}},\qquad
  \Delta f_{d}=\frac{2\Delta v_{r}}{\lambda}.
  \]
  Range separation is the integral of \(\Delta v_{r}\). They share a Doppler bin only while \(|\Delta v_{r}|\) is inside the radar’s velocity resolution, and a range bin only while the integrated offset is inside \(c\tau/2\). A zero-Doppler notch removes the cloud only when the wind’s radial speed is inside that notch. The USAF sentence that chaff velocity “relative to the radar is zero” should not be coded literally.
- RCS versus time, as a cloud object rather than \(N\) fibres: \(\sigma(t)=\sigma_{\infty}(f)\,(1-e^{-t/\tau_{b}})\) until decay, with \(\sigma_{\infty}\) already the in-cell, polarization-weighted value. The handbook point used by Choi et al. is \(0.6\sigma_{\infty}\) at \(t=0.5\,\mathrm{s}\), which implies \(\tau_{b}\approx0.55\,\mathrm{s}\) if the exponential is forced through that one point (\(-\,0.5/\ln0.4\)). That \(\tau_{b}\) is an inference from one handbook point, not a universal constant. Naval seduction models in other reports use many seconds; early CFD of loose filaments shows tens of milliseconds. Aircraft self-protection bloom in the USAF text is “fast enough to finish inside the cell,” which is a requirement on \(\tau_{b}\), not a measured \(\tau_{b}\).
- Internal motion (shear, turbulence, differential fall) makes the return fluctuate about that mean and spreads Doppler around the wind mean. A single-scatterer cloud with a Swerling draw on \(\sigma(t)\) captures the mean power. A Gaussian Doppler width is the next term if the dwell resolves it. It is not required to integrate a million dipoles.

## What a radar model should do with chaff

### Takeaway

Chaff enters the radar as another scatterer: its own position, its own velocity (wind plus fall), and an RCS that depends on frequency, polarization, and time. Power adds, incoherently, into whatever range–Doppler–angle cell that state occupies. Seduction and distraction are two different relationships between that cell and the tracker’s gate, not two different RCS formulas.

### Cited Findings

- USAF training text: the resolution cell that the cloud must bloom inside is fixed by pulse width, horizontal beamwidth, vertical beamwidth, and range. Self-protection chaff is discussed as a false target that can take an automatic track only if that bloom happens in the cell and the RCS exceeds the aircraft. Against Doppler processing, chaff that has joined the air mass is rejected when its radial speed is not the target’s. **Qualitative, training text.** — [Electronic Warfare Fundamentals](https://archive.org/details/electronicwarfarefundamentals_201908)
- Open patent text, defining the words at gate level and not as an employment procedure: a seduction decoy is deployed after the missile has locked, is activated inside a track gate, and is given a larger radar cross section than the protected object so the gate can transfer. A distraction decoy is activated before lock-on. A confusion decoy is a more distant false target used to crowd search. — [US8223061](https://patentimages.storage.googleapis.com/80/91/a6/640e52baf204ce/US8223061.pdf)
- A conference note on naval seduction, indexed text: seduction chaff is for a threat already locked; wind carries the cloud through the missile’s range gate and can take the gate with it. — [du Plessis, Aardvark AOC 2014 slides](http://www.aardvarkaoc.co.za/wp-content/Proceedings/201405%20Aardvark%20AOC/JP.pdf)
- Schleher’s books were not opened. Choi et al. list Van Brunt, *Applied ECM*, vol. 1 (1978), among references for chaff, but the book itself was not opened. The gate definitions used here are the patent’s and the USAF resolution-cell description, not a page of Schleher.
- Statistical chaff return, unclassified DTIC abstract: a cloud of half-wave dipoles spaced so that coupling can be ignored; fluctuations come from dipole motion; the report derives first- and second-order field statistics and spectra of voltage, RCS, and phase, and discusses MTI. **Model.** The closed-form spectrum was not extracted. — [ADA013929](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/tr/pdf/ADA013929.pdf)
- Mature-cloud Doppler spectrum treated as Gaussian about the cloud’s radial velocity (indexed ADA151928, citing Nathanson). **Model.** — [ADA151928](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/tr/pdf/ADA151928.pdf)
- IOP towed-decoy paper, opened, states the two-source angle rule that also applies to a chaff puff still inside the beam: if the two received powers are equal, the angle-track point sits in the middle; if the decoy (or cloud) return is stronger, the track point is biased toward it. If the opening angle exceeds the seeker’s angle resolution, the seeker can separate the two in angle. — [Zhao and Wang, J. Phys.: Conf. Ser. 1982, 2021](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1742-6596/1982/1/012023/pdf)

### Inferences

- One radar dwell should evaluate every airborne object that falls in the beam, including the chaff cloud, with the monostatic radar equation at that object’s range and that object’s \(\sigma(f,t,\mathrm{pol})\). Received power from object \(i\) is proportional to \(\sigma_{i}/R_{i}^{4}\). Incoherent scatterers in the same range–Doppler cell add in power. The cloud is clutter-like because \(\sigma\) is an average over many random phases and should fluctuate (exponential power about the mean is the usual Rayleigh-voltage model; ADA013929 is the statistical justification, without a new formula here).
- Cell sizes the gate logic needs, from the quantities the USAF text names: range resolution \(\Delta R=c\tau/2\) for pulse length \(\tau\); cross-range \(R\theta\) for each beamwidth; Doppler resolution \(\Delta v=\lambda/(2T)\) for a coherent interval \(T\), or the tracker’s velocity-gate width if that is the actual window. These resolutions are radar properties. The cloud does not have a miss probability of its own.
- Distraction, as a gate fact: at the moment a track is started, the cloud’s range–Doppler–angle cell is not the aircraft’s cell. The acquisition logic can start on the cell with the larger return. There is no walk of an existing gate.
- Seduction, as a gate fact: the cloud is born inside the existing range and Doppler gate (and inside the beam). While both returns are inside the gate, the gate’s reported range and radial speed are the RCS-weighted centroids
  \[
  \hat{R}=\frac{\sum_{i}\sigma_{i}R_{i}}{\sum_{i}\sigma_{i}},\qquad
  \hat{v}_{r}=\frac{\sum_{i}\sigma_{i}v_{r,i}}{\sum_{i}\sigma_{i}}.
  \]
  If the gate recenters on \((\hat{R},\hat{v}_{r})\) and \(\sigma_{\mathrm{chaff}}>\sigma_{\mathrm{ac}}\), the center walks toward the cloud as geometry separates them. If separation carries the aircraft outside the gate before the centroid has moved onto the cloud, the gate stays with the aircraft. Angle, while both are in the beam, uses the same weights on angle instead of range (IOP statement for two sources).
- A Doppler gate already centered on the aircraft, with width smaller than \(\Delta f_{d}=2\Delta v_{r}/\lambda\), never contains the cloud. That is a physics result of the velocity model, not a tactic.

### Gaps

- Schleher’s and Van Brunt’s chapters were not opened, so their exact sentences on seduction versus distraction are not quoted. The patent and the USAF resolution-cell text are the opened substitutes.
- No opened source gave a universal numerical gate width. The simulator should take \(\tau\), beamwidths, and the Doppler window from the radar model.

## Flares: spectral radiant intensity on a drag trajectory

### Takeaway

A flare is a point mass under gravity and aerodynamic drag, with a time-varying spectral radiant intensity \(J(\lambda,t)\) in \(\mathrm{W\,sr^{-1}\,m^{-1}}\), or its integral over a seeker band in \(\mathrm{W\,sr^{-1}}\). It is not a dimensionless heat added to the aircraft. A hot continuum and a \(\mathrm{CO}_{2}\) plume do not produce the same in-band number, even when both are bright somewhere in the infrared.

### Cited Findings

- METU thesis, opened: flare motion is integrated with gravity and drag, explicitly not a vacuum free-fall parabola. Radiant output is a function of time because burn duration and rise time are finite. Rise time must be short enough that intensity is up before the flare leaves the seeker field of view, and is longer at higher altitude, where the flare also burns longer. The thesis’s performance statement of minimum burn time is the time until the flare is no longer in the field of view (a geometry criterion, citing their reference [9], not a measured cartridge duration). **Model structure and literature criteria inside the thesis.** — [METU M.Sc. thesis, 2013](https://open.metu.edu.tr/bitstream/handle/11511/23023/index.pdf)
- Same thesis, spectral contrast: conventional-flare peak radiance “about \(1\)–\(3\,\mu\mathrm{m}\)” because the burning metal is at \(2000\)–\(2200\,\mathrm{K}\); aircraft skin “approximately \(300\,\mathrm{K}\)” peaks at \(8\)–\(12\,\mu\mathrm{m}\). A dual-band seeker discriminates on the ratio of band radiances. Their imaging model forms an intensity centroid by weighting pixel positions by gray level and dividing by total intensity, then converts pixel offset to angle with the seeker field of view (their example detector is \(256\times256\) and one stated field of view is \(6^{\circ}\)). Spectral ratio in that model is long-wave over mid-wave. **Temperatures and band peaks are stated as literature fact in the thesis; the \(6^{\circ}\) field and the ratio threshold they later use are that simulator’s settings.** — [same METU thesis](https://open.metu.edu.tr/bitstream/handle/11511/23023/index.pdf)
- Same thesis, Wien’s law as printed: wavelength of maximum emission times temperature, equation (1.12), constant written \(2897.756\), \(\lambda\) in \(\mu\mathrm{m}\), \(T\) in kelvin. They also invoke Planck’s law and a spectral emissivity. **Standard radiation laws as used in the thesis.** — [same METU thesis](https://open.metu.edu.tr/bitstream/handle/11511/23023/index.pdf)
- USAF training text: a conventional cartridge (MJU-7 is the example) burns at \(2000\) to \(2200\,\mathrm{K}\). Rise time is time to peak intensity and “varies dramatically with altitude”: longer burn and longer rise at high altitude. Burn time is how long emission continues and therefore how long a simple seeker can be held off the aircraft. Most threats they discuss work in \(1\)–\(5\,\mu\mathrm{m}\); they state that in afterburner the aircraft’s infrared intensity peaks near \(1.5\,\mu\mathrm{m}\). The chemical name of the grain is in that text and is not repeated here. **Training text; temperatures and the \(1.5\,\mu\mathrm{m}\) afterburner peak are stated, not measured in the document.** — [Electronic Warfare Fundamentals, ch. 14–15](https://archive.org/details/electronicwarfarefundamentals_201908)
- NASA combustion-radiation report, indexed PDF: gases emit and absorb in discrete bands. \(\mathrm{CO}_{2}\) band centers listed at \(2.0\), \(2.7\), \(4.3\), \(9.4\), \(10.4\), and \(15.0\,\mu\mathrm{m}\). In their mid-infrared setup only the \(2.7\,\mu\mathrm{m}\) water band and the \(4.3\,\mu\mathrm{m}\) \(\mathrm{CO}_{2}\) band mattered, plus soot, which “emits a continuous spectrum.” Inside the combustor, \(\mathrm{CO}_{2}\) was treated as blackbody-like near \(4.45\,\mu\mathrm{m}\) (the band core at \(4.3\,\mu\mathrm{m}\) is eaten by atmospheric \(\mathrm{CO}_{2}\) on the path). Temperature was inverted from Planck’s law at that wavelength. **Measured flame spectra plus a band model.** This is a combustor, not an aircraft plume, but it is the spectral mechanism. — [NASA TM, 19810010595](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19810010595/downloads/19810010595.pdf)
- Open aircraft-exhaust radiation model (Chinese Journal of Aeronautics, 2017): skin/wall emission matters in both \(3\)–\(5\,\mu\mathrm{m}\) and \(8\)–\(14\,\mu\mathrm{m}\); gas emission matters mainly in \(3\)–\(5\,\mu\mathrm{m}\); in the plume, \(\mathrm{CO}_{2}\) outweighs water vapor. A companion rocket-plume paper lists \(\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O}\) at \(2.70\)–\(2.95\,\mu\mathrm{m}\), \(\mathrm{CO}_{2}\) at \(4.20\)–\(4.45\,\mu\mathrm{m}\), and \(\mathrm{CO}\) at \(4.45\)–\(4.84\,\mu\mathrm{m}\). **Models, with the rocket bands compared to measurements in that paper.** — [Chinese Journal of Aeronautics, aircraft exhaust, 2017](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1000936117300493); [rocket plume spectra, 2017](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1000936117300912)
- An ACM trajectory paper, indexed PDF text: drag \(F_{D}=\tfrac{1}{2}\rho V^{2}C_{D}A\), with \(A\) the area projected normal to the velocity. In that run, flares “decelerate rapidly” and separate from the aircraft; the illustrated run lasts \(5.4\,\mathrm{s}\) with a \(1\,\mathrm{s}\) dispense interval and a \(0.1\,\mathrm{s}\) step. **Model; no universal \(C_{D}\) or ejection speed in the extract.** — [ACM paper 2685617.2685687](https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.5555/2685617.2685687)
- Pyrophoric-decoy simulation report, indexed DTIC text: operational intensity has to be reached “in a fraction of a second”; “exact values” of time to peak “are difficult to find in the open literature” because they depend on composition and packing. They define peak radiant intensity of an isotropic source from power and area. **Model, and an explicit statement that open rise-time numbers are scarce.** — [ADA375218](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/tr/pdf/ADA375218.pdf)
- SPIE 2009 decoy comparison, indexed article text: in-band intensity means the \(3\)–\(5\,\mu\mathrm{m}\) integral; burn time is the interval between the two crossings of \(10\%\) of peak intensity. Static burns were smooth; dynamic (in flight) burns were dominated by the peak. **Measurement definition. The extract did not include a single peak in \(\mathrm{W\,sr^{-1}}\) that can be quoted without the figure.** — [SPIE 7483, 74830L](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/253268097_Comparison_of_the_emission_of_IR_decoy_flare_under_controlled_laboratory_and_on-field_conditions)
- Patent language for the shape of \(J(t)\), not a measured cartridge: \(t_{\mathrm{rise}}\) from \(10\%\) to \(90\%\) of peak, \(t_{\mathrm{peak}}\) above \(90\%\), \(t_{\mathrm{fall}}\) from \(90\%\) back to \(10\%\), with \(J\) in \(\mathrm{W\,sr^{-1}}\) in a named band. **A definition.** — [US6662700B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US6662700B2/en)

### Inferences

- Spectral radiant intensity of a small source that is close to a gray body of projected area \(A(t)\), emissivity \(\varepsilon(\lambda)\), and temperature \(T(t)\):
  \[
  J_{\lambda}(\lambda,t)=\varepsilon(\lambda)\,A(t)\,L_{\lambda}^{bb}(T(t)),\qquad
  L_{\lambda}^{bb}=\frac{2hc^{2}}{\lambda^{5}}\frac{1}{e^{hc/\lambda kT}-1}
  \]
  in \(\mathrm{W\,sr^{-1}\,m^{-1}}\), and
  \[
  J_{\mathrm{band}}(t)=\int_{\lambda_{1}}^{\lambda_{2}}J_{\lambda}(\lambda,t)\,d\lambda
  \]
  in \(\mathrm{W\,sr^{-1}}\). Irradiance at a seeker aperture, one source, is \(E=J_{\mathrm{band}}(t)\,\tau_{\mathrm{atm}}(\lambda)/R^{2}\) in \(\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}}\), with \(\tau_{\mathrm{atm}}\) inside the integral if the band is wide. METU’s Wien constant gives \(\lambda_{\max}T=2897.756\,\mu\mathrm{m\,K}\). At \(2100\,\mathrm{K}\), \(\lambda_{\max}\approx1.38\,\mu\mathrm{m}\), inside the \(1\)–\(3\,\mu\mathrm{m}\) peak they state. At \(300\,\mathrm{K}\), \(\lambda_{\max}\approx9.7\,\mu\mathrm{m}\), inside \(8\)–\(12\,\mu\mathrm{m}\).
- A plume is not that gray body. Its mid-infrared energy is concentrated in vibration–rotation bands, especially \(\mathrm{CO}_{2}\) near \(4.2\)–\(4.5\,\mu\mathrm{m}\), with a soot continuum only if soot is present (NASA). Atmospheric \(\mathrm{CO}_{2}\) removes the middle of the \(4.3\,\mu\mathrm{m}\) band, so a path that includes the atmosphere sees the band edges (the NASA measurement sat at \(4.45\,\mu\mathrm{m}\)) rather than a smooth Planck curve. A seeker band that is only the \(\mathrm{CO}_{2}\) slice, or a ratio of that slice to an adjacent continuum slice (METU’s MWIR/LWIR ratio is the same idea with wider bands), therefore returns a different number for a \(2000\)–\(2200\,\mathrm{K}\) continuum than for a plume of lower kinetic temperature whose \(3\)–\(5\,\mu\mathrm{m}\) energy is mostly the band. If the flare’s \(J_{\lambda}\) is negligible inside the seeker’s \([\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}]\), that seeker records \(E\approx0\) no matter how large \(J\) is in another band.
- Point-mass trajectory, the equation in the ACM paper plus gravity, which is what METU says it integrates:
  \[
  m\frac{d\mathbf{v}}{dt}=m\mathbf{g}-\tfrac{1}{2}\rho\,C_{D}A\,|\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{air}}|\,(\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{air}}).
  \]
  Initial \(\mathbf{v}\) is aircraft velocity plus an ejection velocity in body axes. Mass, \(C_{D}\), and \(A\) change if the grain is consumed; open sources say the aerodynamics are messy (asymmetric, burning, mass loss) and then use a constant-\(C_{D}\) point mass anyway. No opened source gave a standard ejection speed in \(\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\) or a standard \(C_{D}\). Both are input data.
- A usable \(J_{\mathrm{band}}(t)\) shape, marked as a model not a measurement: zero until an ignition delay, a rise, a plateau or peak, a fall. The \(10\%\)/\(90\%\) definitions (patent; SPIE uses \(10\%\) of peak for burn duration) are the right axes. Altitude lengthens rise and burn (METU and the USAF text). Dynamic flight emphasizes the peak relative to a static burn (SPIE). Open literature, by ADA375218’s own statement, does not support a single public rise time in milliseconds. “A fraction of a second” is the coarsest open claim.
- Orders of magnitude exist in historical test reports (hundreds to thousands of \(\mathrm{W\,sr^{-1}}\) in near- or mid-infrared bands, burns from a few seconds to about a minute). Those reports also give compositions. The compositions are omitted, and the intensities are not copied forward as a default, because they are device-specific and the open dynamic \(3\)–\(5\,\mu\mathrm{m}\) peak for a modern cartridge was not in a table that could be quoted cleanly. The simulator should take \(J_{\mathrm{band}}(t)\) as data.

### Gaps

- No opened source stated a generic ejection speed, cartridge mass, or \(C_{D}\). The ACM paper shows the drag law and that separation is rapid, without those numbers.
- No opened source gave a public \(J(t)\) table in \(\mathrm{W\,sr^{-1}}\) versus seconds for a current aircraft cartridge without also specifying a composition. ADA375218 says those rise times are scarce on purpose.
- The Infrared Handbook flare chapter was not opened.
- METU’s \(2000\)–\(2200\,\mathrm{K}\) and \(1\)–\(3\,\mu\mathrm{m}\) statement is about the flare continuum versus skin, not versus the \(4.3\,\mu\mathrm{m}\) plume band. The plume contrast is from the NASA and Chinese Journal of Aeronautics sources, not from METU.

## Spatial separation: one instantaneous field of view

### Takeaway

The seeker does not see “the aircraft plus a heat bonus.” It sees a set of sources, each with a line-of-sight angle and an in-band irradiance. Two sources collapse to one angle measurement when their angular separation is inside the instantaneous field of view (imaging or scanning) or, for a single-detector reticle, when both are still inside the total field that the reticle chops.

### Cited Findings

- Angular separation is ordinary geometry. METU converts a focal-plane centroid to an angle error proportional to (pixel offset from center) times (field of view) / (detector size). Their equation (2.14) is that scaling; the printed text is garbled by column extraction, but the surrounding sentences define \(\varepsilon\) as the error angle, FOV as the seeker field (\(6^{\circ}\) in that study), and \(X_{D},Y_{D}\) as the detector dimensions (\(256\times256\)). — [METU thesis](https://open.metu.edu.tr/bitstream/handle/11511/23023/index.pdf)
- Intensity centroid, same thesis, equation (2.12)–(2.13): horizontal and vertical pixel positions of a selected object are weighted by pixel intensity and divided by the total intensity of those pixels. Two objects that the segmenter has already split produce two centroids. — [same METU thesis](https://open.metu.edu.tr/bitstream/handle/11511/23023/index.pdf)
- Imaging seekers in that thesis are either scanning (a small instantaneous field of view swept across the total field) or staring (the whole field on a focal-plane array). Dual-band examples they list operate in MWIR combined with UV, SWIR, or LWIR. — [same METU thesis](https://open.metu.edu.tr/bitstream/handle/11511/23023/index.pdf)
- Spin-scan / chopper reticle: one detector, a patterned optic spun so that the image is interrupted periodically. Modulation carries the pointing error. Near boresight the on-axis image covers several spokes at once and the modulated signal collapses (the center null). An off-axis source, such as a separating flare, produces a large chopped signal while the aircraft in the null produces little. Con-scan spins the image over a fixed reticle so that a centered target is still modulated; a flare still adds a second modulation but does not silence the aircraft by parking it in a null. **Descriptive, secondary.** — [Wikipedia, Infrared homing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infrared_homing)
- NPS notes: a reticle seeker “locks onto the brightest infrared object in its field of view.” If the flare’s infrared radiant emission exceeds the target’s, “the seeker will follow the ejected flare.” **Textbook statement of the brightest-source rule for that class of seeker.** — [NPS infrared guidance notes](https://calhoun.nps.edu/bitstreams/8d630ca0-f4cf-48f1-b901-fb9fc7d695dc/download)

### Inferences

- From the seeker at \(\mathbf{p}_{m}\), with sources at \(\mathbf{p}_{a}\) and \(\mathbf{p}_{f}\),
  \[
  \hat{\mathbf{u}}_{a}=\frac{\mathbf{p}_{a}-\mathbf{p}_{m}}{|\mathbf{p}_{a}-\mathbf{p}_{m}|},\quad
  \hat{\mathbf{u}}_{f}=\frac{\mathbf{p}_{f}-\mathbf{p}_{m}}{|\mathbf{p}_{f}-\mathbf{p}_{m}|},\quad
  \psi=\arccos(\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{a}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{f}).
  \]
  For small \(\psi\), \(\psi\approx|\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{a}-\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{f}|\).
- One imaging sample treats them as one blob when \(\psi\) is less than the instantaneous field of view (one detector element, or one scan resolution cell) and both fall inside the total field. The measured angle is then the irradiance centroid, which is METU’s intensity centroid applied to two unresolved sources:
  \[
  \hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}=\frac{E_{a}\boldsymbol{\theta}_{a}+E_{f}\boldsymbol{\theta}_{f}}{E_{a}+E_{f}},\qquad
  E_{i}=\frac{J_{i,\mathrm{band}}\,\tau_{i}}{R_{i}^{2}}.
  \]
  Use \(E\), not \(J\), because the two ranges differ. \(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\) lies on the segment joining the two angles, closer to the larger \(E\).
- A reticle has one detector. There is no spatial pixel. Both sources contribute for as long as both are inside the total field of view. The detector current is the sum of the chopped irradiances. Spin-scan’s center null means a source’s contribution to the error signal is not proportional to \(E\) alone: a bright source on boresight can contribute less AC than a dimmer source off axis (Wikipedia). Con-scan does not have that null. The NPS “brightest source” rule is the right first model only away from the null.
- Once \(\psi\) exceeds the resolution (IFOV for imaging, total field or track gate for a reticle), the seeker has two angles. What it steers on is then a data-association choice, not a radiometric identity.

### Gaps

- A full modulation equation for a rising-sun or wagon-wheel reticle (METU names those patterns) was not extracted as a usable formula. The center-null behavior is cited from Wikipedia, not from a re-derived optical integral.
- No opened source gave a universal IFOV in milliradians. METU’s \(6^{\circ}\) total field is one thesis’s input.

## Multiple sources and walk-off

### Takeaway

Whether the track point follows the aircraft, the flare, or a point between them is fixed by the in-band irradiance ratio, the angular separation rate, and whether both sources are still inside the field or gate that the tracker is allowed to use. A fast rise or a wrong band ratio can make an advanced tracker reject the brighter source; a simple centroid or reticle will not.

### Cited Findings

- METU: if the seeker cannot tell flare from target, it locks the flare because the flare radiates more. Spectral flares are described as radiatively matched to the aircraft and therefore not removed by a band-ratio test that removes a conventional flare. Their identification uses, among other things, instantaneous separation rate, intensity rise time, area, and the dual-band ratio. The kinematic gate keeps a source only if its new angle lies near the predicted angle. **This is a model of an imaging seeker with counter-countermeasures, and it states the physical observables.** — [METU thesis](https://open.metu.edu.tr/bitstream/handle/11511/23023/index.pdf)
- NPS: the non-imaging seeker follows the ejected flare when the flare’s radiant emission exceeds the target’s. — [NPS notes](https://calhoun.nps.edu/bitstreams/8d630ca0-f4cf-48f1-b901-fb9fc7d695dc/download)
- USAF training text: rise time and burn time set how quickly and for how long the flare can hold a simple seeker; the same text notes that some seekers treat a very fast rise as the signature of a flare. **Training text.** — [Electronic Warfare Fundamentals](https://archive.org/details/electronicwarfarefundamentals_201908)
- Wikipedia: spin-scan is particularly easy to pull off because the aircraft sits in the center null while the departing flare’s modulation grows. Con-scan still sees the centered aircraft. — [Wikipedia, Infrared homing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infrared_homing)

### Inferences

- While both sources share one measurement, \(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\) above moves toward the flare at a rate set by \(E_{f}/E_{a}\) and by \(d\psi/dt\). If \(E_{f}\gg E_{a}\), \(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\approx\boldsymbol{\theta}_{f}\) and the tracker walks off with the flare. If \(E_{f}\ll E_{a}\), it stays on the aircraft. If the ratio is of order one, it tracks a centroid and the missile flies between them. The angular rate matters because the gate or the field is finite: separation that outruns the gate leaves only one source inside, which is a different outcome from a slow drift of the centroid.
- The quantities that decide the branch, all physical, are: in-band \(E_{f}/E_{a}\) (so \(J_{\mathrm{band}}\), range, and atmospheric transmission); \(d\psi/dt\) from the two trajectories and the seeker motion; the field or gate half-angle; and, for a reticle, the modulation weight (center null versus con-scan). Rise time enters twice: it sets \(E_{f}(t)\) for a simple tracker, and it is itself a discriminant for a tracker that rejects a fast rise (USAF text; METU lists rise time among observables).
- Spectral walk-off fails closed when \(J\) in the seeker’s band is not the \(J\) that is large. A continuum that is bright at \(1\)–\(3\,\mu\mathrm{m}\) and a plume that is bright at \(4.3\,\mu\mathrm{m}\) can have any ratio inside a narrow band, including a ratio that favors the aircraft.

### Gaps

- No opened paper gave a numerical critical ratio (for example “\(2:1\) walks off a centroid tracker”) that is independent of field of view and kinematics. The ratio and the angle have to be integrated together.
- METU’s specific gates (their spectral threshold and pixel-fraction tests) are that model’s knobs. They are not physical constants and are not repeated here as recommended settings.

## Towed radar decoy

### Takeaway

A towed decoy is a second radar source on a line of known length. It has its own angle, its own range, and a Doppler set by the velocity of the body on the end of the line (passive reflector) or by the waveform it retransmits (active repeater). While the line still subtends less than a beamwidth and less than a range cell, the tracker sees one biased centroid, not two targets.

### Cited Findings

- IOP paper, opened: the decoy is connected by a tow line and, with the aircraft, forms a dual point source inside the seeker’s instantaneous beam. If the decoy’s equivalent interfering power equals the aircraft echo, the angle-track point is midway between them and the authors’ design note says the line should then be longer than twice a warhead kill radius (that kill-radius rule is their design criterion, not a law of electromagnetics). If the decoy is stronger, the track point moves toward the decoy and the line can be shorter. If the angle exceeds the seeker’s resolution, the seeker can separate them in angle, so the line also has an upper geometric bound. Their extracted upper-bound formula is \(L=R\,\theta/\sin(\alpha-\theta)\), with \(R\) the missile-to-decoy range, \(\theta\) the beamwidth, and \(\alpha\) the aspect angle in their figure; the PDF text around the sine is badly broken, so the clean small-angle form is the inference below. They state usual tow lengths of \(90\)–\(120\,\mathrm{m}\) for a transport and about \(100\,\mathrm{m}\) for a fighter, cited to their reference [8]. **Those lengths are reported practice, not a derived optimum. The equal-power midpoint is the radar statement to keep.** — [Zhao and Wang, 2021](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1742-6596/1982/1/012023/pdf)
- Same paper: an active decoy can be made to imitate speed and heading, so a seeker that only compares motion may not split the two. That is a statement about a repeater’s waveform, not about the kinematics of the tow body. — [same IOP paper](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1742-6596/1982/1/012023/pdf)
- A kinematic-model article, indexed text: with tow chord \(L\) and orientation \(\phi_{L}\), \(\mathbf{R}_{MD}=\mathbf{R}_{MT}+\mathbf{L}\). A line of about \(150\,\mathrm{m}\) is still one point source when \(L/R_{MT}\) is much smaller than the beamwidth; they illustrate \(0.15\,\mathrm{rad}\) (\(\approx8^{\circ}\)). **Model geometry.** — [Journal of Electronic Defense / Free Library summary](https://www.thefreelibrary.com/Towed-decoy+effectiveness%3a+a+kinematic+model.-a08916227)

### Inferences

- State in the simulator: aircraft position and velocity, line length \(L\), and a unit vector along the line in body or wind axes (a taut line lies near \(-\hat{\mathbf{v}}\), with sag as an optional angle). Decoy position is \(\mathbf{p}_{d}=\mathbf{p}_{a}-L\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{\mathrm{tow}}\). If \(L\) is fixed and the line is straight, \(\mathbf{v}_{d}=\mathbf{v}_{a}\).
- Passive signature: an RCS \(\sigma_{d}(f,\mathrm{aspect})\) at \(\mathbf{p}_{d}\), Doppler \(f_{d}=2(\mathbf{v}_{d}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{d})/\lambda\) using the line of sight to the decoy, not to the aircraft. Even with \(\mathbf{v}_{d}=\mathbf{v}_{a}\),
  \[
  \Delta f_{d}=\frac{2}{\lambda}\mathbf{v}_{a}\cdot(\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{a}-\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{d})
  \]
  is nonzero because the sightlines differ. At long range the sightline angle is \(\psi\approx L_{\perp}/R\), so \(\Delta f_{d}\) is small. Range difference is the component of the tow vector along the sightline, \(\Delta R\approx\mathbf{L}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{u}}\), which can be a large fraction of \(L\) and can exceed \(c\tau/2\) while the angle is still unresolved.
- Active signature: the source position is still \(\mathbf{p}_{d}\), but the amplitude is set by radiated power (effective radiated power in the seeker’s direction) and the Doppler is whatever the repeater puts on the waveform. The IOP paper’s “simulate the speed” clause is this second case. The simulator should not invent a repeater technique; it only needs a position, a power, and a Doppler offset as data.
- Unresolved pair: if \(\psi<\theta_{\mathrm{beam}}\) and \(|\Delta R|\) is inside one range cell and \(|\Delta f_{d}|\) is inside one Doppler cell, the tracker’s angle (and range, if both fall in the gate) is the power-weighted centroid. Equal power puts it halfway along the line (IOP). The decoy has not “left” the cell; it has only shifted the aim point by a fraction of \(L\).
- Resolved pair: when \(\psi>\theta_{\mathrm{beam}}\), or \(|\Delta R|\) exceeds the range cell, the two sources are separate measurements. Which one the gate follows is the same seduction-versus-original-target question as for chaff.

### Gaps

- The IOP trig expression for maximum line length is not reliable in the extracted PDF. The small-angle test \(\psi\approx L_{\perp}/R<\theta\) is the geometry actually justified by their prose.
- No opened source gave a passive towed-body RCS versus frequency that is safe to treat as generic. \(\sigma_{d}(f)\) or ERP should be input data. Corner-reflector patents exist; they specify particular bodies and are not used as a default RCS.
- Sag, cable drag, and the difference between chord length and arc length were not given a validated formula in an opened paper. A straight chord of length \(L\) is the model the kinematic article uses.

## Dispenser interface

### Takeaway

The aircraft object should not embed a flare’s burn curve or a chaff cloud’s bloom. It should emit a list of release events. Each event carries direction in body axes, ejection speed, and a payload type whose signature is a function of time since release.

### Cited Findings

- METU’s countermeasure-dispenser model, as a software interface rather than a procedure: dispense time, which dispenser, dispensing velocity, dispensing angle, and the flare’s body-axis position at release. They note real installations as several dispensers at fixed angles on the airframe; the angles and the timing are inputs. Flare infrared output is a separate time function. — [METU thesis, ch. 5](https://open.metu.edu.tr/bitstream/handle/11511/23023/index.pdf)
- USAF text: bloom rate depends on where the dispenser sits in the wake, and on how many bundles are released, because that sets in-cell RCS. Dispense rate is discussed as the control that sets cloud RCS relative to aircraft RCS. **The dependence is physical; the text’s employment advice is not carried forward.** — [Electronic Warfare Fundamentals](https://archive.org/details/electronicwarfarefundamentals_201908)
- Choi et al. treat bloom fraction versus time and \(\sigma=0.17N\lambda^{2}\) as properties of the cartridge and the frequency, and the aircraft’s motion as what carries the cloud through a cell. — [Choi et al., 2023](https://ojs.imeti.org/index.php/PETI/article/view/11150)

### Inferences

- Data on the aircraft, not code in the flare class: a set of stations, each with a body-axis direction \(\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{b}\), an ejection speed \(v_{e}\) (metres per second), a payload identifier, and a release program that is only a list of times or an interval plus a count. At release \(k\),
  \[
  \mathbf{v}(0)=\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{ac}}+v_{e}\,R_{\mathrm{body}\to\mathrm{world}}\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{b},\qquad
  \mathbf{p}(0)=\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{station}}.
  \]
- Payload data, chaff: cut lengths (or one length per bundle), \(N\) per cut, optional \(\eta\) factors, \(v_{\mathrm{fall}}\), bloom time constant or a table \(\sigma(t)/\sigma_{\infty}\), and polarization. \(\sigma(f,t)\) is then computed as in the chaff section. Do not store a single RCS.
- Payload data, flare: \(m\), \(C_{D}\), \(A\), and \(J_{\mathrm{band}}(t)\) or \(T(t)\), \(\varepsilon(\lambda)\), \(A_{\mathrm{rad}}(t)\) per band the seekers use. Ignition delay, rise, peak, and tail are parameters of \(J(t)\), in seconds and \(\mathrm{W\,sr^{-1}}\).
- Interval and count only create more objects. They do not change the equation of one object. Overlapping blooms add as separate clouds until a model explicitly merges them; power still sums in a shared cell.

### Gaps

- No opened source provided a standard body-axis angle set or a standard \(v_{e}\). METU treats both as inputs. Hard-coding either would invent a munition.

## Failure cases the physics should produce

### Takeaway

The same objects already fail in four ways that do not require a special “miss probability”: the dipole is the wrong length for the radar frequency, the flare’s spectrum misses the seeker band, the second source never leaves the resolution cell, or the cloud leaves the beam.

### Cited Findings

- Wrong length: measured dipole peaks track \(0.86\lambda^{2}\) only near resonance, and resonance is not exactly \(c/(2L)\) (Balleri). A full-wave wire has a broadside null in the mono-static model (IOP materials paper). A modeled S-band comparison of two cut-length families differed by \(35\,\mathrm{dB}\) of reflectivity (JAMC, citing Arnott). Cross-polarized average \(0.057\lambda^{2}\) versus co-polarized \(0.17\lambda^{2}\) (Stellenbosch) is the polarization version of the same failure. Birdnesting and spacing below \(2\lambda\) cut \(N_{\mathrm{eff}}\) (Bilkent).
- Wrong spectrum: conventional flare peaks near \(1\)–\(3\,\mu\mathrm{m}\) at \(2000\)–\(2200\,\mathrm{K}\) while \(300\,\mathrm{K}\) skin peaks near \(8\)–\(12\,\mu\mathrm{m}\) (METU); plume gas emission is banded, dominated by \(\mathrm{CO}_{2}\) near \(4.3\,\mu\mathrm{m}\) in the mid-infrared (NASA; Chinese Journal of Aeronautics). A band integral outside the flare’s \(J_{\lambda}\) is zero.
- Never leaves the cell: both the USAF resolution-cell description and the IOP two-source geometry say that separation smaller than the range cell, the Doppler bin, and the beam produces one centroid, not two tracks. Equal power puts the angle halfway between the sources (IOP).
- Leaves the beam: fall speeds of order \(0.3\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\) (NOAA, Butters/Bilkent) integrated in time move the cloud out of a narrow elevation beam; horizontal wind moves it out of azimuth. The USAF text requires the bloom to finish while the cloud is still in the cell; a slow bloom or a fast aircraft-to-wind radial speed does the opposite. Doppler processing drops a cloud whose radial speed is not the target’s once the dipole has joined the air mass (USAF text, with the wind-radial caveat in the chaff section).

### Inferences

- Code these as consequences of \(\sigma(f)\), \(J_{\mathrm{band}}(t)\), and the two trajectories. Do not add a random miss draw on top.
- Numeric illustration, inference only: glass chaff at \(v_{t}=0.3\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\), seeker beam \(1^{\circ}\) full width (\(0.017\,\mathrm{rad}\)), range \(2\,\mathrm{km}\). The beam is about \(35\,\mathrm{m}\) across. From the beam center, pure fall takes about \(60\,\mathrm{s}\) to reach the edge. Over the same minute an aircraft at \(200\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\) relative to the wind is \(12\,\mathrm{km}\) away along track. Falling out of the beam is slow; flying out of the same cell in range and angle is fast. A cloud released with the wrong Doppler relative to a narrow velocity gate is excluded in one dwell, with no wait for geometry.
- A flare whose \(J_{\mathrm{band}}=0\) in the seeker’s band never enters the irradiance sum. A flare that is bright but already outside the field of view when \(J\) peaks never enters either (METU’s rise-time criterion).
- A tow line that is short compared with \(c\tau/2\) and compared with \(R\theta\) never produces a second detection. It only slides the centroid along the line by \(\sigma_{d}/(\sigma_{a}+\sigma_{d})\) of the line’s subtended angle.

### Gaps

- “Out of the beam” depends on whether the beam is steering after the aircraft, staring, or tracking the centroid. That is radar-model state, not a chaff property. The cloud’s motion is still the wind plus fall.

## Implementation notes for missilesim

### Takeaway

Add three entities — chaff cloud, flare, towed decoy — plus a dispenser that only spawns them. Each entity has a trajectory and a signature function of time and frequency or wavelength. A radar dwell sums radar-equation returns by range–Doppler cell. An infrared dwell sums band irradiances and collapses angles only when the sources share an instantaneous field of view.

### Cited Findings

- Current flare, from `src/objects/Flare.h` and `src/objects/Flare.cpp` only. `FlareLaunchRequest` is one world-frame event: position, velocity, `mass` default \(0.9\,\mathrm{kg}\), `dragCoefficient` default \(1.1\), `crossSectionalArea` default \(0.018\,\mathrm{m}^{2}\), `lifetime` default \(4.0\,\mathrm{s}\), `heatSignature` default \(5.0\), `heatDecayRate` default \(1.4\). `Flare::update` calls `PhysicsObject::update` and then
  \[
  H\leftarrow H\exp(-r\,\Delta t)
  \]
  with \(r=\) `heatDecayRate`. The object goes inactive when lifetime reaches zero or \(H\le0.01\). There is no rise, no wavelength, no band, no dispenser count or body-axis direction, and no unit on `heatSignature`. The drag law itself is not in these two files; only \(C_{D}\) and area are stored for the base class.
- Signature and trajectory facts used to replace that model are the ones cited above: \(\sigma_{1}\approx0.15\lambda^{2}\)–\(0.17\lambda^{2}\), bloom as a time function, fall about \(0.26\)–\(0.6\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\) by type, \(J\) in \(\mathrm{W\,sr^{-1}}\) with a rise and a spectrum, centroid \(\sum E\theta/\sum E\), tow line as a second source.

### Inferences

- **Entities.** `ChaffCloud`: release position and velocity; wind velocity (from the atmosphere model); \(v_{\mathrm{fall}}\); dipole length or a list of lengths; \(N\); \(\eta\) factors or a single effectiveness; bloom function; polarization flag. Mean position integrates wind plus fall after a short \(\tau\) (or instantly, which matches the USAF wording). Mean \(\sigma(f,t)=N_{\mathrm{eff}}(t)\,\sigma_{1}(f)\) with \(\sigma_{1}=0.17\lambda^{2}\) or \(0.15\lambda^{2}\) at resonance and a resonance curve, or a table, off resonance. `Flare`: keep the point mass (\(m\), \(C_{D}\), \(A\), initial world velocity) and replace `heatSignature` with \(J_{b}(t)\) per seeker band \(b\). `TowedDecoy`: attached to an aircraft id, length \(L\), body-axis tow direction, passive \(\sigma(f)\) and/or active power and Doppler offset. It is not expendable and is not spawned by the interval/count list.
- **Dispenser data, not a program.** Per station: body-axis direction, ejection speed, payload id. Per release command: time, station, count, interval. The command writes initial conditions and a payload; it does not score a miss.
- **Time-varying signatures.** Chaff: \(\sigma(f,t)=\sigma_{\infty}(f)\,(1-e^{-t/\tau_{b}})\) for \(t\) less than a persistence time, then optional decay as the cloud leaves the cell or the user table says so. \(\sigma_{\infty}(f)\) is zero, or the off-resonance model, when \(2L/\lambda\) is far from \(1\). Flare: \(J_{b}(t)=0\) until ignition delay, then rise, peak, fall, in \(\mathrm{W\,sr^{-1}}\). Bands with no emission stay zero. Do not decay a scalar from \(t=0\) and call it heat; the current exponential starts at its maximum and has no spectrum, so it cannot miss a band and cannot arrive late.
- **Radar dwell.** For each dwell at frequency \(f\) and polarization \(p\), for each of aircraft, chaff clouds, and towed decoy in the beam: compute range, range rate, and \(\sigma\) or active power. Bin by \(\Delta R=c\tau/2\) and by the Doppler window. Sum powers in a bin. If a tracker gate is active, its reported range, range rate, and angle are the \(\sigma\)-weighted centroids of whatever fell inside the gate. A new cloud in a different bin can only steal a track if acquisition runs again (distraction). A cloud born inside the gate moves the centroid (seduction) only while \(\sigma_{\mathrm{cloud}}\) is competitive and the geometry has not yet carried one of them outside the gate. A decoy with \(\mathbf{v}_{d}=\mathbf{v}_{a}\) still gets its own bin when \(\mathbf{L}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{u}}\) exceeds \(\Delta R\), and its own angle when \(L_{\perp}/R\) exceeds the beam.
- **Infrared dwell.** For each source, \(E_{b}=J_{b}(t)\,\tau_{\mathrm{atm},b}/R^{2}\). Build the list of angles. Merge two entries when \(\psi\) is inside the IFOV (imaging) or when both are inside the reticle field (one detector). Merged angle \(=\sum E\boldsymbol{\theta}/\sum E\). A spin-scan model should additionally suppress the AC contribution of a source inside the center null; otherwise it behaves like a centroid tracker and will not reproduce the null. The summed \(E\) in each band is also the input to a ratio test. Sources with \(E_{b}=0\) are absent.
- **What the current flare will do if left as it is.** With the defaults \(m=0.9\,\mathrm{kg}\), \(C_{D}=1.1\), \(A=0.018\,\mathrm{m}^{2}\), a quadratic drag law at \(\rho=1.225\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}\) implies a sea-level terminal speed near \(27\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\) (from \(mg=\tfrac{1}{2}\rho C_{D}A v^{2}\)). That number is an inference from the code defaults, not a measured flare, and `PhysicsObject` was not read, so the drag law is only the interface those two getters imply. Heat starts at \(5\) and is \(5e^{-1.4t}\), gone by \(4\,\mathrm{s}\) or when the scalar drops below \(0.01\). Any seeker that treats this scalar as in-band irradiance will see a source that is brightest at release, identical in every band, and unable to fall out of a band it was never given.
- **Failure cases fall out of the functions.** Off-resonance cut: \(\sigma_{1}(f)\) small, cloud never exceeds aircraft in the cell. Wrong band: \(J_{b}=0\), irradiance sum ignores the flare. Short tow or slow separation: both returns stay in one cell, centroid shifts by a fraction of the separation, no second track. Cloud versus beam: integrate wind and fall; if the resulting angle leaves the beam, drop that dwell’s return. None of these is a random number.

### Gaps

- `PhysicsObject::update` was not read, by the constraint to use only `Flare.cpp` and `Flare.h`. The drag equation attributed to the current flare is the standard quadratic form implied by storing \(C_{D}\) and area, and should be confirmed before relying on the \(27\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\) figure.
- Default numerical \(J(t)\) and default \(\tau_{b}\) are not provided, because open sources do not support a single public cartridge curve. They belong in data, next to mass and \(C_{D}\).
