# European infrared air-to-air missiles (Fox 2)

Scope is the air-to-air IR rounds named in the task, as of 28 September 2026. Every figure below was taken from a page opened for this note. Tags are [published], [derived], or [unknown]. Nothing here was derived, because no source pair gave both thrust and burn time (or total impulse) for the same motor. MICA EM, Meteor, Skyflash, Super 530, and AMRAAM are not dossier subjects. MICA EM numbers appear only where a source uses them to bound MICA IR. MICA NG appears only to show that dual-pulse propulsion is the next missile, not the fielded MICA IR.

Live MBDA product pages publish mass, length, and diameter, and almost no kinematic envelope. Secondary compilations (Wikipedia, designation-systems.net, Airforce Technology) fill the gaps and often disagree. Those disagreements are left standing.

## R.550 Magic I

### Takeaway
Magic I is a French Matra rear-aspect, canard-controlled, single-stage solid IR missile with a cooled lead-sulfide seeker. Service began in the mid-1970s. A 2026 front-line inventory was not documented; the English Wikipedia treats Magic 1 as a former fit on several export fleets, while Magic II was still the in-service round India was replacing in 2026.

### Cited Findings
- Designation R.550 Magic / Magic Mk 1, also expanded Missile Auto-Guidé Interception et Combat. Country: France. Manufacturer: Matra (later MBDA France). [published] — [French Wikipedia, Matra R550 Magic](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matra_R550_Magic)
- Development from 1968 to compete with AIM-9. First firing 11 January 1972 from a Gloster Meteor at CEV against a CT.20. Series production from 1976 for Mirage III, Mirage F1, then Mirage 2000. French naval fit on F-8 Crusader and Super Étendard. [published] — [French Wikipedia, Matra R550 Magic](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matra_R550_Magic)
- Infobox service dates on that page: 1975 (Magic) then 1986 (Magic II). [published] — [French Wikipedia, Matra R550 Magic](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matra_R550_Magic)
- English Wikipedia infobox instead says in service 1968 (Magic) and 1986 (Magic 2). The article carries a "needs more citations" banner. 1968 matches the French development start, not the French production date. [published, conflicting] — [English Wikipedia, R.550 Magic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R.550_Magic)
- Magic Mk 1 can attack any non-frontal target inside a 140° area, below 18,000 m (limitations above that ceiling). In close combat it is effective from about 300 m and can be fired from an aircraft flying at more than 1,300 km/h and manoeuvring at 6 g. [published] — [French Wikipedia, Matra R550 Magic](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matra_R550_Magic)
- Seeker: SAT AD3601, lead sulfide (PbS), cooled by liquid nitrogen. Infrared, band I. Magic 1 can only be fired from the rear. Transparent nose dome. Magic 2 replaced AD3601 with the all-aspect AD3633 and has an opaque dome. [published] — [French Wikipedia, Matra R550 Magic](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matra_R550_Magic); [English Wikipedia, R.550 Magic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R.550_Magic)
- English article, after describing Magic 2: seeker gimbal limit 30° off boresight (half-angle, not a full angle). The sentence is not explicitly limited to one variant. [published, variant not split] — [English Wikipedia, R.550 Magic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R.550_Magic)
- Same article: cueing before launch from the aircraft radar, a helmet-mounted designation system, an optoelectronic sight, or by pointing and uncaging. Lead-sulfide homing is described as sensitive and resistant to thermal clutter. The paragraph also says "all-aspect," which conflicts with the rear-aspect limit it states for Magic 1. [published, internally inconsistent] — [English Wikipedia, R.550 Magic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R.550_Magic)
- Motor: SNPE composite single-stage rocket. [published] — [French Wikipedia, Matra R550 Magic](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matra_R550_Magic)
- English infobox: "Richard single-stage butylene solid-propellant rocket." The body text of the same article says "It has a Romeo" motor and then "Richard single-stage butylene," and assigns Mach 3 to Magic 1 and Mach 2 to Magic 2. [published, internally inconsistent] — [English Wikipedia, R.550 Magic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R.550_Magic)
- French infobox, not split by variant: launch mass 89 kg; length 2.75 m; body diameter 0.157 m; span 0.66 m; speed Mach 3; range 0.3 to 15 km; warhead 12.5 kg; guidance infrared. [published] — [French Wikipedia, Matra R550 Magic](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matra_R550_Magic)
- English infobox: mass 89 kg; length 2.72 m and a second length field "2.75 Meters"; diameter 157 mm; wingspan 0.66 m; pre-fragmented HE warhead 12.7 kg; contact and RF proximity fuzing; range 10 km (Magic 1) and 20 km (Magic 2); ceiling 18,000 m; Mach 3 (Magic 1) and Mach 2 (Magic 2). [published, conflicts with the French table and with other sentences on the same page] — [English Wikipedia, R.550 Magic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R.550_Magic)
- English body: 13 kg pre-fragmented controlled-splinter warhead, stated as 14.6% of launch mass; proximity fuze; armed 1.8 s after launch; minimum weapon-employment zone 0.3 km; self-destruct after 26 s if it misses. [published] — [English Wikipedia, R.550 Magic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R.550_Magic)
- Control: four fixed fins, four movable fins immediately behind them, four notched tail fins. Canard motion would roll the tail, so the tail fins are free to spin on bearings. No thrust-vectoring is described. English body: Magic 1 can withstand 35 g and Magic 2 50 g after launch. [published] — [English Wikipedia, R.550 Magic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R.550_Magic)
- French text: in-flight guidance by cruciform canard surfaces. [published] — [French Wikipedia, Matra R550 Magic](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matra_R550_Magic)
- English body: "no minimum launch speed," including as a helicopter armament. [published, thinly sourced] — [English Wikipedia, R.550 Magic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R.550_Magic)
- English Wikipedia lists former Magic 1 operators including Argentina (Mirage IIIEA, Dagger, Mirage 5P Mara, Super Étendard) and Australia (Mirage IIIO). It still lists at least one current user with "Magic 1 and 2 on Mirage 2000," but the country name is a flag image and was not recoverable as text. [published] — [English Wikipedia, R.550 Magic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R.550_Magic)
- Propellant mass, burn time, thrust, total impulse, seeker track rate, acquisition range in km, TVC angle, lethal radius, and a stated IRCCM technique beyond "immunity to thermal clutter": [unknown] on pages opened.

### Inferences
- The 0.3 km minimum in the English article is presented as the result of the 1.8 s arming delay. That is the article's statement, not an independent derivation.
- Rail launch from a fighter hardpoint or wingtip is the described carriage. Release from a moving aircraft means the missile inherits shooter velocity. That inheritance is not given as a separate specification.
- Canard control without TVC implies little steering authority at very low dynamic pressure. The "no minimum launch speed" sentence does not quantify post-launch control at low speed, so low-speed steering stays [unknown].
- Mach 2 for Magic 2 on the English page is not used as a Magic I figure and is not reconciled with the French Mach 3 table. Magic I's only opened speed figure that is explicitly Magic 1 is Mach 3, with no altitude or launch-speed condition.

### Gaps
- No opened manufacturer datasheet. French and English tables disagree on length (2.75 m vs 2.72 m), warhead (12.5 kg vs 12.7 kg vs a 13 kg body sentence), range (0.3–15 km combined vs 10 km for Magic 1), and motor name (SNPE composite vs Romeo vs Richard butylene).
- Burn time and thrust were not on any page whose body rendered. A GlobalSecurity specifications table and a Forecast International extract seen in search did not render as readable page text when opened, so their 2.2 s / impulse figures are not used.
- Whether any air force still holds live Magic I rounds in 2026 was not established. The round is a legacy rear-aspect weapon relative to Magic II and MICA IR.
- Seeker cooling is liquid nitrogen for AD3601 [published]. Detector is a single PbS element class, not an imaging array [published]. Off-boresight gimbal 30° may or may not be the Magic I head. Rear-aspect acquisition range in km: [unknown]. Head-on acquisition: not a Magic I capability [published]. Lock-on after launch: [unknown], and not described. Helmet cue: claimed only in the mixed English description.

## R.550 Magic II

### Takeaway
Magic II is the 1986 all-aspect development of Magic I: a more sensitive seeker (AD3633) and a motor described as about 10% more powerful. It was still an in-service Indian Air Force weapon being replaced by ASRAAM as of April 2026. It is not an imaging-IR or thrust-vector missile.

### Cited Findings
- From 1986, Magic was replaced by Matra R550 Magic II, with a more capable seeker and a motor 10% more powerful. [published] — [French Wikipedia, Matra R550 Magic](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matra_R550_Magic)
- The improved Magic II seeker has a filter that separates engine heat from the ionisation associated with afterburner use, which had been causing the missile to detonate early. [published] — [French Wikipedia, Matra R550 Magic](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matra_R550_Magic)
- Magic 2 seeker is the all-aspect AD3633, replacing AD3601. Opaque dome, versus Magic 1's transparent dome. Shorter preparation time before launch. [published] — [English Wikipedia, R.550 Magic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R.550_Magic)
- English Wikipedia: in service 1986; Magic 2 range 20 km; Magic 2 speed Mach 2; Magic 2 airframe "capable of withstanding" 50 g. Gimbal limit 30° off boresight is stated in that paragraph. [published, and the Mach 2 figure conflicts with the unsplit French Mach 3 table] — [English Wikipedia, R.550 Magic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R.550_Magic)
- French unsplit table still applies on that page: 89 kg, 2.75 m, diameter 0.157 m, span 0.66 m, warhead 12.5 kg, range band 0.3–15 km, Mach 3. It is not labelled Magic II only. [published] — [French Wikipedia, Matra R550 Magic](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matra_R550_Magic)
- Same control arrangement as Magic I on the English description: fixed fins, movable canards behind them, free-to-spin notched tail fins. No TVC. [published] — [English Wikipedia, R.550 Magic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R.550_Magic)
- Around April 2026 the Indian Air Force issued requests for proposals to integrate ASRAAM on MiG-29UPG and Jaguar, and the English ASRAAM article says ASRAAM will replace in-service R-73 and R.550 Magic. [published] — [English Wikipedia, ASRAAM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIM-132_ASRAAM)
- English Magic article lists Magic 2 on Mirage 2000B/C as a former operator (country flag not recovered as text) and "Magic 1 and 2 on Mirage 2000" as current for another flag. France's front-line IR successor on Rafale and Mirage 2000-5 is MICA, not Magic. [published] — [English Wikipedia, R.550 Magic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R.550_Magic); [MBDA MICA page](https://www.mbda-systems.com/products/air-dominance/mica-family/mica)
- Propellant mass, the 10% thrust increase in newtons, burn time, seeker track rate, numerical acquisition ranges, fuze arming delay specific to Magic II, lethal radius, and TVC: [unknown].

### Inferences
- A motor "10% more powerful" cannot be turned into thrust or impulse without a baseline thrust. No baseline was opened, so no [derived] figure is stated.
- Magic II should be simulated as an all-aspect canard dogfight missile with a short, single-burn motor and free-rolling tails, not as an imaging focal-plane weapon and not as a thrust-vector weapon. The afterburner-ionisation filter is a specific early IRCCM behaviour: a hot plume spike from reheat must not be treated as the target.

### Gaps
- No Matra/MBDA Magic II datasheet was opened. Range is not stable across opened sources: French combined table 15 km maximum versus English Magic 2 figure of 20 km, with no launch condition (head-on, altitude, shooter speed) on either page.
- English Mach 2 for Magic 2 is contradicted by the French Mach 3 table and is not repeated as a fact. Condition for either speed: [unknown].
- Whether the 30° gimbal, 1.8 s arming delay, 0.3 km minimum, 26 s self-destruct, helmet cue, and 50 g figure are Magic II-specific measurements or carried over from Magic I is not stated except where the English page explicitly says Magic 2 for the 50 g and all-aspect seeker.
- Missilery.info's Magic-2 table (2.7 Mach, 2.2 s, 8 km at altitude, 2 km at low level, 12.5 kg warhead of which 6 kg explosive, spans 470 mm and 650 mm) did not render when the page was opened, so those numbers are not used.
- Low-speed steering: [unknown]. No TVC is published, so control is aerodynamic.

## IRIS-T

### Takeaway
IRIS-T is the German-led imaging-IR dogfight missile with tail control plus thrust-vector vanes and a shaped solid-motor burn, in service since December 2005 and still in service in 2026. It is built to turn hard at low speed, including onto targets behind the shooter, rather than to win on raw motor diameter the way ASRAAM does.

### Cited Findings
- Name: Infra Red Imaging System Tail/Thrust Vector-Controlled. Prime contractor Diehl Defence. Programme nations Germany, Greece, Norway, Italy, Spain, and Sweden. Swedish Air Force designation Rb 98. [published] — [Saab IRIS-T](https://www.saab.com/products/iris-t); [German Wikipedia, IRIS-T](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/IRIS-T)
- In production since 2005 and in service with the partner nations and other users. English Wikipedia: in service December 2005. German Wikipedia: Luftwaffe accepted the first series missile in December 2005 at Rostock-Laage. Not described as retired. [published] — [Saab IRIS-T](https://www.saab.com/products/iris-t); [English Wikipedia, IRIS-T](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IRIS-T); [German Wikipedia, IRIS-T](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/IRIS-T)
- Saab specifications: length 2936 mm; diameter 127 mm; maximum range approximately 25 km; maximum speed Mach 3. No mass, span, or warhead mass on that page. [published] — [Saab IRIS-T](https://www.saab.com/products/iris-t)
- English Wikipedia infobox: mass 87.4 kg; length 2.94 m; diameter 127 mm; wingspan 447 mm; dual-layer HE/fragmentation warhead of 11.4 kg; impact and active radar proximity fuze; solid rocket with thrust-vector control; HTPB propellant. [published] — [English Wikipedia, IRIS-T](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IRIS-T)
- German Wikipedia body, less precise than the infobox that did not render: about 3 m long, diameter 127 mm, about 90 kg. Warhead is an 11.4 kg fragment charge, detonated by impact or a Ku-band radar proximity fuze. [published] — [German Wikipedia, IRIS-T](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/IRIS-T)
- Saab: all-aspect; roll-pitch imaging wide-angle seeker; full-hemisphere engagement; fire-and-forget; lock-on before launch and lock-on after launch; can engage targets behind the launch aircraft; imaging seeker has high resolution, target discrimination, and flare suppression, with acquisition range compatible with the missile's full kinematic range; proximity fuze and high-explosive fragmented warhead; thrust-vector control and a dogfight-optimised motor. [published] — [Saab IRIS-T](https://www.saab.com/products/iris-t)
- Diameter, length, mass, and centre of gravity were chosen for Sidewinder compatibility. The launcher interface supports the older analogue Sidewinder interface and digital interfaces. Mechanical and electrical interface is Sidewinder-compatible. [published] — [Saab IRIS-T](https://www.saab.com/products/iris-t); [German Wikipedia, IRIS-T](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/IRIS-T)
- Seeker, German Wikipedia: not a staring 128×128 array. A scanning mirror and a two-colour linear array of 128×2 elements, angular resolution in the milliradian class, scans the field 80 times per second to build a 128×128 image. Look-angle range ±90° (half-angle each side of the nose) with a high seeker follow rate. English Wikipedia: InSb two-colour seeker, same 128×2 scanning architecture, 128×128 image 80 times per second. [published] — [German Wikipedia, IRIS-T](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/IRIS-T); [English Wikipedia, IRIS-T](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IRIS-T)
- Image processing recognises flares and ignores them. A target library holds views of known military aircraft, with eight of the most vulnerable aimpoints stored per target. Seeker sight range is three to four times that of the old Sidewinder. If a laser blinds the scanner, the image is flooded only while the scan looks at the source; the missile can switch to home-on-jam and fly at the jammer. [published] — [German Wikipedia, IRIS-T](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/IRIS-T)
- English Wikipedia: body-lift/turn claim of 60 g at 60°/s by thrust vectoring, plus LOAL, which is what allows shots at targets behind the aircraft. Steering system in the infobox: 4 exhaust vanes and 4 tail wings. [published] — [English Wikipedia, IRIS-T](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IRIS-T)
- German Wikipedia motor: one solid motor in a composite case, to keep empty mass low and propellant fraction high. Burn shape: a strong boost to separate from the aircraft; a short low-thrust interval intended to let the missile change direction by up to 180°; then acceleration to a maximum of Mach 3; then a sustain burn to limit energy loss. Four guide vanes in the nozzle. Faster than Sidewinder, slightly higher thrust, less drag. No thrust in newtons and no burn time. [published] — [German Wikipedia, IRIS-T](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/IRIS-T)
- Agility at low speed is explicit: tail control plus thrust vectoring and that thrust profile give agility about all axes "even at low speed and high altitude." LOAL uses an inertial navigator (Lital, now Northrop Grumman Italia) when the target is outside the ±90° seeker field at launch, flying the last known target motion. [published] — [German Wikipedia, IRIS-T](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/IRIS-T)
- Eurofighter cueing sources named on the German page: CAPTOR radar, PIRATE IRST, Striker helmet sight, and the missile-approach warners. Rear hemisphere assignment is via the warners. [published] — [German Wikipedia, IRIS-T](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/IRIS-T)
- Saab: "from point-blank up to maximum range." No separate minimum range in metres. [published] — [Saab IRIS-T](https://www.saab.com/products/iris-t)
- Propellant mass, thrust, burn time, total impulse, TVC vane angle, seeker track rate in deg/s (the 60°/s figure is the missile turn rate, not a stated seeker rate), rear and head-on acquisition ranges in km, arming delay, lethal radius, and smoke: [unknown] on pages opened. The German article's "well over 100 g" sentence is the author's inference from a fruit-size metaphor and is not a specification.

### Inferences
- The motor is one grain with a published four-step thrust shape (boost, low thrust, accelerate, sustain). That is not a dual-pulse motor. Dual-pulse is not claimed for the air-to-air IRIS-T on any page opened.
- Sidewinder-rail launch from a fighter means the round leaves with the shooter's velocity. No eject-launch mode was described for the air-to-air weapon.
- ±90° is the seeker look half-angle. Full-sphere shots beyond that cone are LOAL plus the airframe/TVC turn, not a gimbal that looks behind the missile at launch.
- The smaller scanned array "can be cooled better" than a large staring array (German Wikipedia). Coolant species and whether the coolant is onboard or a launcher bottle were not stated. Cooling is therefore only qualitative.
- Mass should not be collapsed to one number. Opened figures are 87.4 kg (English Wikipedia), "about 90 kg" (German Wikipedia body), and no mass at all on the Saab page. Length should prefer Saab's 2936 mm, with 2.94 m (English Wikipedia) beside it. Span 447 mm (English Wikipedia) was the only span opened in full; a 450 mm infobox value did not render on the German page.

### Gaps
- No Diehl air-to-air datasheet PDF was opened. Saab does not publish mass, warhead mass, g, or a seeker pixel count. The 60 g / 60°/s claim is Wikipedia citing Diehl and another source, not a number printed on the Saab page.
- No-escape range: [unknown]. The only range opened from an industry page is "approximately 25 km," with no altitude or aspect condition.
- A Block II air-to-air upgrade was mentioned on a secondary systems page in search and was not opened. No Block II performance is recorded here.
- IRIS-T SL/SLM/SLX are different propulsion and guidance fits. Their 12 km / 40 km / 80 km envelopes are not the air-to-air missile and are excluded.

## AIM-132 ASRAAM, including Block 6

### Takeaway
ASRAAM is a British MBDA tail-controlled, low-drag, 166 mm imaging-IR missile with a dual-burn (boost/sustain) motor and no published thrust-vector control. It has been in RAF service since 1998, operational in 2002, and Block 6 has been in Typhoon service since 1 April 2022. Block 6 changes the seeker and cooling. No opened source gives it a different motor, range, or g limit.

### Cited Findings
- Designation AIM-132 / ASRAAM. Country of origin United Kingdom. Manufacturer MBDA UK (originally BAe Dynamics). In service 1998. First missiles delivered to the RAF in late 1998. Designation-systems: declared ready for RAF operational use in September 2002. Still the RAF within-visual-range missile; not retired. [published] — [English Wikipedia, ASRAAM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIM-132_ASRAAM); [designation-systems.net, AIM-132](https://www.designation-systems.net/dusrm/m-132.html); [MBDA ASRAAM](https://www.mbda-systems.com/product/asraam/)
- MBDA specifications on the live product page: weight 88 kg; length 2.9 m; diameter 166 mm. No range, span, warhead, or speed number on that page. Key features: instantaneous cueing from radar or helmet sight; "highest speed" with no Mach number; all-round target designation; outstanding resistance to countermeasures. Full lock-on before launch and lock-on after launch. Agility is attributed to the 166 mm motor and a very low-drag airframe, not to thrust vectoring. [published] — [MBDA ASRAAM](https://www.mbda-systems.com/product/asraam/)
- Germany left the joint programme because the UK emphasised speed and range and Germany insisted on a thrust-vector dogfight missile. That German requirement became IRIS-T. The fielded AIM-132 has no forward flying surfaces. [published] — [designation-systems.net, AIM-132](https://www.designation-systems.net/dusrm/m-132.html)
- Designation-systems, data for AIM-132A, with an explicit warning that sources vary and the range is a rough estimate: length 2.90 m; fin span 45 cm; diameter 16.6 cm; weight 87 kg; speed Mach 3+; range 15 km (8 nm) sometimes quoted, "the true figure is probably higher"; minimum range around 300 m; propulsion dual-thrust (boost/sustain) solid; warhead 10 kg blast-fragmentation. Low-smoke motor. Very high acceleration off the launch rail. Four cruciform tail surfaces can pull up to 50 g immediately after launch. [published] — [designation-systems.net, AIM-132](https://www.designation-systems.net/dusrm/m-132.html)
- English Wikipedia infobox: mass 88 kg; length 2.90 m; diameter 166 mm (motor diameter); wingspan 450 mm; 10 kg blast/fragmentation; laser proximity fuze and impact; dual-burn, high-impulse solid rocket; air-to-air range 25+ km; surface-to-air 15 km for the Raven adaptation; maximum speed Mach 3+. Body text: 50 g from body lift and tail control; flies at over Mach 3 to ranges in excess of 25 km. The 16.51 cm (6.5 in) motor diameter in the body text is the comparison sentence and differs slightly from MBDA's 166 mm. [published] — [English Wikipedia, ASRAAM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIM-132_ASRAAM)
- Seeker, Blocks 1–5: Hughes, later Raytheon, imaging infrared 128×128 focal-plane array, strapdown inertial guidance, lock-on after launch. Long acquisition range, high countermeasures resistance, approximately 90° off-boresight lock-on, and aimpoint designation (cockpit, engines). Designation-systems states the field of view as +/- 90°, i.e. a half-angle of 90° each side of the nose. [published] — [English Wikipedia, ASRAAM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIM-132_ASRAAM); [designation-systems.net, AIM-132](https://www.designation-systems.net/dusrm/m-132.html)
- UK Defence Journal, quoting MBDA: the missile takes target information from radar or the helmet-mounted sight and can also act as an autonomous infrared search-and-track sensor. The RAAF demonstrated an over-the-shoulder lock-on-after-launch shot against a target behind the wing line of the shooter. [published] — [UK Defence Journal, 2022](https://ukdefencejournal.org.uk/asraam-block-6-enters-service-on-typhoon/)
- Block 6: developed under the ASRAAM Sustainment programme. Minister for Defence Procurement Jeremy Quin: Block 6 was integrated on Typhoon and Initial Operating Capability was formally declared on 1 April 2022. UK Defence Journal: new and updated sub-systems, built-in cooling, and a new British-built seeker with more pixels; no US-made components, so not ITAR-controlled. English Wikipedia: replaces external cooling with an internal cooler; UK-built seeker of higher resolution; F-35 service was still written as "will enter" in 2024. No pixel count for the new seeker was stated. [published] — [UK Defence Journal, 2022](https://ukdefencejournal.org.uk/asraam-block-6-enters-service-on-typhoon/); [English Wikipedia, ASRAAM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIM-132_ASRAAM)
- Neither the ministerial statement, the UK Defence Journal article, the live MBDA page, nor the English Wikipedia Block 6 paragraph says that Block 6 changed burn time, thrust, range, warhead, or control method. A motor change claimed on an unopened Chinese Wikipedia summary is not adopted.
- Propellant mass, thrust, burn time, total impulse, TVC deflection (no TVC is described), seeker track rate, numerical acquisition ranges, arming delay, and lethal radius: [unknown].

### Inferences
- The usual "boost-coast, little or no TVC" description is only half right on the sources opened. TVC is absent: Germany left the programme to get TVC, and every control description that was opened is tail fins plus body lift. The motor is not published as boost-only or as boost then unpowered coast. It is published as dual-thrust boost/sustain (designation-systems) and dual-burn high-impulse (Wikipedia). Sustain thrust and the time split are [unknown], so a sim should not invent a sustain level.
- "50 g immediately after launch" from tail surfaces (designation-systems) means the published control method is aerodynamic, after the motor has accelerated the missile, not jet-vane steering at zero airspeed. Steering at very low speed, before that acceleration, is [unknown] and should not be copied from IRIS-T or MICA.
- Rail launch is explicit ("off the launch rail"). The round therefore inherits shooter velocity. Eject launch was not described for the air-to-air weapon.
- +/- 90° on designation-systems is a half-angle. "Approximately 90-degree off-boresight" on Wikipedia is the same order of claim, not a 90° full angle. It is a lock-on capability, not a measured track rate.
- Block 6 should be the same kinematic body as earlier blocks unless a later source publishes a difference. The seeker change that is safe to model is: cooled imaging focal plane, higher resolution than 128×128, coolant now internal rather than external, plus the same LOAL, helmet/radar cue, and inertial midcourse already published for earlier blocks. Do not invent the new pixel count.
- Live MBDA does not print "in excess of 25 km." That figure is Wikipedia attributing it to MBDA, against designation-systems' explicit 15 km estimate and the statement that the real figure is probably higher. Both stay in the note. A 50 km figure seen on a commercial catalogue was not opened as a manufacturer source and is not used. No-escape range: [unknown].

### Gaps
- MBDA's downloadable ASRAAM datasheet did not resolve at the URL cited from Wikipedia (`/media/19276/download` returned a generic products page).
- Whether Block 6 actually entered F-35 service in 2024 was not confirmed on a page opened in 2026. Typhoon IOC on 1 April 2022 was.
- MBDA's live page still lists the Royal Australian Air Force as an in-service user. English Wikipedia's operator line treats the RAAF as historical. The retirement was not checked on an RAAF page.
- Smoke is "low-smoke" only on the 2002 designation-systems page. MBDA's live page does not repeat it.
- Warhead 10 kg and the laser proximity fuze are designation-systems and Wikipedia, not the live MBDA specification block.

## MICA IR

### Takeaway
MICA IR is a French medium-range imaging-IR missile on the same 112 kg airframe as MICA EM, with inertial midcourse, a data link, lock-on after launch, thrust-vector plus tail control, and a single high-impulse low-smoke solid motor. It entered service in 2000 and is still the fielded IR MICA in 2026. Dual-pulse propulsion and the longer range belong to MICA NG, which has been fired but is not the service round.

### Cited Findings
- Designation MICA IR (Missile d'Interception, de Combat et d'Auto-défense), infrared seeker variant. Country: France. Manufacturer: MBDA (Matra origin). Replaces Magic II in the short-range role and shares the airframe that replaces Super 530 in the intercept role. [published] — [French Wikipedia, MICA](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/MICA); [MBDA MICA](https://www.mbda-systems.com/products/air-dominance/mica-family/mica)
- Service: French Wikipedia infobox, MICA infrarouge 2000 (MICA radar 1996; VL-MICA 2010). Airforce Technology: MICA EM operational in 1999 and MICA IR in service in 2000. ### Inferences
- MICA IR is a medium-range weapon on the sources opened: 60 km class for the IR seeker, with the 80 km figure belonging to the EM seeker or to the unsplit family maximum. It should not be given a Sidewinder-class range, and it should not automatically be given the EM 80 km.
- Midcourse is inertial, with an optional data-link update, then passive IR terminal homing. That is lock-on after launch by construction for long shots. A loft is a possible energy-management law inside "fly to the predicted point," but no opened source says the missile lofts. Do not add a loft until a source says so.
- TVC plus tail fins is the published reason it can be used as a dogfight missile as well as a BVR missile, including high off-boresight helmet shots and the demonstrated rear-sector shot. Vane deflection angle is [unknown]. Ability to steer at low speed is the point of TVC and is supported by "firing up to the platform's maximum angle of attack" and by the rear-sector claim, but no minimum controllable speed was printed.
- Rail and eject launch both leave the missile with the aircraft's velocity at release. That is the definition of an air launch, not a separate MBDA number.
- The fielded motor is one high-impulse, low-smoke solid rocket with TVC. It is not the NG dual-pulse motor. Calling baseline MICA IR dual-pulse would be wrong on the sources opened.
- Warhead mass 12 kg is widely repeated and is on both Wikipedias, but the French wording is "12 kg of explosive," which may not equal total warhead mass, and the 2022 MBDA sheet only says "focused splinters." Treat 12 kg as a secondary published figure, not as a number read off the datasheet.

### Gaps
- Hellenic Air Force MICA IR page (range 0.5–60 km, span 56 cm, Mach 4, 12 kg blast-fragmentation) was indexed in search and returned "Access Denied" when opened. Those figures are not used, except where the same numbers also appear on pages that did open.
- No-escape range, burn time, and a head-on versus tail-chase range split for MICA IR were not published on opened pages. The 60 km IR figure has no altitude or speed condition attached.
- Seeker format (staring array versus scan, pixel count, coolant) for baseline MICA IR was not on the MBDA datasheet. "Dual waveband imaging" and "two infrared bands" are the published detector description. NG is the version described as getting a matrix sensor.
- Fin span 0.48 m is only the unfootnoted French infobox. MBDA does not publish span.
- The 50 g / 30 g numbers disagree in the range at which 30 g remains: "several tens of kilometres" (French, air-launched IR) versus 30 g by 12 km (English, written in the VL section). Both are recorded. Neither is a sea-level, Mach-specified load factor.

## AIM-9L/I and AIM-9L/I-1

### Takeaway
These are included because the seeker and guidance-section electronics are publicly described as different from a US AIM-9L. The motor is not publicly described as different. The only dimensions opened from a German official page match the AIM-9L airframe. No separate German burn time, thrust, g, or range was published on the pages opened.

### Cited Findings
- A 2011 Scramble aviation-magazine archive, in its AIM-9L entry, distinguishes three Lima upgrades. AIM-9L Tactical is an upgraded basic 9L. AIM-9L Genetic adds IRCCM by a removable module in the guidance and control section that provides infrared flare rejection. AIM-9L(I) hard-wires that IRCCM module into the guidance section, with improved counter-countermeasures and an upgraded seeker. Diehl BGT markets AIM-9L(I)-1, which upgrades the AIM-9L(I) guidance section again so that it is "more insensitive to the recognition and shadowing against infrared flares," and which that page calls an operational equivalent of the initially US-only AIM-9M. [published] — [Scramble, Raytheon AIM-9 Sidewinder, capture 24 July 2011](https://web.archive.org/web/20110724165145/http://wiki.scramble.nl/index.php?title=Raytheon_AIM-9_Sidewinder)
- The same Scramble page describes the AIM-9M, not the L/I, as the Lima follow-on with improved background rejection, IRCCM, and a reduced-smoke motor. It does not say the German L/I or L/I-1 changed the rocket motor. Motor difference from a US AIM-9L: [unknown] / not published as different. [published absence] — [Scramble, capture 24 July 2011](https://web.archive.org/web/20110724165145/http://wiki.scramble.nl/index.php?title=Raytheon_AIM-9_Sidewinder)
- Bundeswehr equipment page for "Luft-Luft-Rakete AIM-9L/I Sidewinder," opened from the 21 April 2024 capture (the archive lists later captures through May 2026; those later captures were not opened): used by the Luftwaffe on PA-200 Tornado and EF 2000 Eurofighter. Infrared target seeker, fire-and-forget, engagement from all attack directions. Visible figures on that page: diameter 12.7 cm; mass 84 kg; table values 2.87 m, 12.7 cm, 63 cm, and 84 kg. The table headers did not all render; a search extract of the same URL labels them length, diameter, span, and weight, and labels the seeker line "Infrarot mit Laser-Annäherungssensor." [published] — [Bundeswehr, AIM-9L/I, capture 21 April 2024](https://web.archive.org/web/20240421072453/https://www.bundeswehr.de/de/ausruestung-technik-bundeswehr/ausruestung-bewaffnung/aim-9l-i-sidewinder)
- The Bundeswehr page does not itself explain how the seeker differs from a US AIM-9L. The difference used to justify inclusion is the Scramble description above, not the Bundeswehr card.
- Propellant mass, thrust, burn time, g, range, minimum range, gimbal angle, track rate, acquisition ranges, arming delay, warhead mass, and lethal radius for the German L/I or L/I-1 specifically: [unknown] on pages opened. US AIM-9L figures were not copied in.

### Inferences
- L/I is a guidance-section and IRCCM upgrade of the Lima airframe, and L/I-1 is a further guidance-section upgrade aimed at flare discrimination. A sim can treat the airframe and motor as an AIM-9L until a source publishes a German motor change, and should treat the seeker as an all-aspect reticle seeker with hard-wired flare rejection rather than as an imaging focal plane.
- "More insensitive to recognition and shadowing against infrared flares" is the opened IRCCM description. No circuit, waveform, or detector material for the German head was published on the pages opened.
- Carriage on Tornado and Eurofighter is a rail fit in Luftwaffe service as of the 2024 page. The missile inherits shooter velocity at rail launch. That is not a separate Bundeswehr number.
- Whether those aircraft still carry AIM-9L/I in September 2026, beside IRIS-T, was not re-confirmed. The 2024 official page still listed both Tornado and Eurofighter.

### Gaps
- No Diehl datasheet for AIM-9L/I or AIM-9L/I-1 was opened. No official page states a seeker gimbal, track rate, or detector type (the US Lima InSb / argon description was not re-documented here and is not assumed to be unchanged).
- The laser proximity sensor appears only in the incompletely rendered Bundeswehr table. Impact fuze, warhead type, and smoke were not on the opened German page.
- L/I versus L/I-1 is a guidance-electronics distinction on Scramble. No evidence was opened that L/I-1 has a different motor, mass, or length from L/I.

## Flight character to reproduce

### Takeaway
Each round's trajectory family is different, and the differences that are actually sourced are control method and motor schedule, not a shared Fox-2 curve.

### Cited Findings
- Magic I: short-range rear-aspect canard missile, single-stage solid motor, free-rolling tails, cooled PbS seeker, arming delay about 1.8 s. [published as characterised above] — [French Wikipedia, Matra R550 Magic](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matra_R550_Magic); [English Wikipedia, R.550 Magic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R.550_Magic)
- Magic II: same canard airframe class, all-aspect AD3633 seeker, motor described as 10% more powerful, with a filter against afterburner false targets. [published] — [French Wikipedia, Matra R550 Magic](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matra_R550_Magic)
- IRIS-T: thrust-vector vanes plus tail fins, shaped burn that deliberately drops thrust so the missile can reverse up to 180° and then re-accelerates to Mach 3 and sustains, imaging two-colour scanned seeker, shots from point-blank out to about 25 km including behind the fighter. [published] — [Saab IRIS-T](https://www.saab.com/products/iris-t); [German Wikipedia, IRIS-T](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/IRIS-T)
- ASRAAM: large-diameter dual-burn motor, no foreplanes, tail control and body lift, imaging focal plane, fast flyout and a long within-visual-range shot, Block 6 seeker and internal cooling but no published kinematic change. [published] — [MBDA ASRAAM](https://www.mbda-systems.com/product/asraam/); [designation-systems.net, AIM-132](https://www.designation-systems.net/dusrm/m-132.html)
- MICA IR: medium-range single-pulse low-smoke motor with thrust vectoring and long-chord wings, inertial midcourse and data link, dual-band imaging IR terminal seeker, usable from 500 m to about 60 km and in the rear hemisphere after launch. [published] — [MBDA MICA datasheet PDF, 2022](https://www.mbda-systems.com/sites/mbda/files/2024-07/2022%20MICA%20datasheet.pdf); [French Wikipedia, MICA](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/MICA)
- AIM-9L/I and L/I-1: AIM-9L-class rail missile whose published German difference is a hard-wired, then further improved, flare-rejection guidance section, not a new motor. [published] — [Scramble, capture 24 July 2011](https://web.archive.org/web/20110724165145/http://wiki.scramble.nl/index.php?title=Raytheon_AIM-9_Sidewinder); [Bundeswehr, capture 21 April 2024](https://web.archive.org/web/20240421072453/https://www.bundeswehr.de/de/ausruestung-technik-bundeswehr/ausruestung-bewaffnung/aim-9l-i-sidewinder)

### Inferences
- A sim that gives ASRAAM IRIS-T-style thrust vectoring, or gives fielded MICA IR a second pulse, will contradict the sources opened. A sim that gives Magic I a head-on lock will also contradict them.

### Gaps
- No opened source provided a thrust-time curve, propellant mass, or specific impulse for any of these motors, so none of those quantities is stated.
