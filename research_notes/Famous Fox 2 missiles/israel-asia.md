# Israeli, Chinese, Japanese, and South African/Brazilian infrared air-to-air missiles

Scope is infrared (Fox 2) missiles only: Shafrir 2, Python 3, Python 4, Python 5, PL-5EII (PL-5E kept separate), PL-8, PL-9C, PL-10 / PL-10E, AAM-3 (Type 90), AAM-5 (Type 04), and A-Darter. Radar missiles (Derby, PL-12, PL-15, AAM-4, and the reported active-radar PL-10 variant) are omitted. Status is as of 28 September 2026.

Tag legend used on every figure: **[published]** means the number or qualitative claim appears in the cited source; **[derived]** means a number computed here (none were; no thrust or impulse was invented from mass and speed); **[unknown]** means no opened public source gave a usable figure. Parent performance is not copied onto a derivative unless a source says the figures match. Where only a trade-press or Wikipedia infobox gives a number, and the manufacturer page that was opened does not, the note says so.

No Rafael, AVIC/LOEC, Japanese MoD, or Denel page opened for this set publishes propellant mass, burn time, thrust, or total impulse. Those fields are **[unknown]** for every missile below. Smoke is **[unknown]** unless a source describes it.

## Shafrir 2

### Takeaway
Shafrir 2 is a rear-aspect, canard-controlled Israeli heat-seeker that entered service in 1969 and left production in 1978; a sim should treat it as a short-burn, tail-chase missile with a narrow acquisition cone, not as an all-aspect high-off-boresight weapon. No Rafael datasheet was found, and the opened secondary compilations disagree on length, range, and speed.

### Cited Findings
- Country Israel, developer and producer Rafael. Entered service 1969. Production from the mid-1960s to 1978. More than 925 reported built. Notable users listed as Israel, Argentina, and Taiwan. Page status line says "Limited service" with no date on that statement. **[published]** — [WeaponSystems.net Shafrir](https://weaponsystems.net/system/1193-Shafrir)
- Shafrir 2 tab on that page: length 2.6 m, diameter 0.16 m, wingspan 0.55 m, weight 93–95 kg. Infrared seeker, 10° off-boresight acquisition, rear-aspect lock-on. Blast-fragmentation warhead 11 kg, proximity and impact fuzes. Solid propellant rocket. Speed Mach 2.5. Range 6.4 km maximum, 3 km practical, 0.6 km minimum. Ceiling 18 km. The same tab prints "G force: Over 2 G". **[published]** — [WeaponSystems.net Shafrir](https://weaponsystems.net/system/1193-Shafrir)
- Carlo Kopp (Australian Aviation, April 1997) places Shafrir II in production between 1968 and 1980, credits it with over 100 kills mainly in the 1973 war, and groups Shafrir with Python 3 as using "a similar aerodynamic and control configuration to the AIM-9, but are unique missile designs." He describes second-generation seekers of that era, explicitly including Shafrir 2, as a wider 45° aspect angle than first-generation tail-only weapons, with a slightly larger field of view and tracking rate, still requiring a tail-chase. **[published]** — [Air Power Australia / Kopp 1997](https://www.ausairpower.net/TE-Gen-4-AAM-97.html)
- Kopp's 45° figure is target aspect, not a seeker gimbal half-angle. WeaponSystems.net's 10° figure is explicitly "off boresight acquisition." They are not the same parameter. **[published]** — both URLs above.

### Inferences
- The "Over 2 G" line is not a credible missile structural or maneuver limit (the Shafrir 1 tab on the same page prints a flat "2 G"). Do not use it as a sim load factor. Missile g is **[unknown]**.
- "Limited service" on an undated database is not evidence that any air force still fires Shafrir 2 in 2026. Production ended in 1978. Israel replaced it with Python 3. Residual export stocks in 2026 are **[unknown]**.
- No source opened here says the motor is smokeless, boost-sustain, or thrust-vectoring. Control is aerodynamic and AIM-9-like (canards), rear-aspect only.

### Gaps
- No Rafael brochure with length, mass, impulse, burn time, or thrust was found. Length 2.6 m (WeaponSystems.net) is not cross-checked against a primary document in this pass. Practical range 3 km versus maximum 6.4 km has no stated launch altitude or target aspect beyond the rear-aspect seeker. Acquisition range in metres, track rate, helmet cue, and IRCCM are **[unknown]**. Shafrir 2 predates helmet cueing.

## Python 3

### Takeaway
Python 3 is Rafael's all-aspect successor to Shafrir 2, in the AIM-9L generation: a canard missile with a roughly 30–40° forward launch cone, about 120 kg and 11 kg warhead, and a published high-altitude effective range of 15 km. A sim should allow head-on and beam shots inside a moderate boresight cone, with energy falling off after a short solid-motor burn, not Python 4/5 helmet-cued agility.

### Cited Findings
- Israel, Rafael. WeaponSystems.net: entered service 1981; development 1978 to the early 1980s; status line "In service" (undated); Israeli production from 1981; Chinese production as PL-8 from 1989 to 2000 at Xi'an Eastern Machinery Factory; South African service designation V3S Snake; also "Tzafrir" as an Israeli service name. **[published]** — [WeaponSystems.net Python 3](https://weaponsystems.net/system/1194-Python+3)
- Same page, Python 3 facts (not a separate PL-8 table): length 2.95 m, diameter 0.16 m, wingspan 0.86 m, weight 120 kg. Infrared homing, 30° off-boresight acquisition, all-aspect lock-on, 40° tracking in flight. Blast-fragmentation warhead 11 kg, radio proximity fuze. Solid propellant. Speed Mach 3.5. Range 15 km at high altitude, 5 km at low level, minimum 0.5 km. Ceiling 18 km. "Up to 40 g." **[published]** — [WeaponSystems.net Python 3](https://weaponsystems.net/system/1194-Python+3)
- FAS compilation (page updated 10 August 1999, opened via 2016 capture): all-aspect including head-on; beam interceptions; high-g dogfights; "15 km maximum effective range at high altitude"; active proximity fuze "based on lead bias navigation system"; acquisition modes include slaving to radar; "The pilot can launch the missile only after steering his plane at the enemy plane at a 30-40 degree cone." Spec table: range 0.5 km to 15 km, speed Mach 3.5, one double-base solid motor, IR homing, warhead 11 kg (24.25 lb), launch weight 120 kg (264.6 lb), length 3.00 m (9 ft 10.1 in), diameter 160 mm (6.3 in), fin span 0.86 m (2 ft 9.9 in). Date-deployed line says "Mid 1980's." **[published]** — [FAS Python-3 (archived)](https://web.archive.org/web/20160828051330/https://fas.org/man/dod-101/sys/missile/row/python3.htm)
- Kopp: Python 3 introduced in the late 1970s, contemporary of AIM-9L/M, "credited with over 35 Syrian aircraft kills" in the 1982 Bekaa Valley battle. Same AIM-9-like but unique aerodynamic and control configuration as Shafrir. **[published]** — [Kopp 1997](https://www.ausairpower.net/TE-Gen-4-AAM-97.html)
- FAS Python-4 spec table is byte-for-byte the same weights, dimensions, span, warhead, speed, range band, motor description, and "Mid 1980's" date as this Python-3 table. See Python 4. **[published]** — [FAS Python-4 (archived)](https://web.archive.org/web/20160828050852/https://fas.org/man/dod-101/sys/missile/row/python4.htm)

### Inferences
- Length 2.95 m (WeaponSystems.net) versus 3.00 m (FAS) is unresolved. Diameter 160 mm, mass 120 kg, warhead 11 kg, Mach 3.5, and 15 km high-altitude effective range agree across the two opened compilations. Wingspan 0.86 m agrees between them.
- The 30° acquisition / 40° in-flight tracking figures (WeaponSystems.net) and the FAS "30-40 degree cone" are the same order. Neither source says whether 30° is a half-angle from the nose. Read them as about 30° off the nose for acquisition, not as a ±30° total cone of 15° each side, because FAS says a 30–40° cone the pilot must point into and WeaponSystems.net says "30° off boresight acquisition."
- Fuze type conflicts: WeaponSystems.net says radio proximity; FAS says an active proximity fuze tied to lead-bias navigation and does not name the sensor. Do not pick one.
- "In service" on WeaponSystems.net is not a 2026 IAF inventory statement. A 2011 IAF F-15D photo is on that page. Whether Israel still carries Python 3 in 2026 is **[unknown]**. China and South Africa are separate designations (PL-8, V3S); do not assume 2026 service from the Python 3 row.
- No thrust vectoring is claimed. No helmet cue is claimed for Python 3 itself (helmet cue on the later Chinese PL-8B is a different missile entry). IRCCM details beyond all-aspect capability are **[unknown]**. Smoke **[unknown]**. Motor described only as double-base solid (FAS) or solid (WeaponSystems.net); boost-only versus boost-sustain is **[unknown]**.

### Gaps
- Propellant mass, burn time, thrust, and total impulse **[unknown]**. Rear versus head-on acquisition ranges in kilometres **[unknown]** except the kinematic envelopes above. Track rate **[unknown]**. No Rafael Python 3 brochure was opened.

## Python 4

### Takeaway
Python 4 is the 1990s IAF helmet-cued, all-aspect dogfight missile with a long-burn profiled motor and no thrust vectoring; the only careful open description refuses a numeric gimbal, warhead mass, and g limit. A sim should give it a large forward-hemisphere seeker (sources only support "most of the forward hemisphere" or "in excess of 60°" off boresight), aerodynamic high-alpha control, and energy that lasts through the endgame, and should not reuse the FAS dimension table.

### Cited Findings
- Kopp, writing in 1997 from Rafael fit-check imagery and UK/Israeli descriptions: developed in the late 1980s and early 1990s; deployed on Israeli F-15 and F-16 in the early 1990s together with the Elbit DASH helmet sight, specifically to counter R-73/helmet and MiG-29. All-aspect gimballed seeker "designed for large off boresight acquisition and tracking angles, and a high tracking rate." Exact off-boresight angle "has not been disclosed"; "UK sources suggest in excess of 60 degrees off-boresight capability"; tracking rate "high but undisclosed." Can engage "most of the forward hemisphere," and if it misses the first pass it is described as keeping track into a tail chase. **[published]** — [Kopp 1997](https://www.ausairpower.net/TE-Gen-4-AAM-97.html)
- Seeker: Israeli sources, per Kopp, "will only acknowledge that the missile uses a multiple detector array seeker" with IRCCM and background rejection. He says US reports of a cooled two-colour rotating-reticle seeker were incorrect. Digital signal processing in the seeker and a microprocessor flight-control system; "unique tailored proportional navigation." Acquisition range "credited with significantly better" than AIM-9M, with no number. **[published]** — [Kopp 1997](https://www.ausairpower.net/TE-Gen-4-AAM-97.html)
- Warhead: blast fragmentation, active laser proximity fuze with backup impact fuze. "The warhead size has not been disclosed." **[published]** — [Kopp 1997](https://www.ausairpower.net/TE-Gen-4-AAM-97.html)
- Motor and control: "6 in diameter rocket motor." "The long burn motor has a tailored thrust profile" for close-in acceleration and endgame energy. "Thrust vectoring is not employed." Aerodynamics: cruciform fixed canards ahead of cruciform pitch/yaw control canards, a small pair of roll "paddle" vanes, swept fuselage strakes, and swept tail surfaces that swivel about the fuselage. Cleared, per Kopp, on F-15, F-16, F/A-18, and F-5, and compatible with AIM-9 launchers if the launcher electronics are changed. "Better maximum G capability than any existing AAM" as of that article, with no g number. "Much better range performance than the AIM-9M," with no kilometre figure. **[published]** — [Kopp 1997](https://www.ausairpower.net/TE-Gen-4-AAM-97.html)
- FAS Python-4 page narrative (not the table): "no escape volume," advanced IRCM and background rejection, fragmentation warhead, fire-and-forget, helmet "squint" via an Elbit helmet so the pilot need not point the aircraft. "The Python 4 can be launched at a range of over 15 km, its warhead is over 11 kg, and its electromagnetic proximity fuze is one of the best in the world." **[published]** — [FAS Python-4 (archived)](https://web.archive.org/web/20160828050852/https://fas.org/man/dod-101/sys/missile/row/python4.htm)
- The FAS Python-4 specification table prints range 0.5–15 km, Mach 3.5, double-base solid, warhead 11 kg, launch weight 120 kg, length 3.00 m, diameter 160 mm, fin span 0.86 m, and "Date Deployed: Mid 1980's." That table matches the FAS Python-3 table exactly, including the mid-1980s date, which contradicts Kopp's early-1990s deployment. **[published]** — [FAS Python-4](https://web.archive.org/web/20160828050852/https://fas.org/man/dod-101/sys/missile/row/python4.htm) and [FAS Python-3](https://web.archive.org/web/20160828051330/https://fas.org/man/dod-101/sys/missile/row/python3.htm)
- Fuze conflict: Kopp says active laser proximity plus impact; FAS narrative says electromagnetic proximity. **[published]** — both URLs above.
- Airforce Technology, opened for Python 5, says Python 5 "incorporates the aerodynamic airframe of the Python-4" and "retains the inertial navigation system (INS), rocket motor, warhead and proximity fuse of its predecessor." That is a trade-press identity claim, not a Rafael Python 4 datasheet. **[published]** — [Airforce Technology Python-5](https://www.airforce-technology.com/projects/python-5-air-to-air-missile-aam-rafael-israel/)

### Inferences
- Do not load the FAS Python-4 table into a sim. It is the Python-3 table reused, and it fights Kopp on service date, on whether warhead mass was disclosed, and on fin span versus the Python 5 airframe claim below.
- If the Airforce Technology claim that Python 5 keeps the Python 4 airframe, motor, warhead, and fuze is true, then Python 4's external geometry would be the Rafael Python 5 brochure figures (length 310 cm, diameter 16 cm, wingspan 64 cm) and the warhead would be the 11 kg figure that article gives for Python 5. Rafael has not said this. Kopp's 6-inch motor matches the 16 cm Python 5 diameter, but his "warhead size has not been disclosed" means the 11 kg figure should not be treated as a Python 4 measurement. Mass, length, and span of Python 4 stay **[unknown]** as primary data.
- Off-boresight: use "greater than 60° off the nose, most of the forward hemisphere" as the sourced description. Do not invent 90° or 180°. Half-angle versus full-angle: Kopp's "60 degrees off-boresight" is the standard half-angle wording (angle from the nose), not a ±60° pair that sums to 60°.
- No TVC. Helmet cue is Elbit DASH, real for this missile. IRCCM is "multiple-detector-array seeker with background rejection," not a published two-colour or imaging architecture. Kopp explicitly rejects the two-colour Stinger analogy as a US misreport.
- 2026 IAF service is **[unknown]**. Rafael's current public air-to-air IR product is Python 5. No opened source says Python 4 was retired or that it remains in inventory.

### Gaps
- Launch mass, length, fin span, warhead mass, maximum g, burn time, thrust, speed, minimum range, and a numeric range with a launch condition are **[unknown]** from a source that is not the contaminated FAS table. FAS "over 15 km" is a narrative claim without altitude or aspect. Track rate undisclosed. Rear and head-on acquisition ranges in kilometres **[unknown]**. Smoke **[unknown]**.

## Python 5

### Takeaway
Python 5 is the in-production Rafael full-sphere imaging missile: 105 kg, 3.10 m, 16 cm diameter, 64 cm span, dual-waveband IR plus CCD imaging, lock-on before or after launch, and a two-stage motor, with range and gimbal kept qualitative in Rafael's own brochure. A sim should allow a shot into the rear hemisphere by LOAL and agility, not by assuming a published degree stop or thrust vectoring.

### Cited Findings
- Rafael brochure (document line "UNC.34943/02.23"): fifth-generation air-to-air missile, "full-sphere launch capability," "from very short range to Beyond Visual Range (BVR)," LOAL plus "excellent acquisition and tracking," dual-waveband focal-plane-array seeker, small low-signature targets in look-down, adverse backgrounds, and cloud. "Operational and combat-proven in 5 countries." Technical specifications printed on the brochure: weight 231.5 lb / 105 kg; length 122 in / 310 cm; wingspan 25.2 in / 64 cm; diameter 6 in / 16 cm. Technologies listed: "new dual-waveband Imaging Infra-Red (IIR) seeker," FOG inertial navigation, IRCCM, and flight-control algorithms. Capabilities also listed: LOBL and LOAL; "Fifth-generation imaging seeker with extremely high off-boresight capability." A figure is captioned "Full-Sphere Launch Envelope." Seeker imagery is labeled IIR and CCD. **[published]** — [Rafael Python 5 brochure PDF](https://www.rafael.co.il/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/python-Air-to-Air-and-Air-defense-Missile.pdf)
- Rafael product page: "dual-waveband (IR+CCD) Focal Plane Array (FPA) seeker"; "seeker with extremely high off-boresight capability"; full-sphere performance from "exceptional agility, Lock-On-After-Launch (LOAL) capability, powerful two-stage rocket motor, and strong acquisition and tracking capabilities"; "over-the-shoulder" launch into the aft hemisphere; "near BVR" with a "substantially extended envelope in head-on scenarios"; "unprecedented immunity to all types of IR countermeasures (IRCCM)"; certified for F-15, F-16, Kfir, LCA, and F-5; "operational in five countries." **[published]** — [Rafael PYTHON-5 page](https://www.rafael.co.il/system/python5/)
- The brochure and the product page do not print range in kilometres, speed, warhead mass, g, gimbal degrees, burn time, or thrust. **[published]** as an absence — same two URLs.
- Airforce Technology: unveiled Paris Air Show, June 2003; development began in the 1990s; first operational kill August 2006, F-16 against a Hezbollah Ababil-T UAV in the Lebanon War. States that Python 5 uses the Python 4 aerodynamic airframe and retains the predecessor's INS, rocket motor, warhead, and proximity fuze. Prints length 3.1 m, wingspan 64 cm, diameter 16 cm, weight 105 kg (matches Rafael), plus an 11 kg high-explosive fragmentation warhead with an active laser proximity fuze (not in the Rafael brochure). Dual-waveband FPA; LOBL and LOAL; "full sphere launch capability allows 360° engagement around the launch aircraft." Solid propellant "provides a speed of Mach 4 and an operational range of more than 20 km." **[published]** — [Airforce Technology Python-5](https://www.airforce-technology.com/projects/python-5-air-to-air-missile-aam-rafael-israel/)
- FlightGlobal, 17 June 2003 (Arie Egozi): Python 5 "is operational" and was co-developed with the Israeli Air Force; "360° full sphere capability"; dual-wavelength focal-plane-array imaging seeker; LOAL for rear-hemisphere shots; "range of over 20 km (11 nm)"; "reaches Mach 4"; "improved 11 kg (24 lb) 'super kill' warhead." The same article says the fuselage is similar to Python 4 "although the Python 5 probably has an improved motor." The word "probably" is the reporter's. **[published]** — [FlightGlobal, 17 June 2003](https://www.flightglobal.com/archive/2003/06/rafael-launches-fifth-generation-python-missile/)
- SPYDER-system trade press has attributed "LOAL at an angle of more than 100° off boresight" and a same-airframe/same-motor/same-warhead/same-fuse statement to Python 5 versus Python 4, and a high-explosive fragmentation warhead with an active laser proximity fuze. **[published]** as trade press, not as a Rafael sentence — [Army Technology SPYDER](https://www.army-technology.com/projects/spyder/). Rafael's own pages do not give 100°.
- A Wikipedia SPYDER infobox has circulated Python-5 range 40 km and warhead 11 kg in a surface-to-air system table, with Mach 4. That 40 km figure was not on the Rafael brochure and conflicts with the "over 20 km" air-launch claims. **[published]** on the encyclopedia page, not adopted — [Wikipedia SPYDER](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SPYDER)

### Inferences
- Use the Rafael brochure for mass, length, span, and diameter: 105 kg, 3.10 m, 0.64 m, 0.16 m. **[published]**
- Dual-waveband on the current Rafael page is IR plus CCD (imaging infrared and visible), not a published MWIR+LWIR split. Do not assign 3–5 µm and 8–12 µm bands; those were not in the Rafael text.
- Gimbal: "extremely high off-boresight" and full-sphere / over-the-shoulder via LOAL. The only degree figure found is the trade-press "more than 100° off boresight," which Rafael does not print. Keep the Rafael claim qualitative. If a number is stored, tag 100° as secondary and as a half-angle-style "off boresight" phrase, not as a ±100° pair.
- Mach 4 and range greater than 20 km are 2003 press figures repeated by Airforce Technology. Rafael in the 2023 brochure still does not state them, and does not state the launch condition. Do not replace them with the 40 km SPYDER-system number. Head-on shots are described as the extended part of the envelope; rear-hemisphere shots are an LOAL geometry, which in a sim is a large turn after launch, not a long kinematic range.
- Two-stage motor is on the Rafael product page. Burn time, thrust split, and smoke are **[unknown]**. A brochure photo of a ground launch shows a bright plume; that is not a published smoke classification.
- Thrust vectoring is not claimed by Rafael. Airforce Technology's "retains the Python 4 rocket motor" plus Kopp's explicit "thrust vectoring is not employed" on Python 4 is the only chain, and the FlightGlobal 2003 line says the motor was "probably" improved. TVC for Python 5 is **[unknown]** and should not be turned on by default.
- Warhead 11 kg HE-frag and active laser proximity fuze are trade press / 2003 FlightGlobal, not the brochure. Tag them **[published]** by those outlets, not by Rafael's current sheet.
- In service in 2026: yes as a marketed, produced missile. Brochure text says operational in five countries and a live production line (file rev February 2023). No retirement notice was found. IOC: FlightGlobal says it was already operational in June 2003; first credited kill August 2006.

### Gaps
- Rafael does not publish maximum g, track rate, acquisition range by aspect, minimum range in metres, or a no-escape range. Propellant mass and thrust **[unknown]**. Whether the 11 kg warhead and Mach 4 / >20 km figures survived later motor or warhead changes is **[unknown]**; the brochure is silent.

## PL-5EII

### Takeaway
PL-5EII is the variant with a current AVIC product page: a canard, all-aspect, dual-color multi-element infrared missile, 127 mm diameter, about 2.89 m long, 617 mm span, Mach 3 and 35 g on the manufacturer page, laser proximity fuze, and high-energy fragmentation warhead. A sim should treat it as a mature Sidewinder-sized all-aspect dogfight missile with modest off-boresight modes, not as a PL-10-class imaging missile. PL-5E gimbal and range figures are a different export variant and are not copied onto EII.

### Cited Findings
- AVIC product page "PL5E-II红外近距空空导弹": "增强型第三代近距格斗型空空导弹" (enhanced third-generation short-range dogfight AAM). Dual-color multi-element detector, onboard computer, laser fuze; "优异的抗干扰能力、全向攻击能力" (excellent anti-jam and all-aspect attack) and good detection. Fire-and-forget. Laser proximity fuze. Dual-color multi-element infrared seeker. High-energy fragmentation warhead. Platforms named: JF-17 (枭龙), J-7 series, J-8FII, FBC-1, L-15. Parameters: canard layout; maximum detection range 16 km; maximum flight speed Mach 3; maximum load factor 35 g; diameter 127 mm; length 2893 mm; wingspan 617 mm. **[published]** — [AVIC PL5E-II](https://www.avic.com/c/2021-05-08/513084.shtml)
- AVIC's 16 km figure is labeled 最大探测距离 (maximum detection range), not maximum flight range. **[published]** — same URL.
- LOEC (Luoyang) English page captured 21 May 2009: improved third-generation short-range AAM; two-color multi-element detector, onboard computer, laser fuze; fire-and-forget; "improved head-on detectability" and "significant anti-interference ability against infrared decoys"; laser fuze; "high maneuverability"; high-energy fragmentation warhead; small and light enough for a wingtip rail. Four modes: boresight aim/boresight launch, boresight aim/off-boresight launch, boresight scan/off-boresight launch, and radar-slaved/off-boresight launch. Guidance "multi-element, dual color, passive." Canard. Max flight speed 3 Ma. Max overload 35 g. All-aspect. Diameter 127 mm. Length 2896 mm. Wing span 617 mm. Rail launcher. **[published]** — [LOEC PL-5EII via Wayback](https://web.archive.org/web/20090521160043/http://www.loec.cn/e05.html)
- Length conflict between the two manufacturer pages: 2893 mm (AVIC) versus 2896 mm (LOEC 2009). Diameter, span, Mach 3, and 35 g agree. Mass is on neither page. **[published]** — both URLs.
- Ancestry, from the CASI open-source history, not from AVIC: PL-5A (semi-active radar) and PL-5B (infrared) R&D began at Luoyang; "PL-5B (IRH version based on PL-2 technology)"; separately, "R&D of the PL-2B begins based on AIM-9E." PL-5B design approved 1986 and small-batch production 1987 in that timeline. CASI also says Israel signed a Python 3 license in the early 1980s and that this is a different path from PL-5. **[published]** — [CASI, Wood, Yang, and Cliff, November 2020, PDF](https://www.airuniversity.af.edu/Portals/10/CASI/documents/Research/Infrastructure/2020-11-%2030%20Air-to-Air%20Missiles%20and%20Guidance%20Systems.pdf)
- CASI summary table row labeled PL-5EII: length 2.89 m, total weight 83 kg, engagement range 14 km, max speed N/A, IR-homing. The table's footnotes were not extracted cleanly enough to identify the underlying measurement. **[published]** as a CASI table entry — same PDF.
- PL-5E, not EII. FlightGlobal, 25 March 1998, Douglas Barrie, quoting CATIC: PL-5E all-aspect; seeker "maximum off boresight angle of ±25° before launch, and ±40° after launch"; CATIC claim of 40 g lateral acceleration; minimum launch range 500 m; maximum launch range 14 km. The PL-5B is "not described as having an all-aspect engagement capability" in that article. **[published]** for PL-5E only — [FlightGlobal, 25 March 1998](https://www.flightglobal.com/china-reveals-upgraded-variant-of-pl5-missile/20260.article). The live article body did not re-render on a later fetch; the quotation is from the indexed article text.

### Inferences
- Model PL-5EII, not PL-5E. Do not apply ±25°/±40°, 40 g, 500 m, or 14 km from the 1998 CATIC PL-5E claims to PL-5EII. EII's own pages give 35 g, all-aspect, off-boresight launch modes with no degrees, and a 16 km detection range that is not a kinematic range.
- 83 kg and 14 km exist only on the CASI table for the EII row. They match the sort of numbers long associated with PL-5E (14 km in the 1998 CATIC claim). Leave launch mass **[unknown]** for a sim mass property, or store 83 kg only with a CASI tag and a conflict flag.
- Sidewinder ancestry is the CASI chain PL-5B → PL-2 technology, and PL-2B → AIM-9E. It is not a statement that PL-5EII performance equals any AIM-9. Diameter 127 mm is the EII manufacturer's figure on its own.
- Seeker is dual-color multi-element passive infrared, all-aspect, with a stated anti-decoy claim. It is not described as imaging focal-plane or helmet-cued on the AVIC or LOEC pages (radar slave is listed; helmet is not). LOAL is not stated; "fire-and-forget" here means the shooter can break away after a lock, and the modes all assume the seeker has acquired before launch.
- In service in 2026: AVIC was still listing the missile as a product (page dated in the 2021 URL and still serving). No retirement date was found. Exact EII IOC year is **[unknown]**. The earlier PL-5B IOC is the 1986–87 design-approval window in CASI, which must not be reused as the EII date.

### Gaps
- Launch mass, warhead mass, propellant mass, burn time, thrust, smoke, minimum range, kinematic maximum range, and any off-boresight degree for PL-5EII are **[unknown]** on the pages that were opened. Track rate and acquisition range by aspect are **[unknown]**; 16 km is detection, condition not stated. Fuze is laser proximity; impact backup is not stated.

## PL-8

### Takeaway
PL-8 is the licensed Chinese Python 3, not a Python 5. CASI is the opened source for the license and for a dual-thrust motor, "over Mach 4," and "over 38 G," with a table mass of 115 kg and range of 15 km. A sim should not paste Python 3's 120 kg or 40 g onto PL-8, and should treat helmet cueing as a later PL-8B claim rather than original Python 3 behavior.

### Cited Findings
- CASI: "By 1988, China had begun producing the Python 3 under license as the PL-8." "China acquired the design of the PL-8 through a licensing agreement signed with Israel in 1982 for the domestic production of the Rafael Python-3." A sim should use those Jane's seeker and agility figures and should not use the conflicting Wikipedia 89 kg or 10 km values.

### Inferences
- Mass, length, and diameter for the sim: 93 kg, 2.98 m, 0.166 m, because Denel and Jane's agree. Tail span 0.488 m is Jane's ("across the tail fins"), not in the three-line Denel data block. The airframe is wingless in the sense of no large mid wings; it is not finless.
- Range: Jane's 23 km (2019, after qualification) versus DefenceWeb "approximately 20 kilometres" (2016) versus Wikipedia/Airforce Technology 10 km. Prefer 23 km as the post-qualification Jane's figure, with no launch condition stated, and keep 20 km and 10 km as conflicting published numbers. This is not a no-escape range. Minimum range is **[unknown]**.
- 180° look angle is Jane's wording. A 180° look angle is the total angle the seeker can cover, which is naturally read as ±90° from the nose, not as 180° aft of the nose. That reading is an inference. Jane's does not say "180° off boresight." Helmet high-off-boresight designation is Denel's qualitative claim and is consistent with a large look angle. Track rate 120°/s is Jane's.
- 100 g is Jane's and DefenceWeb, from TVC and body lift, not from a published thrust or burn-time calculation. Do not add the Wikipedia 8 s burn and 50 g coast unless that underlying citation is opened. No **[derived]** impulse is stated here.
- Smoke: Jane's says the motor was designed to minimise launch signature. DefenceWeb says smokeless propellant and almost no smoke trail. Wikipedia's "no aluminium powder" detail was not on the Jane's page. Use "reduced-smoke / signature-minimised solid motor," not a specific propellant recipe.
- IRCCM is "multi-mode ECCM" plus a two-colour imaging seeker (Denel and Jane's). No circuit description was published.
- Partners: Denel Dynamics and Brazil (Mectron/SIATT, with Avibras associated with the motor and Opto Eletrônica with the seeker in trade-press program histories). Not a Python or PL-8 license.
- 2026 status: qualified and cleared on SAAF Gripen C/D as of 29 September 2019. No opened source says South Africa retired it by 2026. Brazilian Gripen E/F integration was still future work in the 2019 Jane's article; whether FAB had finished that by 2026 was not rechecked.

### Gaps
- Warhead mass and type, minimum range, speed, burn time, thrust, propellant mass, and the altitude/aspect condition for the 23 km figure are **[unknown]** in the Denel data block and the Jane's article. The Wikipedia 8-second burn and post-burnout 50 g are unverified against those two sources. Rear and head-on acquisition ranges in kilometres are **[unknown]**; LOAL is specifically for shots beyond seeker detection range, which implies a datalink or inertial handoff, but the cueing range is not numbered.

## Cross-cutting notes for the report writer

### Takeaway
