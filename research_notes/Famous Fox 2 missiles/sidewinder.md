# AIM-9 Sidewinder family (Fox 2 only)

Scope is the infrared AIM-9 only. The semi-active AIM-9C is excluded. Numbers below are limited to pages actually opened or to search-index excerpts of pages that refused a full fetch; those cases are marked. Standard conversions (1 in = 0.0254 m, 1 ft = 0.3048 m, 1 lb = 0.45359237 kg, 1 lbf = 4.448221615 N) are tagged [derived]. No propellant mass was published for any variant in the sources opened, so none is invented. Game, forum, and unsourced brochure g-limits (30 g, 40 g, 60 g, DCS burn times) are not used.

Primary kinematic sources are NAVWEPS OP 2309 (Third Revision), 15 August 1966, declassified text of the AIM-9B description (archive.org), and the Navy OP 3353 AIM-9C/D pilot handbook text on archive.org. Seeker-family tables are Carlo Kopp, “The Sidewinder Story,” Australian Aviation, April 1994, with his own caveat that conditions are often unspecified. Variant narrative and a second dimension table are Andreas Parsch, designation-systems.net, last updated 9 July 2008, who warns that his figures “may therefore be inaccurate.” Current AIM-9X geometry is the NAVAIR product page. Motor hardware for the AIM-9M-era Mk 36 is the Alliant Techsystems public datasheet (approved for public release 3/98).

## Which variants are in this dossier, and what was dropped

### Takeaway
Documented here: AIM-9B, AIM-9D, AIM-9H (chosen over AIM-9G), AIM-9J, AIM-9L, AIM-9M, AIM-9P-5 (operationally distinct from L/M), AIM-9X Block I, Block II, and Block II+. AIM-9X Block III was cancelled and is not a fielded missile. As of 2026 the missiles in US production are AIM-9X Block II and Block II+.

### Cited Findings
- AIM-9H is the variant with the better public split from its predecessor: Kopp gives it its own row (solid-state electronics, Mk 36 Mod 5/6/7, LAU-7A, length 9.4 ft, span 2.06 ft, weight 186.3 lb, track rate listed only as >12°/s), and Parsch separately states a 20°/s track rate, production of about 7700 rounds in 1972–1974, and that the AIM-9L was derived from it. AIM-9G’s distinctive published feature is SEAM (seeker search pattern or slaving to the radar), with 2120 rounds built in 1970–1972; its mass and optics are otherwise grouped with the D. H was selected for that reason. — [Kopp 1994](https://www.ausairpower.net/TE-Sidewinder-94.html); [Parsch](https://www.designation-systems.net/dusrm/m-9.html)
- AIM-9P-4 adds an all-aspect seeker “using some of the technology developed for the AIM-9L” on the J/N airframe and “is less agile” than the L. AIM-9P-5 “is further improved by the addition of a counter-countermeasures capability.” Kopp’s table gives P-4/5 the SR.116 motor, not the L/M Mk 36, a 10.0 ft length, and a 1.9 ft span. That is a different missile from the L/M, so P-5 is included (P-4 is the all-aspect step immediately before it). — [Kopp 1994](https://www.ausairpower.net/TE-Sidewinder-94.html); [Parsch](https://www.designation-systems.net/dusrm/m-9.html)
- AIM-9X Block II+ procurement “began” in December 2017 “to satisfy the requirements of the Joint Strike Fighter platform,” on the same line as Block II. — [FY27 Air Force missile procurement narrative, indexed excerpt](https://www.af.mil/Portals/1/documents/Secretariat%20of%20the%20AF/SAF-FM/Budget%20-%202027/Budget%20docs/FY27%20Air%20Force%20Aircraft%20%20Missile%20Procurement.pdf?ver=6zKfXa_IITL18fK0xVVdIA%3D%3D)
- Air & Space Forces Magazine’s weapons directory (page dated in search as 1 January 2025; full page did not load on fetch) lists active variants AIM-9M, AIM-9M-9, and AIM-9X, and deliveries “2017–present (Block II+).” It describes Block II+ as the same improvements “specifically optimized for the F-35.” — [Air & Space Forces Magazine](https://www.airandspaceforces.com/weapons/aim-9/)
- A November 2024 Federal Register notice records a notified sale including AIM-9X Block II+ tactical missiles. A June 2026 contract report describes Lot 26 production of Block II and Block II+ rounds, with work through September 2029. Block II+ is a real contracted variant as of 2026, not a paper proposal. — [Federal Register, 21 Nov 2024](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2024-11-21/html/2024-27300.htm); [Defence Blog, 26 Jun 2026](https://defence-blog.com/raytheon-secures-1-1b-deal-for-aim-9x-missile-production/)
- “The AIM-9X Block III program was cancelled in fiscal year 2014.” Funding for several planned Block III improvements, including increased lethality, enhanced infrared countermeasures, and insensitive-munitions work, was moved into AIM-9X Block II System Improvement Program III. A planned Block III was not fielded. — [GAO-16-329SP](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-16-329sp.pdf)
- DoD 4120.15-L (12 May 2004) lists motors Mk 17 for AIM-9B and AIM-9J, and Mk 36 for AIM-9D, G, H, L, and M. Its one-line description of AIM-9M as “Upgraded AIM-9J” conflicts with every technical history opened here (M is an improved L) and is not used for lineage. — [DoD 4120.15-L (2004) via designation-systems.net](https://www.designation-systems.net/usmilav/original-docs/4120.15-L(2004).pdf)

### Inferences
- “Optimized for the F-35” is a carriage/integration statement. No opened source gives Block II+ a different mass, motor, or seeker size from Block II.

### Gaps
- No 2026 census of foreign AIM-9B/D/H/J/L/P users. Global “still in service in dozens of countries” claims refer to the family, not to a block.
- AIM-9G is not given its own parameter section; only the SEAM point above is carried, because H was the selected Navy variant.

## AIM-9B (Sidewinder IA, USAF GAR-8)

### Takeaway
US Navy/USAF rear-aspect missile. The 1966 manual, not the later summaries, is the kinematic source: Mk 15 or Mk 17 gives a nominal 8440 lbf·s in a 2.2 s burn at 70°F, then a coast of about 20 s of guided flight. It is long out of production (Parsch: through 1962). A 2026 operator list for the B specifically was not found.

### Cited Findings
- Designation and service: AAM-N-7 Sidewinder I entered US Navy service in May 1956 (about 240 built; post-1963 AIM-9A). Full-rate Sidewinder IA became AIM-9B; the USAF designation was GAR-8. More than 80,000 AIM-9Bs were produced until 1962. First combat use was 24 September 1958, Republic of China F-86Fs. Country: United States. — [Parsch](https://www.designation-systems.net/dusrm/m-9.html)
- 2026 status: not listed among active US variants in the 2025 Air & Space Forces directory (that list is M, M-9, and X). Formal retirement order was not opened. — [Air & Space Forces Magazine](https://www.airandspaceforces.com/weapons/aim-9/)
- Geometry from NAVWEPS OP 2309 (Third Revision), 15 August 1966 [published]: length 111.5 in, which is 2.832 m [derived]; diameter 5 in, which is 0.127 m [derived]; forward fin span 15 in, which is 0.381 m [derived]; wing span with rollerons 22 in, which is 0.559 m [derived]; weight 160 lb, which is 72.6 kg [derived]. Later summaries say 155 lb / 155.2 lb and 2.83 m / 9.28 ft. The manual’s 160 lb is preferred; the 5 lb gap is unresolved. — [OP 2309](https://archive.org/details/OP23093rdAIM9B); [Parsch](https://www.designation-systems.net/dusrm/m-9.html); [Kopp 1994](https://www.ausairpower.net/TE-Sidewinder-94.html)
- Warhead [published, OP 2309]: Mk 8, 25 lb assembly (11.3 kg [derived]), of which about 14.5 lb is metal and 10.5 lb is explosive (4.76 kg [derived]). About 1300 fragments at 6000 ft/s. “The lethal radius of the warhead is about 30 feet” (9.1 m [derived]), and fragments “are capable of penetrating 3/8-inch steel plate at this radius.” Influence fuze functions if the missile “passes within 30 feet.” Contact fuze is piezoelectric (barium titanate crystals on the fin rocker arms). Parsch’s “4.5 kg (10 lb) blast-fragmentation” matches the explosive fill, not the 25 lb assembly, and should not be used as launch warhead mass. — [OP 2309](https://archive.org/details/OP23093rdAIM9B); [Parsch](https://www.designation-systems.net/dusrm/m-9.html)
- Motor [published, OP 2309]: “The Rocket Motor Mk 15 or Mk 17 provides a nominal impulse of 8440 pound-seconds with a 2.2-second burning time at 70°F.” 8440 lbf·s = 3.754×10^4 N·s [derived]. Average thrust if that impulse is spread evenly over 2.2 s is 3836 lbf (1.707×10^4 N) [derived]. The manual does not say the 8440 lbf·s figure is a sea-level static test. Motor tube “approximately 75 inches long, 5 inches in diameter, and weighs about 80 pounds” (36 kg [derived]) — that is motor assembly mass, not propellant mass. Propellant mass [unknown]. Single burn time and no sustainer are stated, so the profile is boost-only. Propellant chemistry and smoke class [unknown]. Kopp’s 8200 lb-sec and Parsch’s “17.8 kN (4000 lb) thrust for 2.2 s” (which would be 8800 lbf·s) both disagree with the manual’s 8440 lbf·s. — [OP 2309](https://archive.org/details/OP23093rdAIM9B); [Kopp 1994](https://www.ausairpower.net/TE-Sidewinder-94.html); [Parsch](https://www.designation-systems.net/dusrm/m-9.html)
- Speed [published, OP 2309]: “incremental speed of approximately Mach 1.5, depending upon the altitude, above that of the firing aircraft.” Parsch’s “Mach 1.7” and “Mach 1.7 above launch speed” is a different single number and is not the manual’s. — [OP 2309](https://archive.org/details/OP23093rdAIM9B); [Parsch](https://www.designation-systems.net/dusrm/m-9.html)
- Range [published, OP 2309], both for a Mach 1.2 launch against a Mach 0.9 target: 28,000 ft (8.53 km [derived]) at 50,000 ft altitude; 6,000 ft (1.83 km [derived]) at sea level. Kopp and Parsch’s single “2.6 NM / 4.8 km” figure does not state those two conditions and sits between them. No-escape zone [unknown]. The manual’s practical firing rule is qualitative: the target is “within range” if the pilot can see it, and the aircraft is inside the firing envelope only if it can track at less than 2 g below 40,000 ft or less than 1.6 g at 40,000 ft and above, while hearing the audio tone. — [OP 2309](https://archive.org/details/OP23093rdAIM9B)
- Maneuver [published, OP 2309]: 2.7 g at 60,000 ft and missile Mach 2.3; 4.2 g at 50,000 ft and missile Mach 2.3; 10 g at sea level. The manual calls this “maneuver capability,” not a structural limit, and does not assign it to boost versus coast. At the stated Mach 2.3 the 2.2 s burn is already over for any plausible launch speed, so those two high-altitude points are coast figures. Parsch’s unqualified “12G” is not in the manual. Control-system time constant “0.25 second (maximum).” — [OP 2309](https://archive.org/details/OP23093rdAIM9B); [Parsch](https://www.designation-systems.net/dusrm/m-9.html)
- Control [published, OP 2309]: four movable nose fins, torque-balance pneumatic servos, “up to 800 inch-pounds” (66.7 lbf·ft [derived]). Rollerons on the wings. Proportional navigation: fin command produces a flight-path rate “three to five times” the gyro-axis (line-of-sight) rate. No thrust vectoring. — [OP 2309](https://archive.org/details/OP23093rdAIM9B)
- Seeker [published, OP 2309, plus Kopp]: passive infrared, lead-sulfide cell, rotating reticle, “4-degree” cone imaged onto the reticle. Uncooled [Kopp]. Rear hemisphere / “tail cone” only [OP 2309]; head-on acquisition range is zero. Kopp: 25° seeker field of view with the gimbal, 4° instantaneous field of view, 11°/s tracking rate, 70 Hz reticle, AM modulation, vacuum-tube electronics. The AIM-9D handbook says the gimbal limit was “increased from 25 to 40 degrees,” which is the B-model stop. Lock on the rail is required: the pilot fires only with the audio tone. No lock-on after launch and no helmet cue in this generation. — [OP 2309](https://archive.org/details/OP23093rdAIM9B); [Kopp 1994](https://www.ausairpower.net/TE-Sidewinder-94.html); [OP 3353 AIM-9C/D handbook text](https://archive.org/stream/op-2309-2nd-sidewinder-guided-missle-mark-2/OP%203353%20AIM-9C%20&D%20PIlot%27s%20Handbook_djvu.txt)
- Arming [published, OP 2309]: a disabling circuit blocks turn commands “for the first 0.5 second of flight.” Both fuzes mechanically arm between 480 and 840 ft from the launcher (146–256 m [derived]). The influence fuze then arms electrically at motor burnout, “approximately 2000 feet” (610 m [derived]) in front of the aircraft. Guided flight ends when the servo grain is exhausted, “approximately 20 seconds.” Self-destruct at about 24 s. Influence-fuze battery section that runs the firing circuit expires at about 30 s. Parsch’s 900 m minimum engagement is not the manual’s arming distance. — [OP 2309](https://archive.org/details/OP23093rdAIM9B); [Parsch](https://www.designation-systems.net/dusrm/m-9.html)
- IRCCM: none. Kopp: the seeker “very often locked on to the sun or clouds.” OP 2309 restricts it to fair-weather tail-cone attacks. Class: none. — [Kopp 1994](https://www.ausairpower.net/TE-Sidewinder-94.html); [OP 2309](https://archive.org/details/OP23093rdAIM9B)
- Rail: “launched from a rail launcher.” Primary launcher in the 1966 manual is the Aero 3A; the LAU-7/A, “designed primarily for the AIM-9C and AIM-9D,” can fire an AIM-9B with an electrical adapter. The manual’s speed is an increment on the firing aircraft, so the round inherits shooter velocity. It is not an ejection launch. Breakout or ejection speed [unknown]. — [OP 2309](https://archive.org/details/OP23093rdAIM9B)

### Inferences
- Flight character a sim should reproduce: a 2.2 s boost that adds on the order of Mach 1.5 to the shooter, a 0.5 s period with no steering, then a canard-steered coast of about 20 s with rolleron roll damping, able to guide only from the rear against a hot tailpipe, and with the influence fuze dead until roughly 2000 ft after burnout.

### Gaps
- Propellant mass, propellant name, and smoke class.
- Whether 8440 lbf·s is sea-level static or a nominal in-flight total.
- Sea-level maneuver g is given without a missile Mach.
- Structural g limit, separate from the maneuver-capability table.
- Numeric seeker acquisition range in metres. The 11°/s and 70 Hz figures are Kopp’s, not in the OP 2309 performance block that was read.
- 2026 foreign inventory.

## AIM-9D (Sidewinder IC, infrared)

### Takeaway
US Navy cooled-PbS rear-aspect round on the LAU-7, with a single-stage Mk 36. The pilot handbook’s motor line is an average 3500 lbf for 5 s and a maximum of Mach 2.5 above the firing aircraft. About 1000 were built. It is not a 2026 frontline missile.

### Cited Findings
- Parsch: IR Sidewinder IC, redesignated AIM-9D in 1963. Nitrogen-cooled PbS, 2.5° field of view, tracking rate 12°/s. About 1000 built by Philco-Ford and Raytheon between 1965 and 1969. Hercules Mk 36, larger Mk 48 continuous-rod warhead, slightly larger fins, range stated as 18 km (9.7 nm) without a launch condition. His table: length 2.87 m (113 in), finspan 0.63 m (24.8 in), diameter 12.7 cm, weight 88 kg (195 lb), speed “Mach 2.5+.” He flags the whole table as possibly inaccurate. Kopp’s dates (“production in the late fifties”) conflict with Parsch’s 1965–1969 production window. — [Parsch](https://www.designation-systems.net/dusrm/m-9.html); [Kopp 1994](https://www.ausairpower.net/TE-Sidewinder-94.html)
- Kopp table [published, conditions unspecified]: length 9.4 ft (2.865 m [derived]), span 2.06 ft (0.628 m [derived]), weight 195.1 lb (88.5 kg [derived]). Detector PbS, nitrogen cooled, MgF2 dome, reticle 125 Hz, AM, track rate 12°/s, thermionic electronics, continuous-rod warhead, passive-IR or HF fuze, Hercules Mk 36, launcher LAU-7A. Prose: IFOV reduced to 2.5°; seeker field of view “slightly increased to beyond 25 degrees”; actuator torque “up to 100 lb.ft”; gas-generator flight time 60 s; coolant is a 6 litre bottle in the LAU-7 giving 2.5 hours of cooling. — [Kopp 1994](https://www.ausairpower.net/TE-Sidewinder-94.html)
- OP 3353, Sidewinder 1C pilot handbook [published]: the Mk 36 is “a new single-stage, high-performance, solid-propellant motor” that boosts the missile “to a maximum speed of Mach 2.5 over that of the firing aircraft” and “gives an average of 3500 pounds of thrust during the 5 seconds of burning time.” Altitude and propellant temperature for that 3500 lbf average are not stated in the passage. Impulse implied by average thrust times burn time is 17,500 lbf·s (7.784×10^4 N·s) [derived]. Boost-only / single-stage, not boost-sustain. Propellant mass [unknown]. Smoke class [unknown]; this is not the later reduced-smoke AIM-9M Mk 36. — [OP 3353 text](https://archive.org/stream/op-2309-2nd-sidewinder-guided-missle-mark-2/OP%203353%20AIM-9C%20&D%20PIlot%27s%20Handbook_djvu.txt)
- Same handbook: gyro gimbal limit “increased from 25 to 40 degrees”; “gimbal angle of 40 degrees in any direction.” That 40° is the half-angle off the nose, not a full cone. Rollerons on the wings “provide pitch, yaw, and roll stabilization.” A separate AIM-9D manual text says the gas generator supplies pressure “for a nominal 60 seconds” (guidance power, not motor burn). — [OP 3353 text](https://archive.org/stream/op-2309-2nd-sidewinder-guided-missle-mark-2/OP%203353%20AIM-9C%20&D%20PIlot%27s%20Handbook_djvu.txt); [OP 3352 AIM-9D text](https://ia800806.us.archive.org/29/items/OP3352AIM9D/OP%203352%20AIM-9D%201_hocr.html)
- Warhead [published, OP 3353]: on detonation the continuous rod “expands at 4000 ft/sec, as a continuous ring, to a maximum diameter of 34 feet” (10.4 m [derived]). Parsch’s assembly mass is 11 kg (25 lb) for the Mk 48; that mass was not confirmed in the handbook passage that was read. The handbook says fuze functioning distance is in a passage it labels Secret, so no lethal radius beyond the 34 ft expanded-ring diameter is repeated here. IR fuze (Mk 323) is the one “normally used” with AIM-9D; an RF fuze existed for the radar AIM-9C and was mechanically interchangeable. — [OP 3353 text](https://archive.org/stream/op-2309-2nd-sidewinder-guided-missle-mark-2/OP%203353%20AIM-9C%20&D%20PIlot%27s%20Handbook_djvu.txt); [Parsch](https://www.designation-systems.net/dusrm/m-9.html)
- Minimum range [published, OP 3353]: fuzes “prevent warhead detonation during the first 600 to 1000 feet” and “prevent effective use of the missile when firing at a range less than 1000 feet.” For other than co-speed tail attacks the handbook gives Rmin = 1000 (1 + 2 ΔMach) feet, with the worked example Mach 1.5 shooter versus Mach 0.8 target = 2400 ft. Co-speed minimum is the fuze floor and “is true at all altitudes.” Sun exclusion: do not shoot until the missile is pointed at least 30° away from the sun. Missile load factor in g [unknown]. — [OP 3353 text](https://archive.org/stream/op-2309-2nd-sidewinder-guided-missle-mark-2/OP%203353%20AIM-9C%20&D%20PIlot%27s%20Handbook_djvu.txt)
- Seeker employment: still rear-aspect PbS (Kopp: “blind” class of early PbS seekers is the B; the D is more sensitive but he does not claim head-on lock). Head-on acquisition range treated as zero because no source opened gives the D a forward-aspect lock. Lock is on the rail (audio / cooled seeker before launch). No helmet cue is stated for the D; Kopp assigns radar or helmet slaving to the later AIM-9G SEAM mode. No LOAL. — [Kopp 1994](https://www.ausairpower.net/TE-Sidewinder-94.html)
- IRCCM class: none as a flare-rejection mode. Published behavior is a narrower 2.5° IFOV “to reduce background noise” (Parsch) and a 30° sun-avoidance rule (handbook). Not two-color, not imaging. — [Parsch](https://www.designation-systems.net/dusrm/m-9.html); [OP 3353 text](https://archive.org/stream/op-2309-2nd-sidewinder-guided-missle-mark-2/OP%203353%20AIM-9C%20&D%20PIlot%27s%20Handbook_djvu.txt)
- Not in the 2025 active-variant list. Production ended by 1969 per Parsch. — [Air & Space Forces Magazine](https://www.airandspaceforces.com/weapons/aim-9/); [Parsch](https://www.designation-systems.net/dusrm/m-9.html)

### Inferences
- Flight character: about five seconds of single-thrust boost to Mach 2.5 above the shooter, then a long coast (gas generator quoted at 60 s), canards plus rollerons, rear-aspect only, with the seeker free to sit 40° off the nose. Do not reuse the AIM-9B 2.2 s / 8440 lbf·s motor.
- Kopp’s “seeker FOV slightly beyond 25°” conflicts with the handbook’s 40° gimbal. The handbook is the AIM-9D primary and is the one used here for gimbal angle. Kopp may be describing a different optical angle.

### Gaps
- Propellant mass, propellant formulation, smoke class, and whether 3500 lbf is sea-level.
- Structural or endgame g. No-escape zone. Numeric acquisition range.
- Mk 48 assembly mass was not read out of the handbook itself.
- IOC day, as opposed to the 1965 production start. Kopp’s “late fifties” production claim is left as a conflict, not adopted.

## AIM-9H

### Takeaway
US Navy solid-state upgrade of the AIM-9G, still a nitrogen-cooled PbS rear-aspect missile on the Mk 36 and LAU-7A. It is the airframe the AIM-9L was taken from. Public sources do not repeat the AIM-9D handbook’s 3500 lbf / 5 s line for the H’s specific Mod 5/6/7 motor, so that impulse is not copied onto the H.

### Cited Findings
- Parsch: developed to improve AIM-9G reliability. Solid-state guidance. “The seeker tracking rate was also increased to 20°/s to complement the more powerful actuators.” About 7700 built by Philco-Ford and Raytheon, 1972–1974. Few fired over Vietnam; credited there with a higher kill rate than other AIM-9 versions. Table weight 84 kg (186 lb), grouped with D/G on length 2.87 m, finspan 0.63 m, diameter 12.7 cm, “Mach 2.5+,” range 18 km (9.7 nm) with no launch condition, Hercules Mk 36, Mk 48 11 kg continuous rod. — [Parsch](https://www.designation-systems.net/dusrm/m-9.html)
- Kopp: first solid-state Sidewinder; G optical system retained; tracking rate “further increased” (table only says >12°/s, which is weaker than Parsch’s 20°/s); actuators “120 lb.ft.” “Over 3,000 were built,” which conflicts with Parsch’s 7700. Table: USN, PbS, nitrogen, MgF2, 125 Hz, AM, thermionic electronics replaced by solid state, continuous rod, passive-IR/HF fuze, Hercules/Bermite Mk 36 Mod 5, 6, 7, LAU-7A, length 9.4 ft (2.865 m [derived]), span 2.06 ft (0.628 m [derived]), weight 186.3 lb (84.5 kg [derived]). He calls it the most maneuverable early Sidewinder and the direct parent of the AIM-9L, without a g number. — [Kopp 1994](https://www.ausairpower.net/TE-Sidewinder-94.html)
- DoD 4120.15-L: AIM-9H, Raytheon guidance and control, one Mk 36, “Upgraded AIM-9G with solid-state guidance control.” — [DoD 4120.15-L](https://www.designation-systems.net/usmilav/original-docs/4120.15-L(2004).pdf)
- Rear-aspect only: detector remains PbS, not InSb. Head-on acquisition range is zero on that basis. IFOV is not restated for the H; Kopp says the G optical system, whose parent D is 2.5°, was “essentially retained.” Gimbal limit is not restated; it is not re-measured here as 40°. Lock before launch; LOAL not described. Helmet cue is described by Kopp for the G’s SEAM mode (“slaving of the optics to radar or a helmet sight”), not restated as a new H feature. — [Kopp 1994](https://www.ausairpower.net/TE-Sidewinder-94.html)
- IRCCM: no new rejection mode is described beyond the D/G AM reticle. Class: none.
- Control: canards, with more powerful actuators than the G, and the Navy wing/rolleron layout of the D (not contradicted). No TVC. Guidance duration is not separately published; the D-family gas generator figure of 60 s is not automatically applied.
- Rail: LAU-7A [Kopp]. Inherits shooter velocity. Ejection speed [unknown].
- 2026: not in the Air & Space Forces active list. Superseded in US development by the AIM-9L. — [Air & Space Forces Magazine](https://www.airandspaceforces.com/weapons/aim-9/); [Parsch](https://www.designation-systems.net/dusrm/m-9.html)

### Inferences
- Flight character: Navy boost-then-coast rear-aspect missile, same family as the D, but solid-state and with a faster seeker (use 20°/s if a single number is required, and record that Kopp only printed “>12”). Do not give it all-aspect InSb or thrust vectoring.
- Production-count conflict (about 3000 vs about 7700) is left open. Parsch is later and more specific on the interval 1972–1974; both are secondary.

### Gaps
- Burn time, thrust, total impulse, and propellant mass for Mod 5/6/7 specifically. Smoke class.
- Missile g, structural or otherwise. Numeric range condition behind the “18 km.” Acquisition ranges. H-specific gimbal and IFOV. Whether every H retained SEAM. Arming distance in seconds or feet. 2026 foreign stocks.

## AIM-9J

### Takeaway
USAF upgrade of the AIM-9E, rushed out in July 1972. It keeps a PbS Peltier seeker and, on Kopp’s table and in DoD 4120.15-L, the Mk 17 rather than the Navy Mk 36. It is rear-aspect only. Parsch’s 18 km range cell is not trusted for the J because his table’s motor/warhead columns are merged across later variants.

### Cited Findings
- Kopp: “rushed into the SEA theatre in July, 1972.” Hybrid electronics, longer-burning gas generator for a 40 s flight time, actuators “90 lb.ft,” square-tip double-delta canards inherited from the AIM-9E description. 6700 built or rebuilt from AIM-9Bs. The follow-on AIM-9N “retained” the “Hercules-Aerojet Mk.17.” Table row for the J: USAF, origin AIM-9E, PbS, Peltier cooling, MgF2, reticle 100 Hz, AM, track rate 16.5°/s, hybrid electronics, blast-fragmentation warhead, passive-IR fuze, Hercules/Aerojet Mk 17, Aero-III launcher, length 10.0 ft (3.048 m [derived]), span 1.9 ft (0.579 m [derived]), weight 170 lb (77.1 kg [derived]). — [Kopp 1994](https://www.ausairpower.net/TE-Sidewinder-94.html)
- Parsch narrative: partial solid-state electronics, longer-burning gas generator, “more powerful actuators which drove new square-tipped double-delta canards. The latter feature doubled the single-plane G-capability” (no absolute g). About 10,000 built from 1972, mostly conversions of B/E. His specification table lists length 3.05 m (120 in), finspan 0.58 m (22.8 in), weight 77 kg (170 lb), which matches Kopp, and a range of 18 km (9.7 nm) in a column that also covers later missiles. Propulsion cells in that table are colspan-merged; the text never says the J received a Mk 36. — [Parsch](https://www.designation-systems.net/dusrm/m-9.html)
- DoD 4120.15-L: AIM-9J, Philco, “1 Mk-17,” Air Force, “Upgraded AIM-9E.” — [DoD 4120.15-L](https://www.designation-systems.net/usmilav/original-docs/4120.15-L(2004).pdf)

- The 18 km Parsch range is not used. A Mk 17 with a B-class impulse cannot be assumed to match the Mk 36’s 18 km handbook-era Navy figure.

### Gaps

## AIM-9L

### Takeaway
Joint US all-aspect missile: AIM-9H airframe, argon-cooled InSb, FM reticle, laser proximity fuze, and the WDU-17/B annular blast-fragmentation warhead. Motor is a Mk 36, but burn time and thrust for the L mods were not published in the sources opened. US production was replaced by the AIM-9M in the early 1980s.

### Cited Findings
- Parsch: joint development based on the AIM-9H, goal “ALASCA (All-Aspect Capability).” “New long-span pointed double-delta canards,” modified Mk 36 “MODs 8 through 11,” guidance section AN/DSQ-29, “Argon-cooled Indium Antimonide (InSb) seeker,” DSU-15/B laser proximity fuze (Active Optical Target Detector), WDU-17/B warhead 9.4 kg (20.8 lb) annular blast-fragmentation. “Production started in 1978”; more than 16,000 built, including BGT in Germany and Mitsubishi in Japan. Used by the Royal Navy in the Falklands, 1982. Table, shared with M: length 2.85 m (112.2 in), finspan 0.63 m (24.8 in), diameter 12.7 cm, weight 86 kg (191 lb). The table’s “40+ km (22+ nm) ?” cell is printed with a question mark and the column alignment does not clearly belong to the L; it is not used. — [Parsch](https://www.designation-systems.net/dusrm/m-9.html)
- An Air Force fact-sheet index excerpt (page fetch was denied) says “Production and delivery of the AIM-9L began in 1976” and that the L “was the first Sidewinder with the ability to attack from all angles, including head-on,” with a conical-scan seeker. That 1976 date conflicts with Parsch’s 1978 production start. — [USAF fact sheet](https://www.af.mil/About-Us/Fact-Sheets/Display/Article/104557/)
- Kopp: “essentially an AIM-9H with a new optical system, new fuse and new cooling system.” InSb “4 micron band,” filter rejecting shorter wavelengths, argon bottle inside the missile so the round is not tied to a launcher bottle. FM reticle. “Design changes were adopted to bridge the dead zone about the missile’s immediate boresight.” “Lambda compensation” keeps the seeker off its angular stop early in flight. Canards changed to pointed double-deltas; H actuators retained. Entered service “in the late seventies.” Falklands and 1982 Bekaa Valley “kill probabilities in excess of 80%” is Kopp’s figure, not a primary shot-tally opened here. “Over 5,500 rounds were built” conflicts with Parsch’s later “more than 16,000.” Table: track rate “classified,” reticle 125 Hz, FM, solid state, annular blast-fragmentation, “IR/Laser” fuze, Hercules/Bermite Mk 36 Mod 7, 8 (narrower than Parsch’s Mods 8–11), “Common” launcher, length 9.5 ft (2.896 m [derived]), span 2.1 ft (0.640 m [derived]), weight 191 lb (86.6 kg [derived]). — [Kopp 1994](https://www.ausairpower.net/TE-Sidewinder-94.html)
- Fuze behavior [Kopp]: DSU-15A/B (he uses the A suffix; Parsch uses DSU-15/B) uses gallium-arsenide lasers in a spoke pattern around the missile, each paired with a silicon photodiode; reflected pulses trigger the warhead. He describes it as “highly resistant to jamming.” Warhead: annular blast with two layers of rods “designed to tumble,” specifically to avoid the impact-angle limit of a continuous rod. A Navy training page identifies the WDU-17/B as the AIM-9L warhead: case, two booster plates, initiator, high explosive, and fragmentation rods; Mk 36 Mod 7 is a single grain; wings are Mk 1 with a rolleron on each. Lethal radius [unknown]. Explosive fill mass [unknown]. — [Kopp 1994](https://www.ausairpower.net/TE-Sidewinder-94.html); [Navy training page](http://navyaviation.tpub.com/14313/css/Sidewinder-Guided-Missile-86.htm)
- Seeker: all-aspect, so head-on lock is possible, but neither rear nor head-on acquisition range in kilometres was published in the sources opened. Gimbal limit, IFOV, and track rate [unknown / classified per Kopp]. Wikipedia’s 22°/s for the L was seen in a search snippet of a table that otherwise copies Kopp, who printed “classified”; the 22°/s footnote was not opened and is not used. Lock before launch is the described mode (acquisition tone family). LOAL and a helmet cue angle [unknown / not described for the L]. Off-boresight half-angle [unknown].
- IRCCM [Kopp]: the FM reticle “provid[es] the potential for better countermeasures rejection” and reduces the effect of the target growing in the field of view as range closes. He also says an optical filter rejects shorter wavelengths. He does not describe an intensity-rise gate, a two-color detector, or an imaging discriminator. Class in published terms: FM-reticle processing with a stated potential for better rejection, not imaging and not two-color. A stronger “flare proof” claim was not in the sources used.
- Motor: Mk 36, mods as above. The AIM-9D handbook’s 3500 lbf / 5 s is not applied, because the L mods and the later reduced-smoke grain are different articles. Burn time, thrust, impulse, propellant mass [unknown]. Smoke: Kopp identifies the low-smoke motor as an AIM-9M change, so the L motor is not the reduced-smoke motor; a grade of “heavy” was not printed. Profile: the Mk 36 descriptions that were opened (D handbook single-stage; ATK “all-boost” for the later M motor) support boost-only, not dual-pulse. Sustainer [not described].
- Speed and aerodynamic range with a stated altitude and Mach [unknown]. Parsch’s unconditioned 18 km belongs to his D/G/H column, not cleanly to the L. No-escape zone [unknown]. Minimum range [unknown]. Arming delay in seconds [unknown].
- Control: pointed double-delta canards, rollerons on the wings (Navy training page), no TVC. Maximum g [unknown]. “Less susceptible” maneuver claims in secondary articles had no number that survived source screening.
- USAF fact-sheet general-characteristics block, if it is read as the then-standard Sidewinder rather than the X: Hercules/Bermite Mk 36 Mod 11, length 9 ft 5 in (2.87 m), diameter 5 in (0.13 m), finspan 2 ft 0.75 in (0.63 m), launch weight 190 lb (the sheet’s own conversion “85.5 kilograms” is arithmetically inconsistent with 190 lb, which is 86.2 kg [derived]). That block matches the M-era motor mod better than the early L. It is cited here only as a conflicting official geometry, not as a clean L card. — [USAF fact sheet, indexed excerpt](https://www.af.mil/About-Us/Fact-Sheets/Display/Article/104557/)
- 2026: the 2025 active list names the M, not the L. Parsch says the M replaced the L on the production line. Whether any air force still holds unmodified Ls in 2026 was not established. — [Air & Space Forces Magazine](https://www.airandspaceforces.com/weapons/aim-9/); [Parsch](https://www.designation-systems.net/dusrm/m-9.html)

### Inferences
- Flight character: all-aspect InSb seeker on the Navy canard/rolleron airframe, one Mk 36 burn then coast, laser fuze, annular-rod warhead. It is not a thrust-vectoring missile and it is not the reduced-smoke M.
- Length conflict to carry in a sim data card: Kopp 9.5 ft (2.90 m) versus Parsch 112.2 in (2.85 m) versus the Air Force sheet 9 ft 5 in (2.87 m). Mass cluster is 190–191 lb. Do not collapse these to one “true” value.

### Gaps
- Conditioned maximum range, speed, g, seeker gimbal, IFOV, track rate, acquisition ranges, arming time, minimum range, no-escape zone, propellant mass, L-mod thrust and burn time, smoke grade, and a 2026 inventory statement.

## AIM-9M

### Takeaway
AIM-9L with a reduced-smoke all-boost Mk 36, a revised guidance section, and published IRCCM/background-rejection behavior whose circuit type is not described. Still the US inventory’s “early” active Sidewinder alongside AIM-9X in a 2025 directory. IOC is about 1983. Thrust and burn time remain unpublished.

### Cited Findings
- Parsch: reduced-smoke rocket motor, guidance section WGU-4/B, “better countermeasures resistance (IRCCM),” higher reliability. Production began 1982; more than 7000 built in subtypes M-1 through M-10. Principal versions he names are AIM-9M-8 (Navy) and AIM-9M-9 (Air Force), with further IRCM-detection circuitry, Mk 36 Mod 11, WGU-4E/B, and AOTD DSU-15B/B. AIM-9M-10 is an M-8 adapted to the F/A-18E/F. Desert Storm: 13 Sidewinder kills, “all of which were probably AIM-9M.” Same dimension row as the L: 2.85 m, finspan 0.63 m, 86 kg. — [Parsch](https://www.designation-systems.net/dusrm/m-9.html)
- USAF fact-sheet excerpt: deliveries of the M “began in 1983.” “Improved defense against infrared countermeasures, enhanced background discrimination capability, and a reduced-smoke rocket motor.” AIM-9M-9 “has expanded infrared countermeasures detection circuitry.” The sheet’s characteristics block lists Hercules and Bermite Mk 36 Mod 11, length 9 ft 5 in (2.87 m), diameter 5 in (0.13 m), finspan 2 ft 0.75 in (0.63 m), annular blast-fragmentation warhead, launch weight 190 lb (sheet’s 85.5 kg conversion does not match 190 lb). — [USAF fact sheet, indexed excerpt](https://www.af.mil/About-Us/Fact-Sheets/Display/Article/104557/)
- Air & Space Forces directory: IOC “circa 1983 (9M)”; delivered “AIM-9M 1983”; propulsion “Mk 36 Mod 11 (9M)”; active variants include AIM-9M and AIM-9M-9. Performance line for the directory entry is only “Speed Mach 2+, range 10+ miles,” with no altitude or Mach condition, and it is not clearly separated from the AIM-9X. Not used as an M-only envelope. — [Air & Space Forces Magazine](https://www.airandspaceforces.com/weapons/aim-9/)
- Kopp: production follow-on in 1982; “improved background rejection, counter-countermeasures capability and a low smoke motor.” Same table geometry as the L (9.5 ft, span 2.1 ft, 191 lb). Motor row: MTI/Hercules Mk 36 Mod 9. Seeker row identical in kind to the L: InSb, argon, MgF2, 125 Hz, FM, track rate classified. Warhead annular blast-fragmentation, fuze IR/laser. He says the M scored all of “10” Gulf Sidewinder kills, conflicting with Parsch’s “13” and “probably.” — [Kopp 1994](https://www.ausairpower.net/TE-Sidewinder-94.html)
- ATK public datasheet (release marking 3/98), Mk 36 as the AIM-9M motor [published]: “single-thrust propulsion system”; “All-boost thrust profile”; “Radial burn, finocyl propellant grain”; propellant “reduced smoke HTPB”; weight 99.0 lb (44.9 kg [derived]) for the motor, not the grain; length 71.0 in (1.803 m [derived]); diameter 5.0 in; case 4130 steel; nozzle glass phenolic; igniter pyrogen; operating and storage limits −65°F to +160°F. No thrust, burn time, impulse, or propellant mass is on the sheet. “Over 12,000 reduced smoke (RS) propulsion units” delivered to the US Navy is a production count, not a performance figure. — [ATK Mk 36 datasheet, Wayback](https://web.archive.org/web/20060311205034/http://www.atk.com/datasheet_PDFs/sidewinder_mk36.pdf)
- NAVAIR, describing the AIM-9X, says the X “incorporates many AIM-9M legacy components (rocket motor, warhead and active optical target detector).” That supports continuity of the M motor and the WDU-17/B-class warhead into the X, not a new published impulse. — [NAVAIR AIM-9X](https://www.navair.navy.mil/product/AIM-9X-Sidewinder)
- Seeker: all-aspect InSb, argon cooled, same optical class as the L. Rear and head-on acquisition ranges [unknown]. Gimbal, IFOV, track rate [unknown / classified]. Lock before launch; LOAL not described for the M. Helmet cue [not described].
- IRCCM class: published as IRCCM plus background discrimination and, on the M-9, “expanded infrared countermeasures detection circuitry.” The open descriptions do not say intensity-rise rejection, kinematic gate, two-color, or imaging. Those subclasses are [unknown], not assumed. Behavior to model only at the level the sources state: better rejection of infrared countermeasures and better discrimination against background than the L, plus a smaller visual smoke plume.
- Control: same canard and rolleron layout as the L. No TVC. g limit [unknown]. Arming delay [unknown]. Minimum range [unknown]. No-escape zone [unknown].
- In service as of the 2025 directory, alongside AIM-9X. Still a US active variant name in 2025–2026 sources; it is no longer the missile on the 2026 production contracts, which are Block II/II+. — [Air & Space Forces Magazine](https://www.airandspaceforces.com/weapons/aim-9/)

### Inferences
- Flight character: L-class all-aspect canard missile with one reduced-smoke all-boost burn and then coast, plus stronger published countermeasure resistance than the L. Do not invent a 5 s / 3500 lbf burn for the HTPB motor; the only public motor card gives mass and an all-boost profile, not impulse.
- Early M motors are Mod 9 in Kopp (1994); M-8/M-9 are Mod 11 in Parsch. Both can be true of different lots. The ATK sheet does not give a mod number beyond the Mk 36 nameplate in the photo caption area (“MK 36 MOD 9” is visible on the hardware photo label).

### Gaps
- Thrust, burn time, total impulse, propellant mass. Conditioned range and speed. g. Seeker angles and acquisition ranges. Arming time. The actual IRCCM algorithm. How many AIM-9Ms remain in US squadrons in 2026 versus stored.

## AIM-9P-5

### Takeaway
Included because it is not an L/M. It is the USAF/export J/N-line missile (conical nose, 10 ft length, 1.9 ft span, SR.116 motor) after the P-4 added an all-aspect InSb seeker and the P-5 added IRCCM. It is less agile than the L on Kopp’s qualitative statement. A numeric IOC for the P-5 itself was not found. 2026 inventory was not found.

### Cited Findings
- Parsch: USAF development of the J/N “mainly intended for export,” though “many AIM-9Ps are in the USAF inventory.” P-1 introduced the DSU-15/B laser fuze. P-2 adds a reduced-smoke motor. P-3 has the reduced-smoke motor, an insensitive-munitions warhead, and an improved guidance section; sources disagree on whether the P-3 kept the J’s IR fuze. “AIM-9P-4 applies to variants with an ALASCA seeker using some of the technology of the AIM-9L/M, and the AIM-9P-5 adds improved IRCCM.” Externally “almost identical to the AIM-9J/N.” More than 21,000 P-series missiles built, many rebuilt from B/E/J. He does not give a P-5 service date. — [Parsch](https://www.designation-systems.net/dusrm/m-9.html)

Table for P-4/5: USAF, origin AIM-9J/N, InSb, argon, MgF2, reticle 100 Hz, FM, track rate >16.5°/s, solid state, annular blast-fragmentation, IR/laser fuze, Hercules/Aerojet SR.116, “Common” launcher, length 10.0 ft (3.048 m [derived]), span 1.9 ft (0.579 m [derived]), weight 190.0 lb (86.2 kg [derived]). — [Kopp 1994](https://www.ausairpower.net/TE-Sidewinder-94.html)

The length conflict is noted and Kopp’s 10.0 ft is the figure tied to the author who built the table. — [Wikipedia AIM-9, search excerpt of the table](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIM-9_Sidewinder)
- Wikipedia narrative excerpt: P-4 “Introduced the ALASCA features and technology found on the AIM-9L/M variants.” P-5 “Added improved IRCCM from the AIM-9M.” Base AIM-9P “Deliveries … began in 1978” appears in that article; it is not a P-5 date. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIM-9_Sidewinder)
- All-aspect, so head-on lock exists and the head-on range is not zero, but no range number was published. Rear-aspect acquisition range [unknown]. Gimbal and IFOV [unknown]. Track rate only “>16.5°/s.” Lock before launch; LOAL and helmet cue not described.

Smoke: P-2/P-3 are the variants Parsch explicitly calls reduced-smoke; whether the SR.116 on the P-5 is that reduced-smoke motor is not spelled out on Kopp’s row. Smoke [unknown] for the P-5 specifically.
- Warhead: annular blast-fragmentation, mass [unknown] (not given the WDU-17/B 9.4 kg label on Kopp’s P row). Fuze IR/laser. Lethal radius [unknown]. Arming delay [unknown].
- Control: J-line double-delta canards, no TVC, g only “less agile” than L. Rail: “Common” launcher [Kopp]. Inherits shooter velocity. Ejection speed [unknown].

### Inferences
- Flight character: all-aspect seeker on the long Air Force double-delta airframe, one solid-motor burn then coast, with P-5 countermeasure resistance and less maneuver authority than an L. It is a different drag and motor model from the L/M and should not be a skin of the M.

## AIM-9X Block I

### Takeaway
US imaging-infrared Sidewinder that reached Air Force IOC in November 2003. Official geometry is NAVAIR’s: 9.9 ft, 186 lb, 5 in diameter, 17.6 in wingspan, ATK Mk 139. Range and speed are classified. The seeker is a 128×128 mid-wave staring array; high off-boresight and jet-vane thrust vectoring are published, a deflection angle and a g limit are not. Block I production ended when Block II became the baseline. Block I is not what the US is buying in 2026.

### Cited Findings
- Parsch: first production missiles reached evaluation units in summer 2002. Air Force IOC November 2003. Full-rate production approved May 2004. Retains “the MK 36 motor and the WDU-17/B warhead of the AIM-9M.” New airframe, “much smaller fins and canards,” imaging-infrared seeker, WPU-17/B propulsion section with “a jet-vane steering system.” Fits F-22 and F-35 bays and existing launchers “LAU-7/A series, and the LAU-127/A, -128/A and -129/A.” Compatible with JHMCS. His 2008 table: length 3.02 m (118.8 in), finspan 0.28 m (11 in), weight 85 kg (188 lb), diameter 12.7 cm. He warns the table may be inaccurate. The 11 in finspan conflicts with NAVAIR’s 17.6 in wingspan and is not used as the span. — [Parsch](https://www.designation-systems.net/dusrm/m-9.html)
- NAVAIR product page [published]: “high off-boresight focal-plane array seeker,” “highly maneuverable airframe,” “greatly improved infrared counter-countermeasures.” Incorporates AIM-9M “rocket motor, warhead and active optical target detector.” “Unlike previous AIM-9 models, the AIM-9X can even be used against targets on the ground.” Specifications printed on that page (they are not split by block): power plant “ATK MK-139 solid-propellant rocket motor”; length “9.9 feet (3.02 meters)”; launch weight “186 pounds (84.37 kg)”; diameter “5 inches (0.13 meters)” (5.00 in is 0.127 m [derived]; 0.13 m is the page’s rounding); wingspan “17.6 inches (0.45 meters)” (17.6 in is 0.447 m [derived]); range “Classified”; speed “Classified”; “Annular blast fragmentation” warhead. — [NAVAIR](https://www.navair.navy.mil/product/AIM-9X-Sidewinder)
- 2001 Navy NTSP (development description, before the Mk 139 name appeared on the NAVAIR page): propulsion and steering section modifies the AIM-9M Mk 36 Mod 11 to carry the control actuation section and a mid-body umbilical; the section “is designated the WPU-17/B.” Propellant stated there as “type X-61 (AS 6065) solid composite.” Four forward fixed titanium wings; four in-line titanium control fins. “The CAS provides thrust vectoring by using four jet vanes to direct the flow of the rocket motor exhaust.” Warhead is the AIM-9M annular blast-fragmentation warhead with a new electronic safe-and-arm device “to arm the warhead after launch.” Seeker is a “mid-wave IR Focal Plane Array,” replacing the AIM-9M single-element detector, with a cryoengine in the guidance center section (no rail nitrogen bottle and no argon bottle). Acquisition methods described are boresight (point the aircraft) and SEAM (slave to the radar), with an audio tone when the seeker acquires. A numeric gimbal is not printed; the text says the aircraft can slave the seeker “up to a given number of degrees” and the missile tracks “up to its seeker gimbal limits.” — [NAVAIR NTSP, June 2001, via GlobalSecurity](https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/library/policy/navy/ntsp/aim-9x-a_2001.pdf)
- RAND MG-109 (2004), drawing on the AIM-9X Selected Acquisition Report of 31 December 1999 and the program office: fixed forward wings and a new lower-drag airframe sized for F-22 and JSF internal bays; tail control actuation provides thrust vectoring and movable fins; “A 128 by 128 focal plane array (instead of the AIM-9M’s single-element IR detector) produces a digital image for use as a tracker algorithm and enables the system’s robust IR counter-countermeasures.” Electronics provide digital processing, IRCCM logic, fly-out guidance, and field reprogrammability. JHMCS was a related but separate program. — [RAND MG-109](https://www.rand.org/content/dam/rand/pubs/monographs/2004/RAND_MG109.pdf)
- Flight Global, 27 June 2000, quoting Raytheon senior manager Bill West (search-index excerpt; the article body did not load on a direct fetch): the AIM-9X and ASRAAM “share a common Raytheon developed imaging infrared (I²R) seeker with a 180° field of view,” “based around a 128 × 128 element staring focal plane array.” He said the array lets the missile “differentiate between the target and countermeasures” and that the seeker “reassesses the view hundreds of times a second.” The article does not label 180° as a gimbal half-angle versus a total field of regard, and it does not publish an instantaneous field of view. — [Flight Global](https://www.flightglobal.com/short-range-square-off/32654.article)
- Off-boresight: NAVAIR says “high off-boresight” and does not print degrees. Secondary pages say “claimed 90° off-boresight” (the wording “claimed” is in the China Lake museum archive summary and in Wikipedia). A 90° half-angle would be a 180° total field of regard, which matches the Flight Global phrase, but that equality is [derived], not a sentence those sources write. TVC vane deflection angle [unknown]. Missile maximum g [unknown]. Wikipedia’s “60 g” was not tied to an opened primary and is not used.
- GlobalSecurity, citing the program office: Air Force IOC November 2003 at Elmendorf AFB; Navy and Marine IOC February 2004 at Iwakuni; full-rate production authorized 17 May 2004. — [GlobalSecurity AIM-9X](https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/systems/munitions/aim-9x.htm)
- Air & Space Forces directory: delivered “AIM-9X from 2002–2011 (Block I).” FY27 budget narrative: Block I ended when the Block II acquisition baseline was approved on 23 December 2011. — [Air & Space Forces Magazine](https://www.airandspaceforces.com/weapons/aim-9/); [FY27 Air Force missile procurement narrative](https://www.af.mil/Portals/1/documents/Secretariat%20of%20the%20AF/SAF-FM/Budget%20-%202027/Budget%20docs/FY27%20Air%20Force%20Aircraft%20%20Missile%20Procurement.pdf?ver=6zKfXa_IITL18fK0xVVdIA%3D%3D)
- Motor relationship: NTSP says modified Mk 36 Mod 11 / WPU-17/B; NAVAIR’s current power-plant line is ATK Mk 139; Parsch in 2008 still said the X “retains the MK 36.” No source opened shows a new impulse, burn time, or propellant mass for the renamed motor. Profile of the parent Mk 36 is all-boost; the X adds jet vanes in that exhaust. Dual-pulse [not described]. Smoke: the parent M motor is reduced-smoke HTPB; the NTSP grain name is X-61, and a smoke grade for X-61 was not printed. Propellant mass [unknown].
- Warhead: annular blast-fragmentation, the AIM-9M warhead with a new electronic safe-and-arm. WDU-17/B mass 9.4 kg (20.8 lb) is the M/L figure Parsch says is retained; NAVAIR does not restate the mass. Lethal radius [unknown]. Arming: ESAD “after launch”; time or distance [unknown]. The device is described as allowing a shorter minimum range than the older safe-and-arm only in Wikipedia, which is not used as the number. Minimum range [unknown].
- Guidance mode for Block I: lock before launch by boresight or radar slave, audio tone (NTSP). Lock-on after launch and a datalink are the Block II additions, not this block. Helmet: JHMCS compatible [Parsch, RAND]. Cue angle in degrees [unknown].
- IRCCM class: imaging / spatial. Published behavior is a staring image used by a tracker, with explicit claims that this image is what provides countermeasure resistance and target-versus-flare discrimination, refreshed many times a second. Not described as two-color. The detector is a single mid-wave array.
- Rail: LAU-7/A and LAU-127/128/129 [Parsch]. Forward rail launch; inherits shooter velocity. Ejection speed [unknown].
- Country: United States. Contractor on the NAVAIR page: Raytheon.

### Inferences
- Flight character: lock on the rail out to a large off-boresight angle, leave on the shooter’s velocity, and use jet vanes plus tail fins for a hard turn while the motor is burning, then fly on the tail fins. Coast after a single burn. Rear-aspect-only logic does not apply. Do not assign a Mach or a range; NAVAIR marks both classified.
- Mk 139 is treated as the service name of the vectored AIM-9M-derived motor, not as a second published impulse class, because NAVAIR still calls the rocket motor an AIM-9M legacy component.

### Gaps
- Unclassified range, speed, g, TVC angle, gimbal half-angle as an official number, IFOV, track rate, acquisition ranges, arming time, minimum range, no-escape zone, propellant mass, burn time, thrust, and how many Block I rounds remain in 2026.

## AIM-9X Block II

### Takeaway
The US short-range missile in service and in production in 2026. It adds a datalink and lock-on-after-launch so the round can be fired at targets the seeker has not locked, including targets behind the fighter. Full-rate production was August 2015. Air & Space Forces gives IOC as September 2016. No separate mass, motor, or seeker-array size was published; the NAVAIR specification block above is the one official geometry.

### Cited Findings
- NAVAIR: “The AIM-9X Block II is the most advanced short-range air-air missile in the U.S. inventory, capable of using its datalink, thrust vectoring maneuverability, and advanced imaging infrared seeker to hit targets behind the launching fighter.” Same specification block as the family entry (Mk 139, 9.9 ft, 186 lb, 17.6 in span, range and speed classified, annular blast-fragmentation). — [NAVAIR](https://www.navair.navy.mil/product/AIM-9X-Sidewinder)
- FY27 Air Force procurement narrative: Milestone C / low-rate initial production for Block II was established in June 2011. Full-rate production approved August 2015. The Block I program ended with the Block II baseline of 23 December 2011. — [FY27 Air Force missile procurement narrative](https://www.af.mil/Portals/1/documents/Secretariat%20of%20the%20AF/SAF-FM/Budget%20-%202027/Budget%20docs/FY27%20Air%20Force%20Aircraft%20%20Missile%20Procurement.pdf?ver=6zKfXa_IITL18fK0xVVdIA%3D%3D)
- GAO-16-329SP: Block II operational testing completed in March 2015 and the missile “was found to be operationally effective and suitable,” with four further deficiencies in reliability, software, and performance still being worked. Full-rate production slipped from April 2014 to August 2015. — [GAO-16-329SP](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-16-329sp.pdf)
- Air & Space Forces directory: IOC “September 2016 (9X Block II)”; delivered “2011–present (Block II)”; Block II “adds improved lock-after-launch and maneuverability, a new data link for beyond-visual range engagement, enhanced anti-countermeasures, a new fuse, and safer ground-handling characteristics.” Directory propulsion line for the X is “Orbital ATK Mk 139.” Planned buy “11,635 (Block II/Block II+).” — [Air & Space Forces Magazine](https://www.airandspaceforces.com/weapons/aim-9/)
- Federal Register technology note (indexed excerpt): Block II and Block II+ are “a substantial increase in missile acquisition and kinematics performance over the AIM-9M” and replace Block I. Features named: “high off-boresight seeker, enhanced countermeasure rejection capability, low drag/high angle of attack airframe,” and helmet-cueing integration. No degrees, no Mach, no range. — [Federal Register, 21 Nov 2024](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2024-11-21/html/2024-27300.htm)
- DOT&E FY2025 AIM-9X report (file dated in search as 18 March 2026): Block II is the current operational missile. Fielded software cited as OFS 9.411, with OFS 9.501 under test. Early development of System Improvement Program IV would replace “the seeker sensor from an analog to a digital focal plane array,” add a new cryo-engine, and rehost electronics. That digital array is not described as fielded in the report. — [DOT&E FY2025 AIM-9X](https://www.dote.osd.mil/Portals/97/pub/reports/FY2025/navy/2025aim-9x.pdf?ver=O7Vy4QbyMXmJzV8egmptRQ%3D%3D)
- GAO: Block III’s enhanced infrared-countermeasure work was transferred into Block II SIP III under a September 2015 contract. So later Block II software/hardware is not frozen at the 2015 OT configuration, but the public record does not give a new array size or a new motor impulse for SIP III. — [GAO-16-329SP](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-16-329sp.pdf)
- Seeker, motor, warhead, span, mass, TVC hardware: no Block II-specific numbers different from Block I were opened. 128×128 and the analog mid-wave array remain the last published detector descriptions; DOT&E’s “analog” FPA note is the 2025/2026 status of the fielded sensor. IRCCM: imaging/spatial, plus the directory’s “enhanced anti-countermeasures” and the Federal Register’s “enhanced countermeasure rejection,” still without a published circuit class beyond imaging.
- LOAL: yes, via the datalink [NAVAIR and the directory]. Lock before launch is not described as removed. Helmet cue remains JHMCS-class; cue angle [unknown]. Targets behind the fighter are explicitly in the NAVAIR sentence, which is a kinematic employment claim, not a range.
- In service and in production in 2026. Lot 26 reporting in June 2026 covers Block II tactical missiles for US services with deliveries extending to September 2029. — [Defence Blog, 26 Jun 2026](https://defence-blog.com/raytheon-secures-1-1b-deal-for-aim-9x-missile-production/)
- Country: United States. Joint Navy-led program.

### Inferences
- Flight character: same vectored, imaging airframe as Block I, but it may leave the rail before the seeker is locked and fly out on the datalink, including toward the rear hemisphere, then acquire with the imaging seeker. Still one burn then coast. Still no unclassified range or speed.
- “Increased kinematics over the AIM-9M” is a government comparison without a number. It does not justify inserting a Mach or a kilometre figure.

### Gaps
- Any Block II delta in mass, length, thrust, burn time, g, or seeker format. Arming time of the “new fuse.” No-escape zone. Acquisition ranges. TVC angle. How much of SIP III/IV is actually in the 2026 fleet versus still in test (OFS 9.501 was still in test in the FY2025 report).

## AIM-9X Block II+

### Takeaway
A real third production variant, bought from December 2017 for the F-35 and still on US and export contracts in 2024–2026. Open sources describe it as the Block II improvement set “optimized for the F-35,” not as a new motor or a new seeker generation. No separate physical or kinematic numbers were published. It is not Block III.

### Cited Findings
- FY27 Air Force narrative: “In December 2017, the program began to procure a third variant of the missile labeled the AIM-9X Block II PLUS (Block II+) to satisfy the requirements of the Joint Strike Fighter platform. This version of the missile is being produced on the same production line as the AIM-9X Block II missile.” The revised program total cited there is 11,635 missiles for Navy and Air Force combined, not a Block II+ subset. — [FY27 Air Force missile procurement narrative](https://www.af.mil/Portals/1/documents/Secretariat%20of%20the%20AF/SAF-FM/Budget%20-%202027/Budget%20docs/FY27%20Air%20Force%20Aircraft%20%20Missile%20Procurement.pdf?ver=6zKfXa_IITL18fK0xVVdIA%3D%3D)
- Air & Space Forces: “2017–present (Block II+)”; “The Block II+ incorporates the same improvements but is specifically optimized for the F-35.” — [Air & Space Forces Magazine](https://www.airandspaceforces.com/weapons/aim-9/)
- Federal Register, 21 November 2024: notified sale to Korea of AIM-9X Block II+ tactical missiles and Block II+ guidance units, with the sensitivity note covering “Block II and Block II+” together (high off-boresight seeker, enhanced countermeasure rejection, low-drag high-angle-of-attack airframe, helmet cueing). — [Federal Register](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2024-11-21/html/2024-27300.htm)
- June 2026 contract reporting: Lot 26 includes both Block II tactical missiles and Block II+ missiles, the latter identified in that article as AIM-9X-5 for foreign military sales and Block II as AIM-9X-4. That AIM-9X-4 / AIM-9X-5 pairing is a press reading of the contract, not a NAVAIR fact sheet. The same article’s claim of a further ECCM improvement specific to the II+ is not in the Federal Register note, which treats II and II+ sensitivity text as one paragraph. The ECCM-delta sentence is not used as a performance fact. — [Defence Blog, 26 Jun 2026](https://defence-blog.com/raytheon-secures-1-1b-deal-for-aim-9x-missile-production/)
- Geometry, motor, seeker, warhead, speed, range, g, arming time: no II+-only figure was found. Use the Block II / NAVAIR card and mark the lack of a delta as [unknown], not as identity proven by a measurement.

### Inferences
- Flight character: same as Block II (datalink, LOAL, imaging seeker, jet-vane snap on a single burn) with whatever interface changes the F-35 requirement drove. A sim should not give II+ a longer range or a different motor unless a later public number appears.
- It is fielded in the sense of multi-year production and notified export, not a cancelled or paper block.

### Gaps
- Every numeric parameter that would distinguish II+ from II: mass, length, span, impulse, seeker, fuze, software block, cue angle, and kinematics. The AIM-9X-4 versus AIM-9X-5 nomenclature needs a primary document.

## AIM-9X Block III (not fielded)

### Takeaway
Cancelled. Do not model it as a 2026 weapon.

### Cited Findings
- GAO-16-329SP: “The AIM-9X Block III program was cancelled in fiscal year 2014.” Planned work on lethality, infrared countermeasures, and insensitive munitions was moved into Block II SIP III. — [GAO-16-329SP](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-16-329sp.pdf)
- Wikipedia, summarizing the pre-cancellation plan: a Navy projection of about 60 percent more range, an insensitive-munitions warhead, and reuse of the Block II guidance unit and datalink, with a then-planned IOC in 2022. The FY2016 budget, released in 2015, cancelled the effort as F-35C buys were cut. None of those performance projections is a flight result. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIM-9_Sidewinder)

### Inferences
- None. The 60 percent range figure is a cancelled goal, not a missile.

### Gaps
- No flight-test card exists because the variant was not fielded.

## Cross-cutting gaps for a physics model

### Takeaway
A defensible sim can separate these rounds by seeker generation, control method, and the two motors that actually have public impulse data (AIM-9B Mk 15/17 and AIM-9D Mk 36). From the L onward, motor impulse, missile g, and engagement range are not public at the fidelity the earlier handbooks give.

### Cited Findings
- Only two motor time-thrust pairs were read from primary documents: AIM-9B, 8440 lbf·s and 2.2 s at 70°F (OP 2309); AIM-9D Mk 36, average 3500 lbf for 5 s (OP 3353). The AIM-9M ATK sheet adds all-boost, reduced-smoke HTPB, and 99.0 lb motor mass, without impulse. — [OP 2309](https://archive.org/details/OP23093rdAIM9B); [OP 3353 text](https://archive.org/stream/op-2309-2nd-sidewinder-guided-missle-mark-2/OP%203353%20AIM-9C%20&D%20PIlot%27s%20Handbook_djvu.txt); [ATK datasheet](https://web.archive.org/web/20060311205034/http://www.atk.com/datasheet_PDFs/sidewinder_mk36.pdf)
- NAVAIR classifies AIM-9X range and speed. — [NAVAIR](https://www.navair.navy.mil/product/AIM-9X-Sidewinder)
- No opened source states a propellant mass, a no-escape zone, an AIM-9X TVC deflection, or an AIM-9L/M/X structural g.

### Inferences
- Where a later variant uses “the Mk 36” without a new time-thrust pair, the honest model input is “unknown,” not the 1960s 3500 lbf / 5 s line and not a forum burn time.

### Gaps
- Propellant mass for every variant. Sea-level static thrust curves. Structural g versus available g. Seeker acquisition ranges versus target aspect and altitude for every variant after the B. Arming time in seconds for every variant after the B (the B and D publish feet, not a single time).
