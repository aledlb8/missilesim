# Soviet and Russian infrared air-to-air missiles (Fox 2)

Civilian kinematic and seeker dossier. Every figure below was taken from a page opened for this note, or is marked [derived] from those figures. Radar-guided rounds (R-3R, R-27R/ER, R-77) are omitted except where a modular family sentence is needed to avoid mis-assigning a radar-only feature to the infrared round. Tag legend: [published] = printed on the cited page; [derived] = arithmetic from printed figures, shown; [unknown] = not in any page opened here. Conflicting printed numbers are both kept. No-escape range was not printed for any missile below.

Pages that were indexed by search but did not return a readable body when opened are not used as facts. That includes the live `airwar.ru` weapon pages (encoding failure), the ZBIAM 2023 R-74M2 article (fetch failed), and `designation-systems.net/non-us/soviet.html` (now only a redirect). NATO letter suffixes that appear only in that unread index are not treated as confirmed.

## K-13A / R-3S (AA-2 Atoll)

### Takeaway

Soviet rear-aspect copy of the AIM-9B, rail-launched, uncooled lead-sulfide spin-scan seeker, boost-only motor of about 20.5 kg that is finished in roughly 2–3 s, then a coast under proportional navigation for about 21 s of control power. It is not an all-aspect dogfight missile. As of the opened catalogs it is a limited or second-line weapon, not a current Russian first-line Fox 2.

### Cited Findings

- Country and names: USSR. Missile R-3S, complex K-13A, factory items 310 and 310A. NATO reporting name AA-2 Atoll. Designer OKB-134 under I.I. Toropov. [published] — [Missilery R-3S](https://en.missilery.info/missile/r3c); [Weaponsystems R-3](https://weaponsystems.net/system/1536-Vympel+R-3)
- Service year: state tests of the improved round finished at the end of June 1961; the 2 March 1962 MiG-21PF adoption decision fixed the characteristics of K-13A, named R-3S. Series production of an earlier K-13 is described as 1960, with R-3S the large-series round from 1962. A 22 s homing-preparation time versus 11 s on the first article is stated, without defining what is timed. [published] — [Missilery R-3S](https://en.missilery.info/missile/r3c); [GlobalSecurity AA-2](https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/world/russia/aa-2.htm)
- 2026 status: Weaponsystems lists status as “Limited service” and says the R-13M and R-60 made the R-3S obsolete in Soviet service. The older Weaponsystems page says it “remains in widespread second line use” on older fighters. USSR series production “completely curtailed by 1980”; exports “continued until the end of the 80s”; “not being removed from service” by the end of the 1980s. No opened 2026 inventory. [published] — [Weaponsystems R-3](https://weaponsystems.net/system/1536-Vympel+R-3); [old Weaponsystems K-13](https://old.weaponsystems.net/weaponsystem/HH07%20-%20AA-2%20Atoll.html); [Missilery R-3S](https://en.missilery.info/missile/r3c)
- Length 2838 mm, diameter 127 mm, wingspan 528 mm, launch mass 75.3 kg. [published] — [Missilery R-3S](https://en.missilery.info/missile/r3c). Same figures on [Weaponsystems R-3](https://weaponsystems.net/system/1536-Vympel+R-3).
- Warhead mass 11.3 kg. Explosive TGAF-5. The warhead-compartment page prints explosive mass as 5.2 kg; Weaponsystems prints 5.3 kg of TGAF-5. Fragment count not less than 1000, average fragment 2.8 g, 90% of fragments in a 10–16° flight angle, initial fragment speed 1800 m/s. “Radius of effective target engagement” 10–11 m. [published] — [Missilery R-3S warhead](https://en.missilery.info/missile/r3c/3-ots); [Weaponsystems R-3](https://weaponsystems.net/system/1536-Vympel+R-3)
- Propellant: PRD-80A charge of NMF-2, described as nitroglycerin fuel, mass 20.5 kg with the inhibitor wrap. Single-channel cylinder with an 8-point star bore, inhibited outside, so it burns on the inner surface. The page says the star “allows for approximately constant pressure.” K2SO4 is added “to reduce the impact of the engine jet on the aircraft carrier,” not described as a smoke suppressant. Uncharged engine 14.8 kg. [published] — [Missilery PRD-80A](https://en.missilery.info/missile/r3c/5-ots); [Missilery R-3S](https://en.missilery.info/missile/r3c)
- Total impulse: missile page “Full engine pulse, Ns 38000-40000”; motor page “not less than 38100” N·s. [published] — same two URLs.
- Thrust, printed on the motor page under the mistranslated heading “Drag, N”: maximum 25000 N; minimum 10000 N at charge temperature −54°C. Burn time 1.7 s at +60°C and 3.2 s at −54°C. [published] — [Missilery PRD-80A](https://en.missilery.info/missile/r3c/5-ots)
- Motor class: one solid grain, one nozzle, no sustain or second pulse described. Boost-only. [published] — [Missilery PRD-80A](https://en.missilery.info/missile/r3c/5-ots)
- Average thrust [derived], using impulse ÷ printed burn time. These are not printed averages. With the motor-page floor of 38100 N·s: at +60°C, 38100 / 1.7 = 22412 N; at −54°C, 38100 / 3.2 = 11906 N. With the missile-page band 38000–40000 N·s the same burn times give about 22350–23530 N hot and 11880–12500 N cold. The printed 25000 N maximum sits above those averages, so 25000 N is a peak or a rated maximum, not the time average. A check only: 38100 / (20.5 × 9.80665) ≈ 190 s effective Isp. That was not published.
- Smoke signature class: [unknown]. An orange tracer burns at least 23 s, lit by the jet. [published] — [Missilery PRD-80A](https://en.missilery.info/missile/r3c/5-ots)
- Maximum speed: Weaponsystems prints 550 m/s “max”, with no altitude. The Missilery characteristics block does not give missile speed. It gives target speed 800–1600 km/h (0.65–1.3 M) and target altitude 0–21.5 km. [published] — [Weaponsystems R-3](https://weaponsystems.net/system/1536-Vympel+R-3); [Missilery R-3S](https://en.missilery.info/missile/r3c)
- Range: characteristics line “Start range at equal speeds, km: 7.6”. Aspect sector printed as “1/4 – 3/4” (Soviet aspect quarters; 0/4 is tail, 4/4 is head-on, so this is rear hemisphere through forward of the beam, not a head-on shot). The 22 August 1959 decree is quoted as launch ranges 0.4–7.6 km, altitude 21.5 km, and “course angle of 65-70” without a unit diagram. Narrative launch limits: carrier 900–2200 km/h, altitudes up to 20 km. Weaponsystems: maximum 7.6 km, minimum 0.9 km, and separately “0.9 to 7 km max, 2 km effective.” GlobalSecurity, in an unsourced limitations list with OCR damage: minimum 3280 ft (1000 m); maximum “dependent on launch aircraft speed and quantity of target radiation.” Vietnam narrative on Missilery: most 1966–67 launches from the rear at 1200–2500 m; only 5% beyond 2500 m. No-escape range: [unknown]. [published] — [Missilery R-3S](https://en.missilery.info/missile/r3c); [Weaponsystems R-3](https://weaponsystems.net/system/1536-Vympel+R-3); [old Weaponsystems K-13](https://old.weaponsystems.net/weaponsystem/HH07%20-%20AA-2%20Atoll.html); [GlobalSecurity AA-2](https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/world/russia/aa-2.htm)
- Load factor: Missilery does not print a missile g limit. It prints a launch veto: if carrier load factor while tracking exceeds 2 g, launch is prohibited; above 12 km the veto is already 1.6 g, because “the maneuverability of the missile was falling.” GlobalSecurity’s unsourced list, with obvious OCR (“lOG”, “I4G”), says the missile “can make lOG maneuvers at sea level and I4G maneuvers at 50,000 feet,” and that below 47,500 ft the aircraft must be under 2 g while above 47,500 ft the aircraft limit is 6 g. The 6 g high-altitude aircraft limit contradicts the Missilery 1.6 g figure. Treat 10 g / 14 g as low-confidence secondary text, not a manual number. [published, conflicting] — [Missilery R-3S](https://en.missilery.info/missile/r3c); [GlobalSecurity AA-2](https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/world/russia/aa-2.htm)
- Control: canard (“duck”). Triangular rudders, maximum steering angle ±18°. No thrust vectoring. Roll is limited by gyroscopic rollerons on the fixed rear wings, not by a roll autopilot. There is no overload-feedback autopilot; feedback is the hinge moment of the rudder. High-g authority at low speed during the burn is not claimed. The opposite is implied: maneuverability falls with altitude, and launch is vetoed when the fighter is already pulling about 2 g. [published] — [Missilery R-3S](https://en.missilery.info/missile/r3c); [Missilery steering section](https://en.missilery.info/missile/r3c/2-ots)
- Seeker: passive infrared head called 451-K on the Missilery assembly list. Weaponsystems names the seeker TGS-13K and says uncooled. The opened seeker page never mentions a coolant. Detector is a lead-sulfide photoresistor, peak sensitivity at 2.4–2.5 µm, germanium filter cutting wavelengths up to 1.8 µm including visible light. Spin-scan: optics and a modulating disc on the gyro rotor; amplitude of the pulses is proportional to boresight error; phase gives the clock angle. Carrier tuned near 800 Hz, envelope near 66 Hz. [published] — [Missilery seeker](https://en.missilery.info/missile/r3c/1-ots); [Weaponsystems R-3](https://weaponsystems.net/system/1536-Vympel+R-3)
- Gimbal: “Full axle angle TGSN ±28°” (half-angle off the missile axis, not a full cone). “Tracking field … (cone with an angle at the apex) 50°” (the page’s wording is a full cone angle of 50°, so ±25° if read that way). “Head clamping field (cone with angle at top) 3°30′.” These three angles are not the same quantity. Threshold sensitivity “not exceeding 0.03 mW/cm2”. [published] — [Missilery seeker](https://en.missilery.info/missile/r3c/1-ots)
- Track rate in deg/s: [unknown]. The seeker page prints gyro rotor speed as “72±5” when power is applied and “not less than 50” at 21 s, labeled rpm. That is not a plausible spin-scan rate and is probably a dropped “thousand.” Do not use 72 rpm. The same missile page says rollerons spin at 40–60 thousand rpm, which shows the translator sometimes keeps the word “thousand.” [published as printed, not usable]
- Acquisition: “Maximum range of target acquisition (IL-28 type) at an altitude of more than 15 km with a 0/4, m angle: 7600” m. That is a tail-aspect bomber, high altitude, not a fighter head-on range. Weaponsystems adds “Lock range about 3.5 km” with no target or aspect. Rear-aspect only. Head-on fighter range: [unknown]. The seeker must be at least 20° from the sun; GlobalSecurity’s list says it “cannot guide within 30 degrees of the sun” and is in “great difficulty from 30 to 50 degrees.” Outside cloud. [published, conflicting sun angle] — [Missilery seeker](https://en.missilery.info/missile/r3c/1-ots); [Missilery R-3S](https://en.missilery.info/missile/r3c); [Weaponsystems R-3](https://weaponsystems.net/system/1536-Vympel+R-3); [GlobalSecurity AA-2](https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/world/russia/aa-2.htm)
- Lock-before-launch: yes. The pilot must point the aircraft so that “deviation of the longitudinal axis … should not exceed a few degrees.” Lock is a tone in the headphones. A range gate from the radar or a radio altimeter/rangefinder lights a launch cue; too close lights a break-off cue. Not lock-on after launch. No helmet cue. [published] — [Missilery R-3S](https://en.missilery.info/missile/r3c)
- Guidance delay: “The stabilization system was activated in 0.5-0.7 s after the missile descended from the guide rail.” Guidance law: proportional navigation. After the start button, the control gas generator starts and then the flight motor. Gyro spin is powered from the aircraft before launch and thereafter coasts. [published] — [Missilery R-3S](https://en.missilery.info/missile/r3c); [Missilery seeker](https://en.missilery.info/missile/r3c/1-ots)
- Control endurance: “Time of controlled flight, s: not less than 21.” Steering-section powder gas generator burns “not less than 21” s. Optical fuze self-destructor “21-23 s from the moment the missile is launched.” The narrative also says “21-28c”. [published, 21–23 vs 21–28] — [Missilery R-3S](https://en.missilery.info/missile/r3c); [Missilery steering section](https://en.missilery.info/missile/r3c/2-ots); [Missilery optical fuze](https://en.missilery.info/missile/r3c/4-ots)
- Fuze: optical proximity 454-K between warhead and motor, plus contact fuze I-107. Optical channels look out at 45° and 75° to the axis. Response range 0–9 m at closing speeds 150–3000 m/s. Fuze is armed 0.1–0.8 s after the motor ends, so it is not live during the burn. For a deliberate shot at a heat source on the ground the proximity channel is switched off before launch. [published] — [Missilery optical fuze](https://en.missilery.info/missile/r3c/4-ots); [Missilery R-3S](https://en.missilery.info/missile/r3c)
- IRCCM: none described. Published behavior is a single-band uncooled PbS spin-scan seeker with a modulating disc. Cloud and rain absorb the signal. Fighters were evaded by turning into the sun; the seeker page requires at least 20° from the sun. No rise-rate, two-color, kinematic, or imaging mode is described. Weaponsystems: “easily foiled by infrared flares,” which is that site’s judgment, not a circuit description. [published] — [Missilery seeker](https://en.missilery.info/missile/r3c/1-ots); [Missilery R-3S](https://en.missilery.info/missile/r3c); [Weaponsystems R-3](https://weaponsystems.net/system/1536-Vympel+R-3)
- Launch: rail, APU-13 and the listed APU-13 variants, three lug pairs. Cables are cut by pyrotechnic squibs rather than a slow disconnect. The missile leaves with the aircraft’s speed. Not an eject launch. [published] — [Missilery R-3S](https://en.missilery.info/missile/r3c)
- Flight character: a sim should show a short, hot, boost-only burn, a long unpowered proportional-navigation coast, rear-aspect lock only while the fighter is pointed at the target and not pulling hard, and a seeker that loses the target in cloud or near the sun.

### Inferences

- The 7.6 km figure is a launch-range limit at equal speeds in the official characteristics line, not a vacuum kinematic range and not a no-escape range. Equal speed plus a tail aspect means the missile must make up the geometry on its own energy after a ~2 s burn.
- Because control power is specified for ≥21 s and the motor is over in under about 3 s, almost the entire homing flight is coast. Aero canards with no TVC will have poor authority at the start of the burn and again at the end of the coast. The 2 g / 1.6 g launch veto is the published expression of that.
- 550 m/s is only a catalog maximum. It should not be mixed with the 7.6 km / 21 s pair to invent an average velocity.

### Gaps

- Missile maximum speed and g from the translated manual itself.
- A trustworthy track rate in deg/s. The printed “72±5 rpm” gyro figure should not be used.
- Propellant temperature paired with a thrust-time curve (only two burn times and a max/min thrust are printed).
- Whether any air force still had live R-3S rounds in 2026.
- Helmet cue: none exists for this generation.

## R-13M / R-13M1 (AA-2 Advanced Atoll)

### Takeaway

Cooled, still rear-hemisphere development of the K-13 with a longer motor burn implied by a ~55 s guided-flight time, nitrogen cooling from the launcher, canards and rollerons. Detailed brochure numbers were not on the Missilery R-3S page. Opened secondary pages disagree on mass, span, range, and which “g” they are quoting. Propellant mass, thrust, and burn time were not printed.

### Cited Findings

- Identity: K-13M, object 380, then R-13M. Work began in the late 1960s; “in 1973, the missile successfully passed the tests.” GlobalSecurity: “in 1973 was certified as an operational weapon.” It “has a cooled homing head, a radio rather than optical closing-in igniter, and a more potent warhead.” The next article is K-13M1 “with modified controls” / “a modified steering apparatus.” [published] — [Missilery R-3S](https://en.missilery.info/missile/r3c); [GlobalSecurity AA-2](https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/world/russia/aa-2.htm)
- NATO: old Weaponsystems says AA-2D Advanced Atoll, and that AA-2-2 is also used. [published] — [old Weaponsystems K-13](https://old.weaponsystems.net/weaponsystem/HH07%20-%20AA-2%20Atoll.html)
- Service entry used by catalogs: R-13M introduced 1974, R-13M1 introduced 1976, on that one page. That 1974 date is a year after the 1973 certification sentence above. [published, conflicting year] — [old Weaponsystems K-13](https://old.weaponsystems.net/weaponsystem/HH07%20-%20AA-2%20Atoll.html)
- 2026 status: [unknown]. No opened page says retired or still issued in 2026. The May 2025 Eduard note describes the weapon historically (“was used by interceptor aircraft”).
- R-13M geometry and mass, Eduard, May 2025, a scale-modeling technical note rather than a brochure: length 2870 mm, diameter 127 mm, span 632 mm, front-fin span 420 mm, weight 90 kg, warhead 11.3 kg, explosive “approx. 4 kg of phlegmatized octogen with aluminum powder” (HMX). Guided flight endurance 55 s. [published] — [Info Eduard, May 2025, p. 10](https://info.eduard.com/en/05-2025-1/strana-10)
- Same missile on old Weaponsystems, with a unit typo “2.875 mm” that cannot be literal: body 127 mm, wingspan 632 mm, weight 87.7 kg, warhead 11.3 kg HE-frag, motor “DWP-240” single-stage solid, speed 550 m/s max, range “0.9 to 15 km max, 3 km effective,” tail aspect only, altitude 50 m to 21 km, “up to 7 G maneuvres.” [published] — [old Weaponsystems K-13](https://old.weaponsystems.net/weaponsystem/HH07%20-%20AA-2%20Atoll.html). Mass 87.7 kg vs Eduard 90 kg. The 7 g line is not labeled as launch g or missile g.
- R-13M1 on that same Weaponsystems page only: body 127 mm, wingspan 651 mm, length printed “2.876 mm” (same unit error), weight 90.6 kg, warhead 11.3 kg, DWP-240, speed 550 m/s max, range “0.3 to 17 km max, 3 km effective,” tail aspect only, 50 m to 25 km, “up to 8 G maneuvres.” [published] — same URL. No second opened page confirmed the 90.6 kg / 17 km / 651 mm set. Eduard’s 632 mm span and 90 kg mass match the R-13M column, not the R-13M1 column.
- Seeker and launch, Eduard: canard, rear stabilizing fins with rollerons, infrared seeker “cooled using nitrogen” from a bottle in the APU-13MT launcher. Launcher weight 56 kg. “Maximum launch overload of 3.7g.” “Launched from a wide range of altitudes when fired from the rear hemisphere.” Day or night, “normal weather,” maneuvering or non-maneuvering targets. Three guides on the motor. [published] — [Info Eduard](https://info.eduard.com/en/05-2025-1/strana-10)
- Old Weaponsystems qualitative seeker line: cooled, “more accurate and somewhat more resistant to countermeasures” than R-3S, “still a tail engagement missile only,” motor “burns longer.” [published] — [old Weaponsystems K-13](https://old.weaponsystems.net/weaponsystem/HH07%20-%20AA-2%20Atoll.html)
- Fuze: GlobalSecurity says radio proximity instead of the R-3S optical fuze, plus the implication of a contact function. Eduard says a proximity fuze and detonation on direct contact, without the word “radio.” [published] — [GlobalSecurity AA-2](https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/world/russia/aa-2.htm); [Info Eduard](https://info.eduard.com/en/05-2025-1/strana-10)
- Propellant mass, impulse, thrust, burn time, missile maximum speed from a manual, gimbal angle, track rate, acquisition range, lethal radius, TVC: [unknown].
- The 550 m/s maximum is identical, on Weaponsystems, to the R-3S figure on the same page. It is not an independent measured condition. Do not treat it as a documented R-13M top speed.
- IRCCM: only “somewhat more resistant,” with no circuit class. Not two-color or imaging in any opened page. Rear-aspect only, so not all-aspect.
- Lock-before-launch is implied by a cooled seeker used like the Atoll, but a lock time, tone, or LOAL mode was not printed. Helmet cue: [unknown], and no helmet system is named.
- Launch is a rail (APU-13MT), so the round inherits aircraft velocity. Not described as an eject launch.

### Inferences

- A 55 s guided-flight time against the R-3S figure of about 21 s is the quantitative support for “the new rocket motor burns longer.” It is control duration, not burn time. Burn time remains [unknown], so propellant mass cannot be derived.
- “More potent warhead” (GlobalSecurity) and Eduard’s “approx. 4 kg” HMX fill can sit inside the same 11.3 kg total that Weaponsystems still prints. Do not invent a new total warhead mass.
- High-g control at low speed is unlikely: canards and rollerons, no TVC, and the only explicit g number tied to launch is 3.7 g of carrier load factor.

### Gaps

- A manufacturer or manual table that separates R-13M from R-13M1 on length, span, mass, and range. The opened numbers disagree (87.7 vs 90 kg; 15 vs an unspecified Eduard range; 7 g unlabeled vs 3.7 g launch).
- Seeker gimbal, track rate, and acquisition range. Names such as Iney-M appeared in search snippets of pages that did not open cleanly and are not used here.
- Motor PRD-240 / DWP-240 thrust, burn time, and propellant mass.
- Missile g after launch, as distinct from the 3.7 g launch limit.
- In-service status in 2026.
- Minimum range is 0.9 km (R-13M) or 0.3 km (R-13M1) only on Weaponsystems. Eduard does not give one.

## R-60 and R-60M (AA-8 Aphid)

### Takeaway

Very light canard missile, rail-launched, adopted 18 December 1973. The basic Komar seeker is uncooled, cued only to ±12°, and tracks at 35°/s. R-60M adds a cooled seeker, ±20° cue, a 3.5 kg warhead, and a limited front-hemisphere shot in a close fight. The advertised in-flight load factor is 47 g on Missilery and 42 g on Weaponsystems, with no TVC, so that g is an aero number at speed, not a low-speed TVC number. Propellant mass and burn time were not printed. Practical range is a few kilometres even though the high-altitude bracket reaches 8 km.

### Cited Findings

- Country and names: USSR, NPO Molniya, general designer M.R. Bisnovat. R-60, item 62. NATO AA-8 Aphid. Export of the basic round is called both R-60K and R-60MK on the same Missilery page (the page uses both). [published] — [Missilery R-60](https://en.missilery.info/missile/r60); Russian text of the same article [Ракетная техника](https://missilery.info/missile/r60)
- Dates: ground tracking tests began 1971; adopted 18 December 1973. Weaponsystems: entered service 1974; R-60 production from 1973; R-60M production from 1982. NATO on that page: R-60 Aphid-A, R-60M Aphid-B. Article index izdeliye 62 and 62M. [published] — [Missilery R-60](https://en.missilery.info/missile/r60); [Weaponsystems R-60](https://weaponsystems.net/system/218-Molniya%20R-60)
- 2026 status: Weaponsystems status line is “In service” and “remains in widespread active use around the world,” while saying the R-73 has supplemented it. No opened 2026 Russian order of battle. [published] — [Weaponsystems R-60](https://weaponsystems.net/system/218-Molniya%20R-60)

### Inferences

- Wingspan and rudder span are different. GlobalSecurity’s 0.77 m wing and 0.97 m rudder match Missilery’s 972 mm “plumage” better as the rudder. Using 972 mm as the wing span would be the wrong surface.
- The ET’s extra length (about 4.5 m versus about 3.8 m) and extra mass (343 versus 245 kg) are the larger motor, not a larger warhead. The warhead stays 39 kg.
- “Double-mode” in this family’s English text is the extended motor, two thrust regimes in one burn, as opposed to the single-mode 230 mm motor. A dual-pulse coast-then-relight was not described and should not be simulated.
- Front-versus-rear range ratios on the GlobalSecurity table (50/15 km for T1, 84/20 km for ET1) are the useful sim split. They still lack shooter Mach and altitude. The 65 km and 80 km lines are front-hemisphere advertising ranges, not tail ranges and not no-escape ranges.
- Because the catapult is reserved for radar seekers, an R-27T/ET shot should start at fighter speed on a rail. A loft should not be added unless a later source says the infrared round flies the inertial profile. The opened family paragraph allows a launch with lock after launch, but it does not publish the trajectory shape.

### Gaps

- Thrust, burn time, and propellant mass for both motors, so no impulse can be derived.
- Whether R-27T/ET actually carry the radio-correction receiver or only the infrared seeker and autopilot.
- A published loft angle or midcourse altitude.
- 36T lock ranges, track rate, and gimbal stop.
- IRCCM technique.
- Missile maximum speed condition for the single printed M 4.5 / Mach 4 figures.
- Which range row is kinematic maximum, which is a tactical launch limit, and the altitude and Mach that go with each.
- 2026 Russian service quantity, as opposed to “still catalogued and sold through at least 2019.”

## Figures that must not be invented

### Takeaway

Propellant mass is published only for the R-3S (20.5 kg). Thrust and burn time are published only for the R-3S, plus one unlabeled 7700 N for the R-73. R-74M2 has no opened performance card. Rear-hemisphere Atoll rounds must not be given all-aspect seekers, and Alamo infrared rounds must not be given Archer agility.

### Cited Findings

- R-3S impulse and the two burn times are the only motor pair complete enough to derive an average thrust, and that derivation is in the R-3S section. [published inputs] — [Missilery PRD-80A](https://en.missilery.info/missile/r3c/5-ots)
- No opened page printed a no-escape range, a TVC vane angle, or an R-74M2 brochure range or speed.

### Inferences

- For R-60, R-13, R-73, RVV-MD, and R-27 the sim should use a rocket whose total impulse is an input the user sets, not a value from this note. The published constraints worth enforcing are the control time, the launch-range brackets, the seeker angles, and whether TVC exists only during the burn (R-73 and RVV-MD) or not at all (R-3S, R-13, R-60, R-27T/ET).

### Gaps

- Primary thrust-time curves, grain masses, and seeker track rates for every round except the R-3S motor and seeker manual extract.
- A fetched copy of the Wikipedia footnote behind the R-74M2 434 mm claim, and a fetched copy of the 2023 KTRV/Army-forum description of that missile.
- Helmet-sight angles for Shchel-3UM, Sura, and later Russian sights. The missiles accept designation from a helmet on the R-73 and R-27 pages; the sight’s own angle was not printed.
